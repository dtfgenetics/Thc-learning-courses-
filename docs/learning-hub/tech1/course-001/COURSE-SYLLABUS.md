## Course description

Safety, Responsible Practice & Cultivation Workflows prepares an entry cultivation technician to perform assigned work safely and reliably inside a controlled cultivation operation. The course emphasizes workplace judgment: recognizing hazards; using supplied PPE, labels and safety information; protecting sanitation and biosecurity boundaries; working from current controlled instructions; preserving plant and material identity; maintaining traceable inventory and waste records; staying inside operator-care authority; creating trustworthy records; and transferring unresolved conditions through a professional handoff.

This is an academic course and performance-evidence package. Completing it does not by itself issue the THC Cultivation Technician I professional credential, a government license, pesticide-applicator authority, respirator clearance, electrical/HVAC authorization, hazardous-energy servicing authority, supervisory authority, or jurisdiction-specific seed-to-sale certification.

## Intended learners

The course is designed for:

- entry-level or aspiring cultivation technicians;
- cultivation employees who need a structured foundation in controlled work practices;
- learners preparing for later Technician I credential requirements; and
- instructors or employers using the public academic material for structured training.

No prior THC Academy professional credential is required to study the public course. A workplace, supervised lab, or jurisdiction may impose its own eligibility, age, employment, PPE, medical-clearance, pesticide, equipment or access requirements; those requirements remain controlling.

## Recommended preparation

Learners should be able to read workplace instructions, interpret basic tables and labels, enter simple records and use a modern web browser. No prior professional cultivation employment is assumed. Learners should review local facility rules before applying course examples in an actual workplace.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of Course 1, the learner should be able to:

1. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-01`** — Identify common cultivation workplace hazards and select the correct routine response: control within authorization, stop, isolate where authorized, or escalate.
2. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-02`** — Use supplied PPE requirements, chemical labels and safety data sheet information to prepare for an assigned cultivation task without exceeding training or authorization.
3. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-03`** — Execute sanitation and controlled-movement procedures that reduce cross-contamination risk between plants, rooms, tools and work zones.
4. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-04`** — Recognize quarantine, restricted-entry, hold and isolation conditions and apply the response defined by the supplied procedure.
5. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-05`** — Identify the correct controlled SOP or work order for a task and distinguish required steps, acceptance criteria, exceptions and escalation points.
6. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-06`** — Preserve plant, batch, lot, room and material identity during routine cultivation movements, transfers and handoffs.
7. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-07`** — Complete routine inventory, movement and waste or destruction records accurately under a supplied vendor-neutral workflow.
8. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-08`** — Detect and report a mismatch between physical material and a paper or digital record without falsifying or forcing reconciliation.
9. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-09`** — Perform permitted pre-use, readiness, cleaning and operator-care checks on assigned cultivation or irrigation equipment using the supplied procedure.
10. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-10`** — Recognize obvious equipment faults, alarms, leaks, clogs or unsafe conditions and produce a useful escalation record without unauthorized repair.
11. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-11`** — Create contemporaneous, legible and attributable cultivation records and correct errors while preserving the original record history.
12. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-12`** — Produce a concise shift handoff that accurately communicates completed work, observations, deviations, unresolved conditions and priorities.

These outcomes map to the six controlled Course 1 competency areas: cultivation workplace safety; cultivation biosecurity; SOP/work-instruction execution; professional quality/documentation; cultivation traceability/inventory; and cultivation equipment readiness/operator care.

## Course sequence

1. **Module 1 — Applied Cultivation Workplace Safety** — hazard recognition, control hierarchy, PPE/HazCom decisions, authorization boundaries and escalation.
2. **Module 2 — Sanitation, Biosecurity & Controlled Movement** — contamination pathways, cleaning/sanitation sequence, quarantine/hold/restricted-entry distinctions and controlled movement.
3. **Module 3 — SOPs, Work Orders, Authority & Escalation** — controlled revisions, task identity, acceptance criteria, exceptions, deviations, incomplete work and truthful status.
4. **Module 4 — Traceability, Material Movement, Inventory & Waste** — identity, genealogy, movement events, reconciliation, waste/disposition records and discrepancy handling.
5. **Module 5 — Equipment Readiness, Operator Care & Fault Reporting** — pre-use readiness, operator-care boundaries, recurring faults, observation versus diagnosis and escalation records.
6. **Module 6 — Records, Shift Handoff & Integrated Workflow** — data integrity, corrections, late entries, reconstructable records, closed-loop handoff and integrated shift reasoning.
7. **Integrated Course 1 practical**.
8. **Course 1 summative final**.

Six formative module assessments are authored and learner-accessible as low-stakes retrieval and remediation checkpoints. The current forms contain 14 items each, but the item inventory and future form depth remain expandable rather than capped at 14. They provide immediate explanatory feedback and are not professional credential evidence.

## Learning experience model

Every module should follow the same high-quality learning cycle so the course teaches judgment instead of rewarding passive reading:

1. **Orient** — explain why the topic matters in cultivation work and what decision the learner will be able to make.
2. **Explain** — teach the scientific, safety, quality or human-factors mechanism behind the procedure.
3. **Show** — pair the concept with a meaningful diagram, annotated photograph, workflow, comparison plate or document example.
4. **Model** — walk through at least one worked example that shows expert reasoning step by step.
5. **Practice with support** — use guided sorting, labeling, sequencing, record review or scenario decisions with immediate feedback.
6. **Retrieve from memory** — use short no-notes checks so the learner must reconstruct the key idea rather than reread it.
7. **Apply in context** — complete a realistic cultivation task, packet, handoff, inspection, traceability exercise or decision scenario.
8. **Reflect and remediate** — explain why an incorrect choice fails, route the learner back to the exact concept, and require another application when needed.
9. **Integrate** — connect the topic to earlier modules so safety, biosecurity, controlled work, traceability, equipment and records operate as one system.

The target is deliberate practice, not page count. A learner should repeatedly **observe → interpret → decide → act within authority → document → verify/handoff**.

### Visual learning standard

Visuals are instructional objects, not decoration. A final course visual must do at least one of the following:

- reveal a structure, pathway or mechanism that is difficult to understand from prose alone;
- train recognition of a real cultivation condition;
- compare correct and incorrect states;
- show sequence, causality or decision logic;
- make records, labels, equipment or process relationships easier to inspect;
- support a practical exercise that asks the learner to identify or decide something.

Photorealistic imagery is preferred for observation training. Scientific diagrams, process flows, reference charts and annotated document examples are preferred for abstract mechanisms and controlled workflows. Every meaningful visual requires accessible alt text and a nearby explanation of what the learner should notice.

## Assessment and grading

### Lesson practice

Lesson practice is low stakes and designed for retrieval, immediate feedback and remediation. It supports learning but is not final academic or professional credential evidence.

### Module assessments

Course 1 contains six learner-accessible formative module assessments, one per module. Current forms contain 14 items, while the underlying item inventory and future form depth remain expandable. The learner runtime presents these as shuffled low-stakes checkpoints with immediate explanatory feedback, remediation guidance and retry support. Module results support learning and readiness for later course-level assessment; they do not independently count as professional credential evidence.

### Course final

The current public Course 1 final contains 36 controlled summative items and is scored server-side. Answer keys and rationales are not returned in the live final-assessment payload. The current academic threshold is **80% overall**.

That 80% value is a **provisional development/academic threshold pending pilot evidence and formal standard setting**. It is not an externally validated professional-certification cut score. No additional competency-floor rule is currently approved for Course 1.

The public Course 1 final is separate from the secure THC Cultivation Technician I credential assessment.

### Integrated practical

The practical is scored across eight domains for 100 points. The current academic rule uses an **80% provisional overall threshold plus zero critical errors**. Operational certification use remains blocked until assessor calibration and validation requirements are completed.

The current domain point totals are controlled by the canonical practical object. Assessor guidance is being refined to improve point-level inter-rater consistency before formal calibration.

## Academic integrity and trustworthy work

Learners are expected to submit their own assessed responses and practical evidence unless an activity explicitly permits collaboration. Academic and operational integrity includes:

- no impersonation or submission of another person's assessed work;
- no fabrication or falsification of observations, measurements, identities, records or evidence;
- no unauthorized answer-key use or sharing during a controlled assessment;
- no presenting invented values as contemporaneous observations; and
- clear attribution when instructor, peer, accessibility or approved technical assistance is used.

These rules do not prohibit legitimate study groups, instructor coaching, accessible presentation or practice with public course materials. The boundary is whether assistance changes or substitutes for the learner performance being assessed.

## Safety, legal and scope boundary

Course material is educational and does not replace workplace procedures, product labels, equipment manuals, occupational-health requirements, pesticide directions, medical clearance or jurisdiction-specific law. Learners must follow the more specific controlling requirement when it applies.

Course examples intentionally distinguish technician observation and escalation from duties that require additional authorization or expertise. The course does not authorize protected equipment servicing, electrical/HVAC repair, pesticide application outside applicable requirements, or release/disposition decisions assigned to another role.

## Evidence and source policy

Course 1 uses controlled references appropriate to each topic. Principal source families include OSHA and NIOSH occupational-safety material, EPA Worker Protection Standard material where pesticide worker-entry concepts are discussed, university-extension sanitation/biosecurity guidance, GS1 traceability concepts, ASTM cannabis/hemp QMS context, HSE handoff/human-factors guidance, and WHO/MHRA data-integrity guidance used explicitly as transferable quality guidance rather than as U.S. cannabis law.

Facility SOPs, product labels, equipment manuals and jurisdiction-specific rules control where they are more specific or impose additional requirements. The course must not convert a transferable quality framework into an unsupported legal claim.
