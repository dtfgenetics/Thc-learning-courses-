## Course description

Safety, Responsible Practice & Cultivation Workflows prepares an entry cultivation technician to perform assigned work safely and reliably inside a controlled cultivation operation. The course emphasizes workplace judgment: recognizing hazards; using supplied PPE, labels, and safety information; protecting sanitation and biosecurity boundaries; working from current controlled instructions; preserving plant and material identity; maintaining traceable inventory and waste records; staying inside operator-care authority; creating trustworthy records; and transferring unresolved conditions through a professional handoff.

This is an academic course and performance-evidence package. Completing it does not by itself issue the THC Cultivation Technician I professional credential, a government license, pesticide-applicator authority, respirator clearance, electrical/HVAC authorization, hazardous-energy servicing authority, supervisory authority, or jurisdiction-specific seed-to-sale certification.

## Intended learners

The course is designed for:

- entry-level or aspiring cultivation technicians;
- cultivation employees who need a structured foundation in controlled work practices;
- learners preparing for later Technician I credential requirements; and
- instructors or employers using the public academic material for structured training.

No prior THC Academy professional credential is required to study the public course. A workplace, supervised lab, or jurisdiction may impose its own eligibility, age, employment, PPE, medical-clearance, pesticide, equipment, or access requirements; those local requirements remain controlling.

## Recommended preparation

Learners should be able to read workplace instructions, interpret basic tables and labels, enter simple records, and use a modern web browser. No prior professional cultivation employment is assumed. Learners should review local facility rules before applying course examples in an actual workplace.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of Course 1, the learner should be able to:

1. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-01`** — Identify common cultivation workplace hazards and select the correct routine response: control within authorization, stop, isolate where authorized, or escalate.
2. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-02`** — Use supplied PPE requirements, chemical labels, and safety data sheet information to prepare for an assigned cultivation task without exceeding training or authorization.
3. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-03`** — Execute sanitation and controlled-movement procedures that reduce cross-contamination risk between plants, rooms, tools, and work zones.
4. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-04`** — Recognize quarantine, restricted-entry, hold, and isolation conditions and apply the response defined by the supplied procedure.
5. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-05`** — Identify the correct controlled SOP or work order for a task and distinguish required steps, acceptance criteria, exceptions, and escalation points.
6. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-06`** — Preserve plant, batch, lot, room, and material identity during routine cultivation movements, transfers, and handoffs.
7. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-07`** — Complete routine inventory, movement, and waste or destruction records accurately under a supplied vendor-neutral workflow.
8. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-08`** — Detect and report a mismatch between physical material and a paper or digital record without falsifying or forcing reconciliation.
9. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-09`** — Perform permitted pre-use, readiness, cleaning, and operator-care checks on assigned cultivation or irrigation equipment using the supplied procedure.
10. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-10`** — Recognize obvious equipment faults, alarms, leaks, clogs, or unsafe conditions and produce a useful escalation record without unauthorized repair.
11. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-11`** — Create contemporaneous, legible, and attributable cultivation records and correct errors while preserving the original record history.
12. **`LO-LH-TECH1-001-12`** — Produce a concise shift handoff that accurately communicates completed work, observations, deviations, unresolved conditions, and priorities.

These outcomes map to the six published Course 1 competencies: Cultivation Workplace Safety; Cultivation Biosecurity; SOP and Work Instruction Execution; Professional Quality and Documentation; Cultivation Traceability and Inventory; and Cultivation Equipment Readiness and Operator Care.

## Course sequence

1. **Module 1 — Applied Cultivation Workplace Safety**  
   Hazard recognition, control hierarchy, PPE/HazCom decisions, authorization boundaries, and escalation.
2. **Module 2 — Sanitation, Biosecurity & Controlled Movement**  
   Contamination pathways, cleaning/sanitation sequence, quarantine/hold/restricted-entry distinctions, and controlled movement.
3. **Module 3 — SOPs, Work Orders, Authority & Escalation**  
   Controlled revisions, task identity, acceptance criteria, exceptions, deviations, incomplete work, and truthful status.
4. **Module 4 — Traceability, Material Movement, Inventory & Waste**  
   Identity, genealogy, movement events, reconciliation, waste/disposition records, and discrepancy handling.
5. **Module 5 — Equipment Readiness, Operator Care & Fault Reporting**  
   Pre-use readiness, operator-care boundaries, recurring faults, observation versus diagnosis, and escalation records.
6. **Module 6 — Records, Shift Handoff & Integrated Workflow**  
   Data integrity, corrections, late entries, reconstructable records, closed-loop handoff, and integrated shift reasoning.
7. **Integrated Course 1 practical**
8. **Course 1 summative final**

## Assessment and grading

### Formative practice

Lesson and module practice is designed for retrieval, feedback, and remediation. Formative work supports learning but does not by itself constitute the final academic completion decision. Learners should use feedback to revisit aligned lessons and Field References before summative attempts.

### Course final

The current Course 1 final contains 36 items sampled from the published final form and is scored server-side. The learner must:

- achieve at least **80% overall**; and
- meet every configured competency minimum:
  - Cultivation Workplace Safety — **70%**
  - Cultivation Biosecurity — **60%**
  - SOP and Work Instruction Execution — **60%**
  - Professional Quality and Documentation — **65%**
  - Cultivation Traceability and Inventory — **65%**
  - Cultivation Equipment Readiness and Operator Care — **60%**

The competency floors prevent a serious weakness in a high-consequence area from being hidden by stronger scores elsewhere. The learner result reports competency and learning-objective performance without publishing the answer key.

Multiple-response items may receive partial credit under the current grader policy. Numeric items may use explicit tolerances or accepted ranges when the canonical item defines them. The scoring algorithm and policy are versioned so the basis of an attempt remains reconstructable.

### Integrated practical

The practical is scored across eight domains for 100 points. The current academic passing rule requires:

- at least **80% overall**;
- every configured practical-domain minimum;
- **zero critical errors**;
- every required evidence output reviewed; and
- every required evidence output verified for a passing decision.

The eight domains are Safety/PPE/stop-work judgment; Sanitation and biosecurity; SOP/work-order execution; Identity and traceability; Inventory/discrepancy reasoning; Equipment readiness/operator-care boundary; Data integrity/documentation; and Shift handoff/communication.

Evaluators use the published four-level anchored rubric: Strong, Competent, Developing, and Insufficient. The server calculates the authoritative practical result. A high point total cannot override a critical error, failed domain floor, or unverified required evidence.

Course 1 does not impose an artificial lifetime attempt maximum. The current course final has no fixed attempt cap or cooldown. ## Academic integrity and trustworthy work

Learners are expected to submit their own assessed responses and practical evidence unless an activity explicitly permits collaboration. Academic and operational integrity includes:

- no impersonation or submission of another person's assessed work;
- no fabrication or falsification of observations, measurements, identities, records, or evidence;
- no unauthorized answer-key use or sharing during a controlled assessment;
- no presenting invented values as contemporaneous observations; and
- clear attribution when instructor, peer, accessibility, or approved technical assistance is used.

Course integrity rules do not prohibit legitimate study groups, instructor coaching, accessible presentation, or practice with public course materials. The boundary is whether assistance changes or substitutes for the learner performance being assessed.

## Safety, legal, and scope boundary

Course material is educational and does not replace workplace procedures, product labels, equipment manuals, occupational-health requirements, pesticide directions, medical clearance, or jurisdiction-specific law. Learners must follow the more specific controlling requirement when it applies.

Course examples intentionally distinguish technician observation and escalation from duties that require additional authorization or expertise. The course does not authorize protected equipment servicing, electrical/HVAC repair, pesticide application outside applicable requirements, or release/disposition decisions assigned to another role.

## Evidence and source policy

Course 1 uses controlled references appropriate to each topic. Primary source families include OSHA and NIOSH occupational-safety guidance, EPA Worker Protection Standard materials where pesticide worker-entry concepts are discussed, university-extension sanitation/biosecurity guidance, GS1 traceability concepts, HSE handoff/human-factors guidance, and MHRA data-integrity guidance used explicitly as transferable quality guidance rather than as U.S. cannabis law.

Facility SOPs, product labels, equipment manuals, and jurisdiction-specific rules control where they are more specific or impose additional requirements. The course should never convert a transferable quality framework into an unsupported legal claim.

## Credential boundary

Passing Course 1 creates academic course evidence only. It does **not** automatically award THC Cultivation Technician I. The professional credential has separate eligibility, secure assessment, governance, decision, issuance, and verification requirements. Course 1 learner content and results must remain separate from secure credential examination keys and signing material.
