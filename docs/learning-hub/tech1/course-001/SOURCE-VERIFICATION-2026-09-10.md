# Course 1 Source Verification — 2026-09-10

**Course:** `COURSE-LH-TECH1-001 — Safety, Responsible Practice & Cultivation Workflows`

This record verifies the principal external educational sources used in the Course 1 production package. Verification means the source was located on the authoritative publisher/agency site and its instructional role was checked. It does not convert transferred quality/human-factors guidance into cannabis law.

## Verified current/authoritative sources

### NIOSH — cannabis workplace hazards

CDC/NIOSH continues to publish `Workplace Safety and Health Hazards | Cannabis` (dated September 3, 2024). It supports the course's cannabis-specific occupational-hazard framing, including chemical, biological, ergonomic/physical, and general safety risks.

### NIOSH — work-related asthma

CDC/NIOSH published `Work-related Asthma: Early Recognition and Prevention` on May 4, 2026. It specifically discusses reported work-related asthma cases in the legalized cannabis industry and supports early recognition, exposure-control, reporting, and medical-evaluation boundaries. It does not support diagnosing an individual learner or worker.

### OSHA — walking-working surfaces and PPE

OSHA 29 CFR 1910.22 remains the authoritative general requirement for clean/orderly walking-working surfaces, correction/guarding of hazardous conditions, and qualified structural repair. OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132 remains the general PPE framework requiring employer hazard assessment and task-appropriate PPE selection.

Course use remains limited to workplace-safety principles and role boundaries; exact applicability depends on the workplace and jurisdiction.

### OSHA — Hazard Communication Standard transition

OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1200 remains the controlling federal Hazard Communication Standard used by Course 1 for label/SDS, workplace-labeling, written-program and employee-information/training concepts. OSHA's May 20, 2024 final rule updated the standard, and OSHA extended the phase-in dates again on January 15, 2026.

As verified on September 13, 2026, OSHA's current regulatory text provides that chemical manufacturers, importers and distributors evaluating substances were required to comply with the modified provisions by May 19, 2026. Employers must, as necessary, update alternative workplace labeling, their hazard communication program and additional employee training for newly identified hazards associated with substances by November 20, 2026. The corresponding mixture deadlines extend to November 19, 2027 for manufacturers/importers/distributors and May 19, 2028 for employer labeling/program/training updates. During the applicable transition period, OSHA permits use of the previous HCS, the updated HCS, or both.

Course 1 intentionally teaches durable worker-level HazCom behaviors rather than memorizing transition dates: identify the product and task, use current workplace labels and SDS information, follow the employer's current HazCom program and task procedure, use required PPE, and escalate missing/conflicting information. Facility training materials must nevertheless be checked against the HCS version and transition obligations that apply at the time of delivery.

### EPA — Worker Protection Standard

EPA's Agricultural Worker Protection Standard pages remain current in 2026 and continue to describe restricted-entry intervals, application restrictions, early-entry protections, labeling/PPE requirements, and employer/handler duties. The course correctly teaches these as pesticide-worker-protection controls distinct from plant-health quarantine.

Jurisdiction, product labeling, and the actual covered agricultural use control operational decisions.

### UMass Extension — greenhouse cleaning/disinfection

UMass Amherst's `Cleaning and Disinfecting the Greenhouse` remains available and current. It supports the distinction between physical cleaning and disinfection and the principle that soil/organic residue can reduce disinfectant effectiveness. Product-specific concentrations/contact times are intentionally not generalized in the course; current labels and SOPs control.

### Penn State Extension — sources of plant disease in greenhouses

Penn State Extension's `Sources Of Plant Disease In Greenhouses` (updated November 20, 2023) remains available and supports instruction that contaminated soil/media can be moved by workers, machinery, crates, tools, and hose ends, and that clean material can be recontaminated. Penn State's 2025 greenhouse disease-risk guidance further supports sanitation of tools/equipment and keeping hose ends off potentially contaminated floors.

### EPA — SOP guidance

EPA continues to host `Guidance for Preparing Standard Operating Procedures, EPA QA/G-6`. **Supersession correction (verified September 24, 2026):** EPA's current Quality Program Directives page identifies **EPA/600/B-07/001, April 2007** as the QA/G-6 version that replaces the March 2001 `EPA/240/B-01/004` document. The controlled reference now records the 2007 version. The instructional principles already used by Course 1 remain aligned—organization-specific SOPs, version control, availability to users, records and quality-system consistency—but future reviews must cite the 2007 document rather than the superseded 2001 edition.

### GS1 — traceability

GS1 continues to publish the `GS1 Global Traceability Standard` and current traceability guidance using Critical Tracking Events and Key Data Elements. The course uses these concepts only as a technology-neutral traceability model; it does not substitute GS1 identifiers or processes for state cannabis seed-to-sale rules.

### ASTM D8556-25 — cannabis/hemp QMS

ASTM currently publishes `D8556-25 Standard Practice for Requirements of a Quality Management System for Cannabis and Hemp Operations`. Its public scope/significance explicitly covers cultivation and other cannabis/hemp operations and states that laws/regulations from authorities having jurisdiction supersede the practice.

ASTM also has active work items proposing revisions to D8556-25. These are **work items under development**, not published superseding standards. Course 1 may note the active revision context but must continue to cite the published D8556-25 unless/until a replacement is actually issued.

### WHO — data integrity

WHO continues to list `TRS 1033 - Annex 4: WHO Guideline on data integrity` dated October 10, 2021. Course 1 uses it as a transferable quality/data-integrity model (including contemporaneous, attributable, reconstructable records), not as cannabis law.

### MHRA — GxP data integrity

UK MHRA continues to publish its GxP data-integrity guidance (originally published 2018; last updated September 27, 2021). Course 1 uses it only as transferred data-governance guidance.

### HSE — shift handover

UK Health and Safety Executive continues to publish `Shift handover`, supporting outgoing preparation, two-way exchange, and incoming cross-checking. It is used as transferable human-factors guidance, not U.S. cannabis regulation.

### W3C — WCAG 2.2

WCAG 2.2 remains a W3C Recommendation. Course 1's delivery/accessibility plan correctly targets WCAG 2.2 AA for rendered web learning and assessment experiences.

## Verification result

One bibliographic supersession was found and corrected: EPA QA/G-6 now points to the 2007 edition that replaced the 2001 version. No principal Course 1 source was found to be retracted or superseded in a way that invalidates the current instructional claims after that correction. The evidence dossier's source-role boundaries remain appropriate. The active OSHA Hazard Communication transition is now explicitly tracked because employer labeling/program/training obligations can change during the life of this course even when the worker-level teaching principles remain stable.
