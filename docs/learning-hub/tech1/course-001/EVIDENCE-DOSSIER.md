# Evidence dossier — COURSE-LH-TECH1-001

**Course:** Safety, Responsible Practice & Cultivation Workflows  
**Purpose:** record the educational sources used to build Course 1 and the limits on what each source can support.  
**Rule:** a source is not used outside its domain. Jurisdiction-specific law, employer SOPs, product labels and equipment manuals supersede generic instructional examples where applicable.

## Module 1 — Applied cultivation workplace safety

Primary evidence:

- **NIOSH cannabis workplace hazards (2024):** cannabis cultivation/processing can involve chemical, biological, ergonomic/physical and general safety hazards, including CO2, ozone, pesticides, plant allergens, microbes, organic particulate, heat, noise, UV, cuts, electrical issues, fire/explosion, slips/trips/falls and work at height.
- **NIOSH work-related asthma update (2026):** newer surveillance evidence reinforces that cannabis cultivation/processing workers can develop work-related asthma; training should emphasize reporting symptoms and controlling exposures rather than self-diagnosis.
- **OSHA 1910.22:** supports clean/orderly walking-working surfaces, dry conditions where feasible, and control of hazards such as leaks and spills.
- **OSHA 1910.132:** supports employer hazard assessment and selection/use of appropriate PPE.
- **OSHA 1910.1200:** supports workplace labels, SDS access and worker information/training for hazardous chemicals.
- **EPA WPS:** supports pesticide restricted-entry awareness, label-based REIs, notifications and PPE/entry restrictions for covered agricultural pesticide work.

Instructional boundary: the course teaches recognition, supplied controls and escalation. It does not grant respirator clearance, pesticide-handler status, electrician authority or medical diagnosis.

## Module 2 — Sanitation, biosecurity & controlled movement

Primary evidence:

- **UMass greenhouse sanitation:** cleaning physically removes debris/soil/organic matter; cleaning should precede disinfection because organic material can reduce disinfectant effectiveness; greenhouse benches, containers, hose nozzles and irrigation systems can be contamination points; labels and precautions control disinfectant use.
- **Penn State greenhouse disease sources:** contaminated soil/media can be moved on workers, equipment, crates, tools and hose ends; clean media/material can be recontaminated by dirty implements or surfaces.
- **EPA WPS:** where pesticide-treated areas are involved, restricted-entry status remains a separate worker-protection control and must not be confused with plant quarantine.

Instructional boundary: sanitation examples are greenhouse principles. Exact chemical selection, concentration, contact time and equipment compatibility come from current labels/SOPs, not from memorized course recipes.

## Module 3 — SOPs, work orders, authority & escalation

Primary evidence:

- **EPA SOP guidance:** SOPs document routine/repetitive processes, support consistency and data quality, should be organization-specific, detailed enough to repeat the activity, and available to people performing the work.
- **OSHA 1910.147:** provides a strong role-boundary model for hazardous-energy servicing, including affected versus authorized employees and documented energy-control procedures for covered servicing.
- **NIOSH cannabis workplace hazards:** supports cultivation-specific examples that make escalation realistic.

Instructional boundary: EPA QA guidance is a quality-system educational model; OSHA applicability depends on the work and classification. Technician I is intentionally trained to stop at servicing or regulated-work boundaries.

## Module 4 — Traceability, material movement, inventory & waste

Primary evidence:

- **GS1 Global Traceability Standard:** supports technology-neutral identification of traceable objects, capture of movement/events, source/destination/location/time, and the principle that different systems can preserve the same traceability information.
- **WHO data integrity:** supports complete, consistent, accurate, attributable, contemporaneous and reconstructable records.
- **ASTM D8556-25:** confirms that cannabis/hemp operations can be organized under explicit QMS requirements and that such requirements can support training and audit systems.
- **ASTM WK85852:** useful only as evidence that cannabis seed-to-sale traceability remains an active standardization topic. It is a work item, not an approved standard, and is not treated as normative course authority.

Instructional boundary: no single seed-to-sale software platform or state reporting workflow is presented as universal. Jurisdiction-specific inventory, waste and destruction rules require local training.

## Module 5 — Equipment readiness, operator care & fault reporting

Primary evidence:

- **OSHA machine guarding:** moving parts and points of operation can cause serious injury and safeguards are required where hazards exist.
- **OSHA 1910.147:** covered servicing involving unexpected energization or stored-energy release requires energy-control procedures and appropriate authorized roles.
- **NIOSH cannabis workplace hazards:** supports cultivation-specific equipment, ergonomic, electrical and physical hazard context.

Instructional boundary: course examples teach pre-use observation, operator-level checks and maintenance-ready escalation. They do not qualify learners for electrical work, machinery servicing, lockout/tagout authorization, HVAC service or engineering tasks.

## Module 6 — Records, shift handoff & integrated workflow

Primary evidence:

- **WHO data integrity:** supports ALCOA+ concepts, corrections that preserve history, complete contextual records and lifecycle data integrity.
- **HSE shift handover:** supports preparation by outgoing personnel, two-way exchange, incoming cross-checking, use of written and verbal information, and explicit attention to safety-critical or unfinished work.
- **GS1 traceability:** supports identity/location/time/event context in movement records and handoffs.

Instructional boundary: WHO/HSE materials are transferred quality/human-factors models. They do not impose cannabis-specific record formats or U.S. legal requirements.

## Cannabis-specific standards context

ASTM Committee D37 develops cannabis/hemp standards in areas including cultivation, QMS, personnel training/assessment/credentialing and traceability. `ASTM D8556-25` is a published cannabis/hemp QMS practice and is appropriate as a standards-alignment reference. `WK85852` is only a work item; the course may note the direction of standards development but must never label the draft work item as an approved standard.

## Research maintenance rule

Before a lesson is promoted beyond draft:

1. verify each cited government/standards/extension URL still resolves;
2. check whether a newer superseding standard or agency update exists;
3. confirm that a statement drawn from a transferred source is clearly labeled as transferable rather than legally controlling;
4. replace product-specific examples with current label/SOP requirements when learners are expected to act;
5. record major evidence changes in the course review history.
