# Module 3 — SOPs, Work Orders, Authority & Escalation

## Why this module matters

Commercial cultivation is repeatable only when workers can tell what instruction is current, what task is assigned, what evidence shows completion, what to do when conditions differ from plan, and where their authority ends. “I usually do it this way” is not a controlled process.

EPA quality guidance defines SOPs as written instructions for routine or repetitive work and emphasizes consistency, organization-specific detail and accessibility to the people performing the work. This course transfers those quality-system ideas into cultivation without pretending EPA QA guidance is cannabis law.

**Controlled references:** `REF-EPA-SOP-QAG6`, `REF-OSHA-1910-147-LOTO`, `REF-NIOSH-CANNABIS-HAZARDS-2024`, `REF-WHO-DATA-INTEGRITY-2021`.

## Lesson 3.1 — Reading controlled SOPs and work orders

### SOP and work order have different jobs

An SOP defines the standard method within a defined scope. A work order applies controlled work to a specific room, batch, quantity, date, asset or shift. They should agree. If they conflict, the technician should not choose whichever instruction is more convenient.

Before work, identify:

- current document/revision;
- task scope;
- prerequisites and authorization;
- plant/material/room/asset identity;
- required tools/materials;
- sequence;
- acceptance criteria;
- required records;
- exceptions/decision points;
- escalation contact.

### Current copies matter

A printed instruction can look official and still be obsolete. EPA SOP guidance stresses accessibility of current instructions to the people performing the work. If the controlled system says revision 5 is current while a binder contains revision 3, resolve the document-control issue before performing affected steps.

### Acceptance criteria convert “done” into evidence

“Finished” should mean more than the worker reached the end of the page. A controlled task may require a verified count, cleanliness state, measurement, completed record, identity check, supervisor signoff or other acceptance condition.

### Worked case

The work order assigns a plant move to Room 3 but lists a batch identifier currently assigned to Room 4. Do not guess which field is correct. The identity conflict must be resolved before movement.

### Guided practice — mark the instruction

Review a supplied SOP/work order and mark:

- prerequisites;
- actions;
- acceptance criteria;
- records;
- exceptions;
- escalation points.

Then identify one planted inconsistency between the SOP and work order.

### Retrieval check

1. What is the difference between an SOP and a work order?
2. Why can an obsolete instruction be dangerous even if workers know it well?
3. What should happen when two controlled identifiers conflict?

## Lesson 3.2 — Working within authorization

### Capability does not equal permission

A technician may understand what an electrical panel does without being permitted to open it. A worker may recognize a pesticide-related restriction without having handler authority. A technician may identify that a pump has failed without being authorized to service it.

Technician I is intentionally an execution-and-escalation role. The course should reward correct boundaries, not heroic improvisation.

### Affected versus authorized work is a useful model

OSHA’s hazardous-energy standard distinguishes affected employees from authorized employees in covered servicing. The course uses this as a role-boundary teaching model: a worker can be affected by servicing and must understand restrictions without being authorized to perform the energy-control work.

Where the next step requires removing/bypassing a guard, opening protected equipment, entering hazardous servicing conditions or performing work outside the operator procedure, the Technician I response is to stop and refer the condition.

### Worked case

A pump alarm appears. The operator SOP allows checking reservoir level, visible leaks and an external breaker indicator. Those checks do not explain the problem. The next troubleshooting step would require opening an electrical control box. Technician I stops at the boundary, records the fault context and escalates.

### Guided practice — authority sorting

Sort each action into one of six categories:

- routine technician action;
- stop/hold;
- supervisor decision;
- maintenance referral;
- safety response;
- regulated/specialist action.

Use a supplied role matrix rather than personal confidence.

### Retrieval check

1. Why does knowing how a repair works not prove authorization?
2. Give one example of an operator-level check and one example of protected servicing.
3. Why are role boundaries part of competency rather than a limitation on competency?

## Lesson 3.3 — Deviations, incomplete work and honest status

### Deviations are operational information

Equipment faults, identity mismatches, alarms, shortages, contamination concerns and other conditions can interrupt the plan. A deviation is not automatically proof that a worker failed. Hiding it, however, removes information the operation needs to improve and can create a larger problem.

### Do not manufacture completion

A task is complete only when required steps and acceptance criteria are met or an authorized exception is documented. If a required observation was missed, the record should show the true status according to procedure. Do not invent a value later and present it as contemporaneous.

WHO data-integrity guidance provides a useful transferred principle: records should remain attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original/true and accurate, as well as complete, consistent, enduring and available. In this course those are quality concepts, not pharmaceutical legal requirements.

### Worked case

A sanitation cycle is interrupted when the correct product is unavailable. The shift is ending. Marking the job complete would create false status. Record what was completed, what was not, why the work stopped, who was notified and what must happen next.

### Guided practice — incomplete work packet

Learners receive a partially completed packet containing:

- one missed measurement;
- one unresolved identity mismatch;
- one incomplete sanitation step;
- one equipment alarm;
- one completed task.

For each, state whether work can continue, must stop, or requires escalation, and write the truthful status for the next shift.

### Module application — controlled work-order review

Complete a mock work order from start to handoff. The scenario includes an obsolete SOP copy, an identifier mismatch and a task that exceeds Technician I authority. Correct performance means catching those issues before they become execution errors.

### Module retrieval

Explain why **procedure compliance**, **authority boundaries**, **deviation reporting**, and **data integrity** belong in the same module. Give one example where a failure in one area creates a problem in another.
