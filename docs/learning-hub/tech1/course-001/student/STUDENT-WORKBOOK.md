# Student Workbook — COURSE-LH-TECH1-001

Complete these activities using the supplied mock records, site procedures, labels, SDS excerpts, equipment instructions, facility maps, status cards, event logs, and work packets. When your real workplace rules differ from a generic example, follow the current authorized workplace rule and document the difference.

The workbook is designed for evidence-producing practice. Do not invent missing facts to make an activity look complete. Preserve uncertainty, record what is actually known, and identify the role or process that must resolve conditions outside Technician I authority.

## Field references

Use the [Course 1 Field Reference Index](job-aids/FIELD-REFERENCE-INDEX.md) during workbook practice. The six quick references are designed to support decision-making without replacing the current controlled workplace source. Use them to reconstruct the decision rule, then verify the actual SOP/work order, label/SDS, equipment instruction, status control, or other current source that governs the scenario.

The references are especially useful during Activities 8–12, where more than one control or competency may apply at the same time.

## Activity 1 — Pre-task hazard brief
Record the task, work area, hazards, controls, required PPE, stop-work conditions, escalation contacts, and any restricted-entry or alarm status. Explain one condition that you can correct routinely and one that requires escalation. Then identify one condition that would require you to reassess the brief after work has already started.

## Activity 2 — Biosecurity movement map
Draw the route of workers, tools, carts, hose ends, incoming plants, and waste through a mock facility. Mark potential cross-contamination points. Redesign the sequence to reduce high-risk backtracking and list the sanitation/release step required at each boundary. For one breach, reconstruct source → route → receiving zone before proposing the response.

## Activity 3 — Controlled instruction review
Given two SOP revisions and one work order, identify which documents are current, the prerequisites, required sequence, acceptance criteria, records to complete, and escalation points. Reconcile room, batch/material, asset, date/shift, and other identifiers with the physical situation. Write what you would do if a required instruction is missing or conflicts with another controlled document or with physical reality.

## Activity 4 — Traceability and reconciliation packet
Create a genealogy chain from source material through movement, harvest container, sample, and waste. Complete a movement event with source, destination, time, identity, and quantity. Then investigate a physical-versus-record mismatch without deleting history or forcing agreement. Show at least one event where a new identifier extends the genealogy instead of replacing the source history.

## Activity 5 — Equipment readiness and fault report
Use a mock pre-use checklist. Classify findings as acceptable, routine operator correction, out-of-service/maintenance escalation, or emergency hazard. Produce a maintenance-ready fault report containing asset ID, location, time, observation, permitted checks, results, process/crop impact, action taken, and who was notified. Separate confirmed observations from unconfirmed hypotheses.

## Activity 6 — Data integrity correction
Correct a supplied paper or digital record while preserving the original history. Explain why backdating, overwriting, guessing, or deleting an inconvenient value weakens reconstructability. For a late entry, distinguish the time the observation occurred from the time the record was actually entered.

## Activity 7 — Shift handoff
Prepare a concise handoff containing completed work, room/material identity, important measurements/observations, deviations, unresolved hazards/equipment conditions, holds/restrictions, and next-shift priorities. A partner must ask clarifying questions, cross-check one critical item against the source record, and read back the highest-risk open conditions before the handoff is considered complete.

## Activity 8 — Integrated shift case
Work through a simulated shift containing a wet aisle, controlled-room restriction, one identity mismatch, one equipment alarm, one missing data field, and an unfinished sanitation task. Prioritize actions, distinguish what is inside Technician I authority, produce the required records, respond to at least one changing condition, reconcile the final physical-versus-recorded state, and prepare the final handoff.

## Activity 9 — Status-stack and access-control challenge
Use a supplied status packet containing a quarantine, a quality hold, a pesticide restricted-entry interval, an equipment out-of-service tag, and a normal production assignment. Build a status-control matrix showing:

- what object, room, material, or equipment each status applies to;
- the purpose of each control;
- whether the control blocks entry, movement, use, release, or another action;
- what evidence or authorized process can clear that specific status;
- which statuses can remain active after another one expires or is released.

Then solve three cases in which one status changes while the others remain in force. Your answer must identify which control changed and which controls did **not** change.

## Activity 10 — Traceability transformation and event reconstruction
Use a mock event packet in which one identified source batch is split into several new containers and one downstream sample is created. Build an input → event → output genealogy that answers:

- **What** identified object or material was involved?
- **Where** did the event occur or move the object?
- **When** did it occur?
- **Why** did the event occur or what process did it represent?
- **Who/what system** recorded or executed it?
- Which new identifiers must remain linked to which source identifiers?

Then identify one planted break in the chain and explain what evidence is missing. Do not repair the chain by inventing an identifier or quantity.

## Activity 11 — Fault recurrence timeline and servicing-boundary challenge
Review a mock equipment packet containing an alarm at three different times, one permitted reset, one permitted external check, a persistent process impact, and a proposed repair that requires guard removal or protected access.

Build a time-ordered fault timeline that separates:

- alarm or symptom;
- permitted operator check;
- check result;
- process/crop impact;
- equipment status;
- notification/ticket;
- unconfirmed hypothesis, if included;
- the exact point where the next action crosses into servicing or hazardous-energy work.

Write the maintenance handoff so another qualified person can reconstruct the condition without relying on your diagnosis.

## Activity 12 — Late-entry and closed-loop handoff challenge
A required observation occurred at 13:55 but was entered at 14:40 under a permitted late-entry process. During shift handoff, the incoming technician correctly repeats the equipment ticket but omits an active material hold.

Produce a combined record/handoff exercise that:

1. preserves the observation time and later entry time as distinct facts;
2. identifies the person/system making the entry and any required reason;
3. keeps the material hold visible as an unresolved condition;
4. requires the outgoing technician to correct the missed read-back;
5. records the next action, timing, and responsible role where known;
6. separates what is observed from what remains uncertain.

## Reflection prompts

1. Which decision in the integrated case depended most on knowing your authority boundary?
2. Where could an accurate record prevent a future crop, safety, or compliance problem?
3. Which observation would be dangerous to convert into an unsupported diagnosis or root-cause claim?
4. How did identity and traceability affect decisions that initially looked like simple production tasks?
5. Which control in the status-stack activity was easiest to confuse with another control, and what evidence separated them?
6. Which handoff item would create the greatest downstream risk if the incoming technician failed to repeat it back correctly?
