# Student Workbook — COURSE-LH-TECH1-001

Complete these activities using the supplied mock records, site procedures, labels, SDS excerpts, equipment instructions, and facility map. When your real workplace rules differ from a generic example, follow the current authorized workplace rule and document the difference.

## Activity 1 — Pre-task hazard brief
Record the task, work area, hazards, controls, required PPE, stop-work conditions, escalation contacts, and any restricted-entry or alarm status. Explain one condition that you can correct routinely and one that requires escalation.

## Activity 2 — Biosecurity movement map
Draw the route of workers, tools, carts, hose ends, incoming plants, and waste through a mock facility. Mark potential cross-contamination points. Redesign the sequence to reduce high-risk backtracking and list the sanitation/release step required at each boundary.

## Activity 3 — Controlled instruction review
Given two SOP revisions and one work order, identify which documents are current, the prerequisites, required sequence, acceptance criteria, records to complete, and escalation points. Write what you would do if a required instruction is missing or conflicts with another controlled document.

## Activity 4 — Traceability and reconciliation packet
Create a genealogy chain from source material through movement, harvest container, sample, and waste. Complete a movement event with source, destination, time, identity, and quantity. Then investigate a physical-versus-record mismatch without deleting history or forcing agreement.

## Activity 5 — Equipment readiness and fault report
Use a mock pre-use checklist. Classify findings as acceptable, routine operator correction, out-of-service/maintenance escalation, or emergency hazard. Produce a maintenance-ready fault report containing asset ID, location, time, observation, permitted checks, results, impact, action taken, and who was notified.

## Activity 6 — Data integrity correction
Correct a supplied paper or digital record while preserving the original history. Explain why backdating, overwriting, guessing, or deleting an inconvenient value weakens reconstructability.

## Activity 7 — Shift handoff
Prepare a concise handoff containing completed work, room/material identity, important measurements/observations, deviations, unresolved hazards/equipment conditions, holds/restrictions, and next-shift priorities. A partner must ask clarifying questions and cross-check one critical item.

## Activity 8 — Integrated shift case
Work through a simulated shift containing a wet aisle, controlled-room restriction, one identity mismatch, one equipment alarm, one missing data field, and an unfinished sanitation task. Prioritize actions, distinguish what is inside Technician I authority, produce the required records, and prepare the final handoff.

## Reflection prompts

1. Which decision in the integrated case depended most on knowing your authority boundary?
2. Where could an accurate record prevent a future crop, safety, or compliance problem?
3. Which observation would be dangerous to convert into an unsupported diagnosis or root-cause claim?
4. How did identity and traceability affect decisions that initially looked like simple production tasks?
