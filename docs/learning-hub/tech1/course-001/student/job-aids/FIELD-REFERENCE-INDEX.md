# Course 1 Field Reference Index

These job aids are compact reminders for use during Course 1 practice, simulation and supervised workplace-equivalent training. They summarize decision logic already taught in the lessons; they do **not** replace current site procedures, product labels, SDSs, equipment manuals, emergency plans, jurisdiction-specific rules or required authorization.

## One-minute cross-check

When more than one problem is happening at once, do not force everything into a single category. Use multiple aids in this order:

1. **Immediate safety/access control first.** Is anyone exposed to a hazard or prohibited from entering/using something?
2. **Preserve identity/status.** Is plant, material, equipment or room identity/status at risk?
3. **Confirm current controlled instruction and authority.** What are you actually allowed to do next?
4. **Execute only the permitted response.** Do not improvise repairs, releases, record corrections or permanent process changes.
5. **Document the real result.** Preserve discrepancies, incomplete work and unresolved conditions.
6. **Handoff what remains open.** Make the next qualified person able to reconstruct the state and next action.

## Strong field behavior

A strong Technician I response usually has four qualities:

- **specific** — identifies the actual room/material/asset/status rather than using vague language;
- **bounded** — distinguishes what the technician can do from what requires another role;
- **reconstructable** — records what happened, when, to what, with what result;
- **closed-loop** — unresolved conditions are escalated and handed off, not silently abandoned.

## Weak field behavior to avoid

Common failure patterns across all six aids include:

- relying on memory instead of current controlled information;
- treating production urgency as permission to bypass a control;
- changing a record simply to make it match expectation;
- diagnosing equipment or plant-health root cause without evidence;
- assuming one released status clears every other active restriction;
- treating an unanswered message or one-way note as a completed handoff;
- marking incomplete work complete because the shift ended.

## Practice connections

The job aids are designed to be used during the student workbook and integrated practical rather than memorized as isolated text.

- Activities 1–3 emphasize safety, status, sanitation and controlled instructions.
- Activities 4–6 emphasize traceability, equipment evidence and record integrity.
- Activities 7–8 integrate handoff and full-shift decisions.
- Activities 9–12 stress status stacking, event genealogy, recurring faults and late-entry/closed-loop handoff.

## Editable reference policy

These six aids are the current Course 1 field-reference set, not a permanent maximum. New aids, role-specific variants, translated versions, accessible formats, workplace-specific overlays and revised decision tools may be added or reorganized as the curriculum improves.
