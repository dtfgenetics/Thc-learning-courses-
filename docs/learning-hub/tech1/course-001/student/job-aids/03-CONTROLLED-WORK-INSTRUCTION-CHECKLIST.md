# Job Aid 3 — Controlled Work Instruction and Authority Checklist

**Use for:** SOP/work-order review, version conflicts, task authorization, deviations, incomplete work and closeout.  
**Course objective:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-05`  
**Workbook practice:** Activities 3 and 8

> Use the current controlled instruction and workplace authority model. This guide does not authorize a Technician I to redesign procedures, approve deviations, release holds, or perform protected servicing.

## Before work: reconcile instruction with reality

Verify each item before beginning:

- **Task/work-order ID** — is this the work you were actually assigned?
- **Room/area** — does the instruction match the physical location?
- **Material/batch/plant identity** — do identifiers match what is physically present?
- **Asset/equipment identity** — are you working on the correct equipment?
- **Current instruction/revision** — is the SOP/work instruction effective and current?
- **Prerequisites** — are training, status, sanitation, PPE, setup and entry conditions satisfied?
- **Sequence** — what steps must occur and in what order?
- **Acceptance criteria** — what result proves the step is complete?
- **Records** — what form/system/event must be completed?
- **Stop/exception conditions** — what requires pause, clarification or deviation handling?
- **Escalation path** — who/what process resolves conditions outside your authority?

## Five-point start check

Before starting, be able to answer:

**CURRENT? → CORRECT SCOPE? → IDENTIFIERS MATCH? → PREREQUISITES MET? → AUTHORIZED?**

If one answer is “no” or “unknown,” do not blend assumptions into the work instruction.

## If documents conflict

Do **not**:

- choose the easier version;
- average two requirements;
- follow the one you remember;
- silently rewrite a field;
- assume the newest-looking document is automatically approved;
- proceed because the task is behind schedule.

Instead:

1. stop at the conflict;
2. preserve the exact document IDs/revisions and conflicting requirement;
3. verify whether one source has defined precedence;
4. use the authorized clarification/deviation/change process;
5. record the resulting instruction or unresolved status.

## Authority test

Ask two separate questions:

1. **Do I know what should happen?**
2. **Am I authorized to make it happen?**

Knowing the likely fix does not create authorization.

| Situation | Technician I action |
|---|---|
| Normal step defined by current SOP | Execute and document |
| Routine adjustment explicitly assigned | Perform within stated limits |
| Missing/contradictory instruction | Stop affected step and escalate |
| Permanent process change | Refer to authorized change-control role/process |
| Hold/release decision | Do not self-release unless explicitly authorized |
| Guard removal/protected access/repair | Stop and refer to qualified servicing process |
| Supervisor unavailable | Use the approved alternate escalation path; do not invent authority |

## Deviation and incomplete-work closeout

A truthful closeout answers:

- what was planned;
- what actually occurred;
- where/when the difference appeared;
- what work was completed;
- what remains incomplete;
- what immediate safe action was taken;
- who was notified;
- what next action/status is pending.

“Incomplete” is an operational status, not a paperwork failure.

## Strong vs weak response

**Weak:** “SOP says one thing, work order says another. I used the step we normally do.”

**Strong:** “Work order WO-184 calls for Room V-2, but the attached instruction lists V-3. I paused before the affected step, preserved both identifiers, notified the designated lead/alternate escalation path, and left the task open pending clarification.”

Why the strong response is better: it preserves the conflict and physical reality without inventing which instruction controls.

## End-of-task closeout

Before marking work complete, confirm:

1. required physical result achieved;
2. acceptance criteria met or exception recorded;
3. material/asset/location identity still correct;
4. required record/event completed;
5. deviations or unresolved conditions visible;
6. next shift/task has the needed handoff information.

## Documentation formula

**instruction ID/revision + work-order/task ID + identity/location + planned step + actual result + exception/deviation + notification + final task status**

## Pocket use

Use the six-word sequence:

**VERIFY → MATCH → EXECUTE → CHECK → DOCUMENT → HANDOFF**

When the instruction and reality do not match, stop at the mismatch rather than making the paperwork look consistent.
