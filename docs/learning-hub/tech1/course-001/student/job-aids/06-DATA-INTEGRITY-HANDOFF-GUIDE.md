# Job Aid 6 — Data Integrity, Record Correction and Shift Handoff Quick Reference

**Use for:** contemporaneous records, corrections, late entries, incomplete work and two-way shift handoff.  
**Course objectives:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-11`, `LO-LH-TECH1-001-12`  
**Workbook practice:** Activities 6, 7, 8 and 12

> The ALCOA+ concepts used here are a transferable record-quality model. They are not presented as a universal U.S. cannabis regulatory requirement. Follow the current site/jurisdiction record process where it is more specific.

## Reconstructable record test

A useful record should allow another qualified person to determine:

**WHAT happened → TO WHAT/WHERE → WHEN it happened → WHO performed/recorded it → WHAT RESULT occurred → WHAT REMAINS OPEN**

Use these quality checks:

- **Attributable** — who performed/recorded the action is clear.
- **Legible** — another person can read/interpret it.
- **Contemporaneous** — entered when the event occurred or handled transparently as a late entry.
- **Original / true copy** — source history is preserved.
- **Accurate** — reflects the actual observation/result.
- **Complete** — required context and unresolved facts are present.
- **Consistent** — sequence/timing makes sense and conflicts are visible.
- **Enduring** — record is retained in the approved system/form.
- **Available** — retrievable when needed by the authorized process.

## Observation time versus entry time

When an observation happened earlier than the permitted entry:

- preserve the **actual observation/event time**;
- record the **actual entry time**;
- identify the recorder;
- include the required reason/process for a late entry;
- do not backdate the record to make it look contemporaneous.

The record should reveal the delay, not hide it.

## Correcting an error

Use the approved correction mechanism and preserve history.

Do **not**:

- erase or overwrite the original outside the approved audit/correction process;
- backdate;
- invent a missing measurement;
- replace an observed value with an expected value;
- ask someone else to sign for work they did not perform;
- convert an unresolved discrepancy into a “corrected” value without evidence.

## Strong vs weak correction

**Weak:** Change “09:00” to “09:20” with no trace because 09:20 is the correct completion time.

**Strong:** Preserve the original 09:00 entry, use the approved correction method to enter 09:20, identify who made the correction and when, and include the required reason/source evidence so the history remains reconstructable.

## Handoff priority order

Transfer high-risk/open conditions before routine production notes:

1. active safety hazards or alarms;
2. restricted entry, quarantine, hold or other active status;
3. identity/traceability discrepancies;
4. equipment out-of-service condition or open maintenance ticket;
5. incomplete time-sensitive work;
6. important measurements/observations and deviations;
7. completed work and routine next priorities.

## Closed-loop handoff sequence

**PREPARE → WRITE → EXCHANGE → CROSS-CHECK → READ BACK → CORRECT MISUNDERSTANDING → OWN NEXT ACTION**

A note left in a binder or chat is not automatically a completed handoff when the supplied process requires two-way exchange.

## Handoff content checklist

Include:

- shift/date and relevant area;
- completed work;
- room/material/asset identity;
- important measurements/observations;
- deviations/discrepancies;
- unresolved safety/equipment conditions;
- holds/restrictions/quarantine status;
- unfinished work;
- next action and timing where known;
- responsible role/owner where known;
- notification/ticket status;
- incoming questions/cross-check;
- corrected read-back if the receiver misses a critical condition.

## Strong vs weak handoff

**Weak:** “Zone 2 alarm earlier. Maintenance knows. Plants okay. Check tomorrow.”

**Strong:** “Zone 2 low-flow alarm recurred at 14:12 and 15:05. External line routing and valve checks completed within operator scope; alarm remained active. Controller IC-2 is in site-defined out-of-service status. Ticket M-248 open. Bench B irrigation remains incomplete. Incoming technician read back the ticket correctly but initially omitted the active Bench C material hold; hold status was corrected during handoff. Next action: maintenance review and authorized hold disposition before affected work resumes.”

Why the strong handoff is better: it separates facts, status and pending action; preserves multiple open conditions; and verifies that the receiver actually understood them.

## Incomplete work rule

Do not convert unfinished work into “complete” because the shift is ending. Record:

**completed portion + incomplete portion + reason/status + immediate safe condition + who was notified + next required action**

## Documentation formula

**identity + event/observation time + entry time if different + factual observation/result + action taken + correction/deviation history + unresolved condition + owner/notification + next action**

## Pocket use

Use the sequence:

**RECORD REALITY → PRESERVE HISTORY → SEPARATE FACT FROM THEORY → PRIORITIZE OPEN RISK → VERIFY HANDOFF**

A trustworthy record is one that lets the next qualified person reconstruct what actually happened—not one that merely looks complete.
