# Job Aid 2 — Biosecurity, Sanitation and Controlled Movement

## Think in pathways

Potential contamination pathways include workers, gloves/clothing/footwear, tools, carts, containers, hose ends, incoming plants, media, irrigation components, plant debris, and waste movement.

## Movement sequence

1. Verify zone/status before entry.
2. Perform lower-risk/cleaner work before higher-risk work when the site uses clean-to-dirty sequencing.
3. Use room-specific tools where required.
4. Apply required clothing/glove/footwear changes.
5. Clean shared equipment before disinfection when the approved process requires removal of debris/organic matter.
6. Follow the current product label/SOP for concentration, contact time, compatibility, and safety controls.
7. Do not release quarantine/hold material without the defined release authority.
8. Treat pesticide restricted-entry status separately from plant quarantine or health hold status.

## Quick distinction

| Control | Main purpose |
|---|---|
| Quarantine/isolation/plant-health hold | Control movement of potentially risky plant/material |
| Sanitation/disinfection | Reduce contamination on approved surfaces/equipment using the defined process |
| Pesticide restricted-entry control | Protect workers after covered pesticide application under applicable requirements |

## Breach response

If a dirty tool/cart/person crosses a controlled boundary unexpectedly: stop the uncontrolled movement, preserve the facts, follow the breach/sanitation procedure, and notify the appropriate role. Do not assume that absence of visible symptoms means no contamination risk.
