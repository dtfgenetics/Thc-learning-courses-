# Job Aid 2 — Biosecurity, Sanitation and Status-Control Quick Reference

**Use for:** room entry, controlled movement, sanitation, quarantine/hold decisions, and contamination-breach response.  
**Course objectives:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-03`, `LO-LH-TECH1-001-04`  
**Workbook practice:** Activities 2, 8 and 9

> Follow the current site procedure, approved sanitation process, product label and applicable entry restriction. This guide does not authorize pesticide application or release of quarantined/held material.

## Think in pathways

For contamination to spread, ask three questions:

1. **Source:** Where could contamination originate?
2. **Route:** What can carry it?
3. **Receiver:** What room, plant, surface, tool or material could receive it?

Common routes include workers, gloves/clothing/footwear, tools, carts, containers, hose ends, incoming plants, media, irrigation components, plant debris, waste and shared work surfaces.

## Before crossing a boundary

1. Confirm room/zone identity and current status.
2. Check whether quarantine, quality hold, restricted entry, isolation, out-of-service status or another control applies.
3. Identify the movement object: person, tool, cart, plant, material, waste or equipment.
4. Apply the required clothing/glove/footwear/tool controls.
5. Follow clean-to-dirty or other required sequence when specified.
6. Clean before disinfection when the approved process requires removal of debris/organic matter.
7. Use the correct approved product/process, concentration/contact time and safety controls.
8. Protect the cleaned/disinfected item from recontamination before use or release.
9. Do not release a status you are not authorized to release.

## Status-stack matrix

Different controls can apply at the same time. Clearing one does **not** automatically clear the others.

| Status/control | Primarily controls | Typical question to answer |
|---|---|---|
| Plant-health quarantine/isolation | Movement/contact involving potentially risky plant/material | Can this plant/material leave or contact another zone? |
| Quality/inventory hold | Use, movement, release or disposition of identified material | Has the authorized role released the hold? |
| Pesticide restricted-entry control | Worker entry after a covered pesticide application | Is entry permitted under the applicable label/procedure? |
| Sanitation/disinfection status | Surface/equipment readiness under the defined process | Was the required process completed and protected from recontamination? |
| Equipment out-of-service status | Equipment use | Has authorized maintenance/release returned it to service? |

## Sanitation sequence

**REMOVE GROSS DEBRIS → CLEAN AS REQUIRED → DISINFECT/SANITIZE PER APPROVED PROCESS → MEET CONTACT/PROCESS REQUIREMENT → VERIFY → PROTECT FROM RECONTAMINATION**

Do not assume “looks clean” means the sanitation process was completed correctly.

## Breach response

If a dirty tool/cart/person crosses a controlled boundary unexpectedly:

1. stop further uncontrolled movement;
2. identify the source, route and receiving zone/object;
3. preserve what is known—do not erase the movement history;
4. isolate or hold affected items only as the supplied procedure authorizes;
5. perform the required cleaning/sanitation response;
6. notify the appropriate role;
7. document what crossed, where, when, what was affected and what remains unresolved.

Absence of visible symptoms does not prove that no contamination route existed.

## Strong vs weak response

**Weak:** “The quarantine was released, so the room is clear.”

**Strong:** “The plant-health quarantine was released at 10:15, but the posted pesticide restricted-entry control remains active until its own condition is satisfied. I treat those as separate statuses and do not enter based only on the quarantine release.”

Why the strong response is better: it identifies exactly which status changed and preserves the controls that did not change.

## Recontamination check

After sanitation, ask:

- Was the item placed back onto a dirty surface?
- Did contaminated gloves touch the cleaned item?
- Did a cart/tool cross back through a higher-risk route?
- Was the item stored or transported in a way that defeats the completed process?

A correct sanitation process can still be undermined by what happens afterward.

## Documentation formula

**time + source + route + receiving zone/object + status at time of event + control performed + release/hold authority involved + unresolved condition**

## Handoff trigger

Handoff is required when a breach, hold, quarantine, entry restriction, incomplete sanitation step or pending release decision remains open at shift change.

## Pocket use

Use the six-word sequence:

**SOURCE → ROUTE → RECEIVER → STATUS → CONTROL → VERIFY**

If status or release authority is unclear, stop the affected movement and use the authorized escalation path.
