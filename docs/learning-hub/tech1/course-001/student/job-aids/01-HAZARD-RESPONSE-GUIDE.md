# Job Aid 1 — Hazard Response, PPE and HazCom Quick Reference

**Use for:** pre-task checks, changing conditions, alarms, chemical-handling preparation, and stop/escalate decisions.  
**Course objectives:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-01`, `LO-LH-TECH1-001-02`  
**Workbook practice:** Activities 1, 8 and 9

> This is a field reminder, not a replacement for the current employer procedure, emergency plan, product label, SDS, equipment instruction, or required qualification.

## 30-second pre-task scan

Before starting, answer these in order:

1. **What exactly is the task?** Confirm room, material, asset and work-order identity.
2. **What can hurt someone or change the safe state?** Look for slips/trips, damaged electrical equipment, guards, pressurized/stored energy, chemicals, alarms, heat, CO2, moving equipment and other task-specific hazards.
3. **What controls are already required?** Check the current instruction, posted status, label/SDS and PPE requirement.
4. **Am I authorized to perform the response?** Routine correction is not the same as repair, servicing, pesticide handling, respirator use, energized work or supervisory release authority.
5. **Has anything changed since the brief was made?** If conditions change, reassess before continuing.

## Decision sequence

| Question | Proceed only when… | Stop / clarify / escalate when… |
|---|---|---|
| Task and location | The assigned task, area and identity are clear | Work order, room, material or asset identity is unclear |
| Area status | No controlling alarm/restriction blocks the work, or the supplied procedure explicitly allows the task | An alarm, restricted-entry status, hold, out-of-service status or other control applies and permission is unresolved |
| PPE and controls | Required controls/PPE are available, usable and appropriate to the current task | Required control is missing, damaged, unknown or cannot be used correctly |
| Chemical information | Product identity and current label/SDS/site instruction are available | Container/product is unknown, label information is missing, or required handling information is unresolved |
| Authority | The response is explicitly routine and inside Technician I scope | Action requires repair, guard removal, protected access, energized work, pesticide-handler authority, respirator clearance or another separate qualification |
| Changing conditions | Hazards and controls still match the original plan | Leak, alarm, spill, damage, blocked route, changed room status or another new condition alters the risk |

## Control-strength reminder

Use the strongest control available **within the supplied procedure and your authorization**. Do not treat PPE as a substitute for fixing or isolating a condition when the approved process requires a stronger control.

Examples:

- A wet aisle can be a routine housekeeping correction **only if** there is no unresolved electrical or chemical hazard and the correction is within assigned duties.
- Moisture spreading toward visibly damaged powered equipment is not “just a mop-up.” Prevent exposure where authorized and escalate the electrical hazard.
- An unknown alarm is not cleared by familiarity or production urgency. Follow the alarm/emergency procedure.

## Chemical-information scan

Before using a workplace chemical, verify the product and find the current information for:

- hazard and precautionary statements;
- required handling/storage controls;
- PPE/exposure controls;
- incompatibilities and prohibited uses;
- first-aid/emergency information;
- spill/release response applicable to your role;
- application or re-entry restrictions relevant to the task.

Do not substitute memory for a current label/SDS/site instruction when the task requires those controls.

## Stop-and-escalate triggers

Examples include:

- damaged electrical equipment near moisture;
- missing or defeated machine guard;
- unknown life-safety alarm;
- unlabeled or unidentified chemical container;
- required PPE unavailable or unusable;
- active restricted-entry control that applies to the work area;
- task changing from routine operator work into repair/servicing;
- a new condition that makes the original pre-task brief incomplete.

## Strong vs weak response

**Weak:** “The floor is wet, so I will be careful and finish the task.”

**Strong:** “The wet area is approaching a cart charger with visible cord damage. I stop the affected task, keep people away if that is within the site procedure, report the electrical hazard, and record the task as incomplete until the condition is resolved.”

Why the strong response is better: it identifies the actual hazard combination, acts within authority, preserves task status and does not substitute caution/PPE for an unresolved electrical condition.

## Documentation formula

Record enough for another qualified person to reconstruct the decision:

**time + exact location/asset/material + observation + control used + unresolved condition + person/role notified + task status**

Avoid unsupported diagnosis. “Cord jacket split with moisture 0.5 m away” is an observation. “Internal short circuit” is a diagnosis unless confirmed by an authorized technical process.

## Handoff trigger

Include the condition in handoff when it remains open, changes access/task priority, places equipment out of service, creates a pending ticket/notification, or affects the next shift’s work.

## Pocket use

For mobile or printed use, remember the five-word sequence:

**IDENTIFY → CHECK STATUS → CONTROL → VERIFY AUTHORITY → REASSESS**

If one of those cannot be answered confidently from the current controlled information, pause the task and use the approved escalation path.
