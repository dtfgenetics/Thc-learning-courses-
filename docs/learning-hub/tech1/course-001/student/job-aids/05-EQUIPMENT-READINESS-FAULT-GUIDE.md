# Job Aid 5 — Equipment Readiness, Operator-Care Boundary and Fault Escalation

**Use for:** pre-use checks, readiness decisions, permitted operator care, recurring alarms and maintenance handoff.  
**Course objectives:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-09`, `LO-LH-TECH1-001-10`  
**Workbook practice:** Activities 5, 8 and 11

> Follow the equipment manual, site procedure and assigned authorization. Course completion does not grant repair, electrical, HVAC, hazardous-energy or lockout/tagout authorization.

## Pre-use readiness scan

Use the equipment/site checklist to verify only the items assigned to your role. Common categories include:

- correct asset ID and location;
- required sanitation/cleanliness state;
- visible leaks or damage;
- guards/protective devices in place;
- cords/plugs/connections visibly intact;
- hoses/lines routed correctly and free of obvious external kinks where checking is permitted;
- warning/alarm indicators;
- calibration/readiness status where assigned;
- work-area clearance and access;
- current out-of-service or maintenance status.

## Classify the finding

| Classification | What it means | Technician I response |
|---|---|---|
| **Acceptable** | Readiness criteria are met | Use as assigned and document if required |
| **Routine operator correction** | The procedure explicitly assigns the correction and it can be completed without protected access or hazardous servicing | Correct, verify result and document |
| **Out of service / maintenance escalation** | Readiness criterion failed or next step exceeds operator scope | Do not use; apply supplied status/tagging process and escalate |
| **Emergency hazard** | Condition requires immediate emergency/stop-work response | Follow emergency procedure and prevent exposure within authorization |

## Operator care versus servicing

A task may look simple and still cross the servicing boundary.

Stop and escalate when the next action requires any of the following unless you are separately qualified/authorized under the applicable procedure:

- removing or bypassing a guard;
- reaching into hazardous motion;
- opening a protected electrical compartment;
- exposing energized components;
- disassembling pressurized equipment;
- releasing or controlling stored energy;
- clearing a jam from a protected hazard zone;
- defeating an interlock or safety device;
- repair beyond the defined operator-maintenance task.

## Stored-energy clue check

Before assuming a task is “just mechanical,” ask whether the equipment can retain or release:

- electrical energy;
- pressure;
- gravity/load energy;
- spring/tension energy;
- thermal energy;
- hydraulic/pneumatic energy;
- moving parts that can restart or coast.

If controlling that energy is part of the task, use the qualified servicing process rather than improvising.

## Alarm/fault timeline

For recurring faults, record the sequence rather than only the latest alarm:

1. time of alarm/symptom;
2. exact display/observation;
3. permitted operator check;
4. result of that check;
5. process/crop impact;
6. equipment status after the check;
7. recurrence time if it returns;
8. notification/ticket;
9. unresolved condition;
10. hypothesis only if clearly labeled as unconfirmed.

A repeated reset without understanding the authorized reset rule can hide a persistent fault.

## Strong vs weak fault report

**Weak:** “Pump broken again. Maintenance please fix.”

**Strong:** “14:12 — Irrigation controller IC-2, Zone 2 low-flow alarm. Tank level normal; external branch line visibly kinked. Restored line to normal routed position per operator checklist. Alarm remained active at 14:16. Zone 2 delivery still affected. Controller left in site-defined safe/out-of-service status and ticket M-248 opened. No protected access or disassembly performed.”

Why the strong report is better: it is time-ordered, reproducible, bounded to confirmed observations and tells maintenance what has already been checked.

## Observation versus diagnosis

**Observation:** “Zone 3 has no flow while tank level is normal.”  
**Permitted check/result:** “External valve verified open; no visible leak.”  
**Unconfirmed hypothesis:** “Possible downstream obstruction.”  
**Unsupported diagnosis:** “Pump motor failed.”

Do not turn a plausible theory into a recorded fact.

## Documentation formula

**asset ID + location + time + exact alarm/observation + permitted checks + check results + process/crop impact + equipment status + safe action + notification/ticket + unresolved condition**

## Handoff trigger

Include equipment in handoff whenever it remains out of service, has an unresolved alarm/fault, affects crop/process timing, has an open maintenance ticket or requires a next-shift check.

## Pocket use

Use the sequence:

**IDENTIFY → INSPECT → CLASSIFY → STAY IN SCOPE → DOCUMENT → ESCALATE**

If the next step requires protected access, hazardous-energy control or repair authority you do not have, the correct operator action is to stop and hand it to the qualified process.
