# Module 6 — Records, Shift Handoff & Integrated Workflow

## Why this module matters

Cultivation is a continuous process. The plants, rooms, equipment and open problems remain after an individual shift ends. Reliable records and handoffs allow the next person to understand what happened without reconstructing the shift from memory.

WHO data-integrity guidance provides a useful quality model for reliable records. HSE human-factors guidance shows why shift handover requires preparation, two-way exchange and cross-checking rather than a one-direction note dump. GS1 traceability reinforces the importance of object, location and event context.

**Controlled references:** `REF-WHO-DATA-INTEGRITY-2021`, `REF-HSE-SHIFT-HANDOVER`, `REF-GS1-GLOBAL-TRACEABILITY`, `REF-MHRA-GXP-DATA-INTEGRITY`.

## Lesson 6.1 — Contemporaneous records and data integrity

### Records must be reconstructable

A useful cultivation record allows another qualified person to understand **what happened, to what, where, when, by whom, and with what result**. Context matters. “2.1” is not meaningful if the record does not show whether it is pH, EC, another measurement, which sample/location it belongs to, or when it was taken.

WHO’s data-integrity framework uses the ALCOA+ concepts:

- attributable;
- legible;
- contemporaneous;
- original or true copy;
- accurate;
- complete;
- consistent;
- enduring;
- available.

The course uses these as transferable quality concepts, not pharmaceutical law.

### Record facts when they occur

A value entered much later from memory is weaker evidence than a value captured at the time of observation. If delayed entry is unavoidable, follow the controlled method and preserve the true timing instead of making the entry appear contemporaneous.

### Corrections preserve history

A correction should not silently erase the prior state. Paper or digital systems should preserve the original information and the fact of the correction according to the site procedure. Missing data remain missing until they can be legitimately resolved; do not invent them.

### Worked case

A technician wrote an incorrect EC value on a paper form. The stronger correction preserves the original entry, records the corrected value using the approved method, and keeps the change attributable. Erasing the original or rewriting the sheet destroys history.

### Guided practice — repair the record

Correct a mock cultivation record containing:

- missing units;
- ambiguous batch ID;
- an overwritten value;
- a retrospective entry presented as real-time;
- an unexplained blank;
- a copied value from a different zone.

For each issue, explain the data-integrity principle involved.

### Retrieval check

1. Why is a context-free number weak evidence?
2. What is wrong with making a late entry look contemporaneous?
3. Why should a correction preserve the original history?

## Lesson 6.2 — Professional shift handoff

### Handoff is a controlled transfer of responsibility

HSE describes effective shift handover as involving preparation by outgoing personnel, communication between outgoing and incoming personnel, and cross-checking by incoming staff. It also recommends both written and verbal communication for safety-critical handovers.

A cultivation handoff should make unresolved work visible, not bury it underneath a long list of routine completed tasks.

### Prioritize what the next person needs

A strong handoff identifies:

- critical safety/biosecurity restrictions;
- holds/quarantine/restricted-entry status;
- incomplete work;
- identity or inventory discrepancies;
- equipment alarms/faults;
- abnormal observations;
- time-sensitive next actions;
- people already notified;
- decisions still waiting for authorization.

### Use exact identifiers and bounded language

“Plants bad” is not actionable. “Room F2, north row, Batch B17: four plants with reduced turgor at 16:10; irrigation event confirmed in system; no diagnosis assigned; lead notified for follow-up” is far more useful.

Separate observation from diagnosis. Separate open status from completed status.

### Worked case

Zone 2 emitters 5–8 show no flow at 16:20. Operator checks are complete, maintenance ticket M-44 is open, and the next scheduled irrigation is at 18:00. The next shift needs those facts, not simply “irrigation issue.”

### Guided practice — handoff compression

Learners receive a messy page of shift notes. Convert it into a handoff with five categories:

1. safety/restrictions;
2. crop/material status;
3. equipment/facility status;
4. incomplete work/open deviations;
5. next actions/notifications.

Then role-play a two-way handoff in which the incoming technician asks clarifying questions and repeats back critical open items.

### Retrieval check

1. Why should handoff be two-way rather than only a written note?
2. Which unresolved conditions deserve priority placement?
3. Why should observation and diagnosis be separated?

## Lesson 6.3 — Integrated Technician I workflow

### Read the whole situation before starting the first task

Real work arrives as a packet, not a textbook chapter. A technician may face a work order, prior-shift handoff, room restriction, equipment alarm, inventory discrepancy, sanitation requirement and time-sensitive crop task at the same time.

Competence means prioritizing the controls that block downstream work.

A practical sequence is:

1. review the handoff/work packet;
2. identify safety and entry restrictions;
3. verify current SOP/work order;
4. confirm identity and destination;
5. prepare PPE/sanitation requirements;
6. perform authorized routine work;
7. capture measurements/events as they occur;
8. stop/escalate when authority ends or acceptance criteria fail;
9. reconcile physical and recorded status;
10. hand off unresolved work.

### Integrated scenario

You receive the following at shift start:

- a planned plant move;
- a destination room on hold;
- one tag mismatch in the source group;
- a sanitation station missing the approved product;
- an irrigation pump alarm;
- a prior-shift measurement that was not taken;
- a maintenance ticket already opened.

The wrong approach is to begin moving the plants because that task appears first. The move is blocked by the hold and identity mismatch; room entry may also be blocked by sanitation readiness. The pump receives only permitted checks because maintenance is already involved. The missed measurement remains identified as missing rather than reconstructed. Each unresolved item becomes part of the handoff.

### Capstone-style guided practice

Use the supplied integrated work packet to create:

- priority list;
- stop/continue/escalate decisions;
- controlled instruction check;
- material movement decision;
- discrepancy record;
- equipment escalation note;
- corrected record;
- final handoff.

### Final reflection

Technician I is not defined by knowing every answer. It is defined by executing routine work safely and consistently, protecting identity and records, recognizing abnormal conditions, and escalating correctly instead of improvising beyond role authority.
