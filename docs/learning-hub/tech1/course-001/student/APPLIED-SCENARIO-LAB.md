# Course 1 Applied Scenario Lab

**Course:** `COURSE-LH-TECH1-001 — Safety, Responsible Practice & Cultivation Workflows`

## Purpose

This lab gives learners repeated practice applying Course 1 concepts before the integrated practical. The cases intentionally combine more than one control at a time because real cultivation work rarely arrives as a single-topic question.

For every case, use the same reasoning frame:

1. **Observe** — What facts are actually present?
2. **Control source** — Which SOP, work order, label/SDS, status control, equipment instruction, or record governs?
3. **Authority** — What can Technician I do directly?
4. **Blockers** — What prevents work from continuing?
5. **Record** — What needs to be captured?
6. **Escalate/handoff** — What remains unresolved, and who receives it?

Do not diagnose, repair, relabel, release, substitute, backfill, or destroy evidence unless the supplied procedure explicitly authorizes that action.

---

## Lab 1 — Wet aisle, intact equipment

### Scenario

A small irrigation spill is present in a marked aisle. The nearest powered device is intact, elevated, and outside the wet area. The site routine-spill SOP authorizes Technician I to place a temporary barrier, correct the source if it is a simple hose-position issue, clean the spill, and document recurrence.

### Learner task

- Identify the hazard.
- Decide whether the task can be controlled within Technician I authority.
- Name the condition that would change this into a stop/escalate situation.
- Write a one-sentence record.

### Model feedback

The wet walking surface is the immediate hazard. Because the supplied procedure authorizes the routine spill response and there is no evidence of electrical damage or uncontrolled energized contact, Technician I can apply the routine control. The decision changes if water reaches damaged electrical equipment, the source cannot be safely corrected, the spill involves an unknown chemical, or the procedure's routine limits are exceeded.

A useful record names the location, time, source if confirmed, control completed, and any recurrence requiring follow-up.

## Lab 2 — Similar bottle, wrong sanitizer

### Scenario

The approved sanitation product is unavailable. A coworker offers another container from the same manufacturer with similar branding but a different product identity and different label directions.

### Learner task

- Identify the controlling evidence.
- Decide whether substitution is allowed.
- Explain why brand similarity is weak evidence.
- State what should be handed off if the task cannot be completed.

### Model feedback

The current SOP plus the exact product label control the task. Similar intended use, manufacturer, container color, or brand family does not establish chemical equivalence or authorized use. Technician I should not invent concentration, contact time, PPE, compatibility, or substitution approval.

The handoff should show that sanitation remains incomplete, why it stopped, what approved product/instruction is missing, who was notified, and what condition must be satisfied before completion.

---

## Lab 3 — Clean cart leaves quarantine

### Scenario

A cart was used in quarantine. It looks clean and no plant debris is visible. Propagation needs it immediately.

### Learner task

- Build the source → route → target pathway.
- Explain why appearance does not establish release.
- Identify the minimum decision needed before entry into propagation.

### Model feedback

The source is the quarantine environment/material, the route is the cart and any contact surfaces, and the susceptible target is the cleaner propagation zone. Visual cleanliness does not prove that the approved cleaning/disinfection/release workflow occurred.

The cart follows the controlled sanitation and release process before crossing the zone boundary. Urgency does not erase the pathway.

## Lab 4 — Old SOP, familiar task

### Scenario

The binder at the work station contains SOP revision 3. The controlled document system lists revision 5 as current. The worker says, "The task has not changed much."

### Learner task

- Decide whether the worker can proceed.
- Explain the difference between familiarity and document control.
- Identify what evidence resolves the issue.

### Model feedback

Do not proceed using the obsolete copy when a current controlled source is known to exist. Familiarity is not evidence that revisions are immaterial. Revision 5 must be accessed and its scope, prerequisites, sequence, acceptance criteria, and changes verified.

---

## Lab 5 — Action completed, acceptance failed

### Scenario

A bench sanitation procedure was performed in the required sequence, but final inspection finds visible growing-media residue in one corner. The worker says every step was completed.

### Learner task

- Is the task complete?
- Distinguish the process step from the acceptance criterion.
- State the next action without inventing a remedy.

### Model feedback

Completion requires both execution of required steps and satisfaction of the defined acceptance criterion. "I performed every step" does not override evidence that the required final condition was not achieved. Follow the supplied correction/rework procedure or escalate if it does not specify one.

## Lab 6 — Plant looks right, tag is wrong

### Scenario

A plant physically labeled `V2-103` is listed as `V2-130` in the move order. It looks identical to the surrounding cultivar group.

### Learner task

- Decide whether it can move.
- Explain why cultivar appearance is insufficient.
- Identify what evidence must be preserved.

### Model feedback

The affected movement stops. Appearance cannot replace controlled identity. Preserve the physical tag, work-order entry, current location, time discovered, planned destination, and any recent event history needed for reconciliation. Do not relabel or edit the record solely to make them agree.

---

## Lab 7 — Count mismatch during reconciliation

### Scenario

The system expects 48 plants. The physical count is 47. A second count is also 47.

### Learner task

- Explain what the repeated count establishes.
- List at least three possible categories of cause without diagnosing one.
- Write the correct status.

### Model feedback

The repeated count strengthens confidence that the current physical observation is 47; it does not by itself explain why the system expects 48. Possible categories include delayed entry, unrecorded movement, wrong identity, prior counting error, loss/damage, or system synchronization issues.

The correct status is an unresolved physical-versus-record discrepancy with the confirmed counts, identifiers, time, evidence checked, and escalation owner recorded.

## Lab 8 — One alarm, then recurrence

### Scenario

A low-flow alarm clears after one operator-authorized external correction. Eight minutes later it returns. The operator SOP allows one reset but no disassembly.

### Learner task

- Separate observation, hypothesis, and diagnosis.
- Decide what Technician I can still do.
- Build the minimum fault timeline.

### Model feedback

"Low-flow alarm returned" and "zone remains dry" are observations. "Filter blockage" may be a hypothesis if the scenario allows it, but it is not a diagnosis. Technician I can complete only the checks/reset explicitly authorized, then place the equipment in the required status and escalate.

The timeline should include alarm time, permitted check, result, temporary recovery if any, recurrence, process impact, current status, ticket/notification, and unresolved condition.

## Lab 9 — Measurement without context

### Scenario

A notebook entry reads only `2.1`. No unit, room, plant group, sample, instrument, or time is shown.

### Learner task

- Explain why this number is not usable evidence.
- List the context required to make a cultivation measurement reconstructable.
- Decide whether the missing context may be guessed from nearby entries.

### Model feedback

A number without identity, unit, time, location, method/instrument context, and responsible operator/system cannot reliably support a cultivation decision. Nearby values may help an investigation but do not authorize reconstructing missing facts as though they were original data.

---

## Lab 10 — Late entry

### Scenario

A valid observation occurred at 13:55. It was not recorded until 14:40. The controlled process permits late entry when both times and attribution are preserved.

### Learner task

- State what times belong in the record.
- Explain why changing the timestamp to 13:55 would weaken integrity.
- Identify what remains truthful even though the entry was late.

### Model feedback

Record the actual observation time and the actual entry time, plus the person/system and any required reason. Backdating the entry would falsely imply contemporaneous documentation. The observation can still be valid evidence if the approved late-entry process preserves what actually occurred.

---

## Lab 11 — One status expires, another remains

### Scenario

A pesticide restricted-entry interval expires for a room. A separate plant-health quarantine remains active.

### Learner task

- What changed?
- What did not change?
- Can material or equipment now leave the room automatically?

### Model feedback

Only the pesticide restricted-entry control changed. Quarantine is a different control system with a different purpose and release process. The expiration of one restriction does not release the other, so movement remains governed by the quarantine procedure.

---

## Lab 12 — Weak handoff

### Scenario

Outgoing note: "Irrigation issue. Plants checked. Sanitation mostly done. Maintenance knows."

### Learner task

Rewrite the handoff so the incoming technician can safely act without guessing.

### Model feedback

A strong handoff needs specific identifiers, times, current equipment status, observations, completed versus incomplete work, affected areas/material, ticket/notification, active restrictions, and next action. For example:

> Zone 2 low-flow alarm recurred at 13:28 and 13:44 after the one permitted reset. Zone 2 remained dry at last check. Equipment ticket M-44 is open; no protected servicing was attempted. V-2 sanitation remains incomplete because the approved product was unavailable; no substitute was authorized. Complete only after the approved product/procedure is restored.

The exact wording can vary; the information should not.

# Transfer challenges

After completing Labs 1–12, repeat at least four with one fact changed. Examples:

- the damaged cord is now dry but still visibly damaged;
- the quarantine cart has a documented completed release;
- the identity mismatch is resolved by an authorized correction;
- the recurring alarm does not return after the permitted check;
- the missing record context can be recovered from a controlled instrument export;
- the incoming worker correctly reads back every open condition.

For each change, answer:

1. Which original decision changes?
2. Which decision stays the same?
3. What new evidence supports the change?
4. What action is still outside Technician I authority?

# Readiness check

You are ready for the integrated practical when you can solve unfamiliar versions of these cases without relying on the model feedback and can explain **why** the correct action is stronger than the most tempting wrong action.
