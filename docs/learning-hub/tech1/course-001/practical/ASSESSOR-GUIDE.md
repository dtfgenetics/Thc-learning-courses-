# Course 1 Practical — Assessor Guide

**Practical:** `PRACTICAL-LH-TECH1-001-WORKFLOW`  
**Current rubric revision:** practical v1.1.0

## Purpose

This guide standardizes scoring for the Course 1 integrated practical. It does not declare the rubric calibrated; calibration must occur with multiple assessors and representative performances before operational certification use.

## Administration rules

- Use one approved candidate form without altering the construct mid-attempt.
- Provide only the prompts permitted by the form.
- Record every prompt or clarification given.
- Score observable decisions, records, and boundaries; do not score personality, confidence, speed, accent, handwriting style, or unsupported impressions.
- If an accessibility accommodation changes presentation but not the skill being measured, do not penalize the learner for using it.
- Stop the simulation if continuing would create an actual safety risk in a live training environment.

## Scoring method

The practical remains a **100-point integrated performance assessment** with one current provisional overall academic threshold and the published critical-error rule. The new criterion anchors improve repeatability inside each scoring domain; they **do not create separate domain pass/fail cut scores**.

Each domain contains observable criteria whose available points sum exactly to that domain's points. For each criterion, choose the anchor that best matches the observed performance:

- **Full** — award all available criterion points when the full-credit evidence is demonstrated.
- **Partial** — award one-half of the criterion points when the partial-credit description is the best match.
- **None** — award zero points when the no-credit description is the best match.

Sum the criterion points to obtain the domain score. Do not invent intermediate criterion levels or award points for impressions that are not represented in observable evidence. The evaluator workspace accepts domain scores in 0.5-point increments because half-credit on odd-point criteria can produce a half point.

For every finalized domain, record the observable evidence supporting the score. Record permitted prompts or clarifications when they affected the performance. Finalization is blocked when a scored domain lacks its observed-evidence note.

The current overall **80/100 value is a provisional academic development threshold pending pilot evidence and documented standard setting**. It is not a Technician I credential cut score and must not be converted into a production certification claim before the required validation work is accepted.

## Rubric anchors

The canonical point allocations and wording live in `content/performance-assessments/PRACTICAL-LH-TECH1-001-WORKFLOW.json`. The evaluator workspace renders those anchors directly, so this guide explains how to apply them without creating a second competing rubric.

### 1. Safety / PPE / stop-work judgment — 18 points

Score four anchored criteria:

- recognize seeded hazards and immediate controls — 6 points;
- use supplied PPE and chemical safety information — 4 points;
- stay inside authority and use stop-work/escalation — 5 points;
- create a useful safety decision record — 3 points.

A high score requires correct hazard recognition, supplied-information use, authority boundaries, and a reconstructable decision record. An action meeting a published critical-error definition is handled under the critical-error rule regardless of points earned elsewhere.

### 2. Sanitation / biosecurity — 14 points

Score four anchored criteria:

- recognize contamination and movement pathways — 4 points;
- execute the supplied cleaning and sanitation sequence — 4 points;
- respect quarantine, hold, REI, and movement restrictions — 4 points;
- stay inside product and procedure authorization — 2 points.

Assess the supplied workflow, not an assessor's preferred facility method. Do not award credit for invented chemical directions or for treating plant-health quarantine and pesticide restricted-entry controls as interchangeable.

### 3. SOP / work-order execution — 12 points

Score four anchored criteria:

- verify the current controlled instruction — 3 points;
- execute required steps and acceptance criteria — 4 points;
- handle missing or conflicting instructions — 3 points;
- document deviations and incomplete work honestly — 2 points.

The candidate is expected to use the supplied controlled source, distinguish physical activity from controlled completion, and escalate instruction conflicts rather than improvise around them.

### 4. Identity / traceability — 16 points

Score four anchored criteria:

- verify physical identity before movement — 5 points;
- preserve source, destination, time, and event context — 4 points;
- block and preserve identity discrepancies — 4 points;
- maintain genealogy through handoff — 3 points.

Do not award credit for making the physical material match the record by unauthorized relabeling, or for continuing movement through an unresolved identity discrepancy.

### 5. Inventory / discrepancy reasoning — 10 points

Score four anchored criteria:

- compare physical evidence with the controlled record — 3 points;
- separate confirmed facts from unresolved facts — 2 points;
- preserve discrepancy evidence — 2 points;
- escalate disposition decisions to the authorized process — 3 points.

The construct is truthful reconciliation reasoning and role-bounded escalation, not the ability to force a numerical match.

### 6. Equipment readiness / operator-care boundary — 10 points

Score four anchored criteria:

- complete permitted pre-use readiness checks — 3 points;
- stay inside the operator-care boundary — 3 points;
- describe faults without unsupported diagnosis — 2 points;
- escalate recurring or unsafe faults appropriately — 2 points.

A candidate does not gain extra credit for performing protected servicing or technical diagnosis outside the supplied Technician I authority.

### 7. Data integrity / documentation — 12 points

Score four anchored criteria:

- create timely, context-rich records — 4 points;
- preserve attribution and readable meaning — 2 points;
- correct records while preserving original history — 3 points;
- handle missing and late data honestly — 3 points.

The goal is reconstructable truth, not paperwork that merely looks complete. Fabricating or deliberately forcing a record can also trigger the critical-error rule.

### 8. Handoff / communication — 8 points

Score four anchored criteria:

- communicate completed work accurately — 2 points;
- transfer open hazards, holds, faults, and discrepancies — 2 points;
- identify ownership and next actions — 2 points;
- use closed-loop, fact-based communication — 2 points.

Score the accuracy and reconstructability of the handoff, not presentation style, accent, personality, or speed.

## Critical-error decision rule

Do not infer a critical error from an ordinary knowledge mistake. Apply a critical error only when the candidate performs or clearly commits to a prohibited behavior defined in the practical, including deliberate falsification, bypassing an explicit hold/restricted-entry control, unauthorized protected servicing, knowingly continuing an explicit unsafe condition, or causing avoidable unrecoverable identity loss.

Every critical-error decision must record:

- the exact candidate action/statement;
- the controlling instruction that was violated;
- why the failure is construct-relevant;
- whether a prompt occurred beforehand;
- assessor name/ID and time.

A critical error overrides the point result only as defined by the canonical practical. Do not create additional unwritten critical-error categories during scoring.

## Scoring record

For each domain record:

- points awarded from the canonical criterion anchors;
- observed evidence supporting the score;
- prompts or clarifications used, if any.

For the practical as a whole also record:

- the review state and controlled reference for each required evidence output;
- any critical-error finding with the required supporting facts;
- private evaluator notes where needed;
- learner-facing feedback/remediation;
- follow-up or reassessment status.

Do not convert the development 80/100 threshold into a production claim until the standard-setting/calibration record authorizes it.

## Calibration use

Calibration should test whether independent assessors applying the same criterion anchors to the same performance assign comparable criterion/domain scores and critical-error decisions. Disagreement should be traced to wording, evidence sufficiency, prompt effects, or form ambiguity before changing the candidate's result standard.

The anchors are expected to improve inter-rater consistency; they do not prove it. Record and analyze calibration evidence before operational certification use.
