# Course 1 Instructor Response Exemplars

**Course:** `COURSE-LH-TECH1-001 — Safety, Responsible Practice & Cultivation Workflows`

## Purpose

Use these examples to coach the quality of learner reasoning and workplace records. They are not answer keys to be memorized. Change identifiers, times, locations, equipment, and scenario details when using them in class or remediation.

A strong response should be judged by whether it is accurate, bounded by role authority, reconstructable, and useful to the next qualified person—not by whether it matches the wording below.

---

## 1. Pre-task hazard brief

### Weak response

> Wet floor near equipment. Be careful. Wear gloves. Tell supervisor if needed.

### Why it is weak

- does not identify the exact location or equipment;
- does not distinguish the slip hazard from electrical exposure;
- proposes gloves without tying PPE to the assigned task;
- does not state whether work continues, stops, or the area is controlled;
- makes escalation conditional without explaining the trigger.

### Stronger response

> **Area:** Room V-2 entrance, powered cart charger C-04. **Condition:** moisture is spreading toward a charger with visible cord damage. **Immediate action:** stop use of C-04; prevent access to the affected area using the authorized local control; do not touch or open the charger. **Task status:** movement through the affected path is paused until the hazard is controlled. **Escalation:** electrical/maintenance response per site procedure. **Separate task PPE:** sanitation PPE remains gloves and eye protection per the assigned sanitation instruction; that PPE does not make the electrical condition acceptable.

### Coaching question

"Which part of this response controls exposure, and which part transfers the next action to an authorized role?"

## 2. PPE / label / SDS preparation

### Weak response

> The SDS says it is irritating, so use gloves, goggles, and a respirator.

### Why it is weak

- treats generic hazard information as task-specific PPE authorization;
- adds respiratory protection without evidence that the task/procedure requires it or that the worker is cleared/trained;
- does not identify product-label or site-procedure controls.

### Stronger response

> The assigned sanitation instruction requires gloves and eye protection for this task. The product label controls use conditions and any product-specific restrictions. The SDS provides hazard and emergency information that supports preparation but does not independently authorize a different task or respirator use. If the label, work instruction, or site respiratory-protection program requires a control not available to me, I stop and escalate rather than improvise.

### Coaching question

"Which document tells you what the product may be used for, and which tells you how this employer assigned the task?"

## 3. Biosecurity movement plan

### Weak response

> Clean the tools before moving rooms and spray the cart with disinfectant.

### Why it is weak

- does not describe movement direction or contamination source;
- does not show cleaning before disinfection where required;
- ignores workers, wheels, hose ends, waste, and recontamination after cleaning;
- gives no verification point.

### Stronger response

> Move clean tools from the clean storage point into Zone A before entering higher-risk Zone B. The cart returning from Zone B does not cross back into the clean corridor until visible debris is removed and the approved cleaning/disinfection sequence is completed. Hose ends stay off the floor and do not cross the zone boundary. Waste leaves by the designated dirty route. After sanitation, tools and cart surfaces are protected from contact with the dirty staging area before reuse. Completion is documented at the boundary-control point.

### Coaching question

"What could recontaminate the cart after you disinfect it?"

## 4. Status-stack decision

### Weak response

> The quarantine was cleared, so the bench can be worked even though the pesticide sign is still posted.

### Why it is weak

- assumes clearing one status clears another independent status;
- fails to identify the controlling entry restriction;
- may imply release authority the learner does not hold.

### Stronger response

> The quarantine release changes only the quarantine status. The active pesticide restricted-entry notice remains a separate entry control and must be evaluated under the applicable label/site procedure before entry. I do not treat the bench as generally released until every applicable restriction has been checked and the responsible role/process confirms access.

### Coaching question

"Which status changed, and which status did not?"

---

## 5. Controlled instruction conflict

### Weak response

> The work order says Revision 6 and the binder has Revision 5, but the differences seem minor, so I would follow the newer parts I remember.

### Why it is weak

- blends conflicting instructions;
- relies on memory rather than controlled-document status;
- fails to preserve the conflict as evidence;
- begins work before authority is established.

### Stronger response

> I pause the affected task and preserve both document identities. The work order references Revision 6, while the available controlled copy is Revision 5. I verify the current controlled instruction through the site's document-control path and escalate the conflict before performing the affected step. I do not create a hybrid procedure from memory or choose whichever step appears more current.

### Coaching question

"What evidence would establish which instruction is controlled for this task?"

---

## 6. Identity genealogy

### Weak response

> Tray T-18 became two transplant batches, so I assigned new labels B-41 and B-42 and threw away the old tray label.

### Why it is weak

- creates output identities without preserving their relationship to the source;
- removes evidence before the transformation is reconstructable;
- does not record the event, time, or destination.

### Stronger response

> Source tray T-18 was split under Work Order W-77 at 10:15 into Batch B-41 for Room V-2 and Batch B-42 for Room V-3. The event record links both new batches back to T-18 and preserves the source identity, event time, destinations, counts, and responsible record. The physical source identifier is not discarded until the controlled process has preserved the genealogy.

### Coaching question

"If B-42 shows a later problem, can you reconstruct exactly which source material produced it?"

---

## 7. Inventory discrepancy

### Weak response

> The system says 48 and I counted 47, so I changed the system to 47.

### Why it is weak

- assumes the physical count is automatically correct;
- changes controlled data without a supported correction basis;
- destroys the original discrepancy;
- records no verification or escalation.

### Stronger response

> Work order and system show 48; two independent physical counts show 47. Identifier checks are complete and no authorized recent movement explains the difference. I preserve both values, pause finalization of the affected movement, document the checks performed and the unresolved one-unit discrepancy, and escalate under the inventory-reconciliation procedure. I do not edit either value merely to make them agree.

### Coaching question

"What evidence proves which side is wrong? If you cannot prove it, what should remain visible?"

---

## 8. Waste / disposition record

### Weak response

> Waste container unknown. Thrown away so it cannot be mixed with good material.

### Why it is weak

- destroys evidence before identity/status resolution;
- does not preserve source, quantity, disposition authority, or time;
- may bypass required local destruction/disposition controls.

### Stronger response

> Unidentified cultivation-waste container found at Waste Staging 2 at 14:05. Source identity could not be confirmed from the label or adjacent records. Container remains physically separated and identified as unresolved; it was not combined with other waste. Quality/authorized disposition role notified. Final destruction/disposition is pending the local controlled process.

### Coaching question

"What would be impossible to reconstruct if the container were discarded now?"

---

## 9. Equipment readiness

### Weak response

> Guard is missing, but the machine runs normally. Use extra caution until maintenance gets there.

### Why it is weak

- treats behavior as a substitute for a required safeguard;
- allows continued use after a failed readiness criterion;
- does not control equipment status.

### Stronger response

> Asset TR-2 fails the pre-use check because the required guard is absent. The asset is not used. I apply the site's out-of-service/reporting process within my authority and notify the maintenance/authorized role. I do not improvise a temporary guard or operate the asset based on careful technique.

### Coaching question

"What is the purpose of a readiness criterion if passing performance can override it?"

---

## 10. Recurring fault report

### Weak response

> Pump keeps failing. Probably clogged. Reset three times. Maintenance needs to fix it.

### Why it is weak

- turns a hypothesis into a diagnosis;
- does not identify asset, zone, times, alarm, checks, or crop impact;
- repeated resetting may conceal recurrence.

### Stronger response

> **Asset:** Pump P-2 serving Zone 3. **14:10:** low-flow alarm; Zone 3 showed no flow. Permitted check: reservoir level normal, accessible external line not visibly kinked. One permitted reset cleared the alarm. **14:16:** same alarm recurred and Zone 3 remained dry. No protected access or disassembly performed. Scheduled irrigation did not complete. Asset/status controlled per site procedure; Maintenance Ticket M-44 opened. Cause not confirmed.

### Coaching question

"Which sentence is direct observation, and which sentence would become an unsupported diagnosis if you added it?"

---

## 11. Data correction / late entry

### Weak response

> I forgot to record pH at 10:00 but remember it was about 6.1, so I entered 6.1 with a 10:00 timestamp.

### Why it is weak

- represents memory as contemporaneous measured data;
- hides the actual entry time;
- does not preserve the missing-data condition.

### Stronger response

> Required 10:00 pH entry is missing. No instrument/system record is available to verify the value. I do not enter a remembered estimate as measured data. I follow the facility's missing-data/late-entry process, identify the missing observation, record the actual time of the late note if required, preserve attribution, and escalate if the missing result affects a release or process decision.

### Coaching question

"What fact do you actually know now: the measurement value, or that the measurement record is missing?"

---

## 12. Shift handoff

### Weak response

> Room mostly done. Pump issue sent to maintenance. Plants okay. Check tomorrow.

### Why it is weak

- hides exact identity and open work;
- gives no timing, ticket, restriction, or next action;
- does not support incoming cross-check or ownership transfer.

### Stronger response

> **Open item 1 — Zone 3 / Pump P-2:** low-flow alarm recurred at 14:16 after one permitted reset; Ticket M-44 open; Zone 3 irrigation incomplete; verify ticket/status before the 18:00 irrigation event. **Open item 2 — Batch B-17 / Bench C:** plant-health hold remains active; no movement performed; lead notified. **Open item 3 — Movement W-77:** Batch B-41 complete; B-42 paused due to identifier discrepancy; discrepancy record D-12 open. Incoming technician read back all three open items and confirmed the 18:00 timing.

### Coaching question

"If the outgoing worker leaves now, can the incoming worker tell what must happen next without guessing?"

## 13. Integrated prioritization

### Weak response

> I would start with the first task on the work order and deal with issues as they come up.

### Why it is weak

- ignores dependencies and restrictions that can block downstream tasks;
- treats task order as overriding safety, identity, access, or equipment controls.

### Stronger response

> Before acting, I review the packet for controls that change whether the work can proceed. The active access restriction and identity mismatch block the planned movement; the missing sanitation product blocks the required preparation unless an approved contingency exists; the recurring pump alarm is a separate equipment/process issue that remains inside the operator escalation path. I prioritize conditions that create exposure or can cause irreversible identity/record loss before routine production work.

### Coaching question

"Which problem can cause the most irreversible harm if you proceed before resolving it?"

---

## How to use exemplars without teaching to the answer

- change scenario facts and identifiers;
- ask learners to critique a response before showing a stronger version;
- require learners to identify **why** each additional field matters;
- present two partially correct responses and ask which is more defensible;
- remove one critical field from a strong response and ask what is lost;
- do not reuse these exact examples as secure credential items.
