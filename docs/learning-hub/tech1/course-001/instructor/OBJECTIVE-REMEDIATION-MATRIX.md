# Course 1 Objective-Linked Remediation Matrix

**Course:** `COURSE-LH-TECH1-001 — Safety, Responsible Practice & Cultivation Workflows`

## Purpose

Use this matrix when a learner misses formative or summative items, produces weak workbook evidence, or shows a non-critical practical-performance gap. Remediation is tied to the controlled learning objective and the reasoning error that caused the miss. Do not remediate by simply showing the keyed answer or requiring repeated attempts on the same item.

A remediation cycle should normally follow this sequence:

1. identify the objective and the learner's reasoning error;
2. ask the diagnostic prompt before reteaching;
3. return the learner to the smallest useful lesson/workbook segment;
4. require the learner to explain or perform the corrected decision rule;
5. reassess with a different but equivalent case;
6. record the objective, activity used, evidence observed, and whether the learner is ready to continue.

The examples below are expandable guidance, not fixed scripts or content limits.

---

## `LO-LH-TECH1-001-01`

**Controlled objective:** Identify common cultivation workplace hazards and select the correct routine response: control within authorization, stop, isolate where authorized, or escalate.

**Common misconceptions**

- Production urgency changes the safety threshold.
- A hazard is acceptable if the worker can personally avoid it.
- Recognizing a problem automatically authorizes repair or technical intervention.
- Once a task starts, the original hazard assessment does not need to be reconsidered.

**Diagnostic prompts**

- "What changed in the work area, and does that change the original control decision?"
- "Which part of your response is actually inside Technician I authority?"
- "If you cannot eliminate the hazard yourself, what is the next controlled action?"

**Corrective coaching**

Rebuild the decision as: **observe condition → judge exposure/risk → use the strongest authorized routine control → stop/isolate where authorized when the condition is not controlled → escalate to the role that owns the next action**. Schedule pressure does not create authorization.

**Return-to-practice**

- Lessons 1.1 and 1.3.
- Workbook Activity 1, Pre-task hazard brief.
- Rework the hazard-control decision flow using a different room condition.

**Reassessment**

Present a changed-condition case in which a routine task becomes unsafe after work begins. Require the learner to state what changed, whether work can continue, the authorized immediate action, and the escalation path.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-001-02`

**Controlled objective:** Use supplied PPE requirements, chemical labels and safety data sheet information to prepare for an assigned cultivation task without exceeding training or authorization.

**Common misconceptions**

- More PPE is automatically safer than the specified PPE.
- An SDS can replace the product label or the employer's task procedure.
- Familiarity with a chemical allows a worker to skip the current label/instruction.
- PPE selection grants permission to perform a task that was not assigned or authorized.

**Diagnostic prompts**

- "Which source tells you what the assigned task requires?"
- "What is the difference between hazard information and authorization to perform the task?"
- "Which PPE or restriction is task-specific rather than a personal preference?"

**Corrective coaching**

Use the sequence **assigned task → current procedure/product label → applicable hazard/SDS information → required controls/PPE → restrictions/authorization check**. Do not improvise a chemical use or task scope from generic hazard information.

**Return-to-practice**

- Lesson 1.2.
- Workbook Activity 1.
- Compare a mock label, SDS excerpt, and work instruction and identify what each document contributes.

**Reassessment**

Give a new sanitation-task packet with a label, SDS excerpt, and site instruction. Require the learner to select required controls and explain which source supports each choice.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-001-03`

**Controlled objective:** Execute sanitation and controlled-movement procedures that reduce cross-contamination risk between plants, rooms, tools and work zones.

**Common misconceptions**

- Disinfection works reliably without prior cleaning when visible debris remains.
- A sanitized tool can move anywhere without considering the movement pathway.
- Contamination control is only about plants, not people, carts, hoses, waste, containers, or tools.
- A completed sanitation step cannot be undone by recontamination.

**Diagnostic prompts**

- "What is the source, route, and receiving zone in this contamination pathway?"
- "What must happen before the disinfectant step can be effective under the supplied process?"
- "What could recontaminate the cleaned object before it reaches the destination?"

**Corrective coaching**

Teach pathway reasoning: **source → carrier/route → boundary → receiving zone**. Cleaning/disinfection is one control inside the pathway; sequencing, movement direction, separation, and protection from recontamination also matter.

**Return-to-practice**

- Lessons 2.1 and 2.2.
- Workbook Activity 2, Biosecurity movement map.
- Workbook Activity 9, Status-stack and access-control challenge, when access status affects movement.

**Reassessment**

Give a new people/tool/cart movement map containing one backtracking route and one recontamination opportunity. Require the learner to redesign the sequence and justify each control.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-001-04`

**Controlled objective:** Recognize quarantine, restricted-entry, hold and isolation conditions and apply the response defined by the supplied procedure.

**Common misconceptions**

- Quarantine, hold, isolation, and pesticide restricted-entry are interchangeable labels.
- One status automatically cancels another.
- A technician can release a status after the original reason appears resolved.
- A work order overrides an active restriction.

**Diagnostic prompts**

- "What does each posted status control?"
- "Can more than one restriction apply to the same area or material at the same time?"
- "Who has authority to release each status in this scenario?"

**Corrective coaching**

Make the learner evaluate each status independently, determine its scope, identify the controlling procedure/role, and then apply the **most restrictive combination that actually applies**. Never infer release authority.

**Return-to-practice**

- Lesson 2.3.
- Workbook Activity 9, Status-stack and access-control challenge.
- Rebuild the quarantine/hold/REI comparison from memory, then verify it against the lesson.

**Reassessment**

Provide a case with two simultaneous statuses and a scheduled task. Require the learner to identify which work is blocked, which work can proceed, and which role must authorize release.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-001-05`

**Controlled objective:** Identify the correct controlled SOP or work order for a task and distinguish required steps, acceptance criteria, exceptions and escalation points.

**Common misconceptions**

- The newest-looking document is automatically the current controlled instruction.
- Two conflicting instructions can be blended into a reasonable compromise.
- A worker may continue until the conflict becomes operationally important.
- Capability or experience substitutes for documented authority.

**Diagnostic prompts**

- "What proves this is the current controlled instruction?"
- "Which prerequisite or acceptance criterion is not satisfied?"
- "What should be preserved when two documents disagree?"

**Corrective coaching**

Use **document identity/revision/status → scope → prerequisites → sequence → acceptance criteria → required records → exception/escalation path**. A conflict is evidence to preserve and escalate, not a cue to invent a hybrid procedure.

**Return-to-practice**

- Lessons 3.1 through 3.3.
- Workbook Activity 3, Controlled instruction review.
- Use the controlled-document anatomy visual and mark the fields that establish authority.

**Reassessment**

Give a different work order and SOP revision pair containing one status conflict and one unmet prerequisite. Require the learner to decide whether work can begin and document the reason.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-001-06`

**Controlled objective:** Preserve plant, batch, lot, room and material identity during routine cultivation movements, transfers and handoffs.

**Common misconceptions**

- A new identifier replaces the need to preserve the source relationship.
- Visual appearance can resolve an identifier mismatch.
- Material in the same room can be treated as interchangeable.
- Identity only matters at final inventory reconciliation.

**Diagnostic prompts**

- "What was the source identity, what event occurred, and what is the resulting identity?"
- "Where is the link between the old and new identifiers?"
- "At what point could this genealogy become unrecoverable?"

**Corrective coaching**

Teach identity as a chain of **traceable object + event + input/output relationship + location/time/context**. A new identifier extends genealogy; it does not erase history.

**Return-to-practice**

- Lesson 4.1.
- Workbook Activity 4, Traceability and reconciliation packet.
- Workbook Activity 10, Transformation and event reconstruction lab.

**Reassessment**

Give a split/merge transformation case and require the learner to reconstruct input identities, outputs, event relationship, location, and timing.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-001-07`

**Controlled objective:** Complete routine inventory, movement and waste or destruction records accurately under a supplied vendor-neutral workflow.

**Common misconceptions**

- The software value is automatically more authoritative than physical evidence.
- Vendor-specific screen steps are the professional competency being measured.
- Waste no longer requires identity or event reconstruction because it will be discarded.
- A movement can be documented before it occurs to save time.

**Diagnostic prompts**

- "What physical event actually occurred?"
- "Which stable data fields are needed regardless of software vendor?"
- "What must remain traceable through a waste/disposition event?"

**Corrective coaching**

Focus on stable event data: **identity, source, destination/state, quantity/count where applicable, time, responsible record, and relationship to prior/new identifiers**. Records must describe the physical event, not manufacture one.

**Return-to-practice**

- Lessons 4.2 and 4.3.
- Workbook Activity 4.
- Workbook Activity 10.

**Reassessment**

Provide a vendor-neutral movement/waste packet with one missing event field and one premature disposition entry. Require the learner to identify the defect and produce a reconstructable correction/escalation.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-001-08`

**Controlled objective:** Detect and report a mismatch between physical material and a paper or digital record without falsifying or forcing reconciliation.

**Common misconceptions**

- The goal of reconciliation is to make the two values match by the end of the task.
- A likely explanation is enough basis to edit the record.
- Disposing of questionable material resolves the discrepancy.
- A recount that repeats the mismatch proves the record is wrong.

**Diagnostic prompts**

- "What is confirmed physical evidence, what is confirmed record evidence, and what remains unknown?"
- "Which permitted checks have actually been completed?"
- "What action would destroy evidence before the discrepancy is resolved?"

**Corrective coaching**

Reconciliation is comparison, not forced agreement. Preserve both states, perform permitted verification, document the unresolved difference, control affected movement/disposition, and escalate when the discrepancy cannot be resolved under procedure.

**Return-to-practice**

- Lessons 4.2 and 4.3.
- Workbook Activity 4.
- Use the inventory-reconciliation flow visual.

**Reassessment**

Give a new physical-versus-record discrepancy with a plausible but unverified explanation. Require the learner to separate facts, hypotheses, permitted checks, and next authorized action.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-001-09`

**Controlled objective:** Perform permitted pre-use, readiness, cleaning and operator-care checks on assigned cultivation or irrigation equipment using the supplied procedure.

**Common misconceptions**

- If equipment worked on the prior shift, pre-use checks can be shortened.
- A small repair is operator care because it appears easy.
- A stopped machine is safe to enter or reach into.
- Familiarity with similar equipment expands the local operator procedure.

**Diagnostic prompts**

- "What is the last action explicitly assigned to the operator?"
- "Does the next step require guard removal, protected access, disassembly, or energy control?"
- "What controlled status should the asset have after this failed readiness check?"

**Corrective coaching**

Use **asset/status verification → external/assigned readiness checks → classify finding → routine operator correction only if explicitly assigned → out-of-service/escalation when the boundary is crossed**.

**Return-to-practice**

- Lessons 5.1 and 5.2.
- Workbook Activity 5.
- Workbook Activity 11, Recurring-fault and servicing-boundary lab.

**Reassessment**

Give a different equipment case where one action is allowed and the next apparently simple action crosses into servicing. Require the learner to mark the exact boundary.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-001-10`

**Controlled objective:** Recognize obvious equipment faults, alarms, leaks, clogs or unsafe conditions and produce a useful escalation record without unauthorized repair.

**Common misconceptions**

- An alarm identifies root cause.
- Repeated reset is acceptable if each reset briefly restores operation.
- "Broken" is sufficient fault-report detail.
- A plausible hypothesis should be documented as the confirmed cause.

**Diagnostic prompts**

- "What did you observe directly, and what are you inferring?"
- "What changed over time?"
- "Which permitted checks were performed and what did they show?"
- "What information will the next qualified person need to investigate efficiently?"

**Corrective coaching**

Build a fault timeline: **asset/location → first symptom/alarm/time → permitted checks/results → recurrence → process/crop impact → safe action/status → escalation/ticket**. Keep hypotheses clearly labeled and separate from confirmed facts.

**Return-to-practice**

- Lesson 5.3.
- Workbook Activity 5.
- Workbook Activity 11.

**Reassessment**

Provide a recurring-alarm sequence with conflicting indicators. Require a maintenance-ready escalation that contains no unsupported root-cause claim.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-001-11`

**Controlled objective:** Create contemporaneous, legible and attributable cultivation records and correct errors while preserving the original record history.

**Common misconceptions**

- A remembered value can be entered later using the original event time as if it were contemporaneous.
- Correcting an error means replacing the old value so only the correct value remains.
- A number without identity, unit, location, or time is sufficient if the team knows the context.
- Missing information should be filled with a reasonable estimate to make the record complete.

**Diagnostic prompts**

- "When was the observation made and when was the entry created?"
- "Can another person reconstruct the original value and the correction?"
- "What context is required for this result to remain meaningful?"

**Corrective coaching**

Preserve **observation time, entry time where relevant, attribution, identity/context, original data, correction history, and honest missing-data status**. A clean-looking record is not better than a truthful one.

**Return-to-practice**

- Lesson 6.1.
- Workbook Activity 6.
- Workbook Activity 12, Late-entry and closed-loop handoff lab.

**Reassessment**

Give a record packet with one late entry, one incorrect entry, one missing value, and one context-free measurement. Require different treatment for each defect.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-001-12`

**Controlled objective:** Produce a concise shift handoff that accurately communicates completed work, observations, deviations, unresolved conditions and priorities.

**Common misconceptions**

- Mentioning an issue is enough to transfer responsibility.
- A long list of completed work is a strong handoff even if open risks are buried.
- Written handoff does not require incoming confirmation.
- Maintenance or hold status can be omitted because another department owns it.

**Diagnostic prompts**

- "What remains unresolved at the moment of handoff?"
- "Which open item is highest risk or most time-sensitive?"
- "Who owns the next action, and how do you know the incoming worker understood it?"

**Corrective coaching**

Use **prepare → prioritize open risk/restrictions → identify exact object/location → state factual status → state action already taken → identify next action/timing/owner → incoming questions and read-back/cross-check**.

**Return-to-practice**

- Lessons 6.2 and 6.3.
- Workbook Activity 7.
- Workbook Activity 12.

**Reassessment**

Give a messy shift log with routine completed work and three open conditions. Require the learner to produce a concise handoff and then answer incoming cross-check questions.

## Integrated remediation after multi-objective performance gaps

If a learner's problem crosses multiple objectives, remediate the **decision chain**, not each objective in isolation. Use Lesson 18 and Workbook Activity 8 with a new scenario packet. Ask the learner to show dependencies: which restriction blocks which task, which identity problem affects which record, which equipment condition changes which production step, and what must survive into handoff.

For a practical critical error, stop the simulation, preserve the evidence of what occurred, identify the affected competency/objective, provide targeted retraining, and reassess with an equivalent scenario. Do not simply resume the same scenario after revealing the correct response.
