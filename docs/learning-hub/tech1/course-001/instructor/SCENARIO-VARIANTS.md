# Course 1 Instructor Scenario Variants

**Course:** `COURSE-LH-TECH1-001 — Safety, Responsible Practice & Cultivation Workflows`

## Purpose

These instructor-only variants support guided practice, remediation, oral questioning, and equivalent reassessment. They deliberately change surface details while preserving the target decision rule. Do not treat the examples as a closed bank; instructors may add equivalent cases when the same objective, evidence boundary, difficulty, and authority limits are preserved.

For remediation, avoid giving the learner the same scenario immediately after explaining the preferred response. Change at least two material features: location/object, sequence of events, competing priority, available evidence, or timing.

# Module 1 — Applied Cultivation Workplace Safety

## Variant 1A — Wet path becomes electrical exposure

**Target:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-01`

A condensation leak starts after work has begun. Water is moving across the route to a portable powered device. The device itself has not alarmed. The task is scheduled to finish in 10 minutes.

**Ask the learner**

- What changed from the original hazard assessment?
- Which immediate controls are inside your authority?
- Does the lack of an alarm make the exposure acceptable?

**Strong reasoning target**

Reassess the changed condition, prevent exposure/stop affected work within authority, control access or equipment use as the site procedure allows, and escalate. Schedule pressure is irrelevant to the safety boundary.

**Misconception to watch**

"Finish quickly because the device has not failed yet."

---

## Variant 1B — PPE is available but the task is not authorized

**Target:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-02`

A coworker asks the learner to apply a product because the required gloves and goggles are available and the SDS is in the cabinet. The learner's assigned work order contains no product-application task.

**Ask the learner**

- What does the available PPE establish?
- What still must establish task authorization?
- Which source controls use of the product?

**Strong reasoning target**

PPE availability does not create task authorization. Verify the work assignment, current product label, employer procedure, and applicable training/authorization; do not accept an unassigned chemical task merely because controls are present.

---

## Variant 1C — Work-related symptom pattern

**Target:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-01`

A worker reports cough and eye irritation that repeatedly worsen during a dusty processing task and improve away from that work area. The learner is not a medical provider.

**Ask the learner**

- What can be observed and reported without diagnosing a disease?
- What immediate workplace controls or reporting path should be considered under the supplied procedure?

**Strong reasoning target**

Recognize a potentially work-related exposure pattern, reduce/stop exposure within the assigned process where appropriate, document the observed pattern, and use the employer's safety/medical reporting path. Do not diagnose a condition or recommend medical treatment.

# Module 2 — Sanitation, Biosecurity & Controlled Movement

## Variant 2A — Clean tool, contaminated route

**Target:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-03`

A tool is cleaned and disinfected correctly, then placed on a cart that just returned from a dirty plant-waste route. The cart is scheduled to enter a clean propagation zone.

**Ask the learner**

- Is the tool still protected by the earlier sanitation step?
- What is the new contamination pathway?

**Strong reasoning target**

The tool can be recontaminated after sanitation. Reconstruct source → carrier → receiving zone and correct the cart/tool boundary before entry.

---

## Variant 2B — Status stack

**Target:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-04`

A bench has a plant-health hold and sits inside a room with an active restricted-entry notice. The plant-health lead releases the hold but the room-entry notice remains active.

**Ask the learner**

- What changed?
- What did not change?
- Can plant work resume?

**Strong reasoning target**

Hold release does not cancel the separate entry restriction. Each status has its own scope and release authority.

---

## Variant 2C — Product substitution pressure

**Target:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-03`

The approved sanitation product is empty. A similar product is available in another department. The next plant movement is scheduled now.

**Ask the learner**

- Can the learner substitute the product based on similarity?
- What evidence is required before an alternative is used?

**Strong reasoning target**

Do not substitute unless the controlled procedure/authorized role permits it and the product's label/use conditions support the task. Use the contingency/escalation path and delay work if required.

# Module 3 — SOPs, Work Orders, Authority & Escalation

## Variant 3A — Work order references an unavailable revision

**Target:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-05`

The work order references SOP Revision 8, but the controlled document system only displays Revision 7 and an archived Revision 8 PDF emailed by a coworker.

**Ask the learner**

- Which file is controlled?
- What evidence is missing?
- Can work begin using the emailed copy?

**Strong reasoning target**

Do not infer controlled status from an archived/email copy. Preserve the conflict and use the document-control/escalation path before affected work begins.

---

## Variant 3B — Usual supervisor unavailable

**Target:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-05`

A task requires approval after an acceptance criterion fails. The learner's normal supervisor is unavailable, but the site procedure lists a backup role.

**Ask the learner**

- Does an unavailable supervisor justify self-approval?
- What is the next authorized path?

**Strong reasoning target**

Follow the documented alternate escalation path. Capability, delay, or supervisor absence does not transfer approval authority to the technician.

---

## Variant 3C — Incomplete task near shift end

**Target:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-05`

A required verification step cannot be completed before shift end because the needed measurement is unavailable. The physical work appears finished.

**Ask the learner**

- Is the task complete?
- What status should the record show?
- What belongs in handoff?

**Strong reasoning target**

Preserve truthful incomplete status, document the missing acceptance evidence, notify/escalate as required, and hand off the exact remaining step rather than backfilling completion.

# Module 4 — Traceability, Material Movement, Inventory & Waste

## Variant 4A — Split genealogy

**Targets:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-06`, `LO-LH-TECH1-001-07`

One source tray is divided into three new transplant batches sent to two rooms. One new batch identifier is written on a temporary note but not yet linked in the controlled record.

**Ask the learner**

- Which identities must be preserved?
- What event links the outputs to the source?
- What work should pause until the temporary identifier is controlled?

**Strong reasoning target**

Preserve the source-to-output genealogy, record the split event and destinations, and do not allow an unlinked temporary identity to become the sole physical identity.

---

## Variant 4B — Physical count and record disagree after a recent move

**Target:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-08`

The system shows 30 units, the physical count is 29, and a prior-shift note mentions a movement but gives no destination identifier.

**Ask the learner**

- What is confirmed?
- What is plausible but unverified?
- What evidence must be preserved?

**Strong reasoning target**

Recount/verify permitted evidence, preserve the discrepancy and incomplete movement information, do not edit either count based on the note, and escalate for controlled reconciliation.

---

## Variant 4C — Digital disposition precedes physical event

**Targets:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-07`, `LO-LH-TECH1-001-08`

A record shows material as destroyed at 13:00, but the sealed identified container is physically present at 14:00.

**Ask the learner**

- Which evidence conflicts?
- Should the material be discarded immediately to match the record?

**Strong reasoning target**

Preserve physical material and digital history, document the mismatch, control further disposition, and escalate. Do not make physical reality conform to an incorrect record.

# Module 5 — Equipment Readiness, Operator Care & Fault Reporting

## Variant 5A — Failed safeguard with normal performance

**Target:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-09`

A machine starts and runs normally, but the pre-use checklist shows that a required interlock indicator failed its test.

**Ask the learner**

- Does normal operation override the readiness criterion?
- What should the equipment status become?

**Strong reasoning target**

A required safeguard/readiness failure controls the decision. Do not use the asset; follow out-of-service/escalation procedures.

---

## Variant 5B — Easy-looking jam beyond the operator boundary

**Target:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-09`

A visible jam can apparently be removed by reaching just inside a guard. The machine is stopped but can restart automatically when the fault clears.

**Ask the learner**

- What makes this different from an external operator check?
- Is a stopped condition the same as controlled hazardous energy?

**Strong reasoning target**

Stop at the servicing boundary; do not reach past the guard. Use the authorized servicing/energy-control process.

---

## Variant 5C — Recurring alarm with changing impact

**Target:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-10`

A permitted reset clears an alarm once. Ten minutes later it recurs, now affecting a second zone. An operator-visible pressure reading is lower than earlier.

**Ask the learner**

- What new evidence should be added to the escalation?
- Does recurrence authorize deeper troubleshooting?

**Strong reasoning target**

Update the timeline with recurrence, expanded impact, readings, and permitted checks. Control the asset/status as required and escalate; do not convert recurrence into servicing authority.

# Module 6 — Records, Shift Handoff & Integrated Workflow

## Variant 6A — Late entry with exact value available from system history

**Target:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-11`

A technician forgot to enter a measurement at the time of observation, but the instrument system retained a timestamped value. The site procedure allows controlled late entry with source attribution.

**Ask the learner**

- How is this different from remembering a value from memory?
- Which times/source information should remain visible?

**Strong reasoning target**

Use the controlled late-entry process and cite the retained source record; preserve actual observation/system timestamp and the later entry/correction history as required. Do not misrepresent the entry as originally contemporaneous.

---

## Variant 6B — Handoff mentions issue but incoming worker misunderstands status

**Target:** `LO-LH-TECH1-001-12`

The outgoing technician says, "Bench 4 hold is handled." The incoming technician repeats back, "So Bench 4 is released." The hold is actually still active; only the lead was notified.

**Ask the learner**

- Is the handoff complete?
- What must be corrected before responsibility transfers?

**Strong reasoning target**

Correct the misunderstanding explicitly: the hold remains active, lead notification is not release, and the next action/owner/timing must be stated and read back.

---

## Variant 6C — Integrated dependency case

**Targets:** multiple Course 1 objectives

A scheduled movement depends on sanitation completion. The approved sanitation product is unavailable. One source identifier also disagrees with the work order. Meanwhile an unrelated equipment alarm is active in another zone.

**Ask the learner**

- Which conditions block the movement independently?
- Which issue is separate but still requires action?
- What must appear in final records and handoff if the movement never occurs?

**Strong reasoning target**

The sanitation-readiness failure and identity mismatch independently block the movement. The separate alarm follows its own operator escalation path. Final records should show the movement did not occur, why it remained blocked, what was escalated, and what the next shift must do.

# Instructor variation rules

When creating new variants:

1. keep the same controlled objective and authority boundary;
2. change surface facts without changing what makes the preferred decision defensible;
3. avoid inserting an unstated facility rule needed to get the answer;
4. identify whether the learner should **act, stop/hold, verify, document, or escalate**;
5. include enough evidence for the learner to reason without guessing;
6. keep transferred regulatory/quality concepts within the evidence boundaries of the course;
7. do not reuse public instructional variants as secure credential-exam items verbatim.
