# Instructor Guide — COURSE-LH-TECH1-001

## Instructional purpose

Teach entry cultivation technicians to execute assigned work safely, protect crop identity and biosecurity, use controlled instructions, document work accurately, perform only authorized operator-level equipment care, and escalate conditions outside their authority.

## Teaching model

Use short explanation followed by worked example, guided practice, retrieval, and a workplace scenario. Avoid lecture-only delivery. Revisit earlier material throughout the course: safety boundaries recur during equipment work; biosecurity recurs during movement; traceability recurs during records and handoff.

## Instructor preparation

Before delivery, verify that examples do not conflict with the current employer SOP, product label, equipment manual, emergency plan, pesticide restrictions, or jurisdiction-specific cannabis rules. Replace mock forms with approved site forms when training is workplace-specific. Do not present the course as granting pesticide-handler, respirator, electrical, HVAC, LOTO-authorized, supervisory, or platform-specific certification.

## Module facilitation

### Module 1 — Applied workplace safety
Emphasize hazard recognition, task-specific PPE/HazCom use, stop-work/escalation, occupational respiratory/allergy awareness, and the distinction between recognizing a fault and being authorized to repair it. Use realistic room-entry, spill, electrical, alarm, and treated-area cases.

### Module 2 — Sanitation and biosecurity
Teach contamination pathways before chemical products. Learners should map movement of people, tools, carts, hose ends, incoming plants, debris, and waste. Reinforce cleaning before disinfection where the approved process requires it, current label/SOP control, quarantine versus pesticide restricted-entry, and release authority.

### Module 3 — SOPs and authority
Require learners to work from current controlled instructions. Practice identifying revision/status, prerequisites, acceptance criteria, documentation, exceptions, and escalation points. Never reward improvisation that exceeds authorization.

### Module 4 — Traceability
Teach identity and event history independent of any one vendor platform. Practice source/destination/time/quantity relationships, genealogy, downtime records, discrepancies, and waste/disposition handoff. A learner must never force a record to match expectation.

### Module 5 — Equipment care
Use pre-use inspections and fault-reporting exercises. Draw a bright line between operator care and servicing. A strong technician can recognize when not to open, bypass, reset, clear, or repair equipment.

### Module 6 — Records and handoff
Apply ALCOA+ as a transferable data-quality model and use two-way handoff practice. Require factual separation among observation, action, result, unresolved condition, and hypothesis. The final scenario should integrate safety, identity, equipment, biosecurity, and documentation.

## Assessment use

Each module has a 12-item formative test with immediate feedback. These tests support retrieval and remediation; they are not the certification exam. The 36-item final course test is summative at course level. The integrated practical is required because knowledge questions alone cannot show safe workflow execution. Current numeric thresholds are developmental until pilot review and standard setting are complete.

## Remediation

When a learner misses an item, route remediation to the exact objective and source-backed lesson. Require explanation of why the preferred response is stronger than the distractors. For a practical critical error, stop the simulation, explain the safety/identity reason, retrain the affected competency, then reassess with an equivalent scenario.

## Accessibility

Provide text equivalents for all visuals, keyboard-operable digital interactions, readable tables, sufficient contrast, captions/transcripts for audio/video, and an accessible alternative when a visual-only task is not itself the competency being measured. Do not use color alone to encode status. Accommodations must preserve the competency rather than lower it.

## Evidence boundaries

Government, standards, university-extension, and transferred quality-system sources are used only within the scope recorded in the evidence dossier. Instructor experience can enrich examples but must not override controlled evidence or create unsupported universal cultivation rules.

## Completion evidence

Retain module-test results, workbook artifacts, practical rubric, final course-test result, remediation record where applicable, and version IDs of the instructional package used.
