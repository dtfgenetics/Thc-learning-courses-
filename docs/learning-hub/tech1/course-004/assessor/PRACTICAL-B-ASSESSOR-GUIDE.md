## Standard administration

1. Verify candidate identity and scenario/form version.
2. Provide the controlled candidate packet: work order, source/feed information, meter SOP, room/zone map, plant-stage/substrate context, crop observation card when assigned, prior irrigation record and target record form.
3. Avoid coaching once scoring begins; neutral clarification may identify where supplied materials are located but may not reveal the correct task decision.
4. Observe work-order/identity verification before execution.
5. Observe meter inspection/readiness and the supplied calibration/verification procedure.
6. Observe pH/EC measurement and recordkeeping with sample source, time and method.
7. Observe irrigation execution and required representative delivery checks.
8. Observe response to local delivery/root-zone exceptions without redirecting the entire room unnecessarily.
9. When a symptom card is assigned, score bounded use of stage, symptom pattern, pH/EC, root-zone and irrigation context; do not reward unsupported nutrient diagnosis.
10. Collect all required deliverables and record assessor interventions/equipment irregularities.

## Evidence anchors

### Work-order / identity verification
Strong evidence confirms current work order, room/zone, batch/source/feed identity and any supplied hold/access condition before execution. Missing or ambiguous identity should trigger clarification rather than assumption.

### Measurement technique and calibration awareness
Strong evidence follows the supplied meter procedure, checks required readiness/calibration status, obtains a representative/stable result and preserves method/context. Do not reward unauthorized calibration/repair beyond the supplied procedure.

### pH/EC and nutrition-context interpretation
Strong evidence keeps pH/EC as contextual measurements, distinguishes EC from nutrient-specific analysis and uses symptom/stage/root-zone/irrigation context without claiming an unsupported deficiency, excess or antagonism.

### Irrigation execution
Strong evidence follows the assigned event/work order without independent recipe, schedule or control redesign.

### Delivery/root-zone problem recognition
Strong evidence verifies representative delivery, detects local faults or changed root-zone behavior and avoids treating one emitter or one reading as proof of a room-wide condition.

### Records and reconciliation
Strong evidence records what actually occurred, including sample/method/time, actual pH/EC, actual irrigation completion, exceptions and discrepancies from the work order.

### Escalation / handoff
Strong evidence identifies unresolved work outside Technician I authority, states who/what requires follow-up and does not hide incomplete work.

## Required deliverables

Collect as applicable to the active form:

1. meter/calibration check record;
2. water/solution measurement log;
3. irrigation work record;
4. zone map with exception;
5. nutrition-context note when a symptom card is assigned;
6. brief handoff describing unresolved condition.

## Prompting and neutrality

Permitted neutral administration includes repeating the written instruction or identifying supplied materials. It does not include telling the candidate which sample is correct, that a meter needs verification, where the seeded clogged emitter is, what nutrient diagnosis to choose, or whether to escalate.

Document any non-neutral prompt and flag affected evidence for review.

## Equipment/scenario irregularities

If a meter, simulation, work order or scenario packet is contradictory or unusable:
- do not score the candidate down for the test-system failure;
- document the irregularity;
- pause/reschedule the affected task when the competency cannot be demonstrated fairly;
- do not silently substitute a different competency demand.

## Accessibility/accommodations

Accommodations may change access/presentation or response method while preserving the measured construct. Do not remove a measurement, identity, execution, representative-delivery, documentation or decision task that is itself part of the competency without formal review of an equivalent method. Record the accommodation used without adding private medical information to the scoring record.

## Remediation/reassessment

After scoring, identify the specific objective/task evidence requiring correction and connect feedback to Course 4 remediation materials. Equivalent reassessment should use fresh sample identities, meter state, zone map, work order, symptom context and delivery exception as appropriate. Final attempt limits, waiting periods and credential consequences remain governance decisions.

## Records for calibration/validation

Retain, under approved privacy/retention policy when established:
- de-identified attempt/candidate identifier;
- practical and scenario/form version;
- assessor identifier;
- category scores and total;
- required deliverables/evidence state;
- prompts/interventions/irregularities;
- accommodation at minimum necessary detail;
- remediation/reassessment status;
- timestamps sufficient to reconstruct administration.

These records support validation work; they are not themselves proof that the practical is validated.
