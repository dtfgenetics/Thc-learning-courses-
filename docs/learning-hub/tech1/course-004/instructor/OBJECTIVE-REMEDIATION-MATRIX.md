# Objective Remediation & Reassessment Matrix — COURSE-LH-TECH1-004

**Course:** Water, Root Zone, Nutrition & Irrigation Fundamentals  
**Status:** development instructional support

Use remediation to correct the learner's reasoning or process failure, then return them to fresh practice and equivalent reassessment. Do not simply repeat the same item until memorized.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-004-01`

**Objective:** Collect and identify the assigned water or nutrient-solution sample and obtain pH and EC readings using the supplied meter procedure while preserving units, sample identity, time and measurement context.

**Common failure signals:** wrong/unidentified sample; target copied as measurement; missing units/method/time; suspect meter result accepted without verification; EC treated as nutrient-specific analysis.

**Corrective coaching**
Rebuild the sequence: work order → sample identity → meter readiness → defined method → stable pH/EC reading → units/context → verification/escalation. Compare a complete record with one that contains a plausible number but no reliable identity or method.

**Return-to-practice**
Provide fresh labeled/unlabeled samples and a different meter-status packet. Require the learner to choose the correct sample, perform only the permitted readiness checks, and document pH/EC with complete context.

**Reassessment**
Use a new scenario containing one suspect reading and one valid reading. The learner must identify which evidence can be used, complete the record, and route unresolved meter/sample uncertainty correctly.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-004-02`

**Objective:** Describe root-zone moisture, saturation, drainage and dryback as repeated observations or trends rather than treating one reading or elapsed time alone as a complete irrigation diagnosis.

**Common failure signals:** defines dryback only by hours; relies on one moisture/EC reading; compares incompatible methods; ignores crop/media/environment context; prescribes a universal target.

**Corrective coaching**
Plot repeated comparable observations on a simple timeline. Ask what can be said about direction/rate versus what remains unknown. Re-teach that method consistency and context are required before comparing periods.

**Return-to-practice**
Use a new seven-day log with changed irrigation times, crop stage and repeated moisture/EC observations. The learner describes the trajectory and identifies the contextual variables relevant to escalation.

**Reassessment**
Give a different trend where the same elapsed interval produces a different moisture trajectory. Require an evidence-based description without inventing a universal schedule or root cause.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-004-03`

**Objective:** Use pH, EC, developmental stage, root-zone condition and symptom pattern as diagnostic context while avoiding unsupported nutrient-deficiency, excess or antagonism claims.

**Common failure signals:** names a deficiency from a photo alone; recommends more fertilizer despite conflicting EC/root-zone evidence; ignores stage or irrigation context; states a possible cause as proven.

**Corrective coaching**
Separate **observation** from **possible explanation** and **supported diagnosis**. Build a context wheel using symptom location/progression, developmental stage, pH and method, EC and method, root-zone condition, irrigation history and environment. Require uncertainty to remain visible when evidence is incomplete.

**Return-to-practice**
Provide a fresh symptom card with pH/EC and root-zone data that do not support a simple visual-chart answer. Learner writes a bounded interpretation and escalation note.

**Reassessment**
Use an equivalent but different symptom/context scenario. Success requires accurate observation and evidence boundaries, not naming a nutrient from appearance.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-004-04`

**Objective:** Execute an authorized routine irrigation or fertigation work order by confirming room or zone, material and required sequence, then verify representative delivery and document completion or deviation.

**Common failure signals:** acts on an outdated/wrong work order; treats controller completion as crop delivery; checks only one convenient location; changes schedule/recipe independently; marks incomplete work complete.

**Corrective coaching**
Walk through identity → readiness → authorized execution → representative delivery → deviation handling → truthful completion. Use a zone map to show why a local failure can exist even when the controller/pump ran.

**Return-to-practice**
Use a new work order with different room/zone/source details and one seeded delivery exception. Learner must detect identity issues, execute the allowed sequence and preserve the local exception.

**Reassessment**
Use another simulated irrigation event with a different delivery fault. Require correct work-order matching, representative verification and completion/deviation documentation.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-004-05`

**Objective:** Perform permitted operator-level irrigation and measurement-equipment readiness checks and escalate leaks, clogs, damaged tools, suspect meters, missed events or other unresolved faults without unauthorized repair.

**Common failure signals:** no readiness check; performs protected calibration/repair; changes control programming; fails to escalate; assumes recognizing a fault grants authority to repair it.

**Corrective coaching**
Sort actions into three columns: permitted operator check, permitted routine correction only when procedure says so, and qualified/authorized escalation. Require the learner to cite the supplied procedure/work order for any intervention.

**Return-to-practice**
Provide fresh meter and irrigation-fault cards with an explicit permitted-action list. Learner chooses only allowed checks/actions and writes the escalation for unresolved faults.

**Reassessment**
Use a new fault combination. The learner must demonstrate the operator boundary and preserve the unresolved state in the record rather than improvising repair.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-004-06`

**Objective:** Create reconstructable irrigation, measurement and root-zone records that distinguish target or work-order instructions from observed results, verification actions, unresolved conditions and shift-handoff needs.

**Common failure signals:** plan copied into actual field; missing sample/method/location/time; incomplete work marked complete; guessed diagnosis recorded as fact; receiving shift cannot tell what remains open.

**Corrective coaching**
Apply the reconstruction test: could a qualified person who was absent identify the work order, actual measurement/delivery, method/context, verification performed, unresolved condition, escalation recipient and next action without guessing? Compare vague versus reconstructable records.

**Return-to-practice**
Give a fresh integrated packet with one suspect measurement, one incomplete irrigation zone and one crop observation. Require separate target/actual, verified/unresolved and observation/diagnosis fields.

**Reassessment**
Use a new integrated shift packet. The learner must produce a complete handoff and may not backfill missing evidence from assumption or memory.

## Reassessment controls

- Use fresh numbers, samples, zone maps, symptoms, work orders and fault patterns.
- Preserve the same objective demand while preventing item memorization.
- Do not expose secure summative answer keys during remediation.
- Retain original attempt and reassessment as separate records when policy requires.
- Practical B retest limits, waiting periods and credential consequences remain governance decisions until formally approved.
- Academic remediation does not itself authorize Technician I credential issuance.
