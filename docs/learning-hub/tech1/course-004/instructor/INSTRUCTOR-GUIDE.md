# Instructor Guide — COURSE-LH-TECH1-004

**Course:** Water, Root Zone, Nutrition & Irrigation Fundamentals  
**Status:** development instructor support  
**Boundary:** instruction supports measurement, controlled execution, verification, documentation and escalation. It does not authorize independent nutrient-formulation design, advanced crop steering, protected controls work, root-cause diagnosis or unauthorized repair.

## Core teaching rules

1. Start with sample/work-order identity before interpreting a number or performing a task.
2. Keep **target/instruction** separate from **actual measurement/delivery**.
3. Require pH/EC units, method, sample identity, time and meter context.
4. Treat dryback as repeated comparable behavior, not a universal number of hours.
5. Treat EC as conductivity context, not nutrient-specific chemistry.
6. Treat visible symptoms as observations that require root-zone, irrigation, stage, pH/EC and environmental context before diagnosis.
7. Require representative irrigation-delivery verification; controller status alone is insufficient evidence of crop delivery.
8. Keep operator checks/corrections inside the supplied procedure and escalate work beyond authorization.
9. Make incomplete and unresolved work visible in the record/handoff.

## Lesson 1 — sample identity and pH/EC

### Emphasize
- source water, prepared solution, runoff/drainage and other samples answer different questions;
- meter readiness and verification precede interpretation;
- planned target values are not actual measurements;
- a suspect measurement should be rechecked within procedure before driving crop action.

### Challenge these misconceptions
- “All EC samples are directly comparable.”
- “EC tells me which nutrient is high or low.”
- “A pH number proves the plant symptom cause.”
- “The recipe target can be entered as the measured result.”

### Facilitation check
Give an unidentified sample and a clean-looking measurement record. The learner should refuse to attach the number to a crop/room until the sample identity and method context are established.

## Lesson 2 — root-zone trend and nutrition context

### Emphasize
- repeated comparable measurements reveal trajectory;
- crop size, roots, light, environment, media/container and irrigation history can change water use;
- root-zone EC trends require consistent sampling method;
- symptom charts can support observation vocabulary but do not prove cause.

### Challenge these misconceptions
- “Dryback equals a fixed number of hours.”
- “One runoff EC tells me the full root-zone condition.”
- “A yellow leaf means increase fertilizer.”
- “The same irrigation frequency should stay correct throughout the crop.”

### Facilitation check
Give two weeks of logs with a changing dryback trajectory. Require the learner to describe the change and relevant context before offering any explanation.

## Lesson 3 — work-order execution and delivery verification

### Emphasize
- current work order, identity and hold/access status are checked before action;
- a pump/controller event is not proof of plant-level delivery;
- representative points exist to detect local failure;
- incomplete work must not be marked complete;
- leaks/clogs/electrical/mechanical faults are handled only within the permitted operator procedure.

### Challenge these misconceptions
- “If the controller says complete, the room is watered.”
- “A dry plant authorizes me to change the schedule.”
- “Recognizing a leak means I should repair it.”

### Facilitation check
Seed one local delivery failure in an otherwise normal run. The learner should preserve the local exception rather than changing the entire room or marking the event complete.

## Lesson 4 — deviations and shift handoff

### Emphasize
- symptom, measurement, irrigation and equipment evidence must stay distinguishable;
- an unresolved issue is a valid record state;
- handoff should state location/identity, actual evidence, checks performed, unresolved condition, escalation and next action;
- never backfill missing evidence as though it were observed contemporaneously.

### Facilitation check
Give a partly completed irrigation event plus a symptom report and suspect measurement. The learner must separate each evidence type and produce a handoff another qualified person can continue without guessing.

## Remediation approach

Remediation should target the learner's evidence/process failure:

- wrong/unidentified sample or measurement context;
- single-point or single-time interpretation where trend is required;
- unsupported nutrient diagnosis;
- execution outside the work order;
- failure to verify actual delivery;
- unauthorized repair/correction;
- non-reconstructable or falsely complete record.

Use new values, maps, logs and work orders for return-to-practice and reassessment.
