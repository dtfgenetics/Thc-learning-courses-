# Course 6 Learner Materials

**Course:** Harvest, Postharvest, Traceability & Shift Handoff  
**State:** draft learner package

## Purpose

Course 6 prepares learners for one of the highest-risk operational transitions in cultivation: moving identified living crop material into controlled postharvest lots and receiving records without losing safety, sanitation, identity, quantity or unresolved conditions.

## Applied practice sequence

### Practice 1 — harvest readiness and stop-work

Learners receive a harvest authorization, room/batch IDs, destination map, pathway information, sanitation status and PPE requirements. Seeded problems include a dirty cart, blocked route, identity conflict or unavailable destination. The learner must:

- verify authorization, source identity and destination;
- verify PPE, tools, carts, labels and pathways;
- distinguish a routine permitted control from a condition requiring stop/hold/escalation;
- reassess if conditions change;
- document what must be resolved before movement resumes.

### Practice 2 — controlled harvest and dry-room receiving

Learners execute a simulated transfer containing identified lots, one suspect contamination condition, a receiving-location issue and environmental/product-condition evidence. The learner must:

- preserve clean handling and lot separation;
- hold/isolate suspect material under the supplied process;
- prevent dirty, mixed or unlabeled material from entering normal flow;
- verify receiving rack/location and receiving identity;
- record condition and environmental context at the correct scope;
- avoid treating elapsed time alone as proof that drying is complete.

### Practice 3 — harvest genealogy and reconciliation

Learners map a source batch into several postharvest outputs, including one hold. A planted outgoing/receiving quantity mismatch must remain visible. The learner must:

- preserve source → transformation → output genealogy;
- record output identity, destination and status;
- preserve held/waste/diverted material in the material history as required;
- compare outgoing and receiving quantities using the supplied method;
- preserve both original records when they differ;
- document discrepancy status and next action without overwriting or averaging evidence.

### Practice 4 — integrated dry-room handoff

Learners complete a harvest packet with normal receiving, partial completion, a held container, a quantity discrepancy, an unavailable destination and a rack requiring follow-up. The final handoff must preserve:

- authorization and source identity;
- output identity and destination;
- quantity and reconciliation status;
- product/environment condition;
- sanitation and hold state;
- deviations/unresolved conditions;
- authorized action completed;
- escalation and receiving-shift next action.

The learner must not invent root cause, final disposition, product release status or completion simply to produce a clean-looking record.

## Formative use

The 12-item M01 assessment is diagnostic/formative. Route objective-level misses to the mapped lesson and a fresh practice packet before reassessment.

## Summative academic use

The 24-item Course 6 final is distinct from the formative bank and is derived from the six controlled objectives taught in the four dedicated Course 6 lessons. `TEST-TO-TEACHING-MAP.md` records the exact instructional provenance. Encyclopedia-only, external-reading-only, or unrelated-course material may not be scored unless first taught directly in Course 6. The assessment remains draft until human assessment/release review. A passing academic result does not replace Practical F or confer independent product-release/disposition authority.
