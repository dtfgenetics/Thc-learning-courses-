## Practical C validation questions

Review whether:

- the scouting route is representative and understandable;
- sign/symptom/mechanical-damage/insufficient-evidence categories can be applied consistently;
- incidence and severity tasks use enough information for one defensible answer;
- spatial/trend evidence is observable without requiring specialist diagnostic authority;
- biosecurity/quarantine behavior is visible and scoreable;
- the task prevents unauthorized pesticide/treatment-selection credit;
- documentation expectations are realistic for an entry-level technician.

## Practical D validation questions

Review whether:

- donor/source/work-order identity is unambiguous except where a deliberate discrepancy is seeded;
- sanitation and clean-to-dirty decisions are observable;
- tray/lot traceability can be scored consistently;
- count reconciliation has a deterministic expected result;
- rooting/acclimatization evidence can be interpreted without requiring protocol redesign;
- hold/quarantine behavior is observable;
- task timing and materials are realistic for the delivery mode.

## Practical E validation questions

Review whether:

- the work-order objective and supplied plant conditions are clear;
- a least-disruptive compliant action can be distinguished from personal technique preference;
- tissue-preservation and sanitation behavior are observable;
- stop/hold conditions are sufficiently defined;
- consistency against criteria can be rated without rewarding unnecessary plant manipulation;
- exceptions/damage and handoff requirements are reconstructable;
- the practical remains appropriate to Technician I rather than a crop-strategy role.

## Cross-form equivalence review

For every alternate form, compare:

- objective/task coverage;
- number and seriousness of seeded conditions;
- information burden;
- irreversible-decision burden;
- sanitation/biosecurity challenge;
- documentation burden;
- cognitive demand;
- expected completion time;
- scoring opportunities by category.

Equivalent forms do not need identical surface details, but they must provide comparable opportunity to demonstrate the controlled competencies.

## Delivery-mode equivalence

For simulation, supervised plant/lab and approved workplace-equivalent delivery, document:

- which behaviors are directly observed versus simulated;
- how tactile/tool-use evidence is preserved;
- how plant or room risk is controlled;
- what evidence substitutes for unavailable live conditions;
- whether the same scoring category can still be judged with comparable confidence.
