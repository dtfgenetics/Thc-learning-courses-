## Course 2 evidence focus

### `LO-LH-TECH1-002-01` — representative observation route

Observe whether the candidate verifies room/batch identity, follows a repeatable inspection route, preserves location context and produces evidence that could support a room-level statement without convenience-only or abnormal-only sampling.

Expected evidence includes the inspection route, crop observations, completed inspection record and annotated room map.

### `LO-LH-TECH1-002-02` — morphology-based stage description

Observe whether the candidate records visible developmental/reproductive morphology and keeps schedule timing separate from biological evidence. Credit direct, reviewable descriptions; do not reward unsupported causal explanations for mixed or unexpected reproductive expression.

Expected evidence is primarily the crop inspection/scouting record.

### `LO-LH-TECH1-002-03` — observation versus diagnosis

Observe whether the candidate separates direct observations, spatial/population pattern, relevant context and provisional interpretation. A candidate should preserve uncertainty when the evidence does not establish a cause and should avoid converting one reading, one symptom or one event into a confirmed diagnosis.

Expected evidence includes crop observations, appropriate contextual interpretation and escalation choices.

### `LO-LH-TECH1-002-04` — reconstructable record and photo evidence

Observe whether the record preserves identity, timing, sample/route context, objective wording, required photos/marked findings, unresolved uncertainty and correction history where applicable. An unlabeled close-up or vague note is weaker evidence than a context-linked set another qualified person can reconstruct.

Expected evidence includes the completed inspection record, annotated room map and shift handoff.

### `LO-LH-TECH1-002-05` — escalation and handoff within authority

Observe whether the candidate performs only supplied/authorized checks, records abnormal conditions, escalates unresolved findings and provides a usable handoff without independently diagnosing, treating, redesigning controls, repairing or calibrating beyond the supplied operator-level procedure.

Expected evidence includes escalation choices, completed inspection record and final verbal or written handoff.

## Scoring boundary

Practical A currently uses a 100-point development model with these categories:

- safe entry and hazard response — 20;
- sanitation/biosecurity — 15;
- systematic crop inspection — 20;
- observation quality — 15;
- environmental interpretation — 10;
- documentation/traceability — 15;
- handoff/escalation — 5.

The current target pass is 80/100, but this is a **development threshold**. Do not represent it as a validated professional cut score until formal standard-setting evidence exists.

For Course 2 interpretation, assessors should give particular attention to systematic crop inspection, observation quality, documentation/traceability and handoff/escalation while preserving the full Practical A rules and safety boundaries.

## Critical evidence defects

The following require explicit documentation and should not be hidden by a high total score:

- unsafe entry or unresolved serious hazard;
- wrong room/batch/plant identity that makes the record unreliable;
- fabrication or forced reconciliation of observations;
- unsupported diagnosis presented as confirmed fact when the evidence does not support it;
- unauthorized treatment, repair, calibration or control redesign;
- handoff that omits a material unresolved condition or required escalation.

The controlling Practical A document requires unresolved serious safety or identity errors to be corrected before the practical is considered complete. Any stronger critical-error rule for credential use must be approved through the program validation/standard-setting process rather than invented in this course guide.

## Evidence capture

For each attempt, retain the applicable versioned evidence:

- practical/form version;
- candidate identifier according to the approved privacy model;
- assessor identifier and qualification status;
- date/time and delivery mode;
- room/scenario/form identifier;
- scored category results;
- criterion-level notes where required;
- required candidate deliverables;
- critical or unresolved conditions;
- remediation/reassessment disposition;
- any accommodation used and its approved basis.

Do not expose private evaluator notes on the learner-facing public record.

## Remediation and reassessment

When Course 2 evidence is insufficient, use the objective-specific remediation matrix. Reassessment must use an equivalent fresh scenario/form that preserves the same objective and cognitive/performance demand. Repeating the identical seeded scenario is not sufficient evidence of transfer.

## Assessor conduct

Assessors should:

- score observable evidence rather than inferred intent;
- avoid coaching during scored portions except where the approved safety protocol requires intervention;
- record interventions that materially affect the attempt;
- apply the same evidence standard across candidates/forms;
- distinguish a candidate correction from evidence that the original action was acceptable;
- route ambiguous scoring cases through the calibration/escalation procedure.

## Validation boundary

This guide is a machine-build deliverable. It does **not** constitute assessor qualification, calibration completion, inter-rater reliability evidence, practical validation, pilot acceptance, standard setting, or release approval.
