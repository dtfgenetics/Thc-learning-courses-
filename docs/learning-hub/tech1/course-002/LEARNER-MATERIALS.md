# Learner Materials — COURSE-LH-TECH1-002

**Course:** Plant Observation, Growth Stages & Crop Records  
**Status:** published owner-approved academic learner package  
**Updated:** 2026-09-22

## Learner purpose

Course 2 trains an entry Technician I learner to observe crops systematically, describe developmental and reproductive morphology from visible evidence, keep observation separate from unsupported diagnosis, and create records/photographs/handoffs that another qualified person can reconstruct.

The learner is not being trained to independently diagnose every crop problem, redesign environmental or irrigation controls, select pesticide treatments, or exceed facility authorization.

## Course workflow

1. **Observe representative crop locations.** Confirm purpose, room/zone/crop identity, observation route and denominator before making a room-level statement.
2. **Describe development from morphology.** Use visible plant and reproductive structures as stage evidence; retain calendar timing as context rather than proof.
3. **Separate evidence from diagnosis.** Record direct observation, pattern and context first; keep interpretation provisional when evidence does not establish one cause.
4. **Create reconstructable records.** Preserve identity, time, photographs, sample context, actions, uncertainty, escalation and next-check ownership.
5. **Hand off unresolved work.** Communicate what was completed, what was observed, what remains uncertain and what the receiving role must verify.

## Lesson 1 — Structured Crop Walks and Representative Observation

**Objectives:** `LO-LH-TECH1-002-01`, `LO-LH-TECH1-002-03`

### Learner activity: representative crop walk

Using the supplied crop-room practice sheet:

- mark the room/zone identity;
- state the observation purpose;
- draw a repeatable route through meaningful production areas;
- number observation points;
- record how many plants/sites are inspected;
- distinguish representative observations from separately identified abnormal follow-up plants;
- classify the visible spatial pattern as isolated, edge-associated, zonal/clustered, widespread, or another documented pattern;
- write one room-level statement that does not overgeneralize from a single plant.

**Download:** `/assets/course2/crop-walk-room-map-practice.svg`

### Evidence check

A useful record should answer: *Where did you look? How many units did you inspect? What pattern did you observe? What evidence supports the room-level statement?*

## Lesson 2 — Growth Stage and Reproductive Morphology in Crop Records

**Objectives:** `LO-LH-TECH1-002-02`, `LO-LH-TECH1-002-03`

### Learner activity: morphology-based stage record

For each supplied plant/crop example:

- identify the visible developmental structures;
- record the developmental-stage description supported by those structures;
- record schedule timing separately when supplied;
- note whether the sampled population is uniform or mixed;
- document unexpected reproductive structures without assigning an unverified genetic or environmental cause;
- identify the evidence that would need to be preserved for escalation.

### Learner decision rule

A calendar label may describe the production schedule. It does not override the morphology actually present. When schedule and observed development differ, preserve both.

## Lesson 3 — Observation, Pattern, Context and Diagnostic Boundaries

**Objectives:** `LO-LH-TECH1-002-03`, `LO-LH-TECH1-002-05`

### Learner activity: evidence-layer sorting

Classify each supplied statement as one of the following:

- direct observation;
- spatial/population pattern;
- contextual evidence;
- provisional interpretation;
- unsupported diagnosis.

Then rewrite weak notes so they contain:

- crop/location identity;
- sample denominator or meaningful distribution context;
- objective description;
- relevant measurements/work history kept separate from confirmed cause;
- uncertainty;
- escalation or next-check state.

### Escalation practice

For sudden widespread wilt, suspected spreading pest/disease evidence, unexpected reproductive morphology, strong root-zone abnormality, meaningful zone differences, or equipment-associated damage, state what the technician can document/check under the supplied procedure and what must be escalated.

## Lesson 4 — Crop Observation Records, Photographs and Shift Handoff

**Objectives:** `LO-LH-TECH1-002-04`, `LO-LH-TECH1-002-05`

### Learner activity A: photo-evidence audit

Audit each synthetic image set for:

- crop/location identity;
- date/time linkage;
- context view;
- representative whole-plant view;
- close detail;
- comparison view where useful;
- adequate lighting and focus;
- edits/filters that could materially alter appearance;
- missing evidence that limits interpretation.

**Download:** `/assets/course2/photo-evidence-audit-practice.svg`

### Learner activity B: reconstructable record and handoff

Complete the observation/handoff worksheet with:

- crop identity;
- date/time;
- purpose and observation route;
- developmental context;
- objective observation and spatial pattern;
- photo references;
- available contextual measurements/history;
- action taken;
- uncertainty/unresolved condition;
- who was notified;
- next check and ownership;
- receiving-person cross-check;
- correction history where an entry changes.

## Integrated application scenario

A scheduled crop walk finds one visibly abnormal plant near the entrance, a smaller group of less-developed plants in one zone, and a new droop pattern after a recent irrigation event. The shift is ending in 20 minutes.

The learner must produce an evidence package that:

1. does not use the entrance plant as the room sample;
2. completes or documents the representative observation route;
3. preserves denominator and spatial pattern;
4. records morphology-based developmental differences separately from the room schedule;
5. treats irrigation timing as context rather than confirmed cause;
6. identifies what is directly observed versus interpreted;
7. selects appropriate context/whole-plant/detail photographs;
8. records any permitted checks/actions;
9. escalates unresolved abnormal conditions within the supplied authority boundary;
10. creates a handoff the next technician can reconstruct and continue.

## Remediation and reassessment

Objective-linked remediation is controlled in `instructor/OBJECTIVE-REMEDIATION-MATRIX.md`. A missed objective is not remediated by simply replaying the same answer key.

The learner must receive:

1. corrective coaching for the missed objective;
2. fresh/equivalent return-to-practice work;
3. reassessment at the same intended cognitive demand using a different but equivalent prompt/scenario.

The matrix covers all five controlled Course 2 objectives. Human reviewers still need to confirm equivalence, fairness, accessibility and instructional sufficiency before final course approval.
