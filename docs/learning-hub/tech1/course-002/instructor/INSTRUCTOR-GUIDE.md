# Instructor Guide — COURSE-LH-TECH1-002

**Course:** Plant Observation, Growth Stages & Crop Records  
**Status:** development instructor/remediation package  
**Date:** 2026-09-15

## Instructional purpose

Course 2 develops disciplined evidence collection rather than unsupported crop diagnosis. Instructors should consistently reinforce the boundary between what a Technician I can directly observe/document and what requires additional evidence, facility procedure, or escalation to a qualified role.

## Core teaching rules

1. Do not reward confident causal labels when the evidence supports only an observation or differential.
2. Require room/zone/crop/plant identity and timing whenever a record is meant to be reconstructable.
3. Require denominator/sample context before accepting room-level prevalence statements.
4. Treat schedule timing as developmental context, not proof that morphology must match the schedule.
5. Require photographs to preserve context, detail, identity/time and faithful appearance.
6. Require unresolved conditions and next-check ownership in handoffs.
7. Keep facility-specific procedures and jurisdiction-specific requirements clearly separated from transferable course principles.

## Lesson facilitation

### Lesson 1 — Structured Crop Walks and Representative Observation

**Target objectives:** `LO-LH-TECH1-002-01`, `LO-LH-TECH1-002-03`

Strong learner evidence should include a defined purpose, repeatable route, meaningful production areas, denominator, spatial pattern and a room-level statement proportional to the sample.

**Common misconception:** the worst-looking plant is automatically representative.

**Remediation:** give the learner a new room map with abnormal plants placed in different zones. Require a route and a statement that separates representative sampling from targeted follow-up inspection.

### Lesson 2 — Growth Stage and Reproductive Morphology in Crop Records

**Target objectives:** `LO-LH-TECH1-002-02`, `LO-LH-TECH1-002-03`

Strong learner evidence should name visible developmental/reproductive structures and preserve schedule timing separately.

**Common misconception:** a week number or cultivar label proves developmental state.

**Remediation:** provide a fresh set of mixed-stage examples with schedule labels intentionally mismatched to morphology. Require the learner to justify each stage description from visible evidence and document exceptions.

### Lesson 3 — Observation, Pattern, Context and Diagnostic Boundaries

**Target objectives:** `LO-LH-TECH1-002-03`, `LO-LH-TECH1-002-05`

Strong learner evidence should separate direct observation, distribution/pattern, contextual history, interpretation and confirmed diagnosis.

**Common misconception:** timing correlation or one familiar symptom confirms cause.

**Remediation:** use a new scenario containing multiple plausible explanations. Require the learner to identify what is known, what is provisional, what additional evidence would discriminate possibilities and what must be escalated.

### Lesson 4 — Crop Observation Records, Photographs and Shift Handoff

**Target objectives:** `LO-LH-TECH1-002-04`, `LO-LH-TECH1-002-05`

Strong learner evidence should allow another qualified person to reconstruct what was inspected, what was seen, what was done, what remains unresolved and what happens next.

**Common misconception:** a close-up photograph or short note is sufficient without context/identity.

**Remediation:** provide a flawed synthetic evidence packet containing unlabeled photos, missing denominator, vague location and incomplete handoff. Require correction without inventing missing facts.

## Applied-practice scoring guidance

Use the following development rubric until the controlled practical crosswalk is finalized:

| Dimension | Meets development expectation | Needs remediation |
|---|---|---|
| Identity/context | Correct crop/location/time/sample context preserved | Record cannot be reliably tied to the inspected crop or time |
| Observation quality | Describes visible/measured evidence without unsupported causation | Causal label replaces observation or important evidence is omitted |
| Representative evidence | Route/denominator/pattern support the scope of the statement | One convenient/abnormal plant is generalized to the crop |
| Developmental evidence | Morphology supports stage description; timing kept contextual | Calendar/cultivar label is treated as proof |
| Photo evidence | Context, representative and detail views are useful and linked to identity/time | Images are misleading, context-poor, unlinked or materially altered |
| Escalation boundary | Authorized checks/actions only; unresolved condition escalated | Learner exceeds role or fails to escalate consequential uncertainty |
| Handoff | Completed, observed, unresolved, next check and cross-check are reconstructable | Receiving role must guess what remains open |

This table supports instruction; it is not a substitute for the controlled `PRACTICAL-TECH1-A` assessor criteria.

## Academic assessment use

- `ASSESS-LH-TECH1-002-M01` is formative and uses immediate feedback.
- `ASSESS-LH-TECH1-002-FINAL` is the draft public-academic summative assessment.
- Both currently declare an 80% development threshold.
- Do not represent these public academic items as the secure Technician I credential exam.
- Do not treat repeated attempts without targeted remediation/equivalent practice as automatically equivalent evidence.

## Remediation model

When a learner misses an objective:

1. identify the objective, not merely the question number;
2. assign the corresponding lesson section and a fresh applied example;
3. require the learner to explain the evidence/decision rule in their own work product;
4. use an equivalent new scenario or practice case rather than simply replaying the same answer;
5. document what was remediated and what remains unresolved.

Final reassessment rules remain subject to assessment/human review and should not be treated as psychometrically validated until that evidence exists.

## Instructor evidence to retain during pilot/review

- recurring misconceptions by objective;
- unclear lesson wording or visual interpretation problems;
- time-on-task problems;
- accessibility barriers;
- assessment items that cue answers, have multiple defensible keys or fail to match instruction;
- practice steps that cannot be performed consistently;
- role/authorization language that learners or employers interpret differently;
- remediation effectiveness and repeated-error patterns.
