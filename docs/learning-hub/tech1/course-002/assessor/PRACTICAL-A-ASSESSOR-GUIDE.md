**Course:** `COURSE-LH-TECH1-002 — Plant Observation, Growth Stages & Crop Records`  
**Practical:** `PRACTICAL-TECH1-A — Safe Room Entry & Crop Inspection`  
**Status:** development support artifact  
**Authority boundary:** this guide does not validate the practical, approve the 80/100 target pass score, or imply evaluator calibration, pilot evidence, or credential-release approval.

## Purpose

Use this guide to administer and score Practical A consistently while Course 2 remains in development. The canonical practical is `docs/academy-v2/practicals/PRACTICAL-TECH1-A-SAFE-ROOM-ENTRY-CROP-INSPECTION.md`; the objective-to-practical mapping is `registry/course2-practical-a-crosswalk.json`. If this guide conflicts with either controlled source, stop scoring and resolve the version mismatch before continuing.

## Assessor prerequisites

Before administering the practical, the assessor should:

- use the current controlled practical, scenario packet, room map, forms and supplied targets/limits;
- understand the facility or simulated-site safety and biosecurity procedures used by the scenario;
- understand the operator-care boundary: the candidate may perform supplied checks but may not invent or perform unauthorized repair, calibration or control redesign;
- verify that candidate identity, practical version, scenario/form identifier, date/time and assessor identity can be recorded;
- review the accessibility/accommodation plan and confirm that accommodations preserve the construct being assessed;
- avoid coaching, leading prompts or diagnostic hints once scoring begins.

## Standard administration sequence

1. Verify candidate and packet identity.
2. Confirm required equipment and scenario materials are available and functional.
3. Read the same task directions used for the current form.
4. Allow the candidate to review the work order and prior handoff.
5. Observe the candidate's PPE selection, hazard response and clean-entry sequence.
6. Observe room/batch identity verification before crop inspection.
7. Observe the inspection route and evidence collection without redirecting the candidate unless a real safety intervention is necessary.
8. Present the environmental/sensor station exactly as specified by the scenario.
9. If assigned, present the canopy-light verification station with the controlled grid, measurement height/orientation, instrument/context and photoperiod record.
10. Collect the completed records, annotated map and handoff.
11. Score from observed evidence and submitted records, not from assumptions about what the candidate intended.
12. Record any assessor intervention, equipment fault, interruption or accommodation that could affect interpretation.

## Scoring model

The controlled practical currently uses a 100-point model:

| Scoring category | Points | Evidence focus |
|---|---:|---|
| Safe entry and hazard response | 20 | PPE selection, recognition of obvious hazards, appropriate response before production work |
| Sanitation/biosecurity | 15 | Clean-entry sequence, sanitation use, movement decisions that protect clean areas |
| Systematic crop inspection | 20 | Correct identity, repeatable route, representative observation rather than convenience sampling |
| Observation quality | 15 | Observable crop evidence, stage/vigor/morphology/stress descriptions, avoidance of unsupported diagnosis |
| Environmental interpretation | 10 | Use of supplied readings, placement/comparison/calibration-verification/logging context, appropriate follow-up/escalation |
| Documentation/traceability | 15 | Location, identity, units, time, measurement context, records sufficient for reconstruction |
| Handoff/escalation | 5 | Clear actionable handoff, appropriate escalation, limits of authority respected |
| **Total** | **100** | |

**Development target pass:** 80/100. The target is inherited from the canonical Practical A document and remains subject to human technical review, pilot evidence and standard-setting/validation work.

## Scoring anchors

Apply the following anchors within each category. Point allocation inside a category should be defined on the active scoring form; do not invent hidden deductions.

### Meets the intended performance demand

Evidence is complete enough to reconstruct what the candidate did, decisions are supported by observable or supplied information, identity/location/context are preserved, and the candidate stays within authorized operator scope.

### Partially meets

The candidate performs the core task but leaves material gaps in repeatability, representativeness, context, documentation or escalation. Score only the evidence demonstrated on the active form.

### Does not yet meet

The task is omitted, performed in a way that defeats the competency being assessed, relies on unsupported diagnosis/assumption, loses identity/traceability, or exceeds the supplied authority boundary.

## Required stop/correction boundary

The canonical practical states that any unresolved serious safety or identity error must be corrected before the practical is considered complete. This is a completion boundary, not permission to invent new automatic-failure rules. When it occurs:

1. intervene when necessary to protect people, equipment, product or controlled identity;
2. document what occurred and what intervention was required;
3. require correction of the unresolved serious safety or identity error before completion;
4. score demonstrated evidence according to the controlled form;
5. route any proposed critical-failure policy change through the validation/governance process rather than applying an assessor-created rule.

## Evidence checklist

Capture the evidence required by Practical A as applicable to the assigned form:

- selected PPE;
- hazard decisions;
- sanitation sequence;
- inspection route;
- crop observations;
- environmental interpretation;
- sensor representativeness decision using placement, comparison, verification/calibration and logging context;
- operator-level readiness check and escalation decision where applicable;
- completed canopy light-verification grid or simulated measurement record when assigned;
- PPFD units, measurement location/height/orientation and instrument/context when assigned;
- photoperiod record and comparison with the supplied scenario target or limit when assigned;
- escalation choices;
- completed inspection record;
- final verbal or written handoff.

## Assessor neutrality and prompting

Permitted neutral administration includes repeating the written direction, clarifying where a supplied form or instrument is located, and addressing safety. It does not include telling the candidate what hazard to notice, which plant to select, how to interpret a reading, what diagnosis to write, which escalation decision is correct, or how to recover points.

If a prompt beyond neutral administration occurs, document the prompt and flag the affected evidence for review.

## Equipment and scenario irregularities

If equipment, simulated data or scenario materials are missing, contradictory or unusable:

- do not score the candidate down for the test-system failure;
- document the irregularity;
- pause or reschedule the affected station when the competency cannot be demonstrated fairly;
- do not silently substitute a different competency demand.

## Accessibility and accommodations

Reasonable accommodations may change presentation or response access while preserving the measured competency. Examples can include accessible-format instructions, assistive technology, additional navigation support unrelated to the target skill, or approved timing adjustments. Do not remove a safety, observation, measurement, documentation or decision task that is itself part of the construct without formal review of an equivalent method.

Record the accommodation used; do not place private medical information in the scoring record.

## Remediation and reassessment

After scoring:

1. identify the specific task/category evidence that did not yet meet the requirement;
2. connect feedback to the relevant Course 2 objective and controlled learning/remediation material;
3. avoid disclosing secure scoring material beyond what is needed for actionable feedback;
4. use a new or equivalent scenario/form for reassessment when required by the final retest policy;
5. retain the original attempt and reassessment as distinct records.

The final retest interval, attempt limits and credential consequences remain governance decisions until approved; this guide does not create them.

## Records to retain for development validation

For each scored attempt retain, according to approved privacy/retention policy when established:

- candidate/de-identified attempt identifier;
- practical and scenario/form version;
- assessor identifier;
- category scores and total;
- evidence checklist state;
- safety/identity correction events;
- accommodations and test irregularities at the minimum necessary level;
- remediation/reassessment status;
- timestamps sufficient to reconstruct administration.

These records become inputs to the calibration and validation packet; they are not themselves proof that the practical is validated.
