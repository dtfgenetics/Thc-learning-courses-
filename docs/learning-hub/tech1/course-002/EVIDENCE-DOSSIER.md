## Evidence policy

Course 2 uses authoritative or peer-reviewed sources for durable scientific and operational principles while preserving scope boundaries. Greenhouse, diagnostic, pharmaceutical-data-integrity and industrial handover sources are used only for transferable principles; they are not presented as proof that their source jurisdiction or regulated framework automatically governs every cannabis cultivation facility.

Facility SOPs, equipment instructions, product labels and jurisdiction-specific rules control where they are more specific.

## Source register

### UMass Amherst Extension — Integrated Pest Management / Greenhouse BMP

**Used in:** `LESSON-LH-TECH1-002-01`  
**Source role:** transferable scouting, representative coverage, location-aware records, denominator and trend-review principles.  
**Public URL:** https://www.umass.edu/agriculture-food-environment/greenhouse-floriculture/greenhouse-best-management-practices-bmp-manual/integrated-pest-management

**Verified scope:** the source instructs scouts to record pest numbers, location and the number of plants inspected and to review scouting records over time. It also states that there is not one universal number of plants/locations to inspect; sampling depends on crop value, potential problems, greenhouse size/type and related context.

**Course boundary:** Course 2 therefore teaches representative observation and denominator/location preservation without inventing one universal cannabis sampling route or sample size.

### Penn State Extension — Overview of Plant Diagnostics

**Used in:** `LESSON-LH-TECH1-002-03`  
**Source role:** transferable diagnostic reasoning involving plant identity, symptoms/signs, environment/care context and distribution/pattern.  
**Public URL:** https://extension.psu.edu/overview-of-plant-diagnostics

**Verified scope:** current Penn State guidance emphasizes that plant problems can have multiple possible causes and that symptoms, signs, plant/site context and damage patterns are diagnostic clues rather than automatic proof of one cause.

**Course boundary:** Technician I learners preserve direct observations, pattern and context and escalate unresolved diagnosis rather than recording an unsupported causal label as fact.

### University of Minnesota Extension — Diagnostic help for fruit and vegetable farmers

**Used in:** `LESSON-LH-TECH1-002-04`  
**Source role:** transferable diagnostic-photography and context principles.  
**Public URL:** https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/specialty-crops/diagnostic-help-for-fruit-and-vegetable-farmers

**Verified scope:** the current page recommends useful natural lighting, context plus closer images, relevant stems/roots/other structures where appropriate, comparison between healthy and affected plants, and sufficient contextual information for diagnosis. The page was reviewed in 2026.

**Course boundary:** these principles support crop-photo evidence quality. They do not make photographs alone sufficient for every diagnosis.

### Richards et al. (2026) — Hermaphroditism in Cannabis sativa L.: Impacts, Inducers, and Industry Implications

**Used in:** `LESSON-LH-TECH1-002-02` as a supplementary public review.  
**Source role:** current peer-reviewed context for Cannabis reproductive morphology and sexual-expression plasticity.  
**Public URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13258890/

**Verified bibliographic status:** published in *Plants* in 2026; open access. The review describes Cannabis as predominantly dioecious and sexual expression as plastic and influenced by interacting genetic, hormonal, developmental, treatment and environmental factors.

**Course boundary:** visible reproductive structures support a morphology observation. A visible structure by itself does not establish why mixed/unexpected expression occurred.

## Claim-control rules for Course 2

1. **Observation before causation.** A symptom, spatial pattern, timing relationship or environmental event is evidence; it is not automatically a confirmed cause.
2. **Morphology before calendar-only staging.** Developmental stage should be supported by observable structures; schedule timing remains contextual.
3. **Representative coverage before room-level statements.** A conspicuous plant does not represent an entire room by default.
4. **Preserve denominator and location.** Counts without the number inspected or location context can mislead.
5. **Photographs need context and fidelity.** Overall/representative/detail views, identity/time linkage and faithful appearance matter.
6. **Operational records remain reconstructable.** Corrections should preserve record history according to the applicable controlled process.
7. **Authority boundaries remain explicit.** Technician I documents, performs authorized checks/actions and escalates unresolved conditions rather than independently exercising higher-level diagnostic/treatment authority.
