# Evidence Dossier — COURSE-LH-TECH1-003

**Course:** Environmental, Light & Sensor Fundamentals  
**Status:** development evidence package; human technical review open  
**Evidence rule:** use reviewed sources for durable principles. Facility SOPs, equipment instructions, labels and applicable jurisdictional requirements control when more specific.

## Purpose

This dossier records why Course 3 makes each major instructional claim, what each cited source can support, and where the course deliberately stops short of universal prescriptions. It is a review aid, not a substitute for subject-matter approval.

## Controlled sources

### `REF-VPD-002`

**Source:** 2025 peer-reviewed Cannabis sativa study on elevated relative humidity and flowering/cannabinoid outcomes.  
**Repository evidence level:** A.  
**Course use:** supports the relationship among canopy temperature, relative humidity, dew point/VPD context and plant-environment interpretation under measured conditions.  
**Do not use it to claim:** one universal VPD or humidity target for every cultivar, stage, facility or production system.

Course 3 therefore teaches learners to record temperature and RH together, use VPD as contextual information, compare observations against supplied facility targets, and avoid turning an educational chart into an independent control command.

### `REF-LIGHT-001`

**Source:** 2019 peer-reviewed review, *An Update on Plant Photobiology and Implications for Cannabis Production*, Frontiers in Plant Science.  
**Repository evidence level:** A.  
**Course use:** supports core photobiology and the distinction between lighting conditions at the plant/canopy and simplified fixture settings. It supports teaching PPFD and photoperiod as measurements/conditions that require correct context.  
**Do not use it to claim:** a single PPFD value, fixture percentage or photoperiod is universally optimal for all production contexts.

Course 3 therefore emphasizes repeatable canopy-plane measurement and preservation of spatial distribution rather than prescribing an independent lighting strategy.

### `REF-MHRA-GXP-DATA-INTEGRITY`

**Source:** UK Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency, *Guidance on GxP data integrity* (2018).  
**Repository evidence level:** standards/government.  
**Course use:** provides a general quality-system model for attributable, legible/permanent, contemporaneous, original, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring and available records.  
**Important boundary:** this is **not** a cannabis regulation and is not presented as a U.S. jurisdictional legal requirement.

Course 3 uses it to support reconstructable measurement and handoff records, including separation of target values, observed measurements, alarm/event information, verification actions and unresolved conditions.

## Lesson evidence map

### Lesson 01 — Temperature, RH and VPD as Contextual Measurements

References: `REF-VPD-002`, `REF-MHRA-GXP-DATA-INTEGRITY`.

Review focus:
- temperature/RH pairing and VPD interpretation are scientifically defensible;
- wording does not turn a measured relationship into a universal target table;
- target, observation and alarm remain distinct record roles.

### Lesson 02 — PPFD, Photoperiod and Repeatable Canopy Light Checks

References: `REF-LIGHT-001`, `REF-MHRA-GXP-DATA-INTEGRITY`.

Review focus:
- PPFD terminology and canopy measurement language are accurate;
- spatial mapping is taught as measurement evidence rather than a lighting-design prescription;
- photoperiod recordkeeping preserves intended versus observed state.

### Lesson 03 — Sensor Placement, Verification and Data-Quality Triage

Reference: `REF-VPD-002`.

Review focus:
- placement, peer comparison and verification/calibration status are used as evidence checks rather than proof of a specific failure;
- operator-level verification remains separate from unauthorized repair or protected calibration changes.

### Lesson 04 — Alarms, Trends, Environmental Records and Shift Handoff

References: `REF-VPD-002`, `REF-LIGHT-001`, `REF-MHRA-GXP-DATA-INTEGRITY`.

Review focus:
- instantaneous values, averages and trends are not treated as interchangeable;
- an alarm is not presented as a diagnosis;
- unresolved conditions and uncertainty remain visible in the handoff.

## Assessment evidence rules

Every scored Course 3 question used by the controlled assessments should:

1. map to one of the five controlled objectives;
2. use evidence references appropriate to the claim being tested;
3. avoid requiring an unsupported universal set point;
4. distinguish what is observed from what is inferred;
5. keep Technician I authority at measurement, documentation, permitted verification and escalation unless the scenario explicitly supplies a broader authorized task.

The strict machine audit checks objective coverage and nonempty references. Human item review must still inspect whether each reference actually supports the tested claim and whether distractors remain technically defensible.

## Visual evidence rules

The visual registry maps references to the four embedded visuals and two practice worksheets. Visuals are instructional interpretations; they must not add numerical thresholds, legal claims or technical conclusions that are absent from the controlled lesson/source evidence.

## Known evidence limits

- The current source set is adequate for the entry-level concepts already claimed but does not justify universal environmental or lighting recipes.
- Equipment-specific calibration, repair and controller procedures must come from the applicable manufacturer/facility source.
- Jurisdiction-specific cannabis compliance claims require current authoritative legal/regulatory sources and are outside this course's general environmental-science evidence package unless explicitly added and reviewed.
- Practical A validation, assessor calibration and pass-standard evidence remain separate human/pilot work.
