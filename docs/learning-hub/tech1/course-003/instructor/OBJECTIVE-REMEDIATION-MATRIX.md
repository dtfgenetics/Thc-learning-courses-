# Objective Remediation & Reassessment Matrix — COURSE-LH-TECH1-003

**Course:** Environmental, Light & Sensor Fundamentals  
**Status:** development instructional support  
**Purpose:** provide objective-specific corrective coaching, fresh return-to-practice and equivalent reassessment while preserving the controlled competency demand.

Remediation is not a second lecture followed by the same question. It should identify the evidence failure, provide targeted practice with changed numbers/context, and then reassess the same objective using a fresh scenario or equivalent item.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-003-01`

**Objective:** Read and record routine temperature, relative humidity and VPD information with correct units, location, time and sensor context while keeping facility targets separate from observed values.

**Common failure signals**
- records RH without the associated temperature when VPD context is required;
- copies a target/set point into the observation field;
- treats VPD as a universal target rule rather than contextual information;
- omits sensor/location/time or units needed to reconstruct the reading.

**Corrective coaching**
Have the learner color-code or label a sample dashboard record into four roles: supplied target, measured temperature/RH, calculated/displayed VPD, and alarm/event. Ask them to explain which fields are observations and which are comparison/context. Re-teach that VPD depends on the measured environmental variables and that facility/crop context governs operational decisions.

**Return-to-practice**
Provide a fresh room snapshot with different temperature/RH values, a supplied target and one alarm state. Require the learner to complete the environmental measurement worksheet with units, location, time and sensor identity and to state what the data supports without changing controls.

**Reassessment**
Use a new scenario in which two spaces share the same RH but differ in temperature. The learner must correctly preserve the paired measurements, distinguish target from observation and explain why the spaces cannot be assumed equivalent from RH alone. Use a new scored item or equivalent record task; do not reuse the failed item verbatim.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-003-02`

**Objective:** Read and document photoperiod, PPFD and basic canopy light-map information using consistent measurement position and orientation without treating one point as representative of an entire canopy.

**Common failure signals**
- reports fixture dimmer percentage as canopy PPFD;
- uses only a center/brightest point as the room result;
- changes grid location, height or sensor orientation between readings without documenting it;
- reports only an average and discards spatial extremes;
- mixes intended photoperiod with observed light/controller state.

**Corrective coaching**
Rebuild the measurement method before discussing the values. Have the learner identify the grid, measurement plane/height, sensor orientation, units and time. Compare a single-point report with a nine-point map and ask what information disappears when the spatial pattern is removed.

**Return-to-practice**
Give a fresh PPFD grid with an intentionally uneven pattern and a supplied photoperiod record. Require all points, consistent method, distribution summary and separate intended-versus-observed photoperiod fields on the practice worksheet.

**Reassessment**
Use an equivalent but different canopy map. The learner must preserve every required point and method detail, identify the spatial pattern without hiding extremes and document a photoperiod discrepancy through the supplied process rather than independently redesigning lighting.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-003-03`

**Objective:** Evaluate whether an environmental reading is representative, suspect or incomplete by checking sensor placement, agreement, calibration status, logging context and crop location.

**Common failure signals**
- treats one outlier as proof that the room or sensor is wrong;
- ignores placement near an intake/outlet, wall, wet surface, fixture or atypical canopy zone;
- ignores comparison readings or verification/calibration status;
- uses an average when the trend/logging context is necessary;
- deletes or moves an outlier to make the data agree.

**Corrective coaching**
Use the sequence: **location → peer/comparison evidence → verification/calibration status → trend/logging context → crop question → classification**. Require the learner to distinguish “locally plausible,” “representative,” “suspect/incomplete,” and “requires escalation.” Emphasize that evidence may narrow possibilities without proving root cause.

**Return-to-practice**
Provide a new outlier scenario with a changed sensor location and peer pattern. The learner completes the sensor-verification section of the worksheet and classifies the reading with a short evidence-based justification.

**Reassessment**
Use a fresh scenario where the outlier is not necessarily faulty. The learner must select the evidence needed to judge representativeness and avoid both automatic acceptance and automatic rejection of the reading.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-003-04`

**Objective:** Perform permitted operator-level sensor and environmental-equipment readiness checks and escalate faults, alarms or conditions that require unauthorized repair or control redesign.

**Common failure signals**
- performs or recommends protected calibration changes, electrical repair or control redesign without authorization;
- does nothing because the learner assumes only specialists can perform any verification;
- recognizes a fault but fails to document/escalate it;
- treats alarm acknowledgement as proof the underlying condition is resolved.

**Corrective coaching**
Separate **recognize**, **verify within supplied procedure**, **document**, and **escalate**. Give examples of operator-level checks explicitly permitted by the scenario versus actions that require qualified/authorized personnel. Make clear that the course does not create repair authority.

**Return-to-practice**
Provide a new alarm/equipment-readiness scenario with a short list of permitted checks. Require the learner to select and document only those checks, identify the boundary they cannot cross, and write the escalation/handoff entry.

**Reassessment**
Use an equivalent scenario with different equipment/status evidence. The learner must distinguish permitted verification from unauthorized intervention and preserve the unresolved condition in the record.

## `LO-LH-TECH1-003-05`

**Objective:** Create reconstructable environmental and light records that distinguish target, measured value, alarm, verification action, unresolved condition and escalation status.

**Common failure signals**
- leaves out location, time, units, sensor/instrument identity or measurement method;
- writes “fixed” when only an alarm was acknowledged or a check was performed;
- merges target and measured values;
- records a guessed root cause as fact;
- handoff does not tell the receiving person what remains open or what to do next.

**Corrective coaching**
Use the reconstruction test: a person who was not present should be able to tell what was intended, what was actually observed, what event/alarm occurred, what verification was completed, what remains unknown, who was notified and what next action is required. Compare a vague record with a complete one and identify every missing evidence field.

**Return-to-practice**
Give a fresh 24-hour trend plus an outlier/alarm. Require a complete environmental/light handoff using the practice worksheet. Do not allow the learner to replace missing evidence with memory or assumptions.

**Reassessment**
Use a new integrated record scenario. The learner must produce a reconstructable handoff that keeps target, observation, alarm/event, verification, uncertainty and escalation status distinct. Equivalent scoring should focus on traceability and evidence integrity rather than matching sample wording.

## Reassessment controls

- Reassessment should use fresh data, locations, maps, readings and wording so success reflects the objective rather than item recall.
- Corrective feedback may identify the failed objective and evidence gap without exposing secure summative answer keys.
- Retain the original attempt and reassessment as distinct records when the final policy requires retention.
- Practical A reassessment, attempt limits, waiting periods and credential consequences remain governance decisions until formally approved; this matrix does not invent them.
- Academic remediation does not itself authorize Technician I credential issuance.
