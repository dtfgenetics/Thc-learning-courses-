# Learner Materials — COURSE-LH-TECH1-003

**Course:** Environmental, Light & Sensor Fundamentals  
**Audience:** Technician I learners working in supervised, self-directed, lab or approved workplace-equivalent settings  
**Status:** development learner package; not credential validation evidence

## How to use this package

Course 3 is about trustworthy measurement and handoff, not memorizing universal environmental set points. Learners should be able to show **what was measured, where, when, with what context, how the reading was checked, what remains uncertain, and what was escalated**.

Facility SOPs, approved targets/limits, product labels and equipment instructions control when they are more specific than the educational examples in this course.

## Lesson 1 — Temperature, RH and VPD as contextual measurements

### Learner goal

Record temperature and relative humidity together, use VPD as contextual information, and distinguish a supplied target from an observed measurement and an alarm/event record.

### Applied activity — environmental record reconstruction

Using a supplied dashboard snapshot and room map:

1. record temperature and RH with units;
2. record displayed or supplied VPD using the method given in the exercise;
3. identify which number is the **target** and which is the **measured value**;
4. record sensor identity/location and observation time;
5. note any alarm/event separately from the measured value;
6. state whether the reading appears representative, suspect/incomplete, or requires follow-up;
7. document only the verification action authorized by the scenario;
8. carry unresolved uncertainty into the handoff rather than inventing a cause.

## Lesson 2 — PPFD, photoperiod and repeatable canopy light checks

### Learner goal

Measure and document a canopy light pattern without substituting fixture settings, one bright point or an average for the complete spatial evidence.

### Applied activity — nine-point canopy map

Using the supplied room/map scenario:

1. follow the specified grid positions;
2. keep measurement height/plane and sensor orientation consistent;
3. record every PPFD point with units and location;
4. preserve the time and instrument/context required by the scenario;
5. summarize distribution without deleting high or low points;
6. record intended photoperiod separately from the observed light/controller state;
7. document any discrepancy and route it through the supplied escalation process rather than independently redesigning lighting.

## Lesson 3 — Sensor placement, verification and data-quality triage

### Learner goal

Decide whether a reading is representative, suspect or incomplete before treating it as a crop-wide condition.

### Sensor-outlier practice

For a supplied outlier:

- identify the sensor and its physical location;
- compare nearby/peer readings;
- review supplied verification/calibration status;
- inspect the relevant trend/logging context;
- identify obvious airflow, equipment, wall, wet-surface, light or canopy-location effects supplied by the scenario;
- perform only the operator-level readiness check allowed by the procedure;
- record what the evidence supports and what it does **not** establish;
- escalate unresolved faults or work outside the role boundary.

## Lesson 4 — Alarms, trends, records and shift handoff

### Learner goal

Create a reconstructable handoff that separates measured evidence, supplied limits/targets, alarms, verification actions, unresolved conditions and next responsibilities.

### Applied activity — trend and alarm handoff

Given a simulated 24-hour trend with a repeated event, one outlier and one alarm:

1. identify what the current value, average and time trend each show;
2. record the alarm identity/time without treating the alarm as a root-cause diagnosis;
3. preserve the relevant measured values and location/sensor context;
4. document the permitted verification step actually completed;
5. state what remains unresolved;
6. identify who was notified under the scenario process;
7. write a receiving-shift next action that another technician can execute.

## Integrated Course 3 scenario

A flowering room's dashboard shows a short environmental alarm near lights-on. The 24-hour average remains within the supplied operating range. One wall sensor differs from the canopy sensors, and a nine-point PPFD map has a brighter center than edges.

Produce a development portfolio record that:

- keeps supplied targets/limits separate from observed values;
- records temperature/RH/VPD with sensor, location, units and time context;
- explains why the average does not erase the repeated trend event;
- evaluates the outlier using placement, peer agreement and verification context without declaring an unsupported root cause;
- preserves every PPFD grid point and the measurement method;
- records intended versus observed photoperiod information supplied by the scenario;
- documents only permitted operator-level checks;
- states unresolved conditions and escalation status;
- ends with a clear shift handoff.

This scenario intentionally combines all five objectives. It does not authorize independent environmental or lighting control redesign.

## Self-check before assessment

A strong learner record should allow another qualified person to answer:

- What value was actually measured?
- What target or limit was supplied for comparison?
- Where and when was the measurement taken?
- Which sensor/instrument and measurement method were used?
- Is the value representative of the question being asked?
- What verification was actually performed?
- What is still uncertain?
- What was escalated, to whom, and what happens next?

If the record cannot answer those questions, return to the relevant practice task before reassessment.

## Assessment boundary

Lesson practice and the 12-item module assessment support learning. The 20-item course final is the controlled summative academic assessment source. Practical A is a separate development performance assessment. Passing the academic course alone must not be represented as Technician I credential issuance or as proof that Practical A has been validated.
