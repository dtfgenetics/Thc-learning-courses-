## Learner-use model

Each outcome uses the same evidence-centered loop:

1. **Orient:** identify the exact question, material/system, time window and controlled instruction.
2. **Observe/measure:** separate direct evidence from interpretation.
3. **Compare:** use an appropriate baseline, cohort, expected state, or controlled target.
4. **Reason:** rank plausible explanations or responses and identify uncertainty.
5. **Act within authority:** complete only assigned/authorized actions; hold or escalate the rest.
6. **Document:** leave a reconstructable record with identifiers, evidence, timing, action and unresolved state.
7. **Reassess:** demonstrate the same outcome in a changed but equivalent context.

## Integrated practice

Use one multi-stage scenario that requires at least three outcomes to interact. Introduce a mid-scenario change that invalidates or weakens an earlier assumption. The learner must update the plan without deleting the earlier evidence, explain what changed, and hand off any unresolved condition.

Minimum evidence outputs:
- problem/priority statement;
- source identifiers and relevant controlled instruction;
- observation/measurement table;
- comparison or baseline;
- ranked reasoning or response options;
- authority decision;
- correction/verification result if authorized;
- unresolved-work handoff.

## Visual production specification

Produce at least **4 primary instructional visuals** for this course, one aligned to each current outcome. Additional visuals are allowed and encouraged when they reduce cognitive load.

Every visual must:
- use the exact current course/lesson terminology;
- avoid invented universal SOPs, legal requirements, product settings or maintenance authority;
- remain readable on a phone without requiring tiny poster text;
- have a learner-facing caption and text alternative outside the image;
- avoid color-only meaning;
- identify illustrative examples as illustrative;
- preserve the distinction between academic training and professional credential issuance.
