# THC-GROW-030 — Genetics and crop planning
**Version:** 0.1.0 draft  
**Status:** Controlled draft — review required  
**Release:** Blocked

## Purpose

Define genetics intake, crop scheduling, capacity and identity controls while preserving genotype-by-environment uncertainty and lot genealogy.

## Scope and boundaries

**Included:** genetic identity; source records; crop schedule; plant-count/capacity planning; sex-expression risk; planned-versus-actual records; lot genealogy.

**Not authorized or not universalized in this draft:** universal cultivar flowering duration; guarantee that cultivar name predicts phenotype; silent renaming of genetics; plant-count decisions that ignore facility/legal capacity; assumption that one environment expresses every genotype the same way.

## Scientific and operational control principles

### 1. Cannabis phenotype and production performance emerge from genotype interacting with environment and management, so crop plans should preserve genetic identity without assuming a cultivar name guarantees one fixed outcome.
**Evidence boundary:** Genetic naming alone does not establish authenticity, stability, flowering duration, yield or chemistry.
**Sources:** `REF-GENETICS-001`, `REF-GENETICS-2026-001`

### 2. Seed, clone and stock-source records should preserve supplier/source, cultivar or line identity, lot/batch, date received and propagation relationship so later performance can be traced to the correct genetics.
**Evidence boundary:** A label is only useful when identity controls prevent mix-ups and undocumented relabeling.
**Sources:** `REF-GENETICS-001`, `REF-POSTHARVEST-001`

### 3. Crop scheduling should incorporate biological readiness and observed development rather than treating planned calendar dates as proof that a crop is ready to move stages.
**Evidence boundary:** The SOP should not universalize one vegetative or flowering duration across genotypes and environments.
**Sources:** `REF-PLANT-BIO-001`, `REF-FLOWER-MORPH-2023-001`

### 4. Unexpected sex expression can occur and may have genetic, developmental and environmental contributors, so flowering plans should include reproductive scouting and an escalation pathway.
**Evidence boundary:** No single stressor or cultivar label should be presented as a universally predictive cause of intersex expression.
**Sources:** `REF-SEX-2026-001`, `REF-PLANT-BIO-001`

### 5. Planned plant counts, room occupancy and turnover should be reconciled against actual survival, readiness, spacing and facility capacity so schedules do not hide biological or operational variance.
**Evidence boundary:** Facility/legal plant-count limits and occupancy requirements remain locally controlled.
**Sources:** `REF-POSTHARVEST-001`

### 6. Crop records should preserve planned versus actual dates, room/zone, genotype, batch genealogy, losses, substitutions and deviations so later review can distinguish genetic, timing and process effects.
**Evidence boundary:** Retrospective schedule changes must not erase the original plan or make the crop appear to have followed it exactly.
**Sources:** `REF-POSTHARVEST-001`, `REF-GENETICS-2026-001`

## Records and reconstructability

For every controlled event, preserve identity, date/time, location, responsible person or system, planned/required condition, observed/measured condition, action taken, unresolved condition, deviation/escalation reference and approval/release state where applicable.

## Deviations

Do not silently correct history or improvise outside assigned authority. Contain the immediate risk where permitted, preserve evidence, identify affected scope, notify the responsible role, and route the event through the applicable deviation/CAPA or technical-review process.

## Training boundary

Course completion supports education but does not by itself create facility authorization, trade licensure, pesticide authority, engineering authority, laboratory qualification or jurisdiction-specific certification.
