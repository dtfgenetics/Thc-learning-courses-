# THC-GROW-030 Learner Guide — Genetics and crop planning
**Draft — not operationally released**

## What this topic controls

Define genetics intake, crop scheduling, capacity and identity controls while preserving genotype-by-environment uncertainty and lot genealogy.

## Core principles

1. Cannabis phenotype and production performance emerge from genotype interacting with environment and management, so crop plans should preserve genetic identity without assuming a cultivar name guarantees one fixed outcome.
   - **Limit:** Genetic naming alone does not establish authenticity, stability, flowering duration, yield or chemistry.
2. Seed, clone and stock-source records should preserve supplier/source, cultivar or line identity, lot/batch, date received and propagation relationship so later performance can be traced to the correct genetics.
   - **Limit:** A label is only useful when identity controls prevent mix-ups and undocumented relabeling.
3. Crop scheduling should incorporate biological readiness and observed development rather than treating planned calendar dates as proof that a crop is ready to move stages.
   - **Limit:** The SOP should not universalize one vegetative or flowering duration across genotypes and environments.
4. Unexpected sex expression can occur and may have genetic, developmental and environmental contributors, so flowering plans should include reproductive scouting and an escalation pathway.
   - **Limit:** No single stressor or cultivar label should be presented as a universally predictive cause of intersex expression.
5. Planned plant counts, room occupancy and turnover should be reconciled against actual survival, readiness, spacing and facility capacity so schedules do not hide biological or operational variance.
   - **Limit:** Facility/legal plant-count limits and occupancy requirements remain locally controlled.
6. Crop records should preserve planned versus actual dates, room/zone, genotype, batch genealogy, losses, substitutions and deviations so later review can distinguish genetic, timing and process effects.
   - **Limit:** Retrospective schedule changes must not erase the original plan or make the crop appear to have followed it exactly.

## Before acting

Verify: **identity → current status → applicable procedure/plan → measurement or observation quality → your authority → required record → escalation trigger**.

## What not to assume

- universal cultivar flowering duration
- guarantee that cultivar name predicts phenotype
- silent renaming of genetics
- plant-count decisions that ignore facility/legal capacity
- assumption that one environment expresses every genotype the same way

## Evidence habit

Record what you observed or measured before writing the conclusion. Preserve uncertainty and the reason for any decision, especially when the final answer depends on cultivar, stage, facility, method or jurisdiction.
