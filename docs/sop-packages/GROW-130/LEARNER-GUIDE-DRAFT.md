# THC-GROW-130 Learner Guide — Harvest readiness and harvest workflow
**Draft — not operationally released**

## Goal

Define an evidence-led cannabis harvest workflow that separates maturity assessment from sanitation, lot identity, authorization, segregation and transfer into postharvest control.

## What the evidence supports

1. Cannabinoid concentration and other inflorescence characteristics change during flowering and harvest timing can materially affect the chemical profile and yield objective.
   - **Boundary:** The timing of peak concentration varies by genotype and production context, so one calendar week cannot be used as a universal harvest rule.
2. Stigma color transition can provide a useful maturity approximation for many cannabis genotypes, but some genotypes reach cannabinoid peaks at earlier color stages.
   - **Boundary:** A mostly-amber rule of thumb is not a substitute for genotype-specific validation or the defined product objective.
3. Glandular trichome development and senescence are influenced by plant age and genotype, and different trichomes on the same inflorescence can be at different developmental stages.
   - **Boundary:** A single trichome image or color count cannot prove whole-lot maturity or exact cannabinoid concentration.
4. Trichome browning and structural senescence can accompany maturation, while stress and postharvest handling can also affect trichome appearance, so visual observations require crop and handling context.
   - **Boundary:** Visual color alone should not be treated as a direct laboratory measurement of cannabinoid content.
5. Representative maturity sampling should preserve plant/zone position, date, genotype, crop stage, sampled tissue and observation method so repeated checks and harvest decisions remain comparable.
   - **Boundary:** Convenience sampling from the most visually mature top flower can overstate lot readiness.
6. Harvest execution should preserve lot identity, tool/equipment sanitation status, affected crop scope, cut/trim location and transfer history so later drying, testing and deviations can be traced back to the source material.
   - **Boundary:** A mature crop is not automatically releasable if identity, sanitation, contamination or quality status is unresolved.
7. Material from different rooms, zones, genotypes or materially different process histories should not be combined in a way that destroys the ability to reconstruct their source and exposure history.
   - **Boundary:** Facility lot-definition rules can differ, but traceability should remain sufficient for investigation and disposition.
8. The harvest-to-drying handoff should communicate exact lot identity, harvest timing, observed maturity evidence, deviations/holds, sanitary status and required next processing step.
   - **Boundary:** Handoff documentation does not clear an existing contamination, testing or quality hold.

## What you must verify before acting

Identity • stage/process • current procedure/plan • measurement quality • authority • required record • escalation trigger.

## Do not assume

- one universal number of flowering weeks
- one universal stigma-color percentage
- one universal trichome-color ratio
- harvest decision from one convenience flower
- mixing lots with uncertain identity
- release of suspect or contaminated material because maturity appears acceptable

## Strong work product

A strong record allows another qualified person to reconstruct what was planned, what actually happened, what evidence was used, what remained uncertain, what action was authorized, and what needs follow-up.
