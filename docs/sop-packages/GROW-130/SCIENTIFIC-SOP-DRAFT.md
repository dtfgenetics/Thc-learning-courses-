# THC-GROW-130 — Harvest readiness and harvest workflow
**Version:** 0.1.0 draft  
**Status:** Controlled draft — review required  
**Release:** Blocked

## Purpose

Define an evidence-led cannabis harvest workflow that separates maturity assessment from sanitation, lot identity, authorization, segregation and transfer into postharvest control.

## Scope

**Included:** maturity assessment; genotype/crop-stage context; stigma and trichome observations; sampling and confirmation; harvest authorization; sanitary cut/handling workflow; lot/batch segregation; tool/equipment status; chain of custody; handoff to drying/postharvest.

**Not universalized or not authorized by this draft:** one universal number of flowering weeks; one universal stigma-color percentage; one universal trichome-color ratio; harvest decision from one convenience flower; mixing lots with uncertain identity; release of suspect or contaminated material because maturity appears acceptable.

## Scientific and operational control principles

### 1. Cannabinoid concentration and other inflorescence characteristics change during flowering and harvest timing can materially affect the chemical profile and yield objective.
**Evidence boundary:** The timing of peak concentration varies by genotype and production context, so one calendar week cannot be used as a universal harvest rule.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-HARVEST-STIGMA-2025-001`

### 2. Stigma color transition can provide a useful maturity approximation for many cannabis genotypes, but some genotypes reach cannabinoid peaks at earlier color stages.
**Evidence boundary:** A mostly-amber rule of thumb is not a substitute for genotype-specific validation or the defined product objective.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-HARVEST-STIGMA-2025-001`

### 3. Glandular trichome development and senescence are influenced by plant age and genotype, and different trichomes on the same inflorescence can be at different developmental stages.
**Evidence boundary:** A single trichome image or color count cannot prove whole-lot maturity or exact cannabinoid concentration.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-TRICHOME-MATURATION-2023-001`

### 4. Trichome browning and structural senescence can accompany maturation, while stress and postharvest handling can also affect trichome appearance, so visual observations require crop and handling context.
**Evidence boundary:** Visual color alone should not be treated as a direct laboratory measurement of cannabinoid content.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-TRICHOME-MATURATION-2023-001`

### 5. Representative maturity sampling should preserve plant/zone position, date, genotype, crop stage, sampled tissue and observation method so repeated checks and harvest decisions remain comparable.
**Evidence boundary:** Convenience sampling from the most visually mature top flower can overstate lot readiness.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-HARVEST-STIGMA-2025-001`, `REF-POSTHARVEST-001`

### 6. Harvest execution should preserve lot identity, tool/equipment sanitation status, affected crop scope, cut/trim location and transfer history so later drying, testing and deviations can be traced back to the source material.
**Evidence boundary:** A mature crop is not automatically releasable if identity, sanitation, contamination or quality status is unresolved.
**Sources:** `REF-POSTHARVEST-001`

### 7. Material from different rooms, zones, genotypes or materially different process histories should not be combined in a way that destroys the ability to reconstruct their source and exposure history.
**Evidence boundary:** Facility lot-definition rules can differ, but traceability should remain sufficient for investigation and disposition.
**Sources:** `REF-POSTHARVEST-001`

### 8. The harvest-to-drying handoff should communicate exact lot identity, harvest timing, observed maturity evidence, deviations/holds, sanitary status and required next processing step.
**Evidence boundary:** Handoff documentation does not clear an existing contamination, testing or quality hold.
**Sources:** `REF-POSTHARVEST-001`

## Measurement, observation and decision quality

Distinguish planned/target values from actual observations and measurements. Preserve method/instrument context, stage, cultivar/lot, location, timing and relevant environmental/root-zone/process context. Do not turn one study treatment, visual cue or facility example into a universal cannabis specification.

## Records

The applicable form must preserve identity, date/time, location, stage/process, source procedure/version, required condition, actual condition, method/instrument when relevant, action, affected scope, deviation/escalation and release/approval state.

## Deviations

Contain immediate risk within authority, preserve the original evidence, define affected scope, notify the responsible role, and document follow-up. Never backfill a record to make execution appear compliant.

## Release gates

- claim-level flowering/maturity review
- facility-specific maturity validation
- sanitation/food-product handling review as applicable
- lot/traceability review
- accessibility review
- independent release approval
