# THC-GROW-040 — Growing media selection and preparation
**Version:** 0.1.0 draft  
**Status:** Controlled draft — review required  
**Release:** Blocked

## Purpose

Define an evidence-led growing-media SOP foundation that treats substrate physical/chemical properties, container geometry, irrigation behavior and crop response as an interacting root-zone system.

## Scope and boundaries

**Included:** media identity and batch traceability; particle/structure and water-air balance; water-holding and drainage behavior; container geometry effects; pH/EC and chemical buffering context; peat/coir/soilless material differences; media-irrigation compatibility; root development and crop-response verification.

**Not authorized or not universalized in this draft:** one universal peat/coco ratio; one universal air-porosity or water-holding target for every container and crop stage; one irrigation schedule applied unchanged across different media; assumption that equal feed EC produces equal root-zone EC across substrates; claim that one medium is universally superior for all genotypes and systems.

## Scientific and operational control principles

### 1. Growing-media physical properties determine how water and air are stored and released around roots, so media selection and irrigation strategy should be evaluated together rather than as separate decisions.
**Evidence boundary:** Reported porosity or water-holding ranges are not universal cannabis release specifications because material source, particle distribution, compaction, container geometry and measurement method matter.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-SOILLESS-MEDIA-2025-001`, `REF-IRRIGATION-001`

### 2. Cannabis growth, root development and yield can differ among peat-based, fibre-amended and coir substrates under otherwise defined conditions, and genotype can modify the response.
**Evidence boundary:** The tested two-genotype pot experiment does not establish a universal ranking of media for other cultivars, facilities or irrigation programs.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-SUBSTRATE-2020-001`

### 3. Peat, coir and other soilless materials differ in physical structure, water retention, aeration, chemical buffering and nutrient behavior, so material identity and supplier/batch characteristics should be preserved in records.
**Evidence boundary:** Generic material names such as 'coco' or 'peat mix' do not prove the exact physical or chemical properties of a delivered batch.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-SOILLESS-MEDIA-2025-001`

### 4. Container height and geometry interact with media pore structure to influence vertical water distribution, drainage and air-filled space, making container configuration part of root-zone interpretation.
**Evidence boundary:** Container effects should be verified in the actual media/container combination rather than inferred only from nominal container volume.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-SOILLESS-MEDIA-2025-001`, `REF-IRRIGATION-001`

### 5. Equal irrigation input or feed EC does not guarantee equal root-zone moisture or solute concentration across media because water retention, drainage, evapotranspiration and ion-buffering behavior can differ.
**Evidence boundary:** A media change should not be compensated automatically by changing feed concentration before delivery, drainage and root-zone trends are measured.
**Sources:** `REF-ROOTZONE-NUTRIENT-002`, `REF-CANNABIS-SOILLESS-MEDIA-2025-001`

### 6. Media pH, EC and exchange/buffering characteristics affect nutrient availability and interpretation, so acceptance and troubleshooting should preserve both physical and chemical evidence.
**Evidence boundary:** A single pH or EC measurement does not describe all nutrient availability or identify the cause of poor root performance.
**Sources:** `REF-SOIL-CEC-001`, `REF-ROOTZONE-NUTRIENT-002`, `REF-CANNABIS-SOILLESS-MEDIA-2025-001`

### 7. Media preparation and amendment should be controlled by exact material identity, batch, measured condition and intended production system so repeated crops can distinguish material variability from crop or irrigation effects.
**Evidence boundary:** A recipe copied by volume without verifying material condition, moisture state and source can change effective physical properties between batches.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-SOILLESS-MEDIA-2025-001`, `REF-CANNABIS-SUBSTRATE-2020-001`

### 8. A media change should be verified after planting through root development, irrigation/drainage behavior, moisture dynamics, root-zone chemistry and crop response before the change is considered successful.
**Evidence boundary:** Acceptable appearance during early growth does not prove later-stage irrigation, nutrition or yield performance.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-SUBSTRATE-2020-001`, `REF-ROOTZONE-NUTRIENT-002`

## Records and reconstructability

For every controlled event, preserve identity, date/time, location, responsible person or system, planned/required condition, observed/measured condition, action taken, unresolved condition, deviation/escalation reference and approval/release state where applicable.

## Deviations

Do not silently correct history or improvise outside assigned authority. Contain the immediate risk where permitted, preserve evidence, identify affected scope, notify the responsible role, and route the event through the applicable deviation/CAPA or technical-review process.

## Training boundary

Course completion supports education but does not by itself create facility authorization, trade licensure, pesticide authority, engineering authority, laboratory qualification or jurisdiction-specific certification.
