# THC-GROW-040 Learner Guide — Growing media selection and preparation
**Draft — not operationally released**

## What this topic controls

Define an evidence-led growing-media SOP foundation that treats substrate physical/chemical properties, container geometry, irrigation behavior and crop response as an interacting root-zone system.

## Core principles

1. Growing-media physical properties determine how water and air are stored and released around roots, so media selection and irrigation strategy should be evaluated together rather than as separate decisions.
   - **Limit:** Reported porosity or water-holding ranges are not universal cannabis release specifications because material source, particle distribution, compaction, container geometry and measurement method matter.
2. Cannabis growth, root development and yield can differ among peat-based, fibre-amended and coir substrates under otherwise defined conditions, and genotype can modify the response.
   - **Limit:** The tested two-genotype pot experiment does not establish a universal ranking of media for other cultivars, facilities or irrigation programs.
3. Peat, coir and other soilless materials differ in physical structure, water retention, aeration, chemical buffering and nutrient behavior, so material identity and supplier/batch characteristics should be preserved in records.
   - **Limit:** Generic material names such as 'coco' or 'peat mix' do not prove the exact physical or chemical properties of a delivered batch.
4. Container height and geometry interact with media pore structure to influence vertical water distribution, drainage and air-filled space, making container configuration part of root-zone interpretation.
   - **Limit:** Container effects should be verified in the actual media/container combination rather than inferred only from nominal container volume.
5. Equal irrigation input or feed EC does not guarantee equal root-zone moisture or solute concentration across media because water retention, drainage, evapotranspiration and ion-buffering behavior can differ.
   - **Limit:** A media change should not be compensated automatically by changing feed concentration before delivery, drainage and root-zone trends are measured.
6. Media pH, EC and exchange/buffering characteristics affect nutrient availability and interpretation, so acceptance and troubleshooting should preserve both physical and chemical evidence.
   - **Limit:** A single pH or EC measurement does not describe all nutrient availability or identify the cause of poor root performance.
7. Media preparation and amendment should be controlled by exact material identity, batch, measured condition and intended production system so repeated crops can distinguish material variability from crop or irrigation effects.
   - **Limit:** A recipe copied by volume without verifying material condition, moisture state and source can change effective physical properties between batches.
8. A media change should be verified after planting through root development, irrigation/drainage behavior, moisture dynamics, root-zone chemistry and crop response before the change is considered successful.
   - **Limit:** Acceptable appearance during early growth does not prove later-stage irrigation, nutrition or yield performance.

## Before acting

Verify: **identity → current status → applicable procedure/plan → measurement or observation quality → your authority → required record → escalation trigger**.

## What not to assume

- one universal peat/coco ratio
- one universal air-porosity or water-holding target for every container and crop stage
- one irrigation schedule applied unchanged across different media
- assumption that equal feed EC produces equal root-zone EC across substrates
- claim that one medium is universally superior for all genotypes and systems

## Evidence habit

Record what you observed or measured before writing the conclusion. Preserve uncertainty and the reason for any decision, especially when the final answer depends on cultivar, stage, facility, method or jurisdiction.
