# THC-GROW-011 — Grow space setup
**Version:** 0.1.0 draft  
**Status:** Controlled draft — review required  
**Release:** Blocked

## Purpose

Define a grow-space commissioning and readiness workflow that verifies safe access, utilities, drainage, environmental measurement, cleaning access and equipment status without turning cultivation training into engineering or licensed-trade authority.

## Scope and boundaries

**Included:** space zoning; safe walking/working surfaces; water and drainage readiness; powered-equipment condition; operator-versus-servicing boundary; environmental sensor placement/representativeness; air movement and environmental verification; cleaning/sanitation access; alarm/emergency readiness; commissioning checklist.

**Not authorized or not universalized in this draft:** electrical system design or modification; HVAC engineering calculations; bypassing safeguards or alarms; covered servicing without hazardous-energy authorization; universal environmental setpoints; acceptance of a room solely because controller setpoints look correct.

## Scientific and operational control principles

### 1. Cannabis cultivation work can involve interacting chemical, biological, ergonomic, electrical, equipment and physical hazards, so grow-space setup should begin with a site-specific hazard and workflow assessment rather than only horticultural targets.
**Evidence boundary:** A generic course hazard list cannot replace the employer's actual hazard assessment, emergency plan or qualified engineering review.
**Sources:** `REF-NIOSH-CANNABIS-HAZARDS-2024`

### 2. Walking and working surfaces should be maintained in a clean, orderly and sufficiently dry condition, making drainage, hose/cable routing and spill control part of cultivation-space readiness.
**Evidence boundary:** This requirement does not authorize unqualified repair of plumbing, electrical or structural defects discovered during inspection.
**Sources:** `REF-OSHA-1910-22-WALKING`

### 3. Damaged powered equipment, defeated safeguards or conditions requiring covered servicing should be removed from normal operation and escalated through the authorized energy-control/maintenance process.
**Evidence boundary:** Taking the cultivation course does not qualify a learner as an authorized electrical, mechanical or lockout/tagout servicing employee.
**Sources:** `REF-OSHA-1910-147-LOTO`, `REF-NIOSH-CANNABIS-HAZARDS-2024`

### 4. Environmental measurements are only useful for commissioning when sensor location and exposure are representative of the crop question being asked; one controller sensor can miss canopy-level spatial gradients.
**Evidence boundary:** Measured treatment values in research should not be copied into universal room setpoints for all cultivars and stages.
**Sources:** `REF-VPD-002`, `REF-ENV-TEMP-2025-001`

### 5. Temperature and humidity conditions can materially affect cannabis growth, flowering development and metabolite outcomes, supporting verification of actual crop-zone conditions before production begins.
**Evidence boundary:** Commissioning verifies the space against the approved facility crop plan; it does not define a universal cannabis climate recipe.
**Sources:** `REF-VPD-002`, `REF-ENV-TEMP-2025-001`

### 6. Space layout should support separation of clean and dirty workflows, routine inspection, sanitation access and safe movement of people, plants, tools and materials.
**Evidence boundary:** Exact aisle widths, equipment spacing and room geometry must follow applicable code, equipment instructions and facility design requirements.
**Sources:** `REF-NIOSH-CANNABIS-HAZARDS-2024`

### 7. A controller command or displayed status is not the same as verified physical delivery, so commissioning should compare intended operation with observable outputs such as airflow, irrigation/drainage, alarms and representative environmental measurements.
**Evidence boundary:** The cultivation SOP should define verification and escalation, not unauthorized technical adjustment of engineered systems.
**Sources:** `REF-VPD-002`, `REF-NIOSH-CANNABIS-HAZARDS-2024`

### 8. Commissioning records should identify the room/zone, equipment or system checked, date/time, required criterion, observed result, unresolved defect, containment and responsible handoff before the area is released for normal use.
**Evidence boundary:** A checklist completion mark does not override an unresolved safety, engineering, sanitation or legal defect.
**Sources:** `REF-NIOSH-CANNABIS-HAZARDS-2024`

## Records and reconstructability

For every controlled event, preserve identity, date/time, location, responsible person or system, planned/required condition, observed/measured condition, action taken, unresolved condition, deviation/escalation reference and approval/release state where applicable.

## Deviations

Do not silently correct history or improvise outside assigned authority. Contain the immediate risk where permitted, preserve evidence, identify affected scope, notify the responsible role, and route the event through the applicable deviation/CAPA or technical-review process.

## Training boundary

Course completion supports education but does not by itself create facility authorization, trade licensure, pesticide authority, engineering authority, laboratory qualification or jurisdiction-specific certification.

## Release gates

- worker-safety review
- electrical/HVAC/plumbing engineering boundary review
- facility-layout/code applicability review
- environmental measurement review
- accessibility review
- independent release approval
