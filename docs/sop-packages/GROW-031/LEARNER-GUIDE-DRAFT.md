# THC-GROW-031 Learner Guide — Seed intake, labeling, and storage
**Draft — not operationally released**

## What this topic controls

Define seed-lot identity, intake, storage, inventory and viability-record controls without claiming one universal storage condition or shelf life.

## Core principles

1. Seed intake should preserve the lot's source and genetic identity before repackaging or sampling so viability and later crop performance remain attributable to the correct material.
   - **Limit:** Matching cultivar names do not justify combining distinct lots or suppliers.
2. Cannabis propagation begins from biologically variable seed material, so germination/viability records should be tied to a defined seed lot, sample size, method and date.
   - **Limit:** A small undocumented sample cannot establish the viability of every remaining seed in a heterogeneous lot.
3. Storage records should preserve container, location, date, relevant environmental condition and access/movement history so changes in viability can be investigated.
   - **Limit:** The course should not invent a universal cannabis seed shelf-life claim unsupported by lot-specific evidence.
4. Seed labels should use stable lot identifiers rather than relying only on informal strain names, allowing splits, samples, germination tests and plant cohorts to be traced back to the source lot.
   - **Limit:** Informal naming conventions must not overwrite controlled lot identity.
5. Inventory adjustments, losses, samples and disposition should be recorded contemporaneously enough that the physical seed count and controlled record can be reconciled.
   - **Limit:** Exact inventory/legal reporting requirements remain jurisdiction- and facility-specific.

## Before acting

Verify: **identity → current status → applicable procedure/plan → measurement or observation quality → your authority → required record → escalation trigger**.

## What not to assume

- universal cannabis seed shelf life
- one storage temperature/RH for every seed lot and container
- discarding source identity after repackaging
- mixing seed lots because cultivar names match
- viability conclusions from undocumented convenience samples

## Evidence habit

Record what you observed or measured before writing the conclusion. Preserve uncertainty and the reason for any decision, especially when the final answer depends on cultivar, stage, facility, method or jurisdiction.
