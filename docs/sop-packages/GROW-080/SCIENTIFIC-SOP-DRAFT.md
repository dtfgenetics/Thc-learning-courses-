# THC-GROW-080 — Mineral nutrition and deficiency diagnosis
**Version:** 0.1.0 draft  
**Status:** Controlled draft — review required  
**Release:** Blocked

## Purpose

Define an evidence-led cannabis nutrition and deficiency-diagnosis SOP foundation without converting bounded nutrient-response studies into universal feed recipes.

## Scope

**Included:** macronutrient and micronutrient roles; deficiency symptom-pattern observation; upper-versus-lower canopy distribution; root-zone and irrigation context; tissue and solution/media testing escalation; nutrient interaction and antagonism; stage/system/genotype dependence; corrective-action verification.

**Not universalized or not authorized by this draft:** universal cannabis NPK recipe; universal EC or ppm feeding chart; diagnosis from leaf color alone; automatic correction from a single tissue or solution result; direct transfer of one hydroponic study's optimum concentrations to other cultivars, stages or media.

## Scientific and operational control principles

### 1. Single-element deficiencies in cannabis can produce recognizable foliar and growth patterns, but symptom onset and tissue nutrient concentration do not always correspond cleanly, so diagnosis should integrate multiple evidence types.
**Evidence boundary:** Controlled single-element omission symptoms do not prove that a visually similar facility problem has the same cause.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-NUTRIENT-DEFICIENCY-2023-001`

### 2. Cannabis nutrient deficiencies can materially reduce vegetative growth and inflorescence yield, making timely detection and evidence-based correction operationally important.
**Evidence boundary:** Magnitude of loss varies by element, severity, duration, genotype, stage and production system.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-NUTRIENT-DEFICIENCY-2023-001`

### 3. Nutrient diagnosis should separate direct observations from hypotheses and review root-zone pH/EC, irrigation/water status, source-water chemistry, formulation history, crop stage and root condition before concluding that a symptom is a primary nutrient deficiency.
**Evidence boundary:** Visual symptomology alone cannot distinguish every deficiency, toxicity, uptake limitation, root disorder or environmental look-alike.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-NUTRIENT-DEFICIENCY-2023-001`

### 4. Cannabis N, P and K responses are interactive rather than independent, and vegetative-stage growth responses can change as combinations of supplied nutrients change.
**Evidence boundary:** Response-surface optima are experimental outcomes for defined conditions, not universal nutrient-solution specifications.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-VEG-NPK-2024-001`

### 5. Flowering-stage cannabis yield responses to nitrogen and phosphorus can be nonlinear, demonstrating that more fertilizer does not automatically produce more yield or quality.
**Evidence boundary:** The tested deep-water-culture ranges and cultivar do not establish general flowering feed targets for other systems.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-FLOWER-NPK-2021-001`

### 6. Vegetative and flowering cannabis studies support treating crop stage as a controlled context for nutrient interpretation rather than carrying one nutrient target unchanged through the entire lifecycle.
**Evidence boundary:** Stage-specific evidence remains genotype- and system-dependent and should be validated before facility adoption.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-VEG-NPK-2024-001`, `REF-CANNABIS-FLOWER-NPK-2021-001`

### 7. Tissue analysis is most useful when sample location, crop stage, symptom status, method and corresponding root-zone or solution context are preserved so results can be compared meaningfully.
**Evidence boundary:** A laboratory concentration without sampling context does not automatically identify root cause or authorize a recipe change.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-NUTRIENT-DEFICIENCY-2023-001`

### 8. Corrective nutrition actions should be verified with follow-up plant observations and compatible root-zone, solution or tissue measurements rather than closed solely because a feed adjustment was made.
**Evidence boundary:** Process correction does not prove recovery until crop and measurement evidence support it.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABIS-NUTRIENT-DEFICIENCY-2023-001`, `REF-CANNABIS-VEG-NPK-2024-001`

## Measurement, observation and decision quality

Distinguish planned/target values from actual observations and measurements. Preserve method/instrument context, stage, cultivar/lot, location, timing and relevant environmental/root-zone/process context. Do not turn one study treatment, visual cue or facility example into a universal cannabis specification.

## Records

The applicable form must preserve identity, date/time, location, stage/process, source procedure/version, required condition, actual condition, method/instrument when relevant, action, affected scope, deviation/escalation and release/approval state.

## Deviations

Contain immediate risk within authority, preserve the original evidence, define affected scope, notify the responsible role, and document follow-up. Never backfill a record to make execution appear compliant.
