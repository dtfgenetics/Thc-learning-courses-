# THC-GROW-140 Learner Guide — Drying, curing, storage, and water activity
**Draft — not operationally released**

## Goal

Define the source-supported claims and evidence boundaries required before authoring the full controlled GROW-140 postharvest SOP package.

## What the evidence supports

1. Drying rate and final product condition depend on interacting process variables including temperature, humidity, airflow, loading, flower geometry and time; room averages alone do not prove uniform product condition.
   - **Boundary:** A room setpoint is a process input, not direct evidence that every location or flower in a lot reached the same condition.
2. Moisture content and water activity describe different properties of dried plant material and should not be treated as interchangeable indicators.
   - **Boundary:** A measured moisture percentage cannot by itself establish microbial or chemical stability, and a water-activity result must be interpreted with method, temperature, sampling and applicable specification.
3. Cannabis/hemp drying methods can alter drying time, decarboxylation, color, microbial load, cannabinoid form and volatile retention, creating quality and safety tradeoffs rather than one universally superior method.
   - **Boundary:** Results from a specific cultivar, apparatus or drying treatment must not be converted directly into a universal cannabis recipe.
4. Curing and sealed storage can change product moisture condition after drying, so sealed packaging does not eliminate the need for representative product measurements and stability checks.
   - **Boundary:** Curing is not a validated remediation method for unsafe microbial contamination or materially nonconforming product.
5. Storage stability is influenced by time, temperature, oxygen, light, humidity/moisture transfer, product matrix and packaging properties, and these factors can affect cannabinoids and volatile compounds differently.
   - **Boundary:** Preserving one compound or using one package technology does not prove preservation of total product quality.
6. Cannabinoid degradation in dried cannabis is time- and temperature-dependent under studied conditions, supporting the need to justify storage specifications and shelf-life with measured stability evidence.
   - **Boundary:** Kinetic findings from a study are not a finished-product expiry date and cannot replace product-specific stability validation.
7. Representative sampling and lot traceability are necessary to investigate within-load variability, drying-room deviations and later storage failures without overgeneralizing from a convenience sample.
   - **Boundary:** A single favorable sample does not demonstrate uniformity across a heterogeneous lot.
8. Postharvest disposition should preserve lot identity, define actual exposure and affected scope, review product-condition and stability evidence, and document authorized release, hold, rework or rejection decisions.
   - **Boundary:** The SOP may define the evidence workflow but should not invent jurisdiction-specific release limits or override required laboratory and regulatory controls.

## What you must verify before acting

Identity • stage/process • current procedure/plan • measurement quality • authority • required record • escalation trigger.

## Do not assume

- universal number of drying days
- one universal room temperature or RH target
- one universal product water-activity release threshold for every jurisdiction and product class
- claims that curing can remediate contaminated material
- universal package or atmosphere claims
- shelf-life claims without product- and package-specific stability evidence

## Strong work product

A strong record allows another qualified person to reconstruct what was planned, what actually happened, what evidence was used, what remained uncertain, what action was authorized, and what needs follow-up.
