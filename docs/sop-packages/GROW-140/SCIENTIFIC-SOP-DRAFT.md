# THC-GROW-140 — Drying, curing, storage, and water activity
**Version:** 0.1.0 draft  
**Status:** Controlled draft — review required  
**Release:** Blocked

## Purpose

Define the source-supported claims and evidence boundaries required before authoring the full controlled GROW-140 postharvest SOP package.

## Scope

**Included:** drying as controlled heat-and-mass transfer; representative product-condition measurement; moisture content versus water activity; within-load spatial variation; curing and moisture redistribution; packaging and storage variables; cannabinoid and volatile stability; microbial-risk escalation; lot traceability and deviation handling.

**Not universalized or not authorized by this draft:** universal number of drying days; one universal room temperature or RH target; one universal product water-activity release threshold for every jurisdiction and product class; claims that curing can remediate contaminated material; universal package or atmosphere claims; shelf-life claims without product- and package-specific stability evidence.

## Scientific and operational control principles

### 1. Drying rate and final product condition depend on interacting process variables including temperature, humidity, airflow, loading, flower geometry and time; room averages alone do not prove uniform product condition.
**Evidence boundary:** A room setpoint is a process input, not direct evidence that every location or flower in a lot reached the same condition.
**Sources:** `REF-POSTHARVEST-001`, `REF-POSTHARVEST-DRY-2025-001`

### 2. Moisture content and water activity describe different properties of dried plant material and should not be treated as interchangeable indicators.
**Evidence boundary:** A measured moisture percentage cannot by itself establish microbial or chemical stability, and a water-activity result must be interpreted with method, temperature, sampling and applicable specification.
**Sources:** `REF-POSTHARVEST-001`, `REF-POSTHARVEST-STABILITY-2026-001`

### 3. Cannabis/hemp drying methods can alter drying time, decarboxylation, color, microbial load, cannabinoid form and volatile retention, creating quality and safety tradeoffs rather than one universally superior method.
**Evidence boundary:** Results from a specific cultivar, apparatus or drying treatment must not be converted directly into a universal cannabis recipe.
**Sources:** `REF-POSTHARVEST-DRY-2025-001`, `REF-POSTHARVEST-VOLATILES-2024-001`

### 4. Curing and sealed storage can change product moisture condition after drying, so sealed packaging does not eliminate the need for representative product measurements and stability checks.
**Evidence boundary:** Curing is not a validated remediation method for unsafe microbial contamination or materially nonconforming product.
**Sources:** `REF-POSTHARVEST-DRY-2025-001`, `REF-POSTHARVEST-STABILITY-2026-001`

### 5. Storage stability is influenced by time, temperature, oxygen, light, humidity/moisture transfer, product matrix and packaging properties, and these factors can affect cannabinoids and volatile compounds differently.
**Evidence boundary:** Preserving one compound or using one package technology does not prove preservation of total product quality.
**Sources:** `REF-POSTHARVEST-STABILITY-2026-001`, `REF-POSTHARVEST-STORAGE-2024-001`, `REF-POSTHARVEST-VOLATILES-2024-001`, `REF-CANNABINOID-THERMAL-STABILITY-2022-001`

### 6. Cannabinoid degradation in dried cannabis is time- and temperature-dependent under studied conditions, supporting the need to justify storage specifications and shelf-life with measured stability evidence.
**Evidence boundary:** Kinetic findings from a study are not a finished-product expiry date and cannot replace product-specific stability validation.
**Sources:** `REF-CANNABINOID-THERMAL-STABILITY-2022-001`, `REF-POSTHARVEST-STABILITY-2026-001`

### 7. Representative sampling and lot traceability are necessary to investigate within-load variability, drying-room deviations and later storage failures without overgeneralizing from a convenience sample.
**Evidence boundary:** A single favorable sample does not demonstrate uniformity across a heterogeneous lot.
**Sources:** `REF-POSTHARVEST-001`, `REF-POSTHARVEST-STABILITY-2026-001`

### 8. Postharvest disposition should preserve lot identity, define actual exposure and affected scope, review product-condition and stability evidence, and document authorized release, hold, rework or rejection decisions.
**Evidence boundary:** The SOP may define the evidence workflow but should not invent jurisdiction-specific release limits or override required laboratory and regulatory controls.
**Sources:** `REF-POSTHARVEST-STABILITY-2026-001`, `REF-POSTHARVEST-001`

## Measurement, observation and decision quality

Distinguish planned/target values from actual observations and measurements. Preserve method/instrument context, stage, cultivar/lot, location, timing and relevant environmental/root-zone/process context. Do not turn one study treatment, visual cue or facility example into a universal cannabis specification.

## Records

The applicable form must preserve identity, date/time, location, stage/process, source procedure/version, required condition, actual condition, method/instrument when relevant, action, affected scope, deviation/escalation and release/approval state.

## Deviations

Contain immediate risk within authority, preserve the original evidence, define affected scope, notify the responsible role, and document follow-up. Never backfill a record to make execution appear compliant.
