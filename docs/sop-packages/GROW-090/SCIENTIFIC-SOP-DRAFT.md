# THC-GROW-090 — Integrated Pest Management
**Version:** 0.1.0 draft  
**Status:** Controlled draft — review required  
**Release:** Blocked

## Purpose

Create a repeatable IPM workflow based on prevention, representative scouting, identification, scope/trend assessment, containment, integrated controls, and post-intervention verification.

This SOP does **not** prescribe a universal pesticide product, rate, interval, tank mix, or pest-count threshold. Any pesticide use must be authorized under the current label, crop/site, jurisdiction, worker-safety requirements, and facility procedure.

## Scope

Applies to routine crop scouting, arthropod findings, trend monitoring, pest-identification evidence, quarantine/containment, cultural and biological-control context, treatment handoff, follow-up and event closure.

## Scientific basis

Cannabis/hemp greenhouse studies document economically relevant piercing-sucking pests including spider mites, hemp russet mite, broad mites, and cannabis aphids. Pest effects depend on organism, population, crop stage, genotype and environment. Research treatment efficacy is context-specific, which is why the operational workflow emphasizes correct identification and verification rather than product recipes.

## Procedure

### 1. Prevent and inspect
Maintain the applicable biosecurity, sanitation, incoming-material and crop-health controls. Use a defined scouting route and repeatable sampling effort.

### 2. Record direct evidence
Record pest organism or sign, life stage when observable, plant part, crop/lot, location, date/time, count or defined severity, damage pattern, and method used.

### 3. Preserve identification confidence
Separate “observed,” “suspected,” “probable,” and “confirmed.” Use magnification, specimen collection or qualified identification when the consequence of error requires more certainty.

### 4. Map distribution
Determine whether the finding is isolated, clustered, edge-associated, room-wide, or linked to a movement/source pattern. Preserve the scouting denominator and route.

### 5. Evaluate trend and crop impact
Compare compatible rounds. Do not claim improvement from a lower trap count if the sampling method changed or crop injury/spatial spread worsened.

### 6. Apply nonchemical controls within authorization
Use approved sanitation, exclusion, removal, environmental/cultural adjustment, biological-control or other authorized measures appropriate to the pest and facility plan.

### 7. Keep pesticide authority separate
Diagnostic confidence does not authorize pesticide selection/application. Route the evidence to the authorized role. The current product label and applicable law control use.

### 8. Verify after intervention
Define when, where and how the crop will be rechecked. Use a compatible monitoring method and document crop impact as well as pest counts/signs.

### 9. Close only with evidence
Event closure should identify affected scope, intervention, follow-up evidence, unresolved risk, recurrence plan, and responsible approval.

## Required records
- scouting route/denominator;
- pest/sign identification evidence;
- distribution/trend record;
- quarantine/containment actions;
- authorized intervention reference;
- follow-up monitoring;
- IPM deviation/escalation;
- closure/recurrence review.
