# THC-GROW-100 — Disease and Disorder Management
**Version:** 0.1.0 draft  
**Status:** Controlled draft — review required  
**Release:** Blocked

## Purpose

Provide a defensible workflow for separating direct observations from diagnoses, ranking biotic and abiotic differentials, containing meaningful spread risk, obtaining consequence-appropriate confirmation, and documenting authorized corrective actions.

The workflow covers important cannabis disease contexts including Fusarium/Pythium root and crown disease, powdery mildew, Botrytis/bud rot, HLVd, and abiotic look-alikes. It does not prescribe universal pesticide or disinfectant recipes.

## Procedure

### 1. Record observations before naming the cause
Document crop/lot, exact location, stage, symptom distribution, affected structures, chronology, severity and comparison plants. Preserve photographs/specimens where useful.

### 2. Check abiotic context
Review irrigation delivery, root-zone moisture/drainage, pH/EC, root condition, recent environment, temperature/RH gradients, crop stress and management changes.

### 3. Build a ranked differential
Include plausible root disease, foliar/flower disease, viroid, arthropod, nutrition/root-zone, environmental and physical-injury explanations as appropriate. Identify evidence that supports and argues against each.

### 4. Contain when spread risk warrants it
Limit unnecessary movement of suspect plants/material/tools and follow the biosecurity SOP while diagnosis is strengthened. Containment is not proof of cause.

### 5. Escalate diagnostic confirmation
When visual evidence is insufficient or consequence is high, use the approved specimen/sampling and diagnostic-laboratory route. Preserve chain of custody and test limitations.

### 6. HLVd boundary
Do not use absence of dudding or visible symptoms to establish HLVd-free status. Follow the approved stock/propagation testing and biosecurity procedure.

### 7. Root/crown disease boundary
Root browning, wilt and stunting can arise from pathogens and abiotic root-zone problems. Compare waterlogging/aeration and root-zone history with pathogen evidence before assigning cause.

### 8. Flower disease boundary
Powdery mildew and Botrytis are documented cannabis diseases, but flower discoloration or visible growth should not automatically be treated as species-level confirmation.

### 9. Authorize and verify controls
Use only controls authorized by the facility and current labels/procedures. Define follow-up observations/tests and evaluate response before closing the event.

### 10. Preserve the diagnostic history
If the working diagnosis changes, retain the original observations/hypothesis and add the new evidence and reasoning. Do not rewrite earlier records to fit the final conclusion.

## Required records
- observation/differential worksheet;
- root-zone/environment evidence;
- containment status;
- specimen/sample chain of custody;
- diagnostic result;
- authorized action;
- follow-up verification;
- disease/disorder deviation/CAPA.
