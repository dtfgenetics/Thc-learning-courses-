# THC-GROW-020 — Biosecurity and Clean Workflow
**Version:** 0.1.0 draft  
**Status:** Controlled draft — review required  
**Release:** Blocked

## Purpose

Establish a repeatable biosecurity workflow that reduces the chance that pathogens, viroids, pests, contaminated plant material, tools, people, water, waste, or equipment move from a higher-risk source into cleaner cultivation areas.

This draft is an operational framework, not a universal sanitizer recipe. Product-specific sanitation chemistry, diagnostic assays, quarantine durations, worker PPE, and release rules must be validated for the facility and current product labels/procedures.

## Scope

Applies to cultivation entry/exit, stock and propagation areas, incoming plant material, tools and carts, plant movement, suspect material, removed plant material, waste movement, and quarantine/release documentation.

It does not authorize pesticide application, laboratory testing outside assigned roles, or use of a disinfectant at a concentration/contact time not approved by its current product instructions and facility procedure.

## Scientific basis

Cannabis greenhouse disease literature supports pathogen-free stock, quarantine of incoming material, routine monitoring/testing, sanitation, irrigation/environment management, and separation of stock/propagation from production as major disease-management controls. HLVd can be asymptomatic, so visual inspection alone cannot establish clean-stock status. Molecular diagnostics can be appropriate where consequence and uncertainty justify testing.

## Roles and authority

**Technician:** follows the effective entry, movement, sanitation, quarantine, observation, and record procedures; stops and escalates when identity/status is uncertain.

**Supervisor/plant-health role:** determines scope, additional monitoring/testing, containment expansion, and release recommendation within assigned authority.

**Authorized diagnostic/quality role:** controls test methods, sample acceptance, result interpretation, and release criteria where laboratory evidence is required.

## Procedure

### 1. Verify area and material status before movement
Confirm the identity and current status of the room/zone, plant lot, tools, carts, containers, and destination. Do not treat “no visible symptoms” as proof that material is unrestricted.

### 2. Maintain directional workflow
Follow the site-defined clean-to-dirty or lower-risk-to-higher-risk sequence. Do not carry tools, gloves, debris, plants, or containers from a suspect/affected area into a cleaner area without the approved transition step.

### 3. Control incoming plant material
Record source, cultivar/line, lot or batch identity, arrival date, condition, and quarantine destination. Keep incoming plant material physically/status separated until the required inspection/testing/release evidence is complete.

### 4. Protect stock and propagation
Use only material that meets the current stock/propagation acceptance criteria. When HLVd or another high-consequence pathogen is in scope, use the approved sampling/testing decision rule rather than visual inspection alone.

### 5. Clean before disinfecting when required
Remove plant debris, soil/media, residues, and other material that can interfere with the approved sanitation process. Then use only the facility-approved product/method according to its effective instructions, compatibility limits, PPE, contact-time and rinsing requirements.

### 6. Control tools, carts and equipment
Assign, segregate, or sanitize shared tools/equipment according to the movement matrix. If a required sanitation step cannot be completed, keep the item out of the cleaner area and document/escalate the exception.

### 7. Isolate suspect findings
When a plant-health finding could represent a meaningful spread risk, preserve exact location and plant/lot identity, limit unnecessary movement, use the applicable containment status, and notify the responsible role. Do not rewrite a working hypothesis as a confirmed diagnosis.

### 8. Manage removed material and waste
Move removed plant material and waste by the defined route/container method. Prevent the waste stream from becoming an uncontrolled pathway back into clean production.

### 9. Document testing and diagnostic evidence
Record specimen/sample identity, collection context, method/laboratory reference, result, date, limitations, and relationship to the affected plant/lot. A test result only supports the scope represented by the sample and method.

### 10. Release only the specific status supported by evidence
Quarantine, sanitation state, pesticide restricted-entry status, quality hold, and equipment out-of-service status are separate controls. Clear only the status for which authorized release evidence exists.

## Required records

- incoming plant-material intake/quarantine record;
- stock/propagation testing record;
- tool/cart/equipment sanitation and movement record;
- suspect plant/isolation record;
- diagnostic sample/result linkage;
- removed-material/waste movement record;
- quarantine-release authorization;
- biosecurity deviation/CAPA record.

## Deviations and escalation

Escalate when identity is uncertain; a clean/dirty boundary is crossed incorrectly; a required sanitation step is missed; a suspect high-consequence pathogen/viroid is observed; testing is invalid/inconclusive; or a quarantine release cannot be supported by the defined evidence.

## Release boundary

This document remains a draft until plant-pathology/biosecurity review, diagnostic-method review, facility-layout validation, sanitation-product/safety review, accessibility review, and independent release approval are complete.
