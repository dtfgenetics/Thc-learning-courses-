# THC-GROW-020 Learner Guide — Biosecurity and Clean Workflow
**Draft — not operationally released**

Biosecurity is the control of **movement and status**, not just “cleaning.”

## The five questions to ask before moving anything

1. **What is it?** Plant/lot, tool, cart, material, sample, waste, or person/workflow.
2. **Where did it come from?** Clean zone, unrestricted production, quarantine, suspect area, or affected area.
3. **What is its current status?** Clean/ready, pending sanitation, quarantine, hold, restricted entry, or out of service.
4. **Where is it going?** Verify that the destination is allowed to receive it.
5. **What evidence clears the move?** Completed sanitation, authorized release, required testing, or other site-defined evidence.

## Important principles

- A healthy-looking plant can still carry an asymptomatic pathogen or viroid.
- Cleaning and disinfection are not the same step.
- More chemical is not automatically more effective or safer.
- A negative test only speaks to the sample, method, timing, and organism tested.
- Quarantine and pesticide restricted-entry status are different controls.
- If status is uncertain, **do not improvise the release**. Preserve identity and escalate.

## Practice scenario

A cutting lot arrives from a new source. The plants look healthy. Correct action: record identity/source, place them in the approved intake/quarantine process, complete required inspection/testing, preserve movement history, and release them only when the responsible role has the required evidence.

## What you should be able to document

Source → identity → location → status → movement → sanitation/testing evidence → release decision.
