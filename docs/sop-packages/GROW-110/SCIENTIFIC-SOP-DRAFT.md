# THC-GROW-110 — Training and canopy management
**Version:** 0.1.0 draft  
**Status:** Controlled draft — review required  
**Release:** Blocked

## Purpose

Define canopy-training decisions, sanitation, plant-response monitoring and recovery checks without presenting one pruning/defoliation percentage or schedule as universal.

## Scope

**Included:** canopy architecture; trellising/LST; topping/HST; pruning/defoliation; tool sanitation; light distribution; microclimate; recovery checks; plant-level work records.

**Not universalized or not authorized by this draft:** universal defoliation percentage; fixed topping node count for every genotype; automatic assumption that more training increases yield; training stressed/unhealthy plants without assessment; copying one lighting/training treatment into every facility.

## Scientific and operational control principles

### 1. Cannabis plant architecture influences light distribution, microclimate and spatial chemical/yield uniformity, so canopy management should be evaluated against the production objective and measured crop response.
**Evidence boundary:** One plant shape or lighting strategy is not universally optimal across cultivar, density and facility conditions.
**Sources:** `REF-CANOPY-001`, `REF-LIGHT-INTERCANOPY-2025-001`

### 2. Pruning and defoliation responses can depend on cultivar and lighting context, so treatment intensity and timing should be controlled and followed by recovery/performance observation.
**Evidence boundary:** Experimental defoliation treatments do not establish a universal percentage or calendar schedule.
**Sources:** `REF-CANOPY-DEFOL-2025-001`

### 3. Low-stress and high-stress training should be treated as different disturbance levels with different recovery needs and should not be applied automatically to weak, diseased or otherwise compromised plants.
**Evidence boundary:** The course does not guarantee yield benefit from any one technique.
**Sources:** `REF-CANOPY-001`

### 4. Canopy work should preserve plant/zone identity, technique, date, operator, removed material and abnormal response so later crop differences can be related to the actual intervention.
**Evidence boundary:** A generic note such as 'defoliated room' is insufficient when intensity or affected plants matter.
**Sources:** `REF-CANOPY-001`

### 5. Tools contacting multiple plants can become a movement pathway for biological contamination, so canopy work should integrate the facility's approved sanitation and clean-to-dirty workflow.
**Evidence boundary:** Specific sanitizers and contact times remain controlled by the biosecurity SOP and current product instructions.
**Sources:** `REF-CANOPY-001`

## Measurement, observation and decision quality

Distinguish planned/target values from actual observations and measurements. Preserve method/instrument context, stage, cultivar/lot, location, timing and relevant environmental/root-zone/process context. Do not turn one study treatment, visual cue or facility example into a universal cannabis specification.

## Records

The applicable form must preserve identity, date/time, location, stage/process, source procedure/version, required condition, actual condition, method/instrument when relevant, action, affected scope, deviation/escalation and release/approval state.

## Deviations

Contain immediate risk within authority, preserve the original evidence, define affected scope, notify the responsible role, and document follow-up. Never backfill a record to make execution appear compliant.
