# THC-GROW-110 Learner Guide — Training and canopy management
**Draft — not operationally released**

## Goal

Define canopy-training decisions, sanitation, plant-response monitoring and recovery checks without presenting one pruning/defoliation percentage or schedule as universal.

## What the evidence supports

1. Cannabis plant architecture influences light distribution, microclimate and spatial chemical/yield uniformity, so canopy management should be evaluated against the production objective and measured crop response.
   - **Boundary:** One plant shape or lighting strategy is not universally optimal across cultivar, density and facility conditions.
2. Pruning and defoliation responses can depend on cultivar and lighting context, so treatment intensity and timing should be controlled and followed by recovery/performance observation.
   - **Boundary:** Experimental defoliation treatments do not establish a universal percentage or calendar schedule.
3. Low-stress and high-stress training should be treated as different disturbance levels with different recovery needs and should not be applied automatically to weak, diseased or otherwise compromised plants.
   - **Boundary:** The course does not guarantee yield benefit from any one technique.
4. Canopy work should preserve plant/zone identity, technique, date, operator, removed material and abnormal response so later crop differences can be related to the actual intervention.
   - **Boundary:** A generic note such as 'defoliated room' is insufficient when intensity or affected plants matter.
5. Tools contacting multiple plants can become a movement pathway for biological contamination, so canopy work should integrate the facility's approved sanitation and clean-to-dirty workflow.
   - **Boundary:** Specific sanitizers and contact times remain controlled by the biosecurity SOP and current product instructions.

## What you must verify before acting

Identity • stage/process • current procedure/plan • measurement quality • authority • required record • escalation trigger.

## Do not assume

- universal defoliation percentage
- fixed topping node count for every genotype
- automatic assumption that more training increases yield
- training stressed/unhealthy plants without assessment
- copying one lighting/training treatment into every facility

## Strong work product

A strong record allows another qualified person to reconstruct what was planned, what actually happened, what evidence was used, what remained uncertain, what action was authorized, and what needs follow-up.
