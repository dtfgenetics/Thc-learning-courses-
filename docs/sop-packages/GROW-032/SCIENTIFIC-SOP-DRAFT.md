# THC-GROW-032 — Germination
**Version:** 0.1.0 draft  
**Status:** Controlled draft — review required  
**Release:** Blocked

## Purpose

Define a repeatable germination workflow that preserves seed-lot identity, moisture/oxygen/temperature context, sanitation, radicle handling and failure evidence without presenting one method as universally best.

## Scope and boundaries

**Included:** seed-lot linkage; method selection; moisture management; temperature/oxygen context; sanitation; radicle handling; success/failure recording; transfer criteria.

**Not authorized or not universalized in this draft:** one universal germination method; guaranteed germination percentage; fixed germination time for every genotype/lot; overwatering as a default response to delayed emergence; discarding failed seeds without recording lot/method context.

## Scientific and operational control principles

### 1. Cannabis seed germination depends on viable seed and suitable water, temperature and oxygen conditions, so method records should preserve the environment in which each lot was tested.
**Evidence boundary:** The evidence does not support one universally superior germination container or technique.
**Sources:** `REF-PROP-001`, `REF-PLANT-BIO-001`

### 2. Germination performance should be calculated from a defined lot/sample and observation window rather than generalized from a few successful seeds.
**Evidence boundary:** Observed sample performance should not be converted into a guaranteed whole-lot rate without an appropriate sampling design.
**Sources:** `REF-PROP-001`

### 3. Excess moisture and poor sanitation can complicate germination and early establishment, supporting controlled moisture and clean handling rather than keeping seeds continuously saturated.
**Evidence boundary:** Exact moisture targets should be validated for the chosen method and substrate rather than copied as a universal number.
**Sources:** `REF-PROP-001`

### 4. Emerging radicles and young seedlings are mechanically vulnerable, so transfer methods should minimize avoidable handling damage and preserve lot/plant identity.
**Evidence boundary:** The SOP should define handling principles, not claim that one transfer timing fits every method and genotype.
**Sources:** `REF-PLANT-BIO-001`, `REF-PROP-001`

### 5. Failed, delayed or contaminated germination batches should retain method, lot, environmental and sanitation evidence so cause can be investigated before repeating or changing the process.
**Evidence boundary:** A failed batch should not automatically be attributed to genetics when method or storage conditions are unresolved.
**Sources:** `REF-PROP-001`

## Records and reconstructability

For every controlled event, preserve identity, date/time, location, responsible person or system, planned/required condition, observed/measured condition, action taken, unresolved condition, deviation/escalation reference and approval/release state where applicable.

## Deviations

Do not silently correct history or improvise outside assigned authority. Contain the immediate risk where permitted, preserve evidence, identify affected scope, notify the responsible role, and route the event through the applicable deviation/CAPA or technical-review process.

## Training boundary

Course completion supports education but does not by itself create facility authorization, trade licensure, pesticide authority, engineering authority, laboratory qualification or jurisdiction-specific certification.
