# THC-GROW-032 Learner Guide — Germination
**Draft — not operationally released**

## What this topic controls

Define a repeatable germination workflow that preserves seed-lot identity, moisture/oxygen/temperature context, sanitation, radicle handling and failure evidence without presenting one method as universally best.

## Core principles

1. Cannabis seed germination depends on viable seed and suitable water, temperature and oxygen conditions, so method records should preserve the environment in which each lot was tested.
   - **Limit:** The evidence does not support one universally superior germination container or technique.
2. Germination performance should be calculated from a defined lot/sample and observation window rather than generalized from a few successful seeds.
   - **Limit:** Observed sample performance should not be converted into a guaranteed whole-lot rate without an appropriate sampling design.
3. Excess moisture and poor sanitation can complicate germination and early establishment, supporting controlled moisture and clean handling rather than keeping seeds continuously saturated.
   - **Limit:** Exact moisture targets should be validated for the chosen method and substrate rather than copied as a universal number.
4. Emerging radicles and young seedlings are mechanically vulnerable, so transfer methods should minimize avoidable handling damage and preserve lot/plant identity.
   - **Limit:** The SOP should define handling principles, not claim that one transfer timing fits every method and genotype.
5. Failed, delayed or contaminated germination batches should retain method, lot, environmental and sanitation evidence so cause can be investigated before repeating or changing the process.
   - **Limit:** A failed batch should not automatically be attributed to genetics when method or storage conditions are unresolved.

## Before acting

Verify: **identity → current status → applicable procedure/plan → measurement or observation quality → your authority → required record → escalation trigger**.

## What not to assume

- one universal germination method
- guaranteed germination percentage
- fixed germination time for every genotype/lot
- overwatering as a default response to delayed emergence
- discarding failed seeds without recording lot/method context

## Evidence habit

Record what you observed or measured before writing the conclusion. Preserve uncertainty and the reason for any decision, especially when the final answer depends on cultivar, stage, facility, method or jurisdiction.
