# THC-GROW-000 Learner Guide — Manual overview, purpose, scope, document control
**Draft — not operationally released**

## What this topic controls

Define the controlled manual architecture, document hierarchy, versioning, applicability, authority and release-state rules for the cultivation SOP system.

## Core principles

1. A controlled SOP system should make purpose, scope, responsibilities, prerequisites, required steps, records and exception/escalation paths clear enough that authorized users can execute and audit the process consistently.
   - **Limit:** The exact required structure may differ by organization or jurisdiction; the SOP architecture must remain subordinate to applicable law, contracts and facility governance.
2. Only the effective controlled version should govern current work, while historical versions must remain traceable so past events can be reconstructed against the instruction that was actually in force.
   - **Limit:** A later revision can guide corrective action but must not be rewritten into the past as if it governed earlier work.
3. Document changes should preserve who changed what, when, why, review/approval status and effective date so process evolution remains reconstructable.
   - **Limit:** The educational framework does not prescribe one software platform or electronic-signature implementation.
4. Background science, training explanation and operational execution instructions should be linked but distinguishable so required steps are not buried in educational narrative.
   - **Limit:** Separating execution from background does not mean workers should be trained without understanding the scientific basis.
5. Controlled procedures should identify the forms, logs, checklists or electronic records needed to capture critical observations, decisions, deviations and approvals.
   - **Limit:** A field should not be added merely to create paperwork; records should support a defined operational, quality, safety or traceability purpose.
6. Jurisdiction-specific rules, product labels, equipment manuals and approved facility procedures override generic educational examples when they impose more specific requirements.
   - **Limit:** Transferable quality standards must not be presented as jurisdiction-specific cannabis regulation.

## Before acting

Verify: **identity → current status → applicable procedure/plan → measurement or observation quality → your authority → required record → escalation trigger**.

## What not to assume

- claim that a generic quality framework is cannabis law
- silent retroactive revision of historical requirements
- use of draft/internal-preview material as an effective operational SOP
- uncontrolled local copies treated as authoritative
- release without versioned approval evidence

## Evidence habit

Record what you observed or measured before writing the conclusion. Preserve uncertainty and the reason for any decision, especially when the final answer depends on cultivar, stage, facility, method or jurisdiction.
