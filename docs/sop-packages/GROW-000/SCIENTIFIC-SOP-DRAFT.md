# THC-GROW-000 — Manual overview, purpose, scope, document control
**Version:** 0.1.0 draft  
**Status:** Controlled draft — review required  
**Release:** Blocked

## Purpose

Define the controlled manual architecture, document hierarchy, versioning, applicability, authority and release-state rules for the cultivation SOP system.

## Scope and boundaries

**Included:** manual purpose/scope; document hierarchy; roles and authorization; effective versions; change control; superseded-document handling; linked forms/records; review/release state; jurisdiction/facility authority boundaries.

**Not authorized or not universalized in this draft:** claim that a generic quality framework is cannabis law; silent retroactive revision of historical requirements; use of draft/internal-preview material as an effective operational SOP; uncontrolled local copies treated as authoritative; release without versioned approval evidence.

## Scientific and operational control principles

### 1. A controlled SOP system should make purpose, scope, responsibilities, prerequisites, required steps, records and exception/escalation paths clear enough that authorized users can execute and audit the process consistently.
**Evidence boundary:** The exact required structure may differ by organization or jurisdiction; the SOP architecture must remain subordinate to applicable law, contracts and facility governance.
**Sources:** `REF-EPA-SOP-QAG6`, `REF-ASTM-E2659-001`

### 2. Only the effective controlled version should govern current work, while historical versions must remain traceable so past events can be reconstructed against the instruction that was actually in force.
**Evidence boundary:** A later revision can guide corrective action but must not be rewritten into the past as if it governed earlier work.
**Sources:** `REF-ASTM-E2659-001`, `REF-MHRA-GXP-DATA-INTEGRITY`

### 3. Document changes should preserve who changed what, when, why, review/approval status and effective date so process evolution remains reconstructable.
**Evidence boundary:** The educational framework does not prescribe one software platform or electronic-signature implementation.
**Sources:** `REF-MHRA-GXP-DATA-INTEGRITY`, `REF-ASTM-E2659-001`

### 4. Background science, training explanation and operational execution instructions should be linked but distinguishable so required steps are not buried in educational narrative.
**Evidence boundary:** Separating execution from background does not mean workers should be trained without understanding the scientific basis.
**Sources:** `REF-EPA-SOP-QAG6`, `REF-ASTM-D8403-001`

### 5. Controlled procedures should identify the forms, logs, checklists or electronic records needed to capture critical observations, decisions, deviations and approvals.
**Evidence boundary:** A field should not be added merely to create paperwork; records should support a defined operational, quality, safety or traceability purpose.
**Sources:** `REF-ASTM-D8403-001`, `REF-MHRA-GXP-DATA-INTEGRITY`

### 6. Jurisdiction-specific rules, product labels, equipment manuals and approved facility procedures override generic educational examples when they impose more specific requirements.
**Evidence boundary:** Transferable quality standards must not be presented as jurisdiction-specific cannabis regulation.
**Sources:** `REF-ASTM-E2659-001`, `REF-EPA-SOP-QAG6`

## Records and reconstructability

For every controlled event, preserve identity, date/time, location, responsible person or system, planned/required condition, observed/measured condition, action taken, unresolved condition, deviation/escalation reference and approval/release state where applicable.

## Deviations

Do not silently correct history or improvise outside assigned authority. Contain the immediate risk where permitted, preserve evidence, identify affected scope, notify the responsible role, and route the event through the applicable deviation/CAPA or technical-review process.

## Training boundary

Course completion supports education but does not by itself create facility authorization, trade licensure, pesticide authority, engineering authority, laboratory qualification or jurisdiction-specific certification.
