# Primary Source Expansion — 2026-09-22

Purpose: strengthen the Academy evidence layer with authoritative regulatory/occupational sources and peer-reviewed Cannabis sativa cultivation research. These sources support claim-level review; they are not universal cultivation prescriptions.

## Source-selection rule

1. Prefer current primary government/regulatory material for safety, pesticide, worker-protection and legal claims.
2. Prefer peer-reviewed primary research for cannabis-specific cultivation claims.
3. Use reviews to establish scope and locate primary studies, not to turn limited evidence into universal rules.
4. Preserve genotype, environment, substrate, treatment and study-design boundaries when translating research into lessons.
5. Do not convert a single study's nutrient concentration, RH/VPD, light intensity, drying treatment or propagation protocol into a universal setpoint.

## Occupational safety and worker protection

- CDC/NIOSH, **Workplace Safety and Health Hazards — Cannabis** (2024): https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/cannabis/about/index.html
  - Supports: chemical, biological, ergonomic/physical and general safety hazard identification; hierarchy-of-controls framing; worker/employer prevention practices.
- NIOSH, **Health Hazard Evaluation Report 2019-0152-3381**: https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/hhe/reports/pdfs/2019-0152-3381.pdf
  - Supports: cannabis cultivation/harvesting/trimming exposure assessment, worker symptoms, PPE and task-specific occupational-health evidence.
- OSHA, **Hazard Prevention and Control**: https://www.osha.gov/safety-management/hazard-prevention
  - Supports: hierarchy of controls and selection of feasible/effective controls.
- OSHA, **Hazard Communication, 29 CFR 1910.1200**: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200
  - Supports: labels, SDS, worker information/training and protective measures.
- OSHA, **Cannabis Industries Local Emphasis Program CPL 24-04 (EP)**: https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/enforcement/directives/CPL_24-04_EP.pdf
  - Supports: cannabis-industry examples including machinery, pesticides, molds/dust, fire/explosion, solvents and CO2-related hazards.

## Pesticide worker protection

- EPA, **Agricultural Worker Protection Standard (WPS)**: https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-worker-safety/agricultural-worker-protection-standard-wps
  - Supports: employer/handler duties, application restrictions, REIs, PPE and respirator requirements.
- EPA, **Restrictions to Protect Workers After Pesticide Applications**: https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-worker-safety/restrictions-protect-workers-after-pesticide-applications
  - Supports: definition and application of REIs and narrow early-entry exceptions.
- EPA, **Restrictions During Pesticide Applications Under the WPS**: https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-worker-safety/restrictions-during-pesticide-applications-under-worker-protection-standard
  - Supports: exclusion from treatment areas and enclosed-space/nursery restrictions.
- EPA, **How EPA Protects Workers from Pesticide Risk** (updated 2026): https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-worker-safety/how-epa-protects-workers-pesticide-risk
  - Supports: label-based risk-management measures, PPE, engineering controls and worker-safety programs.

## Cannabis cultivation science

- Rodriguez-Morrison V, Llewellyn D, Zheng Y. **Cannabis Yield, Potency, and Leaf Photosynthesis Respond Differently to Increasing Light Levels in an Indoor Environment.** Front Plant Sci. 2021;12:646020. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2021.646020
  - Supports: light-response instruction and the distinction between biomass/yield responses and chemical concentration responses.
- Saloner A, Bernstein N. **Nitrogen supply affects cannabinoid and terpenoid profile in medical cannabis.** Industrial Crops and Products. 2021;167:113516. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.indcrop.2021.113516
  - Supports: nitrogen affects plant function, yield and secondary-metabolite profiles; numeric treatment results remain study-specific.
- Saloner A, Bernstein N. **Nitrogen Source Matters: High NH4/NO3 Ratio Reduces Cannabinoids, Terpenoids, and Yield in Medical Cannabis.** Front Plant Sci. 2022;13:830224. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2022.830224
  - Supports: nitrogen form matters, not merely total N.
- Shiponi S, Bernstein N. **The Highs and Lows of P Supply in Medical Cannabis: Effects on Cannabinoids, the Ionome, and Morpho-Physiology.** Front Plant Sci. 2021;12:657323. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2021.657323
  - Supports: phosphorus supply affects physiology, biomass and cannabinoid concentration/content differently.
- Veazie P et al. **Impact of substrate pH and micronutrient fertility rates on Cannabis sativa.** Agrosystems, Geosciences & Environment. 2025. https://doi.org/10.1002/agg2.70044
  - Supports: substrate pH materially affects cannabis growth and tissue nutrient accumulation; results are cultivar/substrate/study-specific.
- Punja ZK et al. **Integrated Management of Pathogens and Microbes in Cannabis sativa L. under Greenhouse Conditions.** 2024. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10974757/
  - Supports: sanitation, pathogen pathways and environmental disease-management context; environmental ranges should be presented as literature guidance, not universal thresholds.
- Chandra S, Lata H, ElSohly MA. **Propagation of Cannabis for Clinical Research.** Front Plant Sci. 2020;11:958. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2020.00958
  - Supports: vegetative propagation, controlled production and environmental parameters relevant to clonal consistency.
- Caplan D et al. / related hemp propagation study, **Evaluation of substrate composition and exogenous hormone application on vegetative propagule rooting success of essential oil hemp.** 2021. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8320905/
  - Supports: substrate and auxin treatment influence rooting; treatment outcomes are experimental, not universal cloning recipes.

## Irrigation and postharvest

- **Subsurface drip irrigation reduces weed infestation and irrigation water use while increasing inflorescence and cannabinoid yield in an outdoor tunnel Cannabis sativa L. production system.** Journal of Cannabis Research. 2025;7:41. https://doi.org/10.1186/s42238-025-00302-x
  - Supports: irrigation-system choice can affect water use efficiency and crop outcomes under the tested outdoor-tunnel conditions.
- Jin D et al. **Postharvest Operations of Cannabis and Their Effect on Cannabinoid Content: A Review.** Bioengineering. 2022;9(8):364. https://doi.org/10.3390/bioengineering9080364
  - Supports: drying, curing, packaging/storage and water-activity/moisture concepts; use as synthesis, with primary studies preferred for exact claims.
- Baek Y, Grab H, Chen C. **Postharvest Drying and Curing Affect Cannabinoid Contents and Microbial Levels in Industrial Hemp.** Plants. 2025;14(3):414. https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14030414
  - Supports: drying method/temperature and curing can alter drying rate, microbial load, decarboxylation, color and moisture under tested conditions.

## Regulatory scope

- USDA AMS, **Hemp Production**: https://www.ams.usda.gov/rules-regulations/hemp
  - Supports federal hemp-program licensing/authorization context. Do not use it as a substitute for state-specific marijuana/cannabis rules.
