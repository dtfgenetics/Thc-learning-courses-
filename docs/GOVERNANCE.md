# Curriculum and Credential Governance

## Credential model

THC Academy maintains two deliberately separate credential layers.

### Education certificates

Education certificates attest that a learner completed an identified THC Academy curriculum and satisfied its published academic requirements. They are tied to the training program itself.

Canonical Technician education certificates are based on:
- the seven Technician I certification courses; and
- the eight Technician II certification courses.

Academic completion may require lessons, course-owned knowledge checks, finals, practical learning activities, and course-completion evidence. Academic completion alone does **not** authorize a professional personnel certification claim.

### Professional personnel certifications

Professional personnel certifications attest to occupational competence against an independently governed credential specification. They are not automatically earned merely by completing THC Academy instruction.

Professional certification requires a separate evidence chain:

`job/task analysis -> competency specification -> eligibility -> secure assessment -> performance evidence -> scoring/decision rules -> standard setting -> credential eligibility -> authorization -> lifecycle management`

Where an equivalent-preparation or challenge pathway is approved, a candidate must be able to satisfy the professional certification eligibility rules without a false requirement that THC Academy training itself be the only acceptable preparation route.

The exact eligibility pathways remain controlled program policy and must not be invented solely to satisfy a software gate.

## Roles

- Author: drafts instructional content.
- Editor: reviews clarity, structure, and accessibility.
- Subject Matter Reviewer: validates scientific accuracy and references.
- Assessment Reviewer: validates item quality and objective alignment.
- Occupational Reviewer: validates job/task and competency relevance.
- Accessibility Reviewer: validates access and accommodation implications.
- Security/Privacy Reviewer: validates candidate-data, exam-security, and record controls.
- Final Approver: authorizes credential-bearing publication or release.

For credential-bearing content, the author must not be the only final approver. Conflicts of interest must be declared and mitigated.

## Review states

`draft -> editorial-review -> scientific-review -> assessment-review -> approved -> published -> superseded -> retired`

Not every non-credential object requires every stage, but summative assessment items, professional credential definitions, credential decision rules, and operational credential forms require controlled review appropriate to their use.

## AI-assisted work

AI may draft, transform, classify, summarize, suggest objectives, propose questions, identify coverage gaps, or assist analysis.

AI-generated or AI-assisted material must not become operational credential-bearing content until a qualified human reviewer approves the exact version.

For credential-bearing artifacts, provenance should preserve at minimum:
- whether AI materially contributed;
- the affected object/version;
- the human reviewer responsible for approval;
- the review domain (scientific, assessment, occupational, accessibility, security, or other);
- the approval decision and date.

AI must not independently make the final decision for:
- credential issuance;
- credential revocation or suspension;
- appeal disposition;
- accommodation approval;
- passing-standard adoption;
- operational exam-form approval;
- assessor authorization.

Automated analysis may support these decisions only where the governing process remains reviewable and human-authorized.

## Impartiality and conflicts of interest

Credential decisions must be protected from commercial, personal, employment, training, or authorship conflicts.

Examples requiring declaration or mitigation include:
- an author acting as the sole approver of their own credential exam;
- an assessor evaluating a close associate or direct report without independent control;
- an employer sponsor attempting to influence pass/fail outcomes;
- commercial pressure to alter cut scores or failure rates;
- representing paid THC Academy preparation as mandatory when the approved certification eligibility model allows equivalent preparation.

The credential program must maintain an auditable impartiality/conflict record and document mitigation before affected credential decisions are finalized.

## Assessment and training separation

Course assessments may verify mastery of THC Academy instruction.

Professional certification examinations must independently verify the approved credential blueprint and occupational competencies. Public course-development items must not silently become secure operational credential items.

Operational forms require controlled private item storage, exposure management, equivalent-form controls, scoring governance, pilot evidence, and formal standard setting where applicable.

## Candidate accommodations

Approved accommodations must change access conditions without changing the occupational construct being assessed unless an approved alternative assessment method is required.

Accommodation records must be kept separate from scoring evidence except for the minimum instructions necessary to administer the accommodation.

Timed assessments must support approved extended-time or equivalent access arrangements without changing the scoring standard.

## Appeals and complaints

Appeals and complaints are separate processes.

An **appeal** challenges a decision affecting a candidate or credential holder, including scoring, accommodation implementation, identity/record matching, credential issuance, revocation, or application of policy.

A **complaint** concerns the conduct or operation of the certification program, including assessor conduct, impartiality, testing conditions, security, privacy, administration, or program practices.

Both processes must:
- preserve the original record;
- maintain an auditable event history;
- identify the authorized reviewer/decision maker;
- protect secure examination content;
- preserve confidentiality and minimum-necessary access.

A complaint must not be treated as an appeal merely because it was submitted by a candidate.

## Credential lifecycle

Professional credentials require explicit lifecycle policy before operational release.

The lifecycle model must support, as applicable:

`active -> expiring -> renewed | expired | suspended | revoked | superseded`

Validity and renewal periods must be based on approved program policy. The repository must not invent a renewal interval simply to make the credential appear complete.

Continuing competence may eventually be demonstrated through approved combinations of continuing education, occupational practice, update modules, reassessment, or re-examination.

Education certificates may have different lifecycle rules from professional personnel certifications.

## Versioning

Published course, lesson, assessment, and credential definitions are immutable. Any material change creates a new version. Historical learner records must reference the exact version completed or assessed.

Professional credential authorization is exact-version evidence. A material change to the credential program, examination specification, scoring rule, practical rubric, or credential decision rule requires impact review and may require renewed validation.

## Security and confidentiality

Operational secure credential items, answer keys, private form manifests, candidate medical/accommodation documentation, and confidential assessor evidence must not be stored in public release surfaces.

Public verification must expose only the minimum information needed to confirm credential identity and status.

Security incidents involving item exposure, impersonation, evidence tampering, unauthorized assistance, or signing/verification compromise require controlled hold, investigation, disposition, and audit history.

## Governance boundary

Passing automated tests proves structural consistency, not occupational validity, psychometric validity, fairness, accessibility approval, legal approval, or authorization to issue a professional credential.

Credential issuance remains fail-closed until the exact-version release dependencies and required human/operational evidence are satisfied.
