# THC Academy Assessment-Based Certificate Program Quality Standard

Status: **Mandatory target standard for credential-bearing Academy courses**

Purpose: Ensure THC Academy credential-bearing courses are substantial educational programs with defensible learning outcomes, evidence-linked instruction, practice, assessment, learner support, records, and continuous improvement. This standard is informed by current public guidance from ASTM E2659-24 for certificate programs, ANSI/IACET continuing-education program design, CDC Quality Training Standards, and Quality Matters Continuing & Professional Education course-design standards.

## 1. Terminology boundary

THC Academy currently issues educational/workforce certificates or internal credentials unless and until an independent personnel-certification/accreditation framework is actually granted.

Do not describe Academy completion as:
- government licensure;
- ANAB/ANSI accreditation;
- IACET accreditation or IACET CEUs;
- ISO/IEC 17024 personnel certification;
- independent third-party professional certification.

A certificate-bearing course must verify achievement of stated learning outcomes through assessment. Attendance, page views, video completion, or lesson completion alone are not sufficient.

## 2. Required program-design chain

Every credential-bearing course must be traceable through:

Occupation / role -> job task -> competency -> proficiency level -> learning objective -> instruction -> guided practice -> assessment -> performance evidence -> credential requirement -> employer-facing verification.

Every course must declare:
- target role and audience;
- prerequisites;
- scope and explicit exclusions;
- measurable course-level outcomes;
- module-level objectives;
- competency mappings;
- assessment mappings;
- completion and passing rules;
- evidence/review policy;
- revision/review date.

## 3. Minimum learner-facing course depth

A production credential-bearing course must contain enough substantive instruction for the stated outcomes. No course may be considered complete when it is only a routing shell pointing to modules.

Each course should normally contain:
- a course overview explaining purpose and workplace relevance;
- prerequisite knowledge/skills;
- course roadmap;
- terminology/glossary support;
- evidence-based core instruction;
- mechanism/cause-and-effect explanation where applicable;
- worked examples;
- realistic workplace scenarios;
- measurement/data interpretation;
- common errors and misconceptions;
- guided practice;
- documentation/traceability exercises;
- visual instruction;
- knowledge checks with feedback;
- module assessments;
- practical/simulation evidence where a skill is performance-relevant;
- final summative assessment;
- remediation and retry rules;
- references and source transparency;
- summary and next steps.

Depth is evaluated by learning coverage and evidence, not by word count alone.

## 4. Lesson quality gate

A complete lesson must include, where applicable:
1. Why the skill matters.
2. Role/authorization boundary.
3. Measurable learning objectives.
4. Prerequisite retrieval.
5. Evidence-linked instruction.
6. Scientific or operational mechanism.
7. At least one worked example.
8. At least one decision/application scenario.
9. Common mistakes or misconceptions.
10. Guided practice or activity.
11. Measurement, calculation, or data interpretation where relevant.
12. Documentation/recordkeeping activity where relevant.
13. Summary/retrieval prompt.
14. References.
15. Purposeful visual support.

For performance-relevant lessons, add a practical/simulation or explicit practical mapping.

## 5. Visual instruction standard

Images must teach rather than decorate.

Prefer:
- annotated plant photography;
- stage progressions;
- anatomy diagrams;
- root-zone diagrams;
- lighting/PPFD maps;
- environmental/VPD diagrams;
- deficiency/toxicity comparisons;
- pest/pathogen identification series;
- equipment diagrams;
- workflow/process diagrams;
- measurement examples;
- before/after comparisons;
- charts and tables.

Every instructional visual must include:
- teaching objective;
- descriptive alt text;
- caption;
- source/provenance/rights state;
- linked lesson/concept;
- review state.

High-quality images are preferred over arbitrary image counts.

## 6. Evidence standard

Scientific and technical claims must use the strongest practical source type:
1. systematic reviews/high-quality reviews;
2. peer-reviewed primary research;
3. government, university-extension, standards-body, or primary institutional guidance;
4. clearly labeled secondary educational sources.

Cannabis-specific evidence should be preferred for cannabis-specific claims. General plant science may support mechanisms that are not cannabis-specific.

Each significant claim must preserve uncertainty and context. Cultivar-, environment-, jurisdiction-, or study-specific findings must not be presented as universal rules.

References should preserve, where available:
- title;
- author/institution;
- year;
- journal/publisher;
- DOI/PMID/PMCID;
- stable URL;
- access/review date.

## 7. Assessment alignment

Every scored assessment item must map to:
- course;
- module;
- competency;
- learning objective;
- cognitive/proficiency level;
- source-backed rationale.

Assessment must sample the intended learning outcomes rather than reward superficial keyword recall.

Use:
- knowledge checks;
- scenario questions;
- calculations;
- data interpretation;
- troubleshooting;
- next-test selection;
- ordering/sequence tasks;
- matching/classification;
- record-review tasks;
- observed/simulated performance.

Credential decisions must not rely only on one fixed multiple-choice form when the stated capability includes practical performance.

## 8. Practical/performance assessment

When a competency describes something a worker must be able to do, the program should collect performance evidence.

A practical must define:
- work order/scenario;
- supplied evidence/data;
- actions/decisions required;
- required records/artifacts;
- rubric;
- critical errors;
- allowed delivery mode;
- linked competency/proficiency;
- remediation/retry policy.

Examples include:
- calibrating or interpreting environmental measurements;
- scouting and documenting pest pressure;
- reviewing irrigation/EC/pH data;
- producing a crop record;
- building a PPFD map;
- diagnosing from evidence while preserving uncertainty;
- sanitation/biosecurity decision making;
- harvest/postharvest QA record review.

## 9. Passing and credential issuance

Before a certificate can be issued:
- required instruction must be completed;
- required formative work must be satisfied where configured;
- summative assessment must meet the published passing standard;
- practical/capstone requirements must be satisfied where applicable;
- identity/reference requirements must be satisfied;
- attempt/cooldown rules must be enforced;
- the attempt must be locked after submission;
- grading must occur after submission;
- credential eligibility must be calculated from durable records.

The passing standard should be justified by the importance and difficulty of the outcomes rather than chosen arbitrarily.

## 10. Item-bank quality

Question banks must support randomized forms and sufficient coverage.

Require:
- more than one item per important outcome;
- scenario/application items, not only recall;
- plausible distractors based on common errors;
- source-backed rationales;
- difficulty/proficiency labeling;
- item review state;
- statistics/pilot data when available;
- retired/revised item history.

High-stakes items remain fail-closed until review requirements are met.

## 11. Learner support and usability

Every course should expose:
- orientation/overview;
- navigation guidance;
- prerequisites;
- estimated effort;
- progress state;
- glossary/reference access;
- technical support path;
- accessibility accommodations/support path;
- feedback/remediation;
- resume/continue behavior;
- certificate requirements.

Mobile, keyboard, focus, contrast, semantic structure, alt text, and reduced-motion requirements apply.

## 12. Program records

Maintain durable records sufficient to explain why a credential was issued:
- learner identity/reference ID;
- course/version;
- assessment version/form;
- responses and score;
- attempt timestamps;
- practical/capstone results;
- rubric evidence;
- reviewer identity where applicable;
- issuance timestamp;
- credential ID/version/status;
- revocation/correction history when program policy allows;
- source-course revision.

Privacy, retention, access, and correction policies must be explicit.

## 13. Evaluation and continuous improvement

Each course must have:
- learner feedback;
- defect/error reporting;
- SME review;
- evidence/source freshness review;
- assessment-item review;
- pilot/usability evidence before high-stakes activation where required;
- periodic revision date;
- change log.

Evaluate both learner satisfaction and whether learners can actually demonstrate the intended outcomes.

## 14. Production-readiness gate

A credential-bearing course is not production-ready unless all required evidence is present:

- course metadata complete;
- measurable outcomes mapped to competencies;
- substantive lessons complete;
- references resolve;
- visuals teach required concepts;
- formative checks exist;
- summative blueprint exists;
- sufficient item-bank depth exists;
- assessment mappings validate;
- practical/performance evidence exists where needed;
- passing/attempt rules exist;
- certificate issuance logic is tested;
- accessibility/usability review completed;
- human SME/assessment review recorded where required;
- pilot evidence recorded where required;
- public wording accurately describes the credential.

A thin course shell may remain in draft/design state but must not be represented as a complete certification course.

## 15. Current Academy priority

The existing repository contains strong examples such as COURSE-CULT-FOUNDATIONS-001, but multiple credential-bearing course objects are currently only thin routing shells. These should be rebuilt course-by-course using this standard, beginning with the shallowest high-value credentials and their associated modules/lessons.

Priority remediation order:

2. Environment and lighting.
3. Water / irrigation and root zone.
4. Nutrition / IPM.
5. Propagation.
6. Canopy / flowering.
7. Postharvest QA.
8. Crop systems / records.
9. Genetics.
10. Outdoor / greenhouse.
11. Home-grow pathway.
12. Pro SOP pathway.

## 16. External quality frameworks used as design references

This standard is an internal THC Academy quality framework. It does not claim conformance or accreditation.

Public design references:
- ASTM E2659-24, Standard Practice for Certificate Programs.
- ANSI/IACET continuing education and training framework.
- CDC Quality Training Standards and Quality E-Learning Checklist.
- Quality Matters Continuing & Professional Education Rubric.

Use those frameworks to improve instructional design, assessment alignment, records, accessibility, learner support, and continuous improvement while preserving THC Academy's cannabis-specific scientific and occupational requirements.
