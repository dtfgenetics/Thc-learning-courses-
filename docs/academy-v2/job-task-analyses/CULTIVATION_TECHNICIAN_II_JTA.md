## Purpose

This JTA defines the work that the Technician II credential is intended to represent. Technician II is an advanced operator/senior technician: more diagnostic and verification-oriented than Technician I, but not a cultivation manager, licensed pesticide applicator, electrician, engineer, final product-disposition authority, or unrestricted maintenance technician.

The role should be able to verify routine systems, interpret crop/environment/root-zone/production evidence, troubleshoot common deviations within supplied authority, reconcile records, support peers with procedure-based coaching, and prepare evidence-based handoffs to supervisors, QA, maintenance, plant-health or other qualified roles.

## Evidence basis

The development baseline combines:

- the current public occupational source baseline using O*NET/BLS agricultural supervisory, crop/nursery/greenhouse and agricultural-management evidence;
- the Technician I JTA and role boundary;
- the current Technician II eight-course curriculum;
- the seven Technician II integrated practicals;
- the Senior Technician Diagnostic Shift capstone;
- current controlled cannabis cultivation science and SOP/QA content already mapped into the course system.

The public occupational evidence is adjacent national evidence, not a cannabis-specific JTA. Ratings below are design ratings only and require validation by multiple current cannabis cultivation SMEs/employers.

## Rating method

Preliminary ratings use 1–5 scales:

- **Importance (I):** contribution to successful Technician II performance.
- **Frequency (F):** expected recurrence in advanced technician work.
- **Consequence/Risk (C):** worker, crop, quality, traceability, compliance or operational consequence of poor performance.

Do not mechanically convert these development ratings into exam weights.

## Detailed task statements

### 1. Multi-factor crop diagnostic workup

The Technician II can:

- define the observed crop problem before assigning cause;
- integrate plant morphology, stage, recent work, environment, irrigation/root-zone evidence, spatial pattern and crop history;
- identify missing evidence and obtain or request appropriate measurements;
- distinguish observation, hypothesis, likely cause and confirmed cause;
- rank plausible differentials;
- choose verify/correct/escalate actions that remain within procedure and authority;
- preserve uncertainty and alternative explanations in the handoff.

**Boundary:** Technician II does not claim specialist diagnosis when evidence is insufficient and does not use credential status to bypass laboratory, plant-pathology, QA or manager authority.

### 2. Sensor, controller and measurement-quality verification

The Technician II can:

- compare sensor readings across reference points and locations;
- verify basic calibration/check status within supplied procedures;
- identify drift, impossible values, stale data or non-representative placement;
- distinguish controller command from delivered physical condition;
- verify alarm and response behavior at the operator level;
- document data-quality limitations before using a value for crop decisions.

**Boundary:** The credential does not confer engineering authority to redesign control systems or defeat safeguards.

### 3. Equipment condition verification and bounded fault escalation

The Technician II can:

- inspect pumps, emitters, sensors, fans, lighting/control outputs and routine cultivation equipment for observable condition;
- perform permitted operator-level verification and care;
- isolate or place equipment out of service when the supplied procedure requires it;
- preserve asset/zone/time/fault evidence;
- determine when the condition exceeds operator authority and route it to maintenance or a qualified trade.

### 4. Fertigation batch execution and verification

The Technician II can:

- interpret an approved batch/work order;
- verify material identity, lot and quantity;
- follow the approved mixing sequence;
- measure and record volume, pH, EC/conductivity or other required batch parameters;
- compare actual with expected values and investigate obvious process errors;
- hold/escalate a batch when acceptance criteria are not met.

**Boundary:** Technician II does not independently invent nutrient recipes or override chemical-safety instructions.

### 5. Root-zone and irrigation trend troubleshooting

The Technician II can:

- interpret irrigation event history, delivery measurements, emitter/output checks, substrate/media context and root-zone trends;
- distinguish localized delivery faults from room-wide scheduling or demand issues;
- review dryback/moisture and root-zone EC trends within the facility method;
- determine what additional measurement is needed before changing the process;
- recommend or execute only changes authorized by the supplied procedure.

### 6. Advanced IPM trend interpretation and treatment follow-up

The Technician II can:

- compare compatible scouting rounds and trap/plant evidence;
- evaluate spatial spread, incidence/severity and crop injury;
- review authorized intervention timing and follow-up evidence;
- identify whether the evidence supports improvement, persistence, recurrence or uncertainty;
- prepare a qualified escalation when treatment selection or pesticide authority is outside scope.

**Boundary:** This credential does not confer pesticide recommendation or applicator authority.

### 7. Propagation performance troubleshooting

The Technician II can:

- compare donor/source lots, rooting/germination success, loss distribution and timing;
- review sanitation history, environment, substrate/root-zone condition and genotype timing;
- distinguish localized handling failure from broader process trends;
- identify the next useful verification step;
- preserve source/lot identity in corrective work.

### 8. Advanced canopy and flowering execution review

The Technician II can:

- interpret advanced work orders for pruning, canopy architecture, trellis/support and flowering-stage crop care;
- compare plant response across cultivar/stage/zone;
- review recovery, uniformity and within-canopy condition;
- identify when training intensity, plant health or reproductive findings warrant hold/escalation;
- document outcomes rather than relying on technique names alone.

### 9. Postharvest process/deviation investigation

The Technician II can:

- compare dry-room/rack/lot trends;
- review product-condition measurements, room history and process timing;
- identify within-load or spatial variation;
- reconstruct affected lot scope/genealogy;
- support hold, additional sampling or escalation decisions without assuming final disposition authority.

### 10. Traceability and inventory reconciliation

The Technician II can:

- compare physical reality with electronic/paper records;
- reconstruct plant/batch/lot genealogy and movement history;
- identify missing, duplicate or mismatched records;
- preserve the original record and route corrections through the approved process;
- prevent unsupported edits made only to force a numerical match.

### 11. Production metrics and trend review

The Technician II can:

- calculate and interpret approved operational metrics such as propagation success, plant loss, task completion, irrigation use, IPM trend, yield, dry-room exceptions and other defined KPIs;
- verify numerator/denominator and time window;
- distinguish normal variation from a change worth investigation;
- communicate limits when definitions or data collection changed.

### 12. Peer task support, shift coordination and manager-ready handoff

The Technician II can:

- demonstrate routine procedure-based work within assigned authority;
- identify execution inconsistency and explain the effective SOP/work order;
- communicate priority, unresolved risk and required next checks;
- support peer correction without changing controlled procedure;
- produce a concise evidence-based end-of-shift report for supervisor/QA/specialist decision making.

**Boundary:** Peer support does not confer personnel-management, disciplinary, formal trainer-signoff or final authorization authority unless separately assigned.

## Cross-cutting performance attributes

Score observable job behavior, not personality:

- evidence discipline;
- safe work and stop/escalate judgment;
- measurement quality;
- traceability;
- data integrity;
- uncertainty communication;
- procedure/version control;
- task prioritization;
- professional peer support;
- accurate handoff.

## Assessment-weighting rule

Final blueprint weights require cannabis-specific SME/employer task ratings plus assessment-validity considerations. Development ratings above are only one input. Weighting must also consider:

- breadth of each domain;
- safety/quality/traceability consequence;
- whether competence is better measured by written/scenario assessment or direct performance;
- overlap with practical/capstone evidence;
- pilot item performance and form construction needs;
- task frequency across different cultivation facility models.

## What Technician II does not certify

The credential does not independently authorize:

- staff hiring, discipline or performance management;
- cultivation-program ownership;
- pesticide recommendation/application outside separate legal qualification;
- electrical, HVAC, refrigerant, pressure-system or engineering work;
- unrestricted equipment repair;
- laboratory method approval;
- final product release/disposition;
- regulatory compliance sign-off;
- management of a department solely by credential possession.

## Formal validation still required

Before occupational-program approval:

1. recruit a representative panel of current cannabis cultivation SMEs/employers;
2. rate each task family independently for frequency, importance, criticality and essentiality;
3. identify facility-type/jurisdiction variation;
4. review the boundary between Technician II, Technician I, specialist roles and Cultivation Lead;
5. nominate missing cannabis-specific task families;
6. reconcile keep/adapt/reject decisions;
7. revise blueprint and practical/capstone coverage from the evidence;
8. record panel composition, method, consensus/disagreement and exact program version;
9. build the structured JTA evidence record using the repository JTA evidence workflow.

## Current design decision

This **12-domain model** is the Technician II development baseline. It converts the existing curriculum map into an explicit occupational analysis while preserving a strict boundary against supervisory, licensed, engineering and final-disposition authority.
