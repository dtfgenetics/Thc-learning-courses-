# THC Cultivation Technician I — Job-Task Analysis Baseline

**Status:** Research-informed draft; requires SME/employer validation before credential release  
**Credential:** `CREDPROG-CULT-TECH-I-001`  
**Architecture:** `docs/THC_ACADEMY_SYSTEM_ARCHITECTURE.md`

## Purpose

This job-task analysis (JTA) defines the occupational work that the THC Cultivation Technician I credential is intended to represent. It is the controlling input for the Technician I competency map, Learning Hub certification courses, course tests, practical assessments, capstone, and credential examination blueprint.

The goal is not to certify an independent grow manager. The target is a dependable entry-level commercial cultivation technician who can execute routine work under supplied SOPs/work orders, protect safety and crop integrity, make required observations and measurements, preserve traceability, maintain accurate records, and escalate conditions outside their authority.

## Evidence basis

The existing THC Academy Technician I curriculum/practical work was compared with current cultivation-technician job postings and current operational expectations. Recent employer postings consistently emphasize combinations of:

- propagation and transplanting;
- pruning, defoliation, trellising and routine canopy work;
- watering/irrigation and, in some roles, pH/EC measurement;
- daily crop-health and environmental observation;
- IPM scouting/reporting;
- harvest and postharvest support;
- sanitation/biosecurity;
- SOP execution;
- METRC or equivalent seed-to-sale/data tracking;
- plant tagging, batch identity, movement, waste/destruction records;
- basic irrigation/equipment care and prompt fault reporting;
- accurate task records and communication with leads.

Current employer examples consulted for this draft include:

## Rating method

Each task receives preliminary 1–5 ratings:

- **Importance (I):** contribution to successful Technician I job performance.
- **Frequency (F):** how commonly the task is expected in routine cultivation work; facility assignment can change frequency.
- **Consequence/Risk (C):** potential worker, crop, quality, traceability, compliance or operational impact of poor performance.

These are design ratings, not psychometric validation data.

## Detailed task statements

### 1. Safe work, PPE and stop-work/escalation

The Technician I can:

- inspect the assigned work area for obvious hazards before beginning work;
- select/use PPE required by the supplied task/SOP;
- recognize water/electricity, slip/trip, cutting-tool, ergonomic, heat, lighting/UV, compressed-gas/CO2, chemical and biological-exposure hazards within training scope;
- follow restricted-area and supplied emergency procedures;
- stop work and escalate conditions outside authorization or competence;
- document a safety concern or incident accurately.

**Boundary:** Technician I does not perform unauthorized electrical, HVAC, pressure-system or chemical/pesticide work solely because they can recognize the hazard.

### 2. Sanitation, biosecurity and controlled movement between zones

The Technician I can:

- follow clean-to-dirty or facility-defined movement rules;
- sanitize tools and work surfaces using supplied procedures;
- perform required glove/clothing/tool changes;
- preserve quarantine/isolation boundaries;
- recognize common contamination routes;
- complete sanitation records;
- identify a breach and escalate it.

### 3. Crop observation and routine scouting

The Technician I can:

- follow a repeatable crop-inspection route;
- distinguish observations from unsupported diagnoses;
- describe changes in color, vigor, structure, moisture appearance, development and visible damage using consistent language;
- record room/zone/plant location;
- capture useful photographs or samples when instructed;
- recognize a condition that requires immediate escalation.

### 4. Environmental monitoring and basic sensor reading

The Technician I can:

- read supplied temperature, RH, VPD, light/photoperiod and other routine dashboard values within the facility workflow;
- verify the basic context of a reading, including room/zone, time and sensor location;
- compare a reading with supplied targets/limits;
- recognize obvious outliers, alarms or non-representative measurements;
- record readings accurately;
- escalate rather than independently redesign environmental control strategy.

### 5. Water, nutrient-solution, pH and EC measurement

The Technician I can:

- identify the required sample type;
- perform permitted meter inspection/rinse/storage/calibration-check steps from supplied SOPs;
- obtain and record pH and EC/conductivity readings;
- preserve units and sample identity;
- recognize obvious measurement problems or outliers;
- repeat/verify within procedure before escalation;
- avoid treating one runoff/drain or spot reading as a complete diagnosis without context.

### 6. Routine irrigation/fertigation execution

The Technician I can:

- interpret an assigned irrigation/fertigation work order;
- confirm correct room/zone/material before execution;
- perform authorized hand watering or routine system operation;
- verify delivery at representative points when required;
- recognize leaks, clogged emitters, missed events, visibly abnormal saturation/dryness or other routine exceptions;
- make only permitted routine corrections;
- record completion and escalate unresolved deviations.

### 7. IPM scouting, documentation and escalation

The Technician I can:

- follow a supplied scouting route and inspection pattern;
- inspect leaves, stems, media/root-zone surfaces, traps and relevant room locations;
- recognize common signs of pest/pathogen/damage concern at the observation level;
- distinguish presence, incidence/severity observations and unsupported diagnosis;
- record location and evidence;
- follow supplied quarantine/escalation procedures.

**Boundary:** the credential does not confer pesticide-applicator authority or independent treatment-selection authority.

### 8. Propagation, transplanting and plant-identity protection

The Technician I can:

- prepare an assigned propagation/transplant work area;
- verify donor/source/lot/plant identity before handling;
- follow supplied seedling, cutting, rooting or transplant sequences within training scope;
- maintain sanitation checkpoints;
- preserve tray/lot/plant labels and counts;
- observe routine rooting/seedling conditions;
- document losses, exceptions and completion.

### 9. Routine canopy and crop-care work

The Technician I can:

- interpret the supplied plant-care work order;
- verify plant/room identity and inspect before beginning;
- perform authorized pruning, defoliation, trellis/support, spacing, training or similar routine tasks to the defined standard;
- protect sanitation between plants/areas;
- avoid unnecessary tissue damage;
- recognize plants/areas requiring hold or escalation;
- document completion.

### 10. Harvest and postharvest handoff

The Technician I can:

- verify harvest work order and lot/batch identity;
- prepare tools/area using supplied sanitation procedures;
- execute authorized harvest/transfer steps;
- protect material identity and contamination control;
- reconcile required counts/weights/movement information;
- inspect basic dry-room/postharvest conditions within assigned scope;
- complete an accurate handoff.

### 11. Traceability, plant/material movement, inventory and waste records

The Technician I can:

- preserve plant, batch, lot, room and material identity during assigned work;
- apply/verify supplied labels or tags within procedure;
- record authorized movements and transfers;
- distinguish physical reality from an incorrect/incomplete digital record and report the discrepancy rather than silently forcing a match;
- complete assigned inventory counts/reconciliations;
- follow supplied waste/destruction documentation workflow;
- use a facility seed-to-sale or internal tracking system at the technician level after platform-specific training.

**Design rule:** core instruction remains vendor-neutral. METRC or other platform-specific simulations can be added as implementation labs/endorsements.

### 12. Routine equipment care, setup checks and fault reporting

The Technician I can:

- inspect assigned tools/equipment for obvious readiness/cleanliness issues;
- perform permitted cleaning, filter/line checks, simple setup or other operator-care tasks explicitly covered by supplied procedures;
- recognize leaks, clogs, damaged tools, failed pumps/emitters, alarm conditions or obvious equipment-response problems;
- document asset/zone/fault context clearly;
- place equipment out of service when supplied procedure requires it;
- escalate faults without unauthorized mechanical/electrical repair.

### 13. SOP/work-order execution, records and shift handoff

The Technician I can:

- identify the correct controlled instruction/work order for the assigned task;
- execute required steps in sequence;
- record work contemporaneously and accurately;
- preserve corrections/history rather than obscuring an error;
- document deviations and incomplete work;
- summarize observations, unresolved issues, actions and priorities for the next shift/lead;
- communicate uncertainty without fabricating a diagnosis or completion record.

## Cross-cutting performance attributes

These are not separate cannabis-science domains, but they materially affect employability and should appear in practical scoring:

- attention to detail;
- reliability and task completion;
- safe physical work and ergonomic awareness;
- sanitation discipline;
- accurate data entry;
- communication and handoff quality;
- working within authority;
- ability to follow controlled instructions;
- recognition of uncertainty/abnormal conditions;
- professional response to correction/feedback.

Do not convert personality preference into credential discrimination. Assess observable job behavior only.

## Assessment weighting rule

Do not mechanically convert preliminary JTA ratings into final exam percentages. Use the ratings as one input alongside:

- breadth of the competency;
- safety/quality/compliance consequence;
- ability to measure the task validly in a written exam versus practical;
- SME/employer judgment;
- pilot item performance;
- redundancy with practical/capstone evidence.

The existing 72-item development form remains a useful pilot form. Its current 80% pass target is **provisional only** until formal standard setting.

## What Technician I does not certify

The credential does not claim that the holder can independently:

- design a cultivation facility;
- engineer HVAC/electrical/CO2 systems;
- diagnose every crop disorder;
- prescribe pesticide programs or act as a licensed pesticide applicator;
- create advanced fertigation/crop-steering strategy;
- lead a breeding program;
- release or dispose of regulated product without required authorization;
- supervise personnel merely by holding Technician I;
- override facility SOPs, law, licensing requirements or manufacturer instructions.

6. reconcile rating disagreements;
7. revise course and assessment weights;
8. document panel composition, method, decisions and version;
9. repeat validation periodically or after meaningful occupational change.

## Current design decision

The **13-task model is the Technician I development baseline**. It supersedes the older 12-domain model for new certification-course design, while preserving the older practicals and content that remain valid.
