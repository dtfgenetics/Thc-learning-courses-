# THC Academy V2 — Cultivation Technician I Curriculum Map

## Purpose

Cultivation Technician I is the first employment-facing THC Academy credential. It is designed for entry professional cultivation work where a learner must safely and consistently execute assigned crop-care tasks, collect and record basic data, recognize abnormal conditions, and escalate issues under facility SOPs.

This credential is not a duplicate science course. It composes existing THC Academy plant-science content with a new job-performance layer.

## Credential design rule

For every job task:

**Understand -> See -> Practice -> Decide -> Perform -> Document**

The learner should receive enough science to understand the task, examples that show correct and incorrect execution, guided practice, realistic decision scenarios, a practical or simulation where useful, and a record or work product that demonstrates the task.

## Recommended pathway

1. THC Safety, Compliance and Responsible Practice
2. THC Cultivation Foundations
3. Technician I Job Practice Units
4. Technician I knowledge and scenario assessment
5. Technician I integrated practical/simulation assessments
6. Technician I portfolio/capstone
7. THC Cultivation Technician I credential

Existing courses should be reused. Technician I adds employment practice rather than republishing the same plant-science lessons.

## New Technician I job-practice layer

The 12 areas above should be packaged as job-practice units. They can be short because they reference the canonical science lessons. Each unit should contain:

1. Role relevance / why the task matters
2. Prerequisite science links
3. Equipment/tools/materials overview
4. SOP/work-order example
5. Demonstration or visual sequence
6. Worked example
7. Common errors
8. Guided practice
9. Decision/escalation scenario
10. Job-task practical or simulation
11. Record/documentation exercise
12. Knowledge check
13. Portfolio artifact where useful

## Integrated practical assessments

Instead of 12 isolated final practical exams, group tasks into realistic workflows:

### Practical A — Safe room entry and crop inspection
Covers safety, sanitation/biosecurity, crop observation, environmental monitoring and recordkeeping.

### Practical B — Water and irrigation shift
Covers meter use, pH/EC interpretation, irrigation execution, root-zone observation and records.

### Practical C — IPM scouting route
Covers sanitation, scouting, image recognition, sample/photo documentation, escalation and records.

### Practical D — Propagation work station
Covers sanitation, plant identity, propagation sequence, environmental observations and propagation records.

### Practical E — Canopy maintenance work order
Covers safe work, sanitation, canopy task execution, crop observation and completion records.

### Practical F — Harvest-to-dry-room handoff
Covers harvest workflow, batch identity, traceability, postharvest handling, environmental checks and documentation.

## Capstone simulation — One cultivation shift

The learner receives a simulated shift containing:
- shift handoff
- room assignments
- environmental dashboard
- work orders
- irrigation information
- crop images
- scouting findings
- plant/batch identifiers
- a minor deviation
- harvest/postharvest handoff information

The learner must prioritize work, identify issues, complete tasks, escalate appropriately, and produce accurate records.

## Credential outcome

A learner earning Cultivation Technician I should be able to say:

> I can safely execute routine cultivation tasks under SOPs, monitor crops and basic environmental/root-zone data, perform sanitation, scouting, propagation, canopy, irrigation, harvest and postharvest support work, document what I did accurately, and recognize when a condition must be escalated.

That statement is the curriculum's employment promise. Every content and assessment object should contribute to proving it.
