# COURSE-LH-TECH1-001 — Safety, Responsible Practice & Cultivation Workflows

**Program:** THC Cultivation Technician I  
**Status:** Public academic curriculum blueprint  
**Course type:** Dedicated THC Learning Hub certification course  
**Credential program:** `CREDPROG-CULT-TECH-I-001`

## Course purpose

Prepare an entry cultivation technician to work safely and reliably inside a controlled cultivation operation: recognize hazards, follow supplied PPE/sanitation/biosecurity requirements, execute controlled SOPs and work orders, preserve plant/material traceability, perform permitted operator-level equipment care, document work accurately, and escalate conditions outside authority.

This is **not** one of the 420 Comprehensive Educational Resources. It is a purpose-built certification course that uses mapped resources as references and deeper study material.

## Occupational basis

Primary Technician I JTA domains:

1. Safe work, PPE and stop-work/escalation
2. Sanitation, biosecurity and controlled movement
11. Traceability, plant/material movement, inventory and waste records
12. Routine equipment care, setup checks and fault reporting
13. SOP/work-order execution, records and shift handoff

Supporting cross-links:

- crop observation for abnormal-condition escalation;
- irrigation/equipment context;
- harvest/propagation identity preservation;
- IPM quarantine and restricted-area awareness.

## Course boundaries

The course teaches recognition, routine execution, documentation and escalation. It does not authorize the learner to:

- perform electrical, HVAC, compressed-gas, pressure-system or engineering repairs;
- act as a licensed pesticide applicator or select pesticide treatments independently;
- enter restricted areas contrary to labels, regulations or facility procedure;
- override facility SOPs or jurisdictional requirements;
- alter controlled inventory records to conceal a discrepancy;
- perform regulated destruction, release or disposition without required authorization.

## Planned duration

Target instructional engagement: approximately **8–10 hours**, plus applied exercises and the integrated course practical. Final duration is set by pilot data rather than by word count.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the course, the learner should be able to:

- `LO-LH-TECH1-001-01` — Identify common cultivation workplace hazards and select the correct routine response: control, stop, isolate where authorized, or escalate.
- `LO-LH-TECH1-001-02` — Use supplied PPE requirements, labels and SDS information to prepare for an assigned cultivation task without exceeding training/authorization.
- `LO-LH-TECH1-001-03` — Execute sanitation and controlled-movement procedures that reduce cross-contamination risk between plants, rooms and work zones.
- `LO-LH-TECH1-001-04` — Recognize quarantine, restricted-entry, hold and isolation conditions and follow the supplied response procedure.
- `LO-LH-TECH1-001-05` — Identify the correct controlled SOP/work order for a task and distinguish required steps, acceptance criteria, exceptions and escalation points.
- `LO-LH-TECH1-001-06` — Preserve plant, batch, lot, room and material identity during routine cultivation movements and handoffs.
- `LO-LH-TECH1-001-07` — Complete routine inventory, movement and waste/destruction records accurately under a supplied vendor-neutral workflow.
- `LO-LH-TECH1-001-08` — Detect and report a mismatch between physical material and a digital/paper record without falsifying or forcing reconciliation.
- `LO-LH-TECH1-001-09` — Perform permitted pre-use/readiness, cleaning and operator-care checks on assigned cultivation/irrigation equipment.
- `LO-LH-TECH1-001-10` — Recognize obvious equipment faults, alarms, leaks, clogs or unsafe conditions and produce a useful escalation record without unauthorized repair.
- `LO-LH-TECH1-001-11` — Create contemporaneous, legible, attributable cultivation records and correct errors without obscuring the original history.
- `LO-LH-TECH1-001-12` — Produce a concise shift handoff that communicates completed work, observations, deviations, unresolved conditions and priorities accurately.

## Module structure

### Module 1 — Applied Cultivation Workplace Safety

**Planned module ID:** `MOD-LH-TECH1-001-SAFETY`

#### Lesson 1.1 — Hazard Recognition and Control Boundaries

Covers:

- hazard versus risk;
- routine pre-task hazard scan;
- hierarchy-of-controls awareness;
- stop-work/escalation logic;
- slips/trips/falls, sharp tools, lifting/repetitive work, wet environments and elevated work;
- water/electricity separation;
- why a technician recognizes hazards without becoming an electrician, HVAC technician or safety engineer.

#### Lesson 1.2 — PPE, Chemical Labels and Safety Data Sheets

Covers:

- task-specific PPE from supplied requirements;
- inspecting/donning/doffing/care at the level required for routine work;
- labels, signal words, pictograms and precautionary statements;
- locating and using relevant SDS information;
- chemical storage/handling boundaries;
- respirator and pesticide-handler authorization boundaries.

#### Lesson 1.3 — Cannabis-Specific Workplace Exposures and Emergency Escalation

Covers:

- CO2 and oxygen-displacement awareness;
- heat/humidity;
- high-intensity light/UV exposure;
- mold, microbial material, plant dust/organic particulate and allergen awareness;
- noise and ergonomic stress;
- fire/electrical concerns;
- symptom/incident reporting;
- emergency versus supervisor/maintenance escalation.

### Module 2 — Sanitation, Biosecurity and Controlled Movement

**Planned module ID:** `MOD-LH-TECH1-001-BIOSEC`

#### Lesson 2.1 — Contamination Routes and Zone Discipline

Covers people, tools, plant material, water, carts/containers, clothing/PPE and workflow as contamination routes; clean-to-dirty concepts; room/zone movement under supplied facility rules.

#### Lesson 2.2 — Tool, Surface and Work-Area Sanitation

Covers correct sequence, contact-time/label-following concepts, cross-contamination prevention, clean/dirty tool control, sanitation records, and why “looks clean” is not equivalent to a verified sanitation process.

#### Lesson 2.3 — Quarantine, Holds and Biosecurity Breach Response

Covers isolation boundaries, restricted movement, incoming material awareness, what to do after a suspected breach, and why unapproved movement can spread a facility-wide problem.

### Module 3 — SOPs, Work Orders, Authority and Escalation

**Planned module ID:** `MOD-LH-TECH1-001-WORKFLOW`

#### Lesson 3.1 — Reading Controlled Instructions

Covers current version, task scope, prerequisites, tools/materials, sequence, acceptance criteria, records, exceptions and revision awareness.

#### Lesson 3.2 — Working Within Authorization

Covers the difference between routine correction, hold/stop, escalation, maintenance referral, safety response and supervisor decision; avoids rewarding improvisation where a controlled instruction should govern.

#### Lesson 3.3 — Deviations and Incomplete Work

Covers recognizing that actual conditions differ from the work order/SOP, preserving evidence, documenting what happened, and communicating unresolved work instead of falsely recording completion.

### Module 4 — Traceability, Material Movement, Inventory and Waste

**Planned module ID:** `MOD-LH-TECH1-001-TRACEABILITY`

#### Lesson 4.1 — Identity Through the Cultivation Workflow

Covers plant, tag, room, batch, lot, material and container identity; why identity continuity matters from propagation through harvest/postharvest; chain-of-custody concepts at technician level.

#### Lesson 4.2 — Movement and Inventory Records

Covers physical movement versus digital/paper movement record, contemporaneous entry, room maps/counts, supplied seed-to-sale workflow, and vendor-neutral data principles.

#### Lesson 4.3 — Discrepancies, Waste and Destruction Documentation

Covers mismatch recognition, recount/verification within procedure, preventing silent reconciliation, authorized waste/destruction workflow, witnessing/approval boundaries, and escalation.

### Module 5 — Equipment Readiness, Operator Care and Fault Reporting

**Planned module ID:** `MOD-LH-TECH1-001-EQUIPMENT`

#### Lesson 5.1 — Pre-Use and Readiness Checks

Covers obvious tool/equipment condition, cleanliness, guards/connections where applicable, correct asset/zone identification, supplied pre-use checks and placing unsafe equipment out of service when instructed.

#### Lesson 5.2 — Routine Operator Care

Covers permitted cleaning, simple filter/line/emitter observations, irrigation leaks/clogs, housekeeping around equipment, and the boundary between operator care and skilled maintenance.

#### Lesson 5.3 — Alarms, Fault Context and Maintenance Escalation

Covers recording what happened, where, when, affected asset/zone, observed condition, alarm/status information, immediate safe actions taken and what remains unresolved.

### Module 6 — Records, Shift Handoff and Integrated Workflow

**Planned module ID:** `MOD-LH-TECH1-001-RECORDS`

#### Lesson 6.1 — Contemporaneous Records and Data Integrity

Covers complete/accurate/attributable/legible records, timestamps/units/identifiers, corrections that preserve history, missing data and why retrospective guessing is unacceptable.

#### Lesson 6.2 — Professional Shift Handoff

Covers completed versus incomplete work, crop/environment/safety observations, equipment faults, traceability discrepancies, holds/escalations and next-shift priorities.

#### Lesson 6.3 — Integrated Technician Workflow Case

Learner works through a supplied shift packet containing a work order, hazard, sanitation entry requirement, plant movement, inventory mismatch, equipment fault and incomplete prior-shift note. The learner must sequence actions, identify authority boundaries, document evidence and produce a handoff.

Course science/safety claims should resolve to controlled references. Initial authoritative anchors include:

## Course practical

**Planned ID:** `PRACTICAL-LH-TECH1-001-WORKFLOW`

Candidate receives:

- mock room/work-area map;
- work order and controlled SOP extracts;
- supplied PPE requirements;
- chemical label/SDS excerpts;
- sanitation entry procedure;
- plant/batch/tag list;
- movement request;
- inventory discrepancy;
- equipment-fault observation/alarm note;
- partial prior-shift handoff.

Candidate must:

- identify hazards and authority boundaries;
- choose the supplied PPE/entry requirements;
- sequence sanitation and work preparation;
- verify identity before movement;
- document movement and discrepancy correctly;
- determine whether equipment condition permits routine operator action or requires escalation;
- correct a sample record without obscuring history;
- produce an end-of-task/shift handoff.

### Critical-error concepts

The practical rubric should designate a small number of construct-relevant critical failures, such as:

- knowingly entering/continuing an explicitly prohibited unsafe condition;
- bypassing a supplied restricted-entry/quarantine instruction;
- intentionally falsifying identity, movement, inventory or completion records;
- performing an explicitly prohibited repair/application action rather than escalating;
- creating a plant/material identity loss that cannot be reconstructed.

Critical-error definitions require reviewer calibration before operational use.

## Course test blueprint

**Planned assessment ID:** `ASSESS-LH-TECH1-001-FINAL`

Recommended development form: **36 scored items**, with additional unscored pilot items permitted.

| Domain | Target scored items |
|---|---:|
| Safety, PPE, HazCom and stop-work | 9 |
| Sanitation and biosecurity | 6 |
| SOP/work-order/authority decisions | 5 |
| Traceability, movement, inventory and waste | 7 |
| Equipment readiness/care/fault escalation | 4 |
| Documentation and shift handoff | 5 |
| **Total** | **36** |

Target item mix:

- approximately 15–20% essential foundational knowledge;
- approximately 30% interpretation/sequence/record items;
- approximately 40–45% realistic decision scenarios;
- approximately 10–15% integrated case-set items.

Avoid trivia and unsupported universal rules. When a facility-specific procedure controls the answer, provide the relevant procedure/excerpt in the item.

## Passing-standard rule

Do **not** hard-code 80% as the final course cut score.

During development:

- collect pilot performance data;
- conduct structured item review;
- define critical-safety/record-integrity criteria separately from total score;
- use a documented criterion-referenced standard-setting process before production release;
- record the approved cut score/rule with the assessment version.

A course-test score alone does not award the Technician I professional credential.
