# Technician II Candidate Retest, Accommodation, Appeal & Security Draft

**Credential:** `CREDPROG-CULT-TECH-II-001`  
**Status:** development policy — human program, assessment, accessibility, privacy/security and organizational approval required before use

## Retest and remediation

- Remediation is required before retesting a failed credential domain, Practical A–G component or capstone component.
- A failed required practical must be repassed individually; compensatory averaging cannot erase a failed required component.
- Retests use an approved equivalent secure form or equivalent practical/capstone variant.
- A credential-blocking critical failure requires reevaluation of the affected domain/component after remediation.
- Final attempt limits, waiting periods and fee rules remain approval-dependent and are not invented in this draft.
- Prior-form exposure, remediation and retest decisions are private controlled records.

## Accommodations

Accommodations change access conditions without changing the occupational construct. Options may include extended time where speed is not the construct, accessible presentation, assistive technology, rest breaks, alternative input/output methods and physically accessible workstation arrangements when essential job-performance evidence is preserved.

Disability/medical documentation is separated from scoring. Assessors receive only the minimum approved administration instructions needed to deliver the accommodation.

## Appeals

A controlled appeal route must cover administration/technical irregularity, scoring or rubric-application error, accommodation implementation failure, identity/record mismatch, policy/procedure error and credential issuance/verification error.

Appeals do not authorize disclosure of secure answer keys, unreleased items/forms, other candidates' records or unrestricted assessor notes. The original record is preserved; the decision, evidence reviewed and authorized decision maker are recorded.

## Security incidents

Item/form compromise, impersonation, unauthorized assistance, evaluator conflict, altered evidence, answer-material leakage or credential-record tampering triggers a controlled hold/investigation process. Evidence must not be silently edited or deleted.

The process supports hold, investigation, documented disposition, candidate communication where appropriate, form/item quarantine, reassessment when justified and security escalation.

## Fairness boundary

Credential decisions must be based on observable requirements and controlled evidence, not personality preference or protected/sensitive characteristics. Automated signals remain reviewable and may not independently make final credential decisions unless specifically validated and approved.
