# Technician I Job-Practice Unit — Harvest Workflow

**Task:** `TASK-CULT-HARVEST-001`  
**Target proficiency:** Operational  
**Primary competencies:** `COMP-FLOWER-001`, `COMP-POSTHARVEST-001`  
**Prerequisite content:** `MOD-FLOWER-001`, `LESSON-FLOWER-MATURITY-003`, `MOD-POSTHARVEST-001`, `LESSON-POSTHARVEST-001`

## What the technician is responsible for

A Cultivation Technician I should be able to execute an assigned harvest while preserving plant/batch identity, cleanliness, traceability, quality, counts/weights and an orderly handoff into drying or the next approved postharvest stage.

The technician is expected to:
- verify harvest authorization, room, batch and destination
- confirm labels/tags/containers before material moves
- prepare clean tools, carts and work surfaces
- protect harvest sequence and lot identity
- cut/handle material according to SOP without unnecessary damage or contamination
- separate waste and production material correctly
- preserve tag/batch genealogy through movement
- record required counts, weights, time and destination
- recognize obvious quality or contamination concerns
- communicate deviations before product is mixed, moved or released
- perform a documented handoff to the drying/postharvest team

Technician I is not independently setting harvest date, final quality disposition or compliance policy. Those decisions belong to authorized cultivation/QA/compliance roles.

## Core job concepts

### Harvest authorization
Maturity observations can inform harvest decisions, but a technician should harvest the correct authorized material, not decide ad hoc that a plant “looks ready.”

### Representative maturity evidence
If the technician supports maturity checks, observations should come from representative floral tissue and repeated locations rather than one dramatic top flower or a fixed calendar week.

### Identity and genealogy
At harvest, plant identity becomes harvested-lot identity. The chain must remain intact through cut, movement, weighing, drying and storage.

### Clean handoff
Harvest is a transition between cultivation and postharvest. The handoff should include what material moved, when, where, under which ID, and any known condition or deviation.

## Routine harvest workflow

### 1. Verify authorization and scope
Confirm:
- room/zone
- batch/plant IDs
- cultivar
- harvest work order
- destination/dry room
- required containers/racks
- labeling/tag procedure
- required weights/counts
- sanitation and PPE

### 2. Prepare the work area
- clean/sanitize tools and contact surfaces
- stage labels/tags before cutting
- stage waste containers
- preserve aisle/access
- verify clean destination containers/racks

### 3. Maintain lot identity during cut and movement
Do not allow unlabeled material to accumulate. Keep harvested material associated with the correct plant/batch and destination throughout the workflow.

### 4. Handle material to preserve quality
Follow SOP for:
- whole-plant or branch cutting
- support/transport
- trim level if part of harvest
- contact surfaces
- compression/stacking limits
- exposure time before dry-room placement

### 5. Identify exceptions
Examples:
- visible mold-like growth
- unexpected pest pressure
- wet/dirty contact surface
- damaged or missing ID
- mixed material
- abnormal odor/condition
- destination not ready

Stop or isolate according to procedure before uncertainty spreads.

### 6. Record and hand off
Capture required:
- harvest date/time
- room/batch IDs
- count/weight
- destination
- operator
- start/end or movement time
- waste records as required
- condition/deviation notes
- receiving person/system confirmation

## Worked examples

### Example 1 — One top flower looks mature
A top flower shows advanced trichome change while lower representative flowers differ.

Technician should record representative observations if assigned, but not substitute one top sample for the authorized harvest decision.

### Example 2 — Missing label after cut
A branch pile has become separated from its identifying tag.

Stop the affected material from moving or mixing. Resolve identity from approved records or place it in the defined hold/escalation process.

### Example 3 — Dry room not ready
Harvest has begun but assigned dry-room racks are unavailable or unsanitary.

Do not improvise an undocumented storage location. Escalate before quality/traceability is compromised.

### Example 4 — Suspect contamination
Visible mold-like material is found on one plant.

Follow the facility's hold/isolation/escalation process. Do not mix the plant into normal harvested material.

### Example 5 — Weight mismatch
Recorded outgoing harvest weight does not match the receiving record.

Reconcile before closing the handoff rather than assuming one side is “close enough.”

## Common Technician I mistakes

- harvesting based on personal maturity preference rather than authorization
- staging labels after material is already cut
- mixing lots on carts or tables
- using dirty tools/contact surfaces
- stacking/compressing material excessively
- allowing suspect material into normal flow
- moving product to an undocumented location
- failing to record destination/time
- leaving weight/count mismatches unresolved
- treating handoff as verbal only

## Guided practice

1. Sequence an authorized harvest from room verification through dry-room handoff.
2. Review maturity images and identify why one sample is not representative.
3. Reconcile a mock harvest count/weight with a receiving record.
4. Classify harvest exceptions into continue, isolate/hold, or escalate.
5. Build a traceability chain from plant ID to harvest lot to dry-room rack.

## Decision scenarios

1. One plant appears immature but is on authorized work order: flag discrepancy rather than independently removing it from scope.
2. Missing tag on cut material: stop movement and resolve identity.
3. Suspect mold on one plant: isolate/escalate before mixing.
4. Dry room not ready: do not create an undocumented temporary location.
5. Cart is dirty: clean/sanitize before use.
6. Receiving weight differs from outgoing weight: reconcile before closing record.
7. Two cultivars are staged next to each other: maintain separate labeled flow.
8. Coworker wants to skip handoff record because receiving staff are present: complete required documentation.

## Documentation exercise

Complete a harvest-to-dry-room record with:
- authorization/work order
- room/batch/cultivar
- plant/lot IDs
- date/time
- operator
- quantity/count/weight
- destination/rack
- sanitation check
- waste record
- quality exceptions
- deviations/holds
- receiving confirmation
