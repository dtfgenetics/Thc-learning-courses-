# Technician I Job-Practice Unit — Sanitation and Biosecurity

**Task:** `TASK-CULT-SANITATION-001`  
**Target proficiency:** Operational  
**Primary competencies:** `COMP-SPACE-BIOSEC-001`, `COMP-IPM-001`  
**Prerequisite content:** `MOD-SPACE-BIOSEC-001`, `MOD-IPM-001`, `LESSON-IPM-BIOSEC-003`

## What the technician is responsible for

A Cultivation Technician I should be able to execute routine sanitation and biosecurity steps consistently enough that the worker does not become a pathway for pests, pathogens or contaminated material.

The technician is expected to:
- follow room-entry and room-exit sanitation procedures
- work from cleaner/lower-risk areas toward dirtier/higher-risk areas when directed
- sanitize or change tools according to the supplied procedure
- control movement of carts, containers, clothing and other potential fomites
- protect quarantine/isolation boundaries
- remove plant debris and manage standing water according to procedure
- maintain plant, tray, room and lot identity while cleaning or moving material
- document sanitation work
- recognize suspicious conditions and escalate them without spreading the suspected problem

## Why this matters

A pest or pathogen problem rarely moves by magic. It moves through pathways.

Common pathways include:
- incoming clones or mother plants
- reused pots/trays
- scissors and pruning tools
- carts
- clothing and gloves
- hands
- crop waste
- shared work surfaces
- standing water
- movement of workers from problem areas into cleaner areas

The technician's job is to break these pathways as part of everyday work.

## Key concepts in job language

### Quarantine

Incoming or suspicious plant material is temporarily separated so it can be observed or handled without immediately exposing the main crop.

### Sanitation

Cleaning and related practices reduce survival or transfer of harmful organisms. Sanitation is not just spraying a chemical; it includes removal of debris, clean workflow, tool control and correct contact/procedure when chemical sanitation is used.

### Fomite

An inanimate object that can carry biological material between locations. In cultivation, a cart, scissors, tray, glove or container can become a fomite.

### Clean-to-dirty workflow

When practical and specified by procedure, work begins with cleaner/younger/lower-risk material and moves toward higher-risk or known-problem areas. Reversing that direction can carry a problem into areas that were previously clean.

## Routine sanitation workflow

### 1. Know the room/zone status

Before entering:
- identify the assigned area
- check whether it has special quarantine/IPM status
- review required PPE/tool restrictions
- confirm entry/exit sequence
- know whether tools are dedicated to the room

Do not assume every cultivation room has the same traffic rules.

### 2. Prepare clean tools and materials

Before touching plants:
- use the correct clean/sanitized tool set
- verify containers/trays are suitable for the task
- keep clean material separated from used/dirty material
- prepare waste containers before the task starts
- maintain labels and identity controls

### 3. Enter according to procedure

Possible controls may include:
- hand washing or glove change
- footwear controls
- room-specific PPE
- tool check
- sign-in/log entry
- direction-of-travel restrictions

The exact procedure is facility specific. The transferable competency is executing the supplied procedure without skipping steps.

### 4. Work in the correct sequence

Within a room or route:
- avoid unnecessary movement back and forth between risk zones
- follow clean-to-dirty sequencing where applicable
- separate suspect plants/material
- do not place clean tools on contaminated surfaces
- avoid moving carts or containers between rooms unless procedure allows it

### 5. Manage tools during work

Follow the facility's rule for:
- between-plant sanitation
- between-row sanitation
- between-room sanitation
- dedicated tools
- required contact time for sanitizer
- rinsing/drying if specified

Never assume a quick wipe is equivalent to the stated sanitation procedure.

### 6. Remove debris and waste correctly

Plant waste can carry pest/pathogen material.

Good workflow:
- collect debris as work proceeds
- use designated waste containers
- avoid dragging waste through clean production areas
- clean spilled media/plant material promptly
- use defined waste routes

### 7. Manage standing water and wet surfaces

Standing water can be both a sanitation and safety concern.

The technician should:
- identify the source where possible
- follow cleanup procedure
- report leaks or recurring water accumulation
- avoid spreading water between zones with carts/tools

### 8. Exit and close the sanitation record

At completion:
- remove waste through the correct route
- clean/sanitize tools and surfaces as specified
- return or isolate tools correctly
- complete sanitation/checklist records
- record unusual findings
- communicate any open issue to the next role/shift

## Quarantine and incoming material

Incoming clones can look healthy and still carry early pest stages or latent disease.

Technician behavior:
- preserve source/lot identity
- place material in the assigned quarantine area
- avoid moving quarantine tools into production
- perform only authorized inspection/handling
- document observations
- follow release/escalation instructions

The Technician I does not independently decide that incoming material is “clean enough” to bypass quarantine.

## Worked example 1 — Tools moving from problem room to propagation

**Situation:** A technician finishes pruning in a room with a known pest issue and is assigned to propagation next.

**Weak response:** carry the same scissors and cart directly into propagation.

**Better response:** follow the facility's exit/decontamination/tool-change process before entering the cleaner propagation area.

**Reasoning:** tools, cart surfaces, gloves or clothing may carry material from the higher-risk room.

## Worked example 2 — Crop waste route

**Situation:** Waste from a problem room is routinely wheeled through propagation because that is the shortest path to disposal.

**Weak response:** accept the route because the waste plants are already cut.

**Better response:** use the designated waste route or escalate the workflow problem so higher-risk material is not unnecessarily transported through a cleaner area.

**Reasoning:** dead/cut material can still carry pests or pathogens.

## Worked example 3 — Suspicious symptoms during clean-room work

**Situation:** A worker finds a cluster of suspicious feeding marks in a room not currently flagged by IPM.

**Weak response:** carry several affected leaves to another room to ask coworkers what they think.

**Better response:** limit unnecessary contact/movement, document the location and observation and follow the supplied IPM escalation procedure.

**Reasoning:** diagnostic curiosity should not create a new contamination pathway.

## Common Technician I mistakes

- bringing incoming clones directly into production
- using the same scissors in multiple rooms without following sanitation rules
- treating gloves as permanently clean
- touching phones, door handles and plants without considering transfer pathways
- moving waste through clean rooms
- failing to preserve plant/lot identity during quarantine or cleanup
- using sanitizer without following the stated concentration/contact procedure
- assuming a visually clean tool is biologically clean
- ignoring standing water because it is “only water”
- cleaning only after a problem appears instead of following preventive routine

Using a supplied SOP, arrange:
- gross debris removal
- cleaner/sanitizer preparation
- tool treatment
- contact time
- rinse/dry step if required
- storage/return

Identify which steps are product- or facility-specific rather than universal.

## Guided practice 3 — Fomite hunt

Review a simulated room image and identify at least eight objects that could move biological material if uncontrolled.

For each object state:
- how it moves
- which zones it contacts
- what control reduces risk

## Decision scenarios

### Scenario A — Healthy-looking incoming clones

**Best response:** quarantine/inspect according to procedure rather than bypassing controls based only on appearance.

### Scenario B — Shared cart from known problem room

**Best response:** follow the cart decontamination/isolation procedure before moving it to a cleaner area.

### Scenario C — Used gloves touch a phone

**Best response:** recognize the phone as a potential transfer surface and follow glove/hand/device sanitation procedure before resuming plant contact.

### Scenario D — Standing water near drain

**Best response:** follow cleanup procedure and report the source/recurrence rather than repeatedly walking equipment through it.

### Scenario E — Suspicious plant in clean area

**Best response:** document, limit unnecessary movement/contact and escalate according to IPM procedure.

### Scenario F — Waste bag tears in hallway

**Best response:** control the spill area and use the defined cleanup/waste procedure rather than dragging the remaining material onward.

## Documentation exercise

- date/time

- sanitation method/product ID when applicable
- required concentration/contact information when supplied by SOP

- unusual observation/deviation
- technician identity/initials according to system design

The exercise should include one incomplete record for the learner to correct.
