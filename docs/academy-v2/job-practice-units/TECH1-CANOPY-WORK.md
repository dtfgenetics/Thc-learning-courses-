# Technician I Job-Practice Unit — Canopy Work

**Task:** `TASK-CULT-CANOPY-001`  
**Target proficiency:** Operational  
**Primary competency:** `COMP-CANOPY-001`  
**Prerequisite content:** `MOD-CANOPY-001`, `LESSON-CANOPY-001`

## What the technician is responsible for

A Cultivation Technician I should be able to execute assigned canopy-maintenance work consistently while preserving plant health, identity, cleanliness and the production objective defined by the work order.

The technician is expected to:
- verify room, batch/cultivar, stage and work order
- understand the intended canopy outcome before touching plants
- use clean tools and protect biosecurity
- perform assigned bending, support, pruning, leaf removal or lower-growth cleanup within SOP limits
- avoid unnecessary tissue damage
- preserve required nodes, branches and flower sites
- maintain aisle/access and support systems
- observe plant response and flag unexpected stress
- record completed work, exceptions and count/area coverage
- stop and escalate when plant condition or work-order ambiguity makes the task unsafe or destructive

Technician I executes a defined canopy strategy. Designing cultivar-specific training programs or optimizing yield architecture belongs in higher credentials.

## Core job concepts

### Structural objective
Canopy work should solve a defined problem such as excessive height, crowding, poor access, uneven branch position, support needs or removal of clearly designated lower growth.

### Least disruptive effective action
The technician should not remove or damage more tissue than needed to accomplish the assigned objective.

### Stage and recovery
Plant vigor and developmental stage matter. A task appropriate for healthy vegetative plants may be inappropriate during stress or a sensitive transition.

### Uniform execution
When a room work order specifies a technique, technicians should apply it consistently enough that plant-to-plant differences reflect crop biology rather than random operator variation.

## Routine canopy workflow

### 1. Verify the work order
Confirm:
- room/zone
- cultivar/batch
- stage
- target task
- tissue/height/branch criteria
- support material
- sanitation requirements
- stop/escalation conditions

### 2. Inspect before action
Look for:
- vigor
- wilting or severe stress
- damaged stems
- pest/disease concerns
- unusual architecture
- plants that clearly do not fit the work order

### 3. Prepare clean tools and support materials
Sanitize tools as required and stage ties, trellis/support or waste collection without blocking aisles.

### 4. Perform the assigned action
Examples:
- reposition branches
- install/support ties
- selective pruning
- remove specifically designated lower growth
- remove damaged/dead material per procedure
- maintain trellis alignment

Avoid turning the work order into a personal training experiment.

### 5. Preserve canopy and plant integrity
Watch for:
- excessive branch tension
- cracked stems
- accidental removal of target growth
- unsupported heavy branches
- uneven application among plants

### 6. Clean and reconcile
Remove waste, sanitize tools, restore aisle/access, record coverage and identify plants requiring follow-up.

## Worked examples

### Example 1 — Weak plant in a pruning room
Most plants are vigorous, but one plant is severely wilted.

**Weak response:** perform the same pruning because the room work order says all plants.

**Better response:** flag the plant and follow the exception/escalation path before adding stress.

### Example 2 — Branch under excessive tension
A tie would require bending a rigid branch beyond a safe angle.

Use a less aggressive position or escalate rather than forcing a break.

### Example 3 — Unclear removal threshold
The instruction says “clean lowers” but gives no defined height/node criterion.

Stop and clarify the work order. Do not invent a personal threshold that makes operator-to-operator results inconsistent.

### Example 4 — Post-task damage
Several stems are cracked after a crew task.

Document location/extent and escalate the pattern instead of treating it as normal training response.

### Example 5 — Support before flower weight
Trellis/support work should preserve intended branch position and access before weight makes late correction more damaging.

## Common Technician I mistakes

- training from habit instead of the work order
- using the same intensity on weak and vigorous plants
- removing tissue without a defined objective
- failing to sanitize tools
- forcing rigid stems
- leaving cut material in production areas
- blocking aisles with support materials
- failing to document exceptions/damage
- making cultivar-level strategy changes without authority
- assuming a “clean” looking canopy proves the job was successful

## Guided practice

1. Given a room photo and work order, mark branches/tissue that should and should not be manipulated.
2. Sort plant examples into proceed, modify within SOP, or escalate.
3. Sequence a canopy work order including sanitation and cleanup.
4. Compare two operators’ results and identify inconsistency against the work standard.
5. Complete a room coverage log with exceptions and follow-up plants.

## Decision scenarios

1. Weak plant in otherwise healthy room: flag and escalate before high-stress work.
2. Work order lacks removal threshold: clarify rather than guess.
3. Stem begins to split during repositioning: stop manipulation and follow damage procedure.
4. Pest sign found while pruning: protect biosecurity and escalate.
5. Support material blocks aisle: correct layout before continuing.
6. Coworker wants to remove extra leaves “because it looks better”: follow defined work order.
7. One cultivar is much shorter than others: do not force identical architecture if the work order does not call for it.
8. Waste is accumulating under benches: complete sanitation/waste removal as part of the task.

## Documentation exercise

Complete a canopy work record with:
- date/time
- room/zone
- batch/cultivar
- growth stage
- work order/task
- area/plant count completed
- sanitation confirmation
- exceptions
- damage observations
- follow-up plants
- operator/handoff notes

## Integrated practical mapping

**Practical E — Canopy Maintenance Work Order**

Candidate must interpret a supplied work order, inspect plants, select appropriate routine actions, maintain sanitation, avoid over-removal, identify stop/escalation conditions and complete the work record.

## Portfolio artifact

**Canopy Work Order Execution Report** including annotated before/after room diagrams, work coverage, exceptions and a short explanation of why actions matched the stated structural objective.

## Completion standard

The learner can execute a defined canopy-maintenance task consistently and safely, protect plant integrity and sanitation, recognize when the work order should not be applied blindly, and create a usable record of work and exceptions.
