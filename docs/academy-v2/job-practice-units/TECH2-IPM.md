# Technician II Job-Practice Unit — Advanced IPM Trend Interpretation

**Task:** `TASK-CULT2-IPM-001`  
**Target proficiency:** Diagnostic  
**Primary competencies:** `COMP-IPM-ADV-001`, `COMP-NUTRIENT-DIAG-001`, `COMP-PRO-QA-001`  
**Prerequisite:** Technician I IPM Scouting; `MOD-IPM-ADV-001`

## What changes from Technician I

Technician I scouts, records incidence/severity and escalates. Technician II must compare repeated rounds, evaluate whether the working diagnosis still fits the evidence, assess response after an authorized intervention, and define where follow-up is needed.

## Evidence model

Interpret together:
- incidence over time
- severity over time
- direct signs versus plant symptoms
- trap counts and trap locations
- spatial spread
- cultivar/stage pattern
- environmental conditions
- irrigation/nutrition context
- quarantine/movement history
- prior intervention and date
- biological-control release or treatment records where applicable

## Trend workflow

### 1. Establish baseline and sampling consistency
A change in counts is meaningful only if route, sample size, trap identity and observation method are comparable.

### 2. Separate diagnosis from response
A plant can improve slowly after pressure is reduced, and visible damage can persist after the causal pressure changes. Evaluate new growth, new signs, incidence and spread—not appearance alone.

### 3. Map spread
Distinguish stable local clusters from movement into new zones, intake/door associations, clone-source associations, irrigation-line patterns or room-wide progression.

### 4. Evaluate intervention follow-up
Ask:
- did incidence stop increasing?
- did trap trend change?
- are new signs still appearing?
- are new zones affected?
- did environmental or sanitation conditions also change?

### 5. Reconsider the working diagnosis when evidence conflicts
If an intervention aimed at one cause produces no expected change, or the spatial/temporal evidence does not fit, revisit competing explanations.

## Worked cases

### Case 1 — Severity stable, incidence rising
Each affected plant remains mildly symptomatic, but the number of affected plants doubles each round.

Interpretation: the problem is expanding despite low individual severity. Escalation priority increases.

### Case 2 — Old damage remains, new growth improves
Existing leaves retain damage but new growth is clean and trap counts decline after intervention.

Interpretation: persistent old symptoms alone do not prove current treatment failure.

### Case 3 — Trap count falls, crop signs expand
Sticky-card counts decline but direct signs on plants expand into new zones.

Interpretation: one indicator is not sufficient. Investigate placement, organism behavior, sampling and crop evidence.

### Case 4 — Problem follows incoming clones
Findings remain concentrated in a quarantined incoming clone batch.

Interpretation: preserve quarantine/genealogy context and do not normalize movement into clean production.

### Case 5 — Symptoms mimic nutrient stress
No direct pest sign is confirmed; symptoms follow one irrigation zone and root-zone data are abnormal.

Interpretation: maintain a plant-health differential rather than forcing an IPM diagnosis.

## Common Technician II mistakes
- comparing counts from different sampling effort without normalization
- calling treatment successful from one improved trap count
- calling treatment failure because old leaf damage remains
- ignoring new-zone spread
- continuing the same diagnosis when evidence no longer fits
- making unauthorized pesticide recommendations
- failing to connect plant-health findings to quarantine, environment or irrigation context

## Guided practice
1. Graph four scouting rounds and classify stable, expanding or resolving trends.
2. Normalize incidence when sample size changes.
3. Compare trap, crop and environmental evidence after an intervention.
4. Identify when the working diagnosis should be reopened.
5. Define follow-up sampling for a newly affected zone.
6. Produce a concise treatment-follow-up summary for an IPM lead.

## Documentation exercise
Produce an **IPM Trend & Follow-Up Report** including comparable sampling, incidence/severity, signs, trap data, spatial map, intervention date, post-intervention trend, contradictory evidence, next follow-up and escalation.

## Practical mapping
**Technician II Practical D — IPM Trend & Treatment Follow-Up** uses multiple rounds of records rather than a one-time identification exercise.

## Portfolio artifact
**Multi-Round IPM Trend Report** demonstrating evidence integration and post-intervention follow-up.

## Completion standard
The learner can interpret repeated scouting and intervention evidence, identify whether pressure is worsening or resolving, reopen a diagnosis when evidence conflicts, and provide an actionable follow-up record without stepping outside authorization.
