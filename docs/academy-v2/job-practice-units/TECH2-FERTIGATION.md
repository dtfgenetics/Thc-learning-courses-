# Technician II Job-Practice Unit — Fertigation Batch Execution & Verification

**Task:** `TASK-CULT2-FERTIGATION-001`  
**Target proficiency:** Operational with diagnostic verification  
**Primary competencies:** `COMP-WATER-QUALITY-ADV-001`, `COMP-IRRIGATION-ADV-001`, `COMP-NUTRIENT-DIAG-001`, `COMP-PRO-SOP-001`  
**Prerequisite:** Technician I Water/pH/EC + Irrigation; `LESSON-WATER-ALKALINITY-001`, `LESSON-WATER-SOURCE-002`, `LESSON-WATER-METER-003`, `LESSON-IRR-UNIFORMITY-001`

## What changes from Technician I

Technician I measures water and executes routine irrigation under an existing SOP. Technician II must execute an approved fertigation batch while checking whether the batch, source water, meters, recipe and delivery system behave as expected.

Technician II should be able to:
- verify source-water identity and current test context
- distinguish pH, alkalinity and EC questions
- confirm recipe version, material identity and batch volume
- follow the approved mixing sequence and safe-handling procedure
- calculate required additions from an approved recipe
- verify meter readiness before trusting a surprising result
- compare expected versus measured pH/EC and volume
- identify likely measurement, recipe, source-water or mixing causes of a mismatch
- document deviations without hiding or correcting records retroactively
- hold or escalate a batch when acceptance criteria are not met

## Workflow

### 1. Verify the work order
Confirm batch ID, recipe/version, target volume, source water, materials, lot IDs, equipment, required measurements and acceptance criteria.

### 2. Verify measurement readiness
Check meter identity, calibration/verification status, clean probe condition, temperature-compensation context where applicable and correct sample handling. A strange value should trigger verification before chemistry is changed.

### 3. Distinguish the water-quality question
- **pH**: current acid/base condition
- **alkalinity**: acid-neutralizing capacity and repeated buffering load
- **EC**: total ionic conductivity, not a nutrient-specific assay
- **source history/ion profile**: context for recurring water-quality behavior

### 4. Execute the approved recipe
Use correct material, sequence, volume and documentation. Technician II does not invent a new nutrient program during batch preparation.

### 5. Reconcile expected and actual values
Compare measured volume, EC, pH and other required values with the approved expectation. If the result is unexpected, ask whether the most likely issue is measurement quality, source-water change, incorrect material/quantity, incomplete mixing, volume error or process deviation.

### 6. Decide accept, verify, hold or escalate
Do not force every batch into range by undocumented adjustment. Preserve the evidence trail.

## Worked cases

### Case 1 — EC is unexpectedly low
The recipe and nominal batch volume appear correct, but EC is much lower than normal.

Strong response: verify meter performance, actual final volume, material identity/additions and mixing completeness before assuming the nutrient program itself is wrong.

### Case 2 — Same pH, different long-term behavior
Two source waters enter at similar pH, but one has much higher alkalinity.

Strong response: recognize that repeated buffering load can differ even when spot pH is similar. Do not treat pH as a substitute for alkalinity.

### Case 3 — Meter disagrees with a verified check
A handheld EC meter reads substantially different from a recently verified reference meter on the same sample.

Strong response: quarantine the suspect measurement from decision-making until the meter is cleaned, checked and verified.

### Case 4 — Wrong recipe version
The printed recipe is superseded by the current controlled version.

Strong response: stop and use the controlled version; document the discrepancy rather than proceeding because the old recipe is familiar.

### Case 5 — Batch was adjusted but not recorded
A coworker made an additional correction but did not enter it in the batch record.

Strong response: treat the batch history as incomplete, reconstruct the event honestly and escalate according to procedure. Do not invent a clean record.

## Common Technician II mistakes
- using pH as a synonym for alkalinity
- treating EC as proof of specific nutrient composition
- changing chemistry before verifying the meter
- ignoring final-volume error
- using an old recipe version
- making undocumented corrections
- assuming source water never changes
- troubleshooting the nutrient formulation before verifying mixing and measurement

## Guided practice
1. Reconcile an approved recipe against a completed batch record and identify three discrepancies.
2. Compare two water reports with similar pH but different alkalinity and explain operational consequences.
3. Calculate approved additions for a changed batch volume.
4. Diagnose an unexpected EC result using a decision tree.
5. Review meter verification records and decide which measurements are trustworthy.
6. Write an accept/verify/hold/escalate decision for four simulated batches.
