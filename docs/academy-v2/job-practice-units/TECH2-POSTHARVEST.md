# Technician II Job-Practice Unit — Postharvest Process Investigation

**Task:** `TASK-CULT2-POSTHARVEST-001`  
**Target proficiency:** Diagnostic  
**Primary competencies:** `COMP-POSTHARVEST-PROCESS-001`, `COMP-POSTHARVEST-QA-001`, `COMP-PRO-QA-001`  
**Prerequisite:** Technician I Harvest/Postharvest; `COURSE-POSTHARVEST-QA-001`

## What changes from Technician I

Technician I performs controlled harvest/postharvest work and reports exceptions. Technician II must reconstruct a postharvest deviation, determine the likely affected scope, distinguish transient from sustained process problems, and assemble evidence for QA disposition.

## Evidence streams

Use together:
- harvest/batch/lot genealogy
- rack/container location
- dry-room temperature/RH trend
- airflow/equipment events
- loading density and timing
- product-condition observations
- representative moisture/water-activity data when supplied
- handling/movement records
- sanitation records
- packaging/storage conditions
- alarms, maintenance and door/opening events

## Investigation workflow

### 1. Define the deviation precisely
Bad: “Dry room was bad.”

Better: “RH exceeded the approved range for 3.5 hours from 01:20–04:50 after dehumidifier fault; racks 7–12 contained Lots A and B during the full excursion, while Lot C entered after recovery.”

### 2. Establish timeline
Place environmental excursion, equipment status, lot movement and product observations on the same timeline.

### 3. Define affected scope
Do not automatically include every product in the facility or exclude lots because they look normal. Use genealogy, location and time in the process.

### 4. Evaluate representative evidence
Compare affected/unaffected racks, product-condition checks and process measurements. One dramatic sample should not define a whole lot without representative context.

### 5. Support hold/follow-up decisions
Technician II can recommend evidence-based hold/isolation or further checks under the procedure, but final product disposition belongs to authorized QA/management roles.

## Worked cases

### Case 1 — Sustained RH excursion
RH remains above the approved range for several hours while two lots occupy the room.

Strong response: verify sensor validity, reconstruct exact time/location exposure, identify the affected lots/racks and support a documented hold/follow-up rather than relying on visual appearance alone.

### Case 2 — Brief door-opening spike
RH spikes during a documented door-opening event and returns rapidly to baseline.

Interpretation: distinguish a short process transient from a sustained control failure. Document it in context.

### Case 3 — One rack remains wetter
One rack differs from others despite room-average conditions being acceptable.

Strong response: investigate local loading/airflow and representative product condition rather than changing the entire room immediately.

### Case 4 — Lot entered after recovery
A lot was moved into the room after the environmental excursion ended.

Strong response: do not include it in affected scope merely because it is now in the same room; use the timeline.

### Case 5 — Identity uncertainty
Two containers were moved and one label is unreadable.

Strong response: stop downstream combination/release and reconstruct identity using genealogy and movement records before proceeding.

## Common Technician II mistakes
- treating room average as proof every rack behaved the same
- defining affected scope without time/location genealogy
- using appearance alone as disposition evidence
- treating every transient as equivalent to a sustained excursion
- ignoring sensor/data-quality questions
- changing or backfilling records to make a deviation disappear
- making final QA release decisions outside role authority

## Guided practice
1. Reconstruct a dry-room excursion timeline from alarms and sensor data.
2. Map lots/racks against the excursion and define affected scope.
3. Compare local rack data with room-average data.
4. Identify missing evidence needed before disposition.
5. Separate immediate containment from root-cause hypothesis.
6. Draft a QA-ready deviation evidence summary.

## Documentation exercise
Produce a **Postharvest Deviation Scope Report** containing event statement, timeline, sensor verification, affected lots/racks, product observations, genealogy, immediate controls, missing evidence and recommended follow-up.

## Practical mapping
**Technician II Practical F — Postharvest Deviation & Lot Scope** requires reconstruction of a dry/storage-room event and defensible definition of affected product.

## Portfolio artifact
**Postharvest Deviation Investigation** with timeline, lot map, evidence table and QA handoff.

## Completion standard
The learner can reconstruct a postharvest process deviation, define affected scope from time/location/genealogy evidence, identify necessary follow-up and prepare a defensible QA handoff without exceeding disposition authority.
