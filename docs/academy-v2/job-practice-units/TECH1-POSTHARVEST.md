# Technician I Job-Practice Unit — Postharvest Handling

**Task:** `TASK-CULT-POSTHARVEST-001`  
**Target proficiency:** Operational  
**Primary competency:** `COMP-POSTHARVEST-001`  
**Prerequisite content:** `MOD-POSTHARVEST-001`, `LESSON-POSTHARVEST-001`, `LESSON-POSTHARVEST-DRY-002`, `LESSON-POSTHARVEST-STORAGE-003`

## What the technician is responsible for

A Cultivation Technician I should be able to support routine drying, handling, packaging-stage preparation and storage tasks while protecting identity, cleanliness, environmental control and product quality.

The technician is expected to:
- verify batch/lot and assigned postharvest stage
- confirm clean racks, containers, tools and work surfaces
- place and space harvested material according to SOP
- read and record dry-room temperature/RH and related observations
- recognize abnormal environmental trends or obvious product-condition concerns
- avoid over-handling, compression, cross-lot mixing and uncontrolled exposure
- perform assigned moisture/condition checks consistently
- preserve lot identity through movement and storage
- complete handoff and storage records
- escalate suspected contamination, abnormal moisture, room excursions, damaged packaging or identity uncertainty

Technician I supports a controlled postharvest process. Establishing final drying specifications, release criteria, laboratory disposition or long-term QA strategy belongs to higher-level roles.

## Core job concepts

### Controlled moisture removal
Drying is not simply waiting for material to feel dry. Temperature, humidity, airflow, flower density, load and time influence moisture movement.

### Uniformity and representative checks
Outer surfaces can appear dry while internal material remains wetter. Product condition should be checked using the supplied representative method.

### Environmental trend
One room reading may be less informative than the pattern over time. Technicians should recognize sustained excursions, repeated cycling or localized problems and escalate them.

### Product protection
Postharvest handling can introduce contamination, physical damage, excess compression, identity loss or uncontrolled moisture exchange if workflow is poor.

### Storage environment
Packaging and storage conditions continue to affect product quality after initial drying.

## Routine postharvest workflow

### 1. Verify lot and stage
Confirm:
- lot/batch ID
- cultivar
- source harvest record
- assigned room/rack/container
- current postharvest stage
- required environmental range/procedure
- sampling/inspection method
- next handoff point

### 2. Prepare clean receiving area
- inspect racks/containers
- clean/sanitize contact surfaces
- verify labels
- keep lots separated
- ensure airflow pathways are not blocked

### 3. Place material consistently
Follow SOP for spacing, rack density, hanging/placement and maximum stacking/compression.

### 4. Monitor environment and product condition
Record assigned:
- temperature
- relative humidity
- equipment/alarm status
- obvious airflow issues
- visual/odor condition
- supplied moisture/condition observations

### 5. Recognize abnormal conditions
Examples:
- sustained RH excursion
- one rack noticeably wetter than the room average
- condensation
- visible mold-like growth
- abnormal odor
- blocked airflow
- damaged label/container
- mixed lots
- unusually rapid surface drying

### 6. Preserve identity during movement
Every move from rack to container, curing/storage vessel or next process must maintain lot identity and record destination/time.

### 7. Complete handoff
Document what moved, quantity, condition, destination, environmental status and unresolved deviations.

## Worked examples

### Example 1 — Surface feels dry
Outer flower surfaces feel dry but stems/interior material remain wetter.

Do not assume the entire lot is ready for packaging. Continue the supplied representative assessment and record the condition.

### Example 2 — One rack differs
One rack remains much wetter than surrounding racks.

Inspect the local airflow/loading context and escalate the localized pattern rather than changing the entire room immediately.

### Example 3 — Room RH excursion
RH is above the supplied range for a sustained period.

Verify the reading, check known equipment/alarm status, record the excursion and follow escalation procedure. Do not simply ignore it because product “looks fine.”

### Example 4 — Damaged label
A container label becomes unreadable during transfer.

Stop movement and resolve identity before combining or storing the material.

### Example 5 — Compression
Material is being packed tightly to save rack space.

Follow density/spacing SOP. Space savings do not justify impaired airflow or unnecessary physical damage.

## Common Technician I mistakes

- judging readiness from touch at one surface only
- treating drying as a fixed number of days
- recording room averages but missing localized rack problems
- overpacking racks/containers
- mixing lots during movement
- ignoring unreadable or detached labels
- handling material unnecessarily
- leaving environmental excursions undocumented
- moving suspect material into normal storage
- failing to record final destination/handoff

## Guided practice

1. Review a 24-hour dry-room chart and identify sustained vs short transient excursions.
2. Compare five rack observations and identify the localized outlier requiring follow-up.
3. Sequence a postharvest receiving and movement workflow with correct traceability points.
4. Classify product-condition examples into normal monitoring, hold/isolate, or escalate.
5. Complete a dry-room handoff using supplied harvest and environmental records.

## Decision scenarios

1. Outer material feels dry but internal check remains wetter: continue controlled assessment; do not package by surface feel alone.
2. One rack has condensation: isolate concern and escalate local environment/loading issue.
3. Room RH spikes briefly during door opening then recovers: document/interpret in context rather than treating every transient as sustained failure.
4. RH remains high across multiple readings: verify and escalate sustained excursion.
5. Label falls off one container: stop movement and resolve identity.
6. Two lots are about to share an unlabeled cart: separate and label before movement.
7. Worker compresses flowers tightly to make room: follow spacing/density SOP.
8. Visible mold-like growth appears: isolate/hold and escalate according to procedure.

## Documentation exercise

Complete a postharvest process record with:
- batch/lot ID
- source harvest record
- room/rack/container
- date/time
- temperature/RH observations
- product-condition observations
- movement/handling events
- quantity
- sanitation confirmation
- deviations/holds
- final destination/handoff
