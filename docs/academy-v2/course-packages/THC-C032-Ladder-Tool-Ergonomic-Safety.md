## Course purpose

This course prepares cultivation learners to make safe access, tool, and manual-handling decisions in real production spaces. It treats safety as a task-design and equipment-condition problem rather than a list of slogans. Learners practice selecting an access method, inspecting and controlling ladder use, selecting and inspecting tools, managing tool use around water/electrical systems, recognizing ergonomic exposure patterns, using the Revised NIOSH Lifting Equation within its intended scope, and redesigning repetitive work.

The course does not authorize learners to perform electrical work outside their role, replace equipment-specific instructions, or treat a generic course rule as a substitute for current workplace/jurisdiction requirements.

## Learning objectives

Learners will be able to:
- select, inspect, position, use, and remove ladders from service based on task and condition;
- evaluate hand and portable powered tools for condition, intended use, guarding/control, work-zone interaction, and energy hazards;
- identify force, repetition, posture, reach, duration, grip/coupling, contact stress, work-height, and recovery factors;
- distinguish lifting tasks appropriate for RNLE analysis from pushing, pulling, carrying, one-handed, unstable, or otherwise out-of-scope handling;
- propose equipment, layout, workflow, and escalation controls and define how improvement will be verified.

## Learner worksheet

### Part A — Access-method decision
For one elevated task, record:
1. target location and required work position;
2. task duration;
3. tools/materials needed;
4. whether one or both hands are needed for work;
5. floor/surface condition;
6. nearby traffic, doors, benches, irrigation, and equipment;
7. selected access method;
8. the condition that would make you stop and choose another method.

### Part B — Ladder inspection
Record Acceptable / Correct Before Use / Remove From Service for:
- rails;
- steps/rungs;
- feet;
- locking/spreader components;
- fasteners;
- labels/capacity information;
- contamination/traction;
- visible repairs or modifications.

Then document disposition, equipment ID, who was notified, and where defective equipment was moved.

### Part C — Tool pre-use decision
For one hand tool and one portable powered tool, record:
- intended task;
- condition;
- guard/control status where applicable;
- accessory fit;
- cord/plug/hose/battery condition as applicable;
- workpiece control;
- operating path;
- nearby people/crop/equipment/water;
- shutdown method;
- stop-work trigger.

### Part D — Ergonomic exposure observation
Observe one task and rate or describe:
- force;
- repetition;
- posture;
- horizontal reach;
- working height;
- duration;
- grip/coupling;
- contact stress;
- push/pull or carry demand;
- recovery/task variation.

Propose one design/equipment control, one workflow control, and one verification measure.

### Part E — RNLE scope check
For a defined lift, list the RNLE input categories: load weight, horizontal hand location, vertical hand location, vertical travel, asymmetry, frequency, duration/rest, and coupling quality. State whether the task is actually within RNLE scope. If not, state why and what other ergonomic assessment or escalation is needed.

## Glossary

- **Access method:** Equipment or method used to reach and work at an elevated location.
- **Asymmetry:** Rotation or displacement away from a straight-ahead lifting position.
- **Composite Lifting Index (CLI):** RNLE output used when a job contains multiple lifting tasks.
- **Coupling:** Quality of the hand-to-object grip during a lift.
- **Critical error:** A defined unsafe action that fails the practical regardless of total points.
- **Ergonomics:** Designing work to reduce injury/discomfort risk by fitting tasks, tools, and environments to people.
- **Guard:** Physical feature intended to reduce access to a hazardous moving-tool area.
- **Lifting Index (LI):** RNLE ratio describing physical demand of a single lift relative to the calculated recommended limit.
- **Pre-use inspection:** Check performed before equipment use to identify unsafe condition or configuration.
- **Recommended Weight Limit (RWL):** Principal calculated load output of the RNLE for a defined lifting task.
- **Remove from service:** Make defective equipment unavailable until corrected through the authorized process.
- **Task rotation:** Deliberate movement between tasks with meaningfully different exposure demands.
- **Working position:** Body/tool position required to complete the task.

## Instructor delivery guide

### Before class
- Verify training equipment is de-energized or appropriately controlled.
- Include at least one clearly defective ladder and one safe ladder or high-quality simulation cards.
- Include safe and unsafe tool-condition examples without exposing learners to an active hazard.
- Prepare a cultivation floor map showing doors, irrigation, drains, carts, benches, and electrical equipment.
- Prepare one defined two-handed lift that can be discussed with RNLE variables and one out-of-scope push/pull/carry task.

### Facilitation emphasis
- Ask learners to explain **why** a task method is acceptable, not merely name a rule.
- Reward stop-work/reposition/redesign decisions.
- Do not present a universal maximum lifting weight.
- Distinguish worker-dependent controls from equipment/layout changes.
- Repeatedly connect tool condition to the surrounding cultivation environment.

### Discussion cases
1. A task is technically reachable from a ladder but requires repeated lateral work. What changes the access decision?
2. A damaged extension cord still powers the tool. Why is function not proof of safe condition?
3. A low-weight tote is lifted 300 times per shift from floor height. Which variables matter besides mass?
4. A team rotates between two repetitive hand-intensive tasks. When is rotation not meaningful recovery?
5. A sensor reset requires daily ladder use. What design change could remove the recurring exposure?

## Visual-production briefs

1. **Access Method Decision Tree** — Task duration, reach, hand requirements, load, surface, traffic, and access-method choice.
2. **Ladder Pre-Use Inspection Map** — Rails, steps, feet, spreaders/locks, fasteners, labels, contamination, defects.
3. **Ladder Setup Zone** — Floor, doorway, cart lane, irrigation, barrier, worker position, overhead equipment.
4. **Stable Reach vs Overreach** — Side-by-side safe repositioning and unsafe lateral reach.
5. **Hand Tool Task-Fit Matrix** — Cut, grip, fasten, pry, adjust; show why intended tool matters.
6. **Portable Power-Tool Pre-Use Flow** — Condition → guard/control → accessory → work zone → energy → shutdown.
7. **Water + Portable Electrical Equipment Map** — Drains, irrigation, cord routing, pinch points, approved-location decision.
8. **Ergonomic Exposure Wheel** — Force, repetition, posture, reach, duration, coupling, contact stress, recovery.
9. **RNLE Variable Diagram** — Weight, H, V, travel, asymmetry, frequency, duration/rest, coupling; clearly marked 'for applicable two-handed lifting tasks.'
10. **Hierarchy of Task Redesign Examples** — Relocate, raise/lower, reduce reach, improve container/handle, cart/lift assist, workflow change.
