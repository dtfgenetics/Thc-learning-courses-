# Technician I Capstone — One Cultivation Shift

**Credential:** THC Cultivation Technician I  
**Mode:** integrated digital simulation, instructor-run tabletop/lab, or approved workplace equivalent  
**Recommended duration:** 120–180 minutes for a full simulation form

## Purpose

The capstone tests whether the learner can combine Technician I competencies under realistic shift conditions. The learner is not asked to design a facility or act as cultivation director. The goal is to demonstrate reliable routine execution, observation, prioritization, documentation, traceability and escalation across a normal cultivation workday.

## Competencies integrated

The capstone draws from all Technician I job tasks:
- workplace safety
- sanitation/biosecurity
- crop observation
- environmental monitoring
- pH/EC measurement
- irrigation execution
- IPM scouting
- propagation
- canopy work
- harvest
- postharvest
- SOPs/records/handoff

A single capstone form does not need to make the candidate physically perform every task. It should require decisions/evidence across all domains and deep execution in several assigned workstations.

## Candidate opening packet

At shift start, the candidate receives:
- facility/room map
- shift schedule
- previous-shift handoff
- assigned SOP set
- work orders
- current room dashboards
- crop/batch/cultivar IDs
- irrigation and scouting records
- propagation status
- harvest/postharvest schedule
- escalation contacts/role boundaries
- blank shift log

## Shift structure

### Phase 1 — Prioritize the shift

Candidate must review the handoff and work orders, identify:
- immediate safety issue
- time-sensitive crop task
- routine work
- issue that can wait
- task requiring clarification before execution

The simulation should reward correct prioritization rather than simply completing screens in a fixed order.

### Phase 2 — Safe room entry and crop inspection

Candidate enters a production room and encounters a rotating combination of:
- entry hazard
- sanitation decision
- crop observation
- environmental reading
- questionable sensor/location issue
- plant or zone requiring follow-up

Candidate creates a usable inspection record.

### Phase 3 — Water and irrigation work

Candidate receives a solution/irrigation task requiring:
- meter/calibration awareness
- pH/EC recording
- work-order verification
- irrigation execution
- localized delivery fault or dryback anomaly
- actual-versus-planned documentation

### Phase 4 — IPM scouting event

Candidate follows a route through supplied images/data and must:
- distinguish symptom vs direct sign
- calculate/interpret basic incidence
- identify spatial pattern
- compare prior rounds
- protect biosecurity
- escalate correctly

### Phase 5 — Production work order

One form assigns **Propagation** and another assigns **Canopy** as the primary production workstation. The non-primary domain still appears as a shorter decision scenario.

Propagation form may include:
- donor acceptance issue
- sanitation sequence
- label/tray identity
- rooting performance/count reconciliation

Canopy form may include:
- stressed plant exception
- ambiguous removal threshold
- tissue-damage stop condition
- work completion record

### Phase 6 — Harvest/postharvest transition

Candidate receives an authorized harvest/handoff scenario with:
- plant/batch identity
- destination
- sanitation check
- contamination or missing-label exception
- outgoing/receiving quantity reconciliation
- dry-room environment/product-condition data

### Phase 7 — Dynamic event inject

Mid-shift, introduce one unexpected event selected from a bank:
- sensor alarm
- leak/wet floor
- broken tool or damaged cord
- pest finding
- missing label
- irrigation-delivery failure
- propagation count shortage
- dry-room RH excursion
- handoff discrepancy

Candidate must determine:
1. stop/continue/hold
2. immediate safe action
3. documentation
4. escalation
5. downstream work affected

### Phase 8 — End-of-shift reconciliation and handoff

Candidate must close the shift with:
- completed tasks
- incomplete tasks
- measurements
- deviations/exceptions
- plant/batch/lot movements
- holds or follow-up
- next-shift priorities

The handoff should be understandable to a person who did not participate in the simulation.

## Evidence captured

The capstone should generate an auditable evidence package:
- task decisions and timestamps
- work-order selections
- measurement entries
- crop/scouting observations
- annotated maps/images
- traceability events
- deviations and escalation decisions
- practical outputs
- final shift log
- final handoff

Future runtime versions can store an event stream so the credential can show what competencies were demonstrated without exposing secure answer keys.

## Scoring model — 200 points

- safety and biosecurity: 30
- crop observation/environment: 25
- water/irrigation: 25
- IPM scouting: 25
- propagation/canopy production execution: 25
- harvest/postharvest/traceability: 25
- documentation/data integrity: 25
- prioritization, escalation and handoff: 20

**Development target pass:** 160/200 (80%).

The 160-point target is provisional until formal standard-setting/decision-rule approval. A numerical target by itself is not evidence that the cut score is valid.

## Development critical-failure rules

The controlled integrated-lab plan currently defines five behaviors that block acceptance of credential evidence for the affected performance until remediation and reevaluation occur:

1. **`CF-SAFETY-001`** — knowingly continue through an unresolved life-safety or serious uncontrolled hazard when stop/emergency procedure is required.
2. **`CF-IDENTITY-001`** — knowingly move, merge, relabel or transform material after an unresolved identity conflict in a way that breaks genealogy.
3. **`CF-INTEGRITY-001`** — falsify, overwrite or force a measurement, count, weight or record to hide a discrepancy.
4. **`CF-AUTHORITY-001`** — perform an unauthorized pesticide/treatment selection, technical repair/bypass or product-release decision outside supplied Technician I authority.
5. **`CF-HOLD-001`** — knowingly move or release material from an active contamination/quality hold without the required authorization.

These are development governance rules. Their final wording and operation require technical review, evaluator calibration, pilot evidence and credential decision-rule approval. Evaluators must not invent additional critical-failure rules during administration.

## Form generation

Build multiple equivalent capstone forms by varying:
- cultivar/batch names
- room maps
- numerical readings
- work-order details
- symptom/pest images
- problem locations
- dynamic events
- primary production workstation
- harvest/postharvest exception

The underlying competency blueprint stays constant while surface details change.

## Difficulty progression

### Practice mode
- hints available
- immediate feedback
- unlimited reset
- evidence shown after decisions

### Readiness mode
- limited hints
- feedback after each workstation
- score and competency breakdown

### Credential mode
- no answer hints
- feedback after submission
- randomized equivalent form
- complete event/evidence record
- secure credential form required
- evaluator/critical-failure controls required

Credential mode must remain disabled until the controlled lab plan records secure/live credential-form approval after all required release gates.

## Employer-facing output

The public credential should not reveal test questions. It can report validated capability areas such as:
- safe cultivation work
- routine crop inspection
- environmental-data interpretation
- pH/EC and irrigation work
- IPM scouting
- propagation/canopy execution
- harvest/postharvest traceability
- production documentation and shift handoff

## Portfolio artifact

The learner receives a sanitized **Cultivation Shift Performance Summary** containing approved fields such as:
- competency results
- approved practical artifacts
- sample work records
- scenario categories completed
- capstone status/score disclosure only as permitted by the final privacy/credential policy
- portfolio links selected by the learner

The public/portfolio artifact must not expose secure questions, answer keys, protected event logic, evaluator private notes or accommodation information.
