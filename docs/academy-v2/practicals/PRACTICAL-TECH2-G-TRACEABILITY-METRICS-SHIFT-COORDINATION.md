# Technician II Practical G — Traceability, Metrics & Shift Coordination

## Purpose
Demonstrate that the candidate can reconcile cultivation records with physical reality, calculate and interpret production metrics, identify record/process discrepancies, support a peer task correction, and deliver a concise supervisor-ready shift handoff.

## Tasks and competencies
- `TASK-CULT2-TRACE-001`
- `TASK-CULT2-METRICS-001`
- `TASK-CULT2-PEER-001`
- `COMP-CROP-PLAN-001`
- `COMP-PRO-SOP-001`
- `COMP-PRO-QA-001`

## Assessment format
The case combines a physical/electronic reconciliation problem with a small production dataset and one peer-execution issue. The candidate is expected to connect record accuracy to operational decisions rather than treat traceability, metrics and handoff as separate paperwork exercises.

Recommended assessment time: 60–75 minutes.

## Candidate receives
- plant/batch/lot or work-order records
- physical count/status/location sheet
- movement/task history
- selected source records with one or more discrepancies
- production dataset covering at least two periods or comparable rooms/batches
- approved metric definitions
- shift priorities and unfinished work
- a peer task scenario involving an execution inconsistency
- applicable SOP/work instruction and escalation route

## Candidate must perform
### Part 1 — Reconciliation
1. Confirm the population and time boundary being reconciled.
2. Compare physical count/status/location with the electronic or source record.
3. Classify each mismatch: missing record, duplicate, wrong location, wrong status, count mismatch, timing mismatch, identity mismatch, or unresolved.
4. Trace the discrepancy backward through movement/work history without overwriting source evidence.
5. Identify the most likely break point and the next record/person/system evidence needed.
6. Prepare a correction/escalation request that states the observed mismatch and supporting evidence.

### Part 2 — Production metrics
The candidate calculates at least four supplied metrics, including:
- propagation success rate
- plant-loss rate
- work-order/task completion rate
- irrigation or resource use per defined production unit
- IPM incidence trend
- harvest yield per defined area or plant count when the case supplies compatible data
- dry-room exception rate
- rework/deviation rate

For each metric, the candidate must state:
- numerator and denominator
- time/population boundary
- result and units
- comparison baseline
- operational interpretation
- one limitation or confounder that could change the conclusion

### Part 3 — Peer support and shift handoff
1. Observe a simulated peer execution error or review a scenario describing one.
2. Identify the exact work instruction or quality expectation that is not being met.
3. Explain or demonstrate the correct routine task step without changing the governing procedure.
4. Confirm understanding by asking the peer to restate or demonstrate the corrected step.
5. Escalate repeated, unsafe, unauthorized, or unresolved performance issues rather than hiding them.
6. Build the end-of-shift handoff in priority order: immediate risk, unresolved deviation, crop/process status, completed work, pending work, evidence location, next action, owner.

## Required calculations
Every form must include:
- at least two percentages
- one rate normalized to a stated unit or population
- one period-to-period or group-to-group comparison

Common formulas:
- success rate = successful units / total started × 100
- loss rate = lost units / population at risk × 100
- completion rate = completed scheduled tasks / scheduled tasks × 100
- exception rate = exception count / relevant processed units × 100
- percent change = (new − old) / old × 100 when the old value is nonzero and the comparison is meaningful

Candidates must identify when changing denominators make a simple percentage comparison misleading.

## Assessor injects
After the candidate has calculated the initial metrics, provide one of the following:
- a late-entered movement record resolves part of the discrepancy
- the denominator changed between reporting periods
- one batch had a different crop duration or plant count
- a task was marked complete electronically but physical verification shows it incomplete
- the peer reports following an outdated printed work instruction

The candidate must update the reconciliation, metric interpretation, or handoff accordingly.

## Scoring rubric — 100 points
| Domain | Points | Full-credit evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Reconciliation method | 20 | Correct population boundary, mismatch classification and source tracing |
| Record integrity | 10 | Preserves source evidence and uses traceable correction path |
| Metric calculations | 20 | Correct formulas, denominators, units and arithmetic |
| Metric interpretation | 15 | Explains operational meaning, trend and limitations |
| Peer task support | 10 | Specific SOP-based correction and verification of understanding |
| Prioritization | 10 | Orders issues by risk, urgency and process impact |
| Shift handoff | 10 | Concise status, evidence, ownership and next action |
| Professional clarity | 5 | Distinguishes fact, assumption and unresolved question |

**Pass standard:** 80/100 and no critical error.

## Critical errors
- knowingly creating or approving a false traceability correction
- deleting/overwriting source evidence to force reconciliation
- using a materially wrong denominator after the issue is apparent and presenting the metric as valid
- concealing an unresolved discrepancy from the next shift
- directing a peer to ignore a governing work instruction rather than following the correction/escalation process

## Evidence produced
- Traceability Reconciliation
- discrepancy investigation trail
- KPI calculation sheet
- KPI interpretation summary
- peer-support observation note
- Senior Technician Shift Handoff

## Assessor closeout questions
1. Which discrepancy is most operationally important and why?
2. Which metric is most likely to mislead if its denominator changes?
3. What evidence would you preserve before requesting a record correction?
4. What are the first three things the next shift must know?
