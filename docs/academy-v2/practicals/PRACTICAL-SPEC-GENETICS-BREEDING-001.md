# Advanced Genetics and Breeding Specialist Practical

**Credential:** THC Advanced Genetics and Breeding Certificate  
**Competencies:** `COMP-BREED-POP-001`, `COMP-BREED-GENOMICS-001`

## Purpose

Assess whether the candidate can move from pedigree and population evidence to a defensible breeding decision without confusing phenotype, genotype, fixation, heritability, marker association or commercial naming.

## Scenario

The candidate receives a multi-generation breeding case containing parental records, family sizes, phenotype measurements, environmental blocks, selection notes, progeny performance and a limited marker dataset. One family contains an exceptional individual whose progeny segregate widely. Another family is more uniform but shows different ranking in a second environment. A marker is associated with the target trait in one family but has not been validated in unrelated germplasm.

## Candidate tasks

1. Reconstruct the pedigree and flag any identity gaps or ambiguous labels.
2. Distinguish observed phenotype from inferred genetic value.
3. Calculate selection proportions for supplied families.
4. Compare family means, within-family variation and progeny consistency.
5. Explain what the observed segregation does and does not establish about fixation.
6. Rank candidate parents using stated breeding objectives and evidence.
7. Compare performance across environments and identify possible genotype-by-environment interaction.
8. Decide whether additional progeny testing, replicated trials or population size is needed.
9. Evaluate the marker association and define the evidence needed before broader marker-assisted use.
10. Propose the next generation/crossing or selection step with a clear objective and traceable identity plan.
11. Produce a breeding decision report that separates established evidence from inference.

## Evidence packet

- parental and generation pedigree records
- family sizes and selection counts
- phenotype tables from at least two environments
- progeny summaries
- source/lot identifiers
- trait-scoring definitions
- marker association summary
- missing-data and identity discrepancy notes

## Quantitative work

Candidate should calculate or interpret:
- selection proportion
- family mean/range
- simple rank stability across environments
- proportion of progeny meeting a defined threshold
- expected implications of sampling a small versus larger segregating population

## Evidence injects

### Inject A — progeny segregation
An apparently elite parent produces a wide progeny distribution. Candidate should revise any claim that the parent is fixed or uniformly transmitting the phenotype.

### Inject B — environment reversal
The top family in Environment A ranks below another family in Environment B. Candidate should address GxE and avoid universal ranking from one environment.

### Inject C — marker limitation
A marker report shows strong association in one related family only. Candidate should require independent validation before broad deployment.

## Required outputs

- pedigree/identity map
- population selection worksheet
- progeny and segregation interpretation
- GxE comparison
- marker-evidence decision
- next-generation breeding plan
- final evidence/inference table
