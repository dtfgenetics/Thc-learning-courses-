# Course Practical — Safety, Responsible Practice & Cultivation Workflows

**Course:** `COURSE-LH-TECH1-001`  
**Practical ID:** `PRACTICAL-LH-TECH1-001-WORKFLOW`  
**Status:** Public academic practical — continuously improvable  
**Mode:** simulated facility, supervised lab, or approved workplace-equivalent simulation

## Purpose

Measure whether the learner can combine the Course 1 competencies in a realistic technician workflow: recognize hazards, use supplied PPE/chemical information, follow sanitation and entry controls, interpret a controlled work order, preserve material identity, handle an inventory discrepancy, stay within equipment-care authority, create trustworthy records, and deliver a useful handoff.

This is a **course practical**, not the final THC Cultivation Technician I credential practical set.

## Candidate packet

The learner receives:

- mock room/work-area map;
- current work order and controlled SOP extracts;
- PPE requirement sheet;
- mock chemical label and SDS excerpt;
- sanitation/entry procedure;
- plant/batch/tag inventory;
- material movement request;
- physical-versus-record inventory discrepancy;
- equipment alarm/fault observation;
- operator-care checklist;
- prior-shift handoff containing one incomplete item;
- blank movement, deviation, maintenance-escalation and shift-handoff forms.

## Seeded conditions

A standard form should include at least:

1. one obvious workplace hazard;
2. one PPE or chemical-information decision;
3. one sanitation or movement-control requirement;
4. one hold/quarantine/restricted-entry condition;
5. one identity mismatch or inventory discrepancy;
6. one equipment condition that permits a routine operator check but not disassembly/repair;
7. one record needing a controlled correction;
8. one incomplete prior-shift task;
9. one condition requiring escalation rather than independent resolution.

Equivalent alternate forms should rotate details while preserving the same constructs.

## Candidate tasks

1. Review the full work packet before beginning.
2. Identify blocking safety, biosecurity, entry and identity conditions.
3. Select the supplied PPE/entry requirements using the work order, label/SDS and procedure.
4. Sequence sanitation and controlled movement correctly.
5. Verify plant/batch/material identity before the proposed movement.
6. Reconcile or escalate the planted inventory discrepancy without falsifying a record.
7. Complete only the operator-level equipment check allowed by the supplied procedure.
8. Produce a maintenance-ready escalation note if the fault remains unresolved.
9. Correct a sample record while preserving the original history.
10. Complete the movement/deviation records accurately.
11. Produce an end-of-task/shift handoff showing completed work, unresolved conditions, notifications and next actions.

## Evidence captured

The assessor records:

- hazard decisions;
- PPE/chemical-information use;
- sanitation/entry sequence;
- hold/quarantine response;
- identity verification;
- discrepancy response;
- equipment-authority decision;
- maintenance escalation quality;
- record correction;
- traceability documentation;
- final handoff.

## Analytic rubric — 100 points

| Dimension | Points | Competent performance |
|---|---:|---|
| Safety / PPE / stop-work judgment | 18 | Recognizes seeded hazards, uses supplied controls, does not exceed role authority |
| Sanitation / biosecurity | 14 | Follows movement and sanitation controls and respects holds/restrictions |
| SOP / work-order execution | 12 | Uses current instructions, identifies acceptance criteria and escalates conflicts |
| Identity / traceability | 16 | Protects plant/material identity and records movements accurately |
| Inventory / waste / discrepancy reasoning | 10 | Preserves evidence and avoids forced reconciliation |
| Equipment readiness / operator-care boundary | 10 | Performs only permitted checks and escalates protected servicing |
| Data integrity / documentation | 12 | Records facts contemporaneously and preserves correction history |
| Handoff / communication | 8 | Communicates factual status, open items, notifications and next actions |
| **Total** | **100** | |

**Development threshold:** 80/100 **for pilot use only**. This is not claimed as an externally validated cut score. Pilot evidence and assessor review must inform the operational standard.

## Critical-error rules

A course-practical attempt is not considered competent while any of the following remains unresolved:

- knowingly continuing into an explicitly prohibited unsafe condition;
- bypassing an explicit restricted-entry, quarantine or hold instruction;
- intentionally falsifying plant/material identity, movement, inventory or completion status;
- performing an explicitly prohibited repair, pesticide-handling action or protected servicing step instead of escalating;
- causing unrecoverable identity loss through an avoidable movement or relabeling error.

Critical-error definitions must be calibrated with assessors so they are applied consistently and only to construct-relevant failures.

## Assessor guidance

- Score observable behavior and produced records, not personality or speed alone.
- Prompt only where the standardized form permits prompting; record prompts used.
- Do not reward unsupported diagnosis when the task only requires observation/escalation.
- Treat local SOP extracts in the candidate packet as controlling for facility-specific details.
- Do not infer pesticide-handler, electrician, HVAC, engineering or maintenance authority from course completion.
- Record any ambiguity in the simulation itself for later form revision.

## Remediation

If the learner fails a noncritical dimension, remediation should target the deficient behavior and allow a new equivalent form. Critical safety, identity or integrity failures require targeted remediation before retry. Retesting should not simply repeat the identical seeded conditions.

## Accessibility

Provide equivalent accessible representations of diagrams, labels, room maps, alarms and forms. Extra time may be allowed where it does not change the construct being measured. The practical must not require color perception alone to identify a hazard, status or control.
