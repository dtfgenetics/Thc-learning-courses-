# Technician II Practical A — Crop Diagnostic Workup

## Purpose
Demonstrate that the candidate can investigate a crop problem using multiple evidence streams, rank competing explanations, select efficient verification steps, and produce a supervisor-ready diagnostic record without confusing symptoms with causes.

## Tasks and competencies
- `TASK-CULT2-DIAG-001`
- `COMP-ENV-ADV-001`
- `COMP-WATER-QUALITY-ADV-001`
- `COMP-IRRIGATION-ADV-001`
- `COMP-NUTRIENT-DIAG-001`
- `COMP-IPM-ADV-001`
- `COMP-PRO-QA-001`

## Assessment format
The assessor provides one primary crop case containing at least three plausible contributing factors. The case may be live, simulated, photographic, tabular, or a combination. The evidence package must include plant observations, room position, environmental history, irrigation/root-zone information, recent work history, and at least one distracting or incomplete data point.

Recommended assessment time: 60–90 minutes.

## Candidate receives
- room/crop map with affected and comparison plants
- crop stage, cultivar/batch, and recent work history
- 24–72 hour environmental trend summary
- irrigation event history and representative delivery measurements
- root-zone or runoff/drain measurements when relevant
- water/fertigation batch information when relevant
- scouting/IPM observations when relevant
- photographs or live specimens showing symptom distribution
- applicable SOP excerpts and escalation path

## Candidate must perform
1. **Define the problem.** State what is abnormal using observable facts: plant part, symptom, severity, distribution, timing, and affected scope.
2. **Map the pattern.** Determine whether the problem is localized, line-related, bench-related, edge-related, stage-related, cultivar-related, random, or room-wide.
3. **Validate evidence quality.** Identify missing timestamps, questionable measurements, stale samples, suspect sensors, or data that cannot yet be compared.
4. **Build a differential.** Generate at least three plausible explanations drawn from environmental, irrigation/root-zone, nutrition/water-quality, pest/pathogen, mechanical, and recent-work categories as appropriate.
5. **Rank hypotheses.** For each explanation, identify supporting evidence, contradicting evidence, and one observation or measurement that would materially change confidence.
6. **Choose verification steps.** Select the safest, fastest, most discriminating checks first. The candidate should prefer measurements that can separate competing explanations rather than simply collecting more of the same data.
7. **Interpret results.** Update the ranking when new evidence is supplied by the assessor. The candidate must be willing to reject the original leading hypothesis if the evidence changes.
8. **Define immediate actions.** Identify actions that protect the crop or preserve evidence while remaining within the technician role and current SOP.
9. **Define escalation.** State what requires supervisor, QA, maintenance, or IPM-program decision ownership.
10. **Document the workup.** Produce a concise diagnostic report that clearly separates facts, hypotheses, verification results, actions taken, open questions, and next checks.

## Required reasoning standard
A strong candidate does not diagnose from a single leaf appearance. The candidate connects spatial pattern, timing, crop stage, environmental load, irrigation delivery, root-zone behavior, recent work, and plant response. A conclusion is considered confirmed only when the available evidence directly supports it and reasonable alternatives have been addressed.

## Embedded calculation tasks
At least one generated case must require two of the following:
- incidence percentage = affected plants / inspected plants × 100
- delivery deviation percentage = (measured − target) / target × 100
- comparison of average readings between affected and unaffected zones
- change in root-zone EC, substrate water content, or environmental value across a defined interval
- simple rate or percentage-point change in symptom incidence

Candidate calculations must include units and enough work to verify the result.

## Assessor injects
After the initial differential, provide two additional pieces of evidence. One should strengthen a hypothesis; the other should challenge either the same hypothesis or a common shortcut. Examples include:
- colocated reference sensor disagrees with the room sensor
- emitter output is reduced on the affected irrigation lateral
- root-zone EC is elevated only where dryback is greatest
- pest observations are present but do not match symptom distribution
- a recent canopy operation occurred only in the affected section

The candidate must revise the differential rather than defend the first answer.

## Scoring rubric — 100 points
| Domain | Points | Full-credit evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Problem definition and crop mapping | 15 | Accurate symptom, scope, timing, spatial pattern, and comparison group |
| Data-quality check | 10 | Detects unreliable/missing evidence and explains its effect on interpretation |
| Differential diagnosis | 20 | At least three plausible hypotheses with evidence for and against each |
| Verification strategy | 20 | Prioritizes discriminating checks, uses appropriate measurements, avoids shotgun changes |
| Data/calculation interpretation | 10 | Correct calculations, units, direction and operational meaning |
| Decision and immediate response | 10 | Evidence-based action proportional to confidence and crop risk |
| Escalation and role judgment | 5 | Routes unresolved/high-impact issues with useful evidence |
| Documentation and handoff | 10 | Reproducible, fact-based report with next action and ownership |

**Pass standard:** 80/100 and no critical error.

## Critical errors
- knowingly using an invalid measurement as proof without verification
- making a broad corrective change that is unsupported by the evidence and creates material crop risk
- contaminating samples or spreading a suspected pest/pathogen through poor handling during the assessment
- altering or fabricating source records
- representing a hypothesis as confirmed after decisive contradictory evidence is provided

## Performance levels
**Exceeds:** integrates evidence efficiently, explicitly manages uncertainty, anticipates confounders, and produces a handoff another technician can reproduce.

**Meets:** reaches a defensible working conclusion through appropriate checks and documentation with no critical error.

**Needs development:** relies primarily on visual diagnosis, fails to compare affected/unaffected areas, collects non-discriminating data, or leaves the next operator unable to reproduce the reasoning.

## Evidence produced
- Crop Diagnostic Report
- annotated crop map
- hypothesis/evidence matrix
- calculation sheet
- verification results
- action/escalation record

## Assessor closeout questions
1. What evidence most increased your confidence, and why?
2. What evidence would most likely prove your current conclusion wrong?
3. What did you deliberately avoid changing before verification?
4. What should the next shift recheck, at what location, and against what comparison?
