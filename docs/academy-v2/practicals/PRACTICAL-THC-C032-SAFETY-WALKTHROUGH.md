# PRACTICAL-THC-C032-SAFETY-WALKTHROUGH

## Integrated Ladder, Tool, and Ergonomic Safety Walkthrough

**Canonical course:** THC-C032 — Ladder, Tool, and Ergonomic Safety  
**Runtime course:** `COURSE-THC-C032`  
**Role anchor:** Cultivation Technician I  
**Job-task anchor:** `TASK-CULT-SAFETY-001`  
**Purpose:** Summative applied performance assessment  
**Score:** 100 points  
**Pass:** 80% and zero critical errors

## Candidate scenario

You are beginning a maintenance-and-crop-support work block in a controlled cultivation area. Three jobs are scheduled: inspect an elevated environmental sensor and trellis connection; prepare and use a portable powered tool for a small maintenance task; and reorganize a manual-handling station where workers repeatedly move containers and push a harvest cart. The assessor will provide staged equipment, photographs, diagrams, or simulation cards representing safe and unsafe conditions.

Your job is not to complete work at any cost. Your job is to show that you can recognize exposure, select the correct work method, stop unsafe work, document what you found, and propose a safer system.

## Station A — access method and ladder inspection

1. Review the elevated task description and decide whether a ladder is an appropriate access method.
2. Explain the work position, expected reach, hand/tool requirements, task duration, surface condition, traffic, and load considerations.
3. Inspect the ladder using a repeatable sequence that covers rails, steps/rungs, feet, locking/spreader components, fasteners, labels, contamination, visible damage, and unauthorized modifications.
4. Classify the ladder as usable, correctable before use, or remove from service.
5. Establish a controlled setup zone and explain how door, cart, bench, irrigation, and pedestrian movement will be managed.
6. Demonstrate or verbalize ascent/descent, load handling, working-position limits, and the point at which you would descend and reposition.

### Assessor injects
At least two injects must be introduced after the candidate begins planning. Examples: a cart contacts the ladder during staging; irrigation creates water near one foot; a sensor target is moved outside controlled reach; a damaged ladder foot is revealed; a doorway becomes active. The candidate must respond to the changed condition instead of continuing from the original plan.

## Station B — portable tool pre-use control

1. Select the appropriate hand or powered tool for the assigned task.
2. Inspect housing, handle, guard/control, accessory, cord/plug or other energy connection, fasteners, and visible condition.
3. Define the tool operating path and identify hands, coworkers, crop equipment, tubing, cords, and surfaces that must remain outside it.
4. Explain the energy connection and shutdown sequence.
5. Identify any condition that requires removal from service, different equipment, or maintenance/electrical escalation.
6. For an electrical scenario, explain how water, drainage, cord routing, damaged insulation, and equipment approval affect the decision.

### Assessor injects
Provide one unsafe tool or connection condition, such as a missing required guard, visibly damaged cord, crushed extension cord, unstable workpiece, or wet-location conflict. Passing performance requires the candidate to stop or redesign the task rather than compensate with speed or PPE alone.

## Station C — ergonomic observation and redesign

1. Observe a simulated cultivation task such as container lifting, harvest-bin handling, hose movement, trimming, low-canopy work, or overhead trellis work.
2. Record force, repetition, posture, reach, duration, coupling/grip, contact stress, work height, and available recovery.
3. For a defined two-handed lift, identify the variables the Revised NIOSH Lifting Equation would require. Do not invent a universal weight limit.
4. State whether the RNLE is appropriate for the task. If the task is pushing, pulling, carrying, one-handed, unstable, or otherwise outside the equation's intended conditions, say so and choose a different assessment path.
5. Propose at least one equipment/layout/engineering change and one workflow or administrative change.
6. Define how the team would verify that the change reduced exposure rather than merely moving the problem.

## Required candidate artifacts

- ladder/access-method decision record;
- ladder inspection record with disposition;
- portable tool pre-use checklist;
- work-zone hazard sketch or annotated scenario;
- ergonomic exposure observation sheet;
- redesign proposal with owner and verification measure;
- final handoff summary listing stop-work decisions, unresolved hazards, and next actions.

## Scoring rubric

### 1. Hazard recognition and task planning — 20 points
- 17–20: Identifies interacting hazards, defines task boundaries and stop-work triggers, and selects controls before work begins.
- 13–16: Identifies primary hazards and workable controls with minor omissions.
- 9–12: Recognizes some hazards but planning remains reactive or incomplete.
- 0–8: Misses major hazards or treats task completion as more important than safe setup.

### 2. Ladder selection, inspection, setup, and use — 20 points
- 17–20: Selects an appropriate access method, inspects systematically, controls setup/traffic, and demonstrates correct climbing/repositioning decisions.
- 13–16: Safe overall with minor missed inspection or setup details.
- 9–12: Requires prompting on inspection, setup, or repositioning.
- 0–8: Unsafe selection or use, or failure to remove defective equipment.

### 3. Tool condition and work-zone controls — 20 points
- 17–20: Correct tool selection, complete pre-use check, controlled operating zone, proper escalation and energy/shutdown planning.
- 13–16: Safe tool decision with minor omissions.
- 9–12: Incomplete tool or work-zone assessment requiring prompts.
- 0–8: Uses or authorizes unsafe/defective equipment or ignores required controls.

### 4. Ergonomic assessment and redesign — 25 points
- 21–25: Evaluates multiple exposure dimensions, uses RNLE only when appropriate, prioritizes design-level changes, and defines verification.
- 16–20: Identifies major ergonomic factors and reasonable controls with limited depth.
- 11–15: Focuses on posture or weight alone and misses important exposure variables.
- 0–10: Relies on personal strength, stretching, or unsupported universal limits instead of task assessment.

### 5. Documentation, escalation, and handoff — 15 points
- 13–15: Records observations, disposition, owner, unresolved risk, and verification steps in a clear handoff.
- 10–12: Records the major decisions but misses some ownership or follow-up detail.
- 7–9: Documentation is incomplete or difficult to use operationally.
- 0–6: Major safety decisions are undocumented or misleading.

## Critical errors

Any critical error fails the practical regardless of numeric score. Critical errors are defined in the machine-readable assessment and include knowingly using a defective ladder, moving an occupied ladder, using a powered tool with a missing required guard or visibly unsafe electrical condition, continuing after an electrical/water stop-work trigger, or knowingly directing manual handling through an immediately controllable equipment/layout hazard.

## Evidence and limitations

This practical uses OSHA ladder/tool/electrical-use requirements and NIOSH ergonomics guidance as source anchors. It does not authorize electrical repair, replace equipment manuals, define every jurisdiction's safety obligations, or claim that the RNLE covers every manual-handling task. The assessor should use equipment and scenarios appropriate to the learner's authorized work setting.
