# PHIB Course 7 Assessor Guide

## Scoring rule

Score only observable candidate evidence against the controlled rubric. Do not award points for presumed intent, reputation, prior experience or an answer that cannot be traced to the submitted case record.

## What strong performance looks like

A strong candidate:
- preserves identities, timestamps, units and evidence provenance;
- separates observation from interpretation;
- maintains a cross-domain differential until discriminating evidence narrows it;
- chooses tests/observations because they can change a decision;
- uses containment proportionate to consequence without presenting it as proof;
- links controls and CAPA to the evidence-supported mechanism;
- defines comparable recovery/effectiveness measurements;
- communicates confidence and residual uncertainty;
- respects role, legal and facility authority boundaries;
- produces a record another qualified person can reconstruct and continue.

## What is not enough

Do not treat the following as sufficient by themselves:
- visual pattern recognition without supporting evidence;
- naming a pest/pathogen without affected-scope or confirmation logic;
- a generic recommendation to “sanitize” or “spray”;
- a CAPA action with no cause linkage or effectiveness check;
- a confident final diagnosis that ignores material conflicting evidence;
- a high numeric score in the presence of a documented critical failure.

## Assessor calibration

Before operational use, assessors must complete calibration with shared benchmark cases. Double-score representative candidate performances during pilot work and reconcile material scoring disagreements. Inter-rater targets remain to be set from pilot evidence.
