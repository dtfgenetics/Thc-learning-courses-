# Technician II Practical D — IPM Trend & Treatment Follow-Up

## Purpose
Demonstrate that the candidate can interpret repeated scouting observations, trap counts, crop symptoms, spatial spread, and intervention history to judge whether an IPM problem is improving, stable, worsening, or uncertain and to select the next verification or escalation step.

## Tasks and competencies
- `TASK-CULT2-IPM-001`
- `COMP-IPM-ADV-001`
- `COMP-PRO-QA-001`
- `COMP-SPACE-BIOSEC-001`

## Assessment format
The assessor provides at least three scouting rounds for the same crop area plus an intervention or biological-control event between rounds. The candidate must interpret change over time rather than diagnose from one isolated count.

Recommended assessment time: 60–75 minutes.

## Candidate receives
- crop/room map with scout locations
- three or more dated scouting records
- sticky-card or trap counts where applicable
- incidence and severity observations
- representative photographs or specimens
- intervention/treatment/biological-control record
- environmental observations relevant to spread or disease pressure
- sanitation/work-traffic history where relevant
- approved IPM action thresholds or decision rules supplied by the training case
- applicable handling, documentation and escalation procedures

## Candidate must perform
1. Confirm that comparison rounds use compatible locations, methods, units and intervals.
2. Separate **incidence** (how much of the population is affected) from **severity** (how badly affected units are damaged).
3. Map the spatial pattern and identify hot spots, fronts of spread, isolated detections, or room-wide change.
4. Calculate at least two trend measures from supplied data.
5. Compare post-intervention evidence with the pre-intervention baseline.
6. State whether the evidence supports improving, stable, worsening, mixed, or indeterminate status.
7. Identify confounders such as changed trap placement, changed scouting effort, missing samples, plant removals, or different observation windows.
8. Evaluate whether the prior action can reasonably be judged yet based on timing and the supplied case information.
9. Select the next scouting, sampling, containment, sanitation, biological-control follow-up, or escalation action specified by the applicable program.
10. Produce a treatment-follow-up report that preserves observations separately from conclusions.

## Calculation set
Every form must require at least two of the following:
- incidence = affected plants / inspected plants × 100
- trap count per trap or per observation interval
- percent change = (new − old) / old × 100 when the denominator is meaningful
- percentage-point change in incidence
- hot-spot share = detections in defined hot spot / total detections × 100
- treatment follow-up comparison between matched sampling locations

If the baseline is zero, the candidate should not report an undefined percent increase as though it were a normal finite percentage; use absolute change or another meaningful comparison.

## Interpretation rules
A single declining trap count does not by itself prove crop-level control. Stronger conclusions use converging evidence: matched trap trends, direct plant observations, incidence/severity movement, spatial spread, crop stage, and appropriate post-intervention timing. Conversely, a rising count in one location may indicate a localized hot spot rather than room-wide failure.

## Assessor injects
After the candidate reaches an initial conclusion, provide one complicating fact such as:
- one trap was moved between rounds
- heavily affected plants were removed after round two
- scouting effort doubled in the final round
- direct plant incidence declined while trap counts rose near an entry point
- symptom severity increased but new-plant incidence did not

The candidate must explain how the new fact changes confidence and what should be rechecked.

## Scoring rubric — 100 points
| Domain | Points | Full-credit evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Data comparability/quality | 15 | Checks location, method, timing and sampling effort before comparing |
| Trend calculations | 15 | Correct calculations with appropriate denominator and units |
| Spatial analysis | 15 | Correct hot-spot/spread interpretation from the crop map |
| Intervention follow-up | 20 | Uses multiple evidence streams to judge response without overclaiming |
| Confounder recognition | 10 | Identifies changes that weaken direct round-to-round comparison |
| Next-step decision | 10 | Selects targeted scouting/containment/follow-up consistent with case rules |
| Biosecurity execution | 5 | Handles route/materials without avoidable spread |
| Documentation/handoff | 10 | Clear trend summary, evidence, uncertainty and next ownership |

**Pass standard:** 80/100 and no critical error.

## Critical errors
- knowingly spreading a suspected pest/pathogen through avoidable handling or route failure
- fabricating scouting or treatment records
- claiming treatment success/failure from evidence the candidate has identified as non-comparable without qualification
- making an unauthorized pesticide-selection or application decision outside the supplied work authorization
- ignoring an escalation threshold clearly crossed in the supplied case

## Evidence produced
- IPM Trend & Treatment Follow-Up Report
- annotated hot-spot map
- trend calculation sheet
- matched-round comparison table
- next-scouting/action recommendation
- escalation record where required

## Assessor closeout questions
1. Which evidence best represents crop impact rather than pest presence alone?
2. What made the scouting rounds comparable or non-comparable?
3. What finding would cause you to expand the affected scope?
4. What should the next technician measure in exactly the same way?
