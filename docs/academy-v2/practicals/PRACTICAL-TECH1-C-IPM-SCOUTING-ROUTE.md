# Practical C — IPM Scouting Route

**Credential:** THC Cultivation Technician I  
**Mode:** image-rich simulation, supervised crop inspection, or approved workplace equivalent  
**Primary tasks:** `TASK-CULT-IPM-001`, `TASK-CULT-SANITATION-001`, `TASK-CULT-OBSERVE-001`, `TASK-CULT-RECORDS-001`

## Purpose

This practical tests whether a candidate can scout a crop in a repeatable way, distinguish symptoms from direct signs, quantify basic incidence/severity, recognize spatial patterns, protect biosecurity and quarantine boundaries, and create a record an IPM lead can use without making unauthorized treatment decisions.

## Candidate packet

The candidate receives:
- scouting SOP
- room map and route
- plant/batch/cultivar IDs
- previous scouting rounds
- trap map and selected trap images/counts
- crop images from several canopy positions
- sanitation, clean-to-dirty movement and isolation/quarantine instructions
- scouting record template
- escalation guide

## Seeded conditions

A practical form should include several of the following:
- clustered symptom pattern
- intake/edge-associated damage
- one direct pest sign
- symptom with insufficient evidence for diagnosis
- increasing incidence across rounds
- one suspicious plant in a clean zone
- trap-count change
- condition isolated to one irrigation line or cultivar
- a tool/material movement choice that could spread a suspect condition into a cleaner zone
- an isolation/quarantine boundary that must be maintained while the finding is escalated

## Candidate tasks

1. Prepare clean scouting tools/PPE.
2. Follow the supplied route and identify high-risk inspection points.
3. Inspect representative canopy levels and leaf surfaces.
4. Classify provided findings as symptom, direct sign, mechanical damage, or insufficient evidence.
5. Calculate simple incidence from the supplied sample.
6. Assign supplied severity categories consistently.
7. Plot findings on the room map.
8. Compare with previous rounds and identify stable, increasing or resolving trends.
9. When a suspect finding threatens a clean area, preserve supplied clean-to-dirty movement, tool-control and isolation/quarantine boundaries; document and report any actual or potential breach.
10. Choose appropriate escalation without making unauthorized pesticide or treatment-selection decisions.
11. Complete the scouting record and handoff.

## Expected evidence

- clean scouting sequence
- route/inspection completeness
- classification accuracy
- incidence/severity calculations
- pattern map
- trend interpretation
- clean-to-dirty/quarantine decision
- escalation decision
- scouting record quality

## Scoring model — 100 points

- sanitation/biosecurity: 15
- route/inspection completeness: 15
- sign vs symptom classification: 15
- incidence/severity: 15
- spatial/trend interpretation: 20
- escalation choice: 10
- documentation/handoff: 10

**Target pass:** 80/100.

## Strong-performance indicators

- does not overstate diagnosis
- captures exact location and plant/tissue context
- notices increasing incidence even when individual symptoms are mild
- protects clean areas from suspect tools/material and maintains supplied isolation/quarantine boundaries
- treats one close-up image as part of broader evidence, not proof of a room-wide problem
- escalates suspected pest/disease evidence without selecting a pesticide or treatment outside the supplied authority

## Deliverables

1. completed scouting sheet
2. annotated room map
3. incidence/severity calculations
4. trap trend note
5. biosecurity/quarantine note where applicable
6. escalation/handoff note

## Portfolio use

A three-round simulated IPM trend report can become a strong employment portfolio artifact showing that the learner can create actionable scouting evidence, protect clean areas and quarantine boundaries, and escalate without merely guessing pest names or treatments.
