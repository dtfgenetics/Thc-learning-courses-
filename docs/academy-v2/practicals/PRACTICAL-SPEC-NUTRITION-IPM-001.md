# Advanced Nutrition Diagnostics and IPM Specialist Practical

**Credential:** THC Advanced Nutrition, Diagnostics and Plant Health Certificate  
**Competencies:** `COMP-NUTRIENT-DIAG-001`, `COMP-IPM-ADV-001`

## Purpose

Assess the candidate's ability to diagnose plant-health deviations using nutrition, root-zone, environment and IPM evidence together. The candidate must avoid symptom-only conclusions and choose an integrated response that protects crop health and biosecurity.

## Scenario

A crop develops interveinal chlorosis, marginal damage and reduced vigor in two zones. Feed EC is stable, but root-zone EC differs by zone. Tissue results show several elements outside historical ranges. Sticky-card counts are increasing for one pest while leaf inspections show both feeding damage and abiotic stress. Recent environmental and irrigation changes complicate the picture.

## Candidate tasks

1. Verify crop identity, stage, affected scope and timing.
2. Classify observed signs and symptoms without assigning cause prematurely.
3. Map incidence and severity by zone.
4. Review feed, pH, EC, irrigation and root-zone trends.
5. Interpret tissue data as supporting evidence rather than a stand-alone diagnosis.
6. Review pest counts, life-stage observations and spatial distribution.
7. Build at least four competing hypotheses spanning nutritional, root-zone, pest/pathogen and environmental causes.
8. Identify evidence for and against each hypothesis.
9. Select discriminating next checks such as representative root-zone samples, emitter verification, close inspection or additional lab evidence.
10. Build an integrated response using prevention, sanitation, environmental/root-zone correction and legally/procedurally permitted IPM actions.
11. Define monitoring metrics, thresholds and follow-up timing.
12. Produce a specialist plant-health handoff.

## Calculations

Candidate should calculate or interpret:
- scouting incidence
- change in trap count over time
- feed-to-root-zone EC difference
- proportion of sampled plants affected
- change in a measured value from baseline

## Evidence injects

### Inject A — emitter fault
Output testing shows one affected zone receives materially less irrigation. Candidate should revise the nutrient interpretation to include concentration/dryback effects.

### Inject B — confirmed pest stage
Microscopy or close inspection confirms an economically important pest stage. Candidate should integrate control with sanitation and monitoring rather than abandoning the root-zone investigation.

### Inject C — misleading tissue result
A tissue value is high, but feed and root-zone data indicate concentration rather than simple deficiency. Candidate should avoid treating tissue concentration as direct proof of plant availability.

## Required outputs

- symptom/sign map
- root-zone/feed trend table
- IPM incidence/severity worksheet
- ranked differential diagnosis
- integrated response plan
- verification schedule
- final handoff
