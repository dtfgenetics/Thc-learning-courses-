# Technician II Practical E — Propagation / Canopy Performance Review

## Purpose
Demonstrate that the candidate can investigate performance variation in either propagation or canopy/flowering work, compare batches or zones fairly, identify process factors that may explain the variation, and recommend the next evidence-based verification step.

## Tasks and competencies
- `TASK-CULT2-PROP-001`
- `TASK-CULT2-CANOPY-001`
- `COMP-PROP-001`
- `COMP-CANOPY-ADV-001`
- `COMP-FLOWER-ADV-001`
- `COMP-PRO-QA-001`

## Assessment format
Each generated form selects one primary workstation: **Propagation** or **Canopy/Flowering**. The secondary domain appears as a shorter transfer scenario. This keeps the practical deep enough to assess actual work while still testing whether the candidate can transfer the same compare-measure-verify logic to another crop operation.

Recommended assessment time: 60–75 minutes.

# Form P — Propagation performance investigation

## Candidate receives
- donor/source identity and batch map
- cutting/propagation dates
- operator/workstation records
- rooting or establishment checks by date
- loss/cull counts and reason codes
- propagation environmental trends
- sanitation records
- media/input lot information where relevant
- cultivar/genotype identifiers
- comparison batch with acceptable performance

## Candidate must
1. Calculate rooting/establishment success and loss rate for the affected and comparison batch.
2. Confirm that batches are comparable by age, genotype, count basis, and assessment timing.
3. Map failure distribution by tray, dome, bench, operator, donor/source, or date.
4. Separate symptoms from likely process causes.
5. Build at least three hypotheses spanning source material, environment, sanitation, media/moisture, handling, and process timing as supported by the case.
6. Identify which records strengthen or weaken each hypothesis.
7. Select one or two verification steps that most efficiently separate the leading explanations.
8. Determine immediate process protection, affected scope, and handoff.
9. Produce a Propagation Performance Review.

## Required calculations
- success rate = established units / started units × 100
- loss rate = failed or culled units / started units × 100
- comparison between batches as percentage-point difference
- optional time-to-root or median/average establishment interval when data are supplied

# Form C — Canopy/flowering performance investigation

## Candidate receives
- work order and completion record
- crop/room map
- cultivar and stage
- before/after canopy measurements or photographs
- environmental/light distribution data supplied by the case
- plant spacing/density information
- recovery or follow-up observations
- comparison zone or prior acceptable run

## Candidate must
1. Verify the work order, stage and intended outcome.
2. Compare actual canopy condition with the specified target using measurable evidence rather than technique names alone.
3. Map uneven height, density, support, light interception, airflow obstruction, injury or delayed recovery.
4. Compare affected and reference zones.
5. Determine whether variation is most consistent with execution, timing, cultivar response, environmental nonuniformity, plant-density differences, or unresolved causes.
6. Select a discriminating follow-up measurement.
7. Recommend the next action supported by the work instruction and crop evidence.
8. Produce a Canopy/Flowering Performance Review.

## Required calculations
At least two per form, such as:
- coefficient or percent variation in height/light measurements from supplied values
- percentage of plants meeting the work-order target
- percentage-point difference in target attainment between zones
- recovery incidence = recovered plants / evaluated plants × 100
- affected-area share of room or bench population

## Secondary transfer scenario
The candidate receives a short case from the non-primary domain and has 10 minutes to state:
- problem definition
- two plausible hypotheses
- strongest next verification
- what would be documented for handoff

## Assessor injects
Introduce one confounder after the initial analysis, such as a genotype difference, changed assessment date, replaced tray, alternate operator, missing environmental interval, or uneven room position. The candidate must decide whether the original comparison remains valid.

## Scoring rubric — 100 points
| Domain | Points | Full-credit evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Comparison design/data quality | 15 | Checks age, stage, genotype, timing and denominator before comparing |
| Performance calculations | 15 | Correct formulas, units/denominators and interpretation |
| Spatial/batch pattern analysis | 15 | Identifies meaningful clustering or subgroup differences |
| Differential reasoning | 20 | Plausible, ranked process explanations tied to evidence |
| Verification strategy | 10 | Chooses measurements capable of separating leading explanations |
| Work-order/process judgment | 10 | Links findings to intended outcome and approved corrective path |
| Transfer scenario | 5 | Applies the same reasoning model in the secondary domain |
| Documentation/handoff | 10 | Clear affected scope, evidence, action, owner and recheck |

**Pass standard:** 80/100 and no critical error.

## Critical errors
- falsifying batch, cull, work-order or performance records
- comparing populations known to be materially different while presenting the result as a controlled comparison
- performing an unauthorized high-impact crop manipulation after the candidate has identified that the work order does not support it
- creating preventable biosecurity cross-contamination during propagation work

## Evidence produced
- Propagation Performance Review **or** Canopy/Flowering Performance Review
- annotated batch/room map
- calculation sheet
- hypothesis/evidence matrix
- follow-up measurement plan
- secondary transfer-case response

## Assessor closeout questions
1. Which difference between the affected and comparison group matters most?
2. Which apparent difference could be a confounder rather than a cause?
3. What should be measured again before the next operational change?
4. How would you explain the finding to the next shift in under one minute?
