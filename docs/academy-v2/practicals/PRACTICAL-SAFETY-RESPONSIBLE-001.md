# PRACTICAL-SAFETY-RESPONSIBLE-001
## Safety and Responsible Practice — Hazard, Authorization and Handoff Practical

Status: **Draft / pilot required before credential use**

Mapped course: `COURSE-SAFETY-RESPONSIBLE-001`  
Mapped role: `ROLE-CULT-TECH-I-001`  
Mapped competencies: `COMP-SAFETY-WORK-001`, `COMP-RESPONSIBLE-LEGAL-001`

## Purpose

Evaluate whether a learner can recognize cultivation workplace hazards, choose proportionate controls, verify authorization before chemical/input use or restricted-area entry, preserve traceable records, and stop/escalate work when safety or authorization is unresolved.

This is a performance assessment. It is not satisfied by completing the online course or passing the multiple-choice final.

## Delivery modes

The practical may be delivered as:

- a controlled simulation;
- a virtual-facility case;
- a supervised lab;
- an approved workplace-equivalent exercise.

The evidence and scoring requirements remain the same across modes.

## Candidate scenario

The candidate begins a cultivation shift with a work order that includes:

1. inspect and prepare a work area;
2. complete a routine plant-care task;
3. verify a labeled input before use;
4. confirm whether a recently treated zone is available for entry;
5. document deviations, controls, and handoff information.

During the exercise the candidate receives a case packet containing a mix of routine and abnormal conditions.

The packet should include at least:

- one obvious physical hazard;
- one less-obvious interacting hazard;
- a product label or label excerpt;
- a Safety Data Sheet or safety-information excerpt when appropriate;
- a treatment/application record with date/time and area identity;
- a jurisdiction or facility-policy source;
- a deliberately ambiguous or incomplete authorization detail;
- a blank hazard/corrective-action record;
- a blank escalation/handoff record.

Examples of hazards that may be used in a controlled scenario include a wet floor near powered equipment, damaged cord, obstructed aisle, unstable ladder, heat-load warning, missing PPE, unlabeled secondary container, or an unresolved reentry restriction.

Do not create an assessment condition that requires an actual learner to enter an unsafe area or handle a hazardous substance unsafely. Unsafe conditions may be represented through simulation, photographs, diagrams, mock labels, locked-out equipment, or supervised demonstration.

## Candidate tasks

### Task 1 — Inspect and prioritize hazards

The candidate must:

- identify hazards from the supplied work area or evidence;
- describe the potential exposure or consequence;
- rank which condition requires immediate stop/control/escalation;
- distinguish source/engineering/administrative/PPE controls;
- select a proportionate control without pretending PPE removes the hazard.

### Task 2 — Verify input or chemical use

The candidate must review the supplied product information and record:

- product identity;
- label/version or supplied controlling document;
- crop/site or task;
- intended use;
- PPE/control requirements;
- reentry or access restrictions when applicable;
- unresolved questions;
- the authoritative source used for the decision.

If the evidence does not establish authorization, the correct action is to stop and escalate rather than infer permission.

### Task 3 — Verify entry/access status

The candidate must use the supplied treatment/access records to determine whether entry is clearly permitted.

The learner must not guess a restricted-entry interval, facility rule, or jurisdictional requirement that is not supplied. When the evidence is incomplete, the learner should identify what information is missing and escalate.

### Task 4 — Produce a traceable record

The candidate must create a record containing enough information to reconstruct the event:

- who;
- what;
- where;
- when;
- affected room/zone/batch or work area;
- evidence reviewed;
- hazard or deviation;
- immediate control;
- unresolved issue;
- escalation target;
- corrective action or follow-up status.

The candidate must preserve planned/original information rather than silently overwrite history.

### Task 5 — Handoff

The candidate gives a concise handoff that separates:

- direct observations;
- verified requirements;
- interpretations;
- unresolved questions;
- actions already taken;
- actions that require authorization.

## Evidence outputs

The assessor retains:

1. completed hazard-control worksheet;
2. chemical/input pre-use verification record;
3. jurisdiction/authority verification note;
4. incident or deviation record;
5. corrective-action and escalation handoff;
6. assessor scoring form;
7. critical-error record, if triggered.

## Scoring

Total: **100 points**

### Hazard recognition and prioritization — 20 points

Full-credit performance:
- identifies the supplied major hazards;
- distinguishes severity/urgency;
- recognizes interacting hazards;
- does not normalize an unsafe condition.

### Hierarchy-of-controls reasoning — 20 points

Full-credit performance:
- prefers removal, isolation, engineering, or task redesign when practical;
- uses administrative controls and PPE as appropriate layers;
- does not treat PPE as permission to continue through an unresolved serious hazard.

### Label and authorization verification — 20 points

Full-credit performance:
- verifies product identity and controlling information;
- checks crop/site/task and worker restrictions supplied by the scenario;
- identifies the governing/authoritative source;
- stops when authorization is unclear.

### Emergency and stop-work response — 15 points

Full-credit performance:
- recognizes when immediate stop-work or emergency escalation is required;
- communicates the hazard clearly;
- does not create additional exposure while attempting a correction.

### Records and traceability — 15 points

Full-credit performance:
- records identities, timing, evidence, actions and deviations accurately;
- preserves history;
- distinguishes observed facts from later interpretation.

### Escalation and handoff quality — 10 points

Full-credit performance:
- identifies the correct unresolved question;
- provides enough evidence for the next authorized person to act;
- stays within Technician I authority.

## Passing standard

Development target:

- at least **80% overall**; and
- **no critical error**.

The 80% cut score is provisional until pilot and standard-setting evidence is recorded.

## Critical errors

Any one of the following produces an automatic practical failure:

1. Knowingly continuing work through an unresolved serious electrical, chemical, heat, machinery, fall, or comparable safety hazard instead of stopping or escalating.
2. Directing or performing chemical/pesticide use when crop, site, label, worker-protection, or jurisdictional authorization is unresolved.
3. Knowingly entering or directing another worker into a restricted or unsafe area without verifying the applicable access/reentry control.
4. Falsifying, erasing, or backdating a safety, application, exposure, incident, authorization, or corrective-action record to conceal a discrepancy.

## Assessor guidance

Assess observable evidence, not confidence or speaking style.

The candidate should receive credit for correctly stopping and escalating an ambiguous condition. Do not penalize a learner for refusing to invent a rule, exposure limit, reentry interval, or legal interpretation that is not established by the provided authoritative evidence.

Assessors must not coach the candidate toward the correct answer during a scored attempt.

Before credential use, the program must establish:

- assessor calibration examples;
- acceptable evidence exemplars;
- borderline-performance examples;
- pilot results;
- inter-rater agreement review;
- accessibility/accommodation procedure;
- retake/remediation procedure;
- source/currentness review for scenario materials.

## Remediation

After an unsuccessful attempt, the learner should receive domain-level feedback without receiving a reusable answer key.

Remediation should target the failed capability, such as:

- hierarchy-of-controls reasoning;
- label/source verification;
- reentry/access verification;
- traceable documentation;
- stop-work/escalation judgment.

A new scored attempt should use a materially different scenario packet.

## Credential boundary

This practical does not grant legal authorization to cultivate, possess, apply pesticides, enter regulated areas, supervise workers, or perform any licensed activity.

It provides educational evidence that the learner can apply the Academy's safety and responsible-practice competencies in a controlled assessment.

Credential use remains disabled until the related course, assessment bank, human review, pilot, standard-setting, accessibility, identity, issuance, and release gates are satisfied.
