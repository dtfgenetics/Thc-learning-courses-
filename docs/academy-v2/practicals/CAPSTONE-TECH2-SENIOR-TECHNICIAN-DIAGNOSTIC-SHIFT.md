# Technician II Capstone — Senior Technician Diagnostic Shift

## Purpose
The capstone is the integrated performance assessment for the THC Cultivation Technician II credential. It tests whether a candidate can operate across multiple crop/process areas during one simulated senior-technician shift: prioritize competing problems, verify evidence quality, troubleshoot methodically, complete routine advanced work, support a peer, preserve records, and deliver a supervisor-ready handoff.

The capstone is not a collection of seven isolated practical stations. It is a single shift narrative in which decisions interact. A candidate who performs calculations correctly but fails to prioritize risk, verify measurements, preserve traceability, or communicate unresolved work has not demonstrated the full role.

## Integrated task coverage
- `TASK-CULT2-DIAG-001` — Multi-Factor Crop Diagnosis
- `TASK-CULT2-SENSOR-001` — Sensors, Controls & Data Quality
- `TASK-CULT2-EQUIPMENT-001` — Cultivation Equipment Verification
- `TASK-CULT2-FERTIGATION-001` — Fertigation Batch Execution & Verification
- `TASK-CULT2-ROOTZONE-001` — Root-Zone & Irrigation Troubleshooting
- `TASK-CULT2-IPM-001` — Advanced IPM Trend Interpretation
- `TASK-CULT2-PROP-001` — Propagation Performance Troubleshooting
- `TASK-CULT2-CANOPY-001` — Advanced Canopy & Flowering Execution
- `TASK-CULT2-POSTHARVEST-001` — Postharvest Process Investigation
- `TASK-CULT2-DEVIATION-001` — Cultivation Deviation Investigation
- `TASK-CULT2-TRACE-001` — Traceability & Inventory Reconciliation
- `TASK-CULT2-METRICS-001` — Production Metrics & Trend Review
- `TASK-CULT2-PEER-001` — Peer Task Support & Handoff

## Recommended delivery
Total candidate time: 3.5–5 hours, delivered as one continuous scenario or two linked sessions using the same shift evidence package.

The assessor should prepare at least:
- two grow/production areas
- one crop-health deviation
- one sensor/equipment or data-quality deviation
- one irrigation/root-zone or fertigation issue
- one IPM trend requiring interpretation
- one record/traceability or postharvest exception
- one small KPI dataset
- one peer-execution scenario
- at least three new-information injects delivered during the shift

At least one initial problem should be a false lead or secondary symptom. At least one problem should change priority when new evidence arrives.

# Shift briefing

## Candidate receives
At the start of the capstone the candidate receives:
- current shift priorities
- room/crop status board
- outstanding work orders
- environmental and irrigation trend summaries
- scouting/IPM summary
- propagation/canopy/postharvest status as applicable
- open maintenance/deviation notes
- physical/electronic traceability snapshot
- production metrics from the current and comparison period
- applicable SOP/work-instruction excerpts

The candidate has 10 minutes to review the briefing and produce an initial priority list.

## Priority model
The candidate should consider:
1. immediate people/equipment safety
2. crop/product containment or identity risk
3. rapidly worsening biological/process risk
4. time-sensitive work that prevents additional loss
5. diagnostic work needed before a decision can be made
6. routine planned work

The candidate must explain why a higher-priority item is being handled before a lower-priority item. The assessor scores the reasoning, not whether the candidate uses identical wording.

# Required capstone phases

## Phase 1 — Triage and data-quality verification
The candidate must:
- identify all reported abnormalities
- distinguish alarms, observations and confirmed conditions
- verify at least one questionable measurement independently
- identify missing or non-comparable data
- create an initial task sequence
- state which issue requires immediate containment or escalation

### Evidence
- initial priority board
- verified measurement record
- list of missing/uncertain evidence

## Phase 2 — Crop diagnostic investigation
The candidate investigates a crop problem using plant pattern, room location, environmental history, irrigation/root-zone data, recent work and scouting evidence.

Candidate must:
- define symptom and scope
- establish a comparison group
- create at least three hypotheses
- rank evidence for/against each
- select and perform/interpret discriminating checks
- revise the differential after an assessor inject
- document a defensible working conclusion and next step

### Evidence
- crop diagnostic report
- annotated map
- hypothesis/evidence matrix

## Phase 3 — Irrigation/root-zone or fertigation investigation
The candidate receives an approved work order plus system/root-zone evidence.

Candidate must:
- verify solution/batch or delivery values as applicable
- perform at least two calculations
- distinguish command from actual delivery
- interpret dryback/root-zone EC or equivalent supplied indicators
- determine whether the strongest evidence points to solution, delivery, timing, substrate response, demand or measurement quality
- take/document the appropriate next action

### Evidence
- batch/delivery verification record
- root-zone trend interpretation
- calculation sheet

## Phase 4 — IPM trend decision
Candidate analyzes repeated scouting rounds and intervention history.

Candidate must:
- check comparability of rounds
- calculate incidence/trend values
- map hot spots/spread
- judge improving/stable/worsening/mixed/uncertain status
- identify a confounder
- define targeted follow-up and escalation

### Evidence
- IPM trend report
- map and calculation sheet

## Phase 5 — Process deviation and traceability scope
The candidate receives a postharvest, movement, inventory or work-order discrepancy.

Candidate must:
- construct a factual timeline
- identify confirmed and potential affected scope
- reconcile physical and electronic records
- preserve source evidence
- identify the probable break point or state why it remains unresolved
- prepare the correction/QA/supervisor handoff

### Evidence
- deviation scope report
- reconciliation trail
- timeline/genealogy map

## Phase 6 — Production metrics
Candidate calculates at least four KPIs from the supplied dataset. The set must include two percentages, one normalized rate and one period/group comparison.

Candidate must state:
- formula
- numerator/denominator
- result and unit
- comparison
- operational interpretation
- one important limitation/confounder

### Evidence
- KPI calculation sheet
- short trend interpretation

## Phase 7 — Peer execution support
A peer is simulated as performing a routine task inconsistently with the supplied work instruction.

Candidate must:
- identify the exact mismatch
- explain/demonstrate the correct step
- confirm peer understanding
- keep the interaction task-focused
- escalate if the issue is repeated, unsafe, unauthorized or unresolved

### Evidence
- peer-support note or assessor observation

## Phase 8 — End-of-shift handoff
The candidate delivers a written and verbal handoff that includes:
- current highest-risk issue
- what was confirmed vs what remains a hypothesis
- actions completed
- crop/process status
- deviations/holds/escalations
- pending work
- location of evidence/records
- next required measurement/check
- named role or work queue that owns each unresolved action

Maximum verbal handoff: 5 minutes. The written handoff should be concise enough for operational use but detailed enough to reproduce the important reasoning.

# Dynamic assessor injects
Use at least three. Example injects:
1. a reference sensor disproves the controller's displayed condition
2. measured emitter output reveals a local delivery restriction
3. an IPM trap was moved, weakening a trend comparison
4. a lot movement record expands postharvest exposure scope
5. a metric denominator changed from the comparison period
6. the peer is using an outdated printed instruction
7. a newly observed symptom appears in a second room

At least one inject must force the candidate to reprioritize. At least one must force the candidate to revise a conclusion.

# Scoring rubric — 200 points
| Domain | Points | Full-credit evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Shift triage and reprioritization | 20 | Orders work by risk/urgency and adapts when evidence changes |
| Data quality and sensor/equipment verification | 20 | Independently verifies questionable data and distinguishes command from response |
| Crop differential diagnosis | 30 | Multi-factor, evidence-ranked reasoning with comparison and hypothesis revision |
| Fertigation/irrigation/root-zone troubleshooting | 25 | Correct measurements/calculations and defensible fault classification |
| IPM trend interpretation | 20 | Comparable trend analysis, spatial reasoning and appropriate follow-up |
| Deviation/traceability scope | 25 | Accurate timeline, affected scope, record preservation and escalation |
| Production metrics | 15 | Correct formulas, denominators, units and operational interpretation |
| Peer task support | 10 | Specific, respectful SOP-based correction and verification of understanding |
| Documentation quality | 15 | Reproducible records separating facts, hypotheses and decisions |
| Final handoff | 20 | Prioritized, concise, supervisor-ready ownership and next-action summary |

**Overall pass:** 160/200 (80%) and no critical failure.

In addition, the candidate must earn at least 60% of points in each of these essential domains:
- data quality/sensor verification
- crop differential diagnosis
- irrigation/root-zone troubleshooting
- deviation/traceability scope
- final handoff

# Critical failures
Any of the following results in a capstone failure regardless of point total:
- unsafe bypass, unauthorized repair, or knowingly unsafe work contrary to the supplied procedure
- falsification, deletion, concealment or intentional alteration of source records
- loss of crop/product identity or intentional mixing of statuses during a traceability/deviation case
- broad corrective action taken despite known contradictory evidence when that action creates material crop/process risk
- failure to act on an explicit serious escalation threshold in the supplied scenario
- representing a disproven hypothesis as confirmed in the final handoff

# Performance descriptors
## Distinguished
Candidate builds a coherent model of the shift, verifies weak evidence early, uses discriminating checks, reprioritizes smoothly, quantifies decisions correctly, protects traceability, and gives a concise handoff that another senior technician can execute without redoing the investigation.

## Competent
Candidate reaches defensible conclusions, completes required checks/calculations, corrects reasoning when new evidence appears, keeps records traceable, and delivers an actionable handoff with no critical failure.

## Not yet competent
Candidate repeatedly diagnoses from appearance alone, trusts controller records without physical verification, applies calculations to incompatible denominators, loses affected-scope logic, fails to revise conclusions after contradictory evidence, or leaves unresolved work without clear ownership.

# Assessor record
The assessor records:
- form/scenario ID
- date and candidate ID
- evidence files reviewed
- points by domain
- critical-failure check
- observed strengths
- observed gaps
- final result
- assessor name/signature or authenticated electronic equivalent

# Candidate reflection
After scoring, candidate answers:
1. Which initial assumption changed most during the shift?
2. Which measurement prevented an incorrect action?
3. Which unresolved issue should receive first attention on the next shift?
4. Which part of your documentation would be hardest for another technician to reproduce, and how would you improve it?
