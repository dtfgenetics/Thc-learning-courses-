# Technician II Practical F — Postharvest Deviation & Lot Scope

## Purpose
Demonstrate that the candidate can investigate a postharvest process deviation, determine the defensible affected scope from time/location/lot evidence, preserve product and records, and communicate a clear evidence package for disposition decisions.

## Tasks and competencies
- `TASK-CULT2-POSTHARVEST-001`
- `TASK-CULT2-DEVIATION-001`
- `COMP-POSTHARVEST-PROCESS-001`
- `COMP-POSTHARVEST-QA-001`
- `COMP-PRO-SOP-001`
- `COMP-PRO-QA-001`

## Assessment format
The assessor supplies a postharvest case involving a dry-room, storage, lot-traceability, weighing, handling, or process-record exception. The candidate must determine what is known, what is potentially affected, and what still requires verification.

Recommended assessment time: 60–75 minutes.

## Candidate receives
- lot/batch identities and genealogy
- harvest and room-entry timestamps
- rack/zone locations
- environmental or process trend data
- product-condition observations at multiple locations
- task, movement, or access records
- relevant SOP/work instruction and version
- deviation trigger or alert
- comparison lot or unaffected zone data

## Candidate must perform
1. Define the deviation using facts: what parameter/process differed, when it began, duration if known, where it occurred, and how it was detected.
2. Verify source data identity, units, timestamps, gaps and instrument status.
3. Construct a timeline linking the deviation to product movement and location.
4. Trace which lots/racks/containers were physically present during the relevant interval.
5. Compare affected, potentially affected and clearly outside-scope material using documented criteria.
6. Review product-condition evidence without assuming that absence of visible change proves absence of process exposure.
7. Identify whether additional sampling, measurement, record reconstruction, or QA review is required.
8. Apply the supplied hold/segregation/status process as required by the training case.
9. Avoid unsupported disposition decisions; prepare evidence for the role that owns release/rework/disposition.
10. Produce a Postharvest Deviation Scope Report.

## Required analysis tasks
Each case must include at least two of:
- excursion duration from timestamped records
- percentage of lots/racks/containers potentially affected
- difference between affected-zone and comparison-zone environmental values
- process yield or weight reconciliation from supplied records
- percentage-point comparison of defect/exception observations

## Scope model
The candidate should classify material using three levels:
- **Confirmed exposure:** evidence places the material in the affected process/location/time window.
- **Potential exposure:** records are incomplete or boundary conditions are uncertain, so exposure cannot yet be excluded.
- **Outside current scope:** evidence demonstrates the material was outside the affected time/location/process boundary.

The candidate must state the evidence for each boundary. The goal is neither to minimize scope nor to automatically place all inventory in scope; the goal is a defensible, traceable determination.

## Assessor injects
After the first scope determination, provide one new fact such as:
- one rack was moved during the excursion
- a logger has a missing interval
- a comparison sensor shows the event was zone-specific
- a lot genealogy record reveals split material
- an operator record conflicts with the electronic movement timestamp

The candidate must revise the timeline/scope or explain why it remains unchanged.

## Scoring rubric — 100 points
| Domain | Points | Full-credit evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Deviation definition | 10 | Specific fact-based description with parameter/process, time and location |
| Data-quality review | 10 | Identifies gaps, timestamp conflicts and measurement limitations |
| Timeline reconstruction | 15 | Correctly orders event, product movements and observations |
| Lot/genealogy scope | 25 | Defensible confirmed/potential/outside-scope classification |
| Product/process interpretation | 15 | Uses trend and condition evidence without overclaiming |
| Immediate control/status handling | 10 | Preserves identity/status and follows supplied hold/segregation process |
| Calculation accuracy | 5 | Correct math and denominator |
| Deviation report/handoff | 10 | Clear evidence package, open questions and decision ownership |

**Pass standard:** 80/100 and no critical error.

## Critical errors
- intentionally mixing material statuses or losing lot identity
- changing source records to remove a discrepancy
- releasing or disposing material when the supplied process assigns that decision to another role
- excluding material from scope despite direct evidence that it was present during the relevant exposure window
- failing to preserve an unresolved material-status concern during handoff

## Evidence produced
- Postharvest Deviation Scope Report
- lot/rack exposure map
- event timeline
- genealogy trace
- calculation sheet
- open-question and follow-up list
- QA/supervisor handoff

## Assessor closeout questions
1. What evidence defines the beginning and end of your affected window?
2. Which material is only potentially affected, and why?
3. What new evidence could reduce or expand the current scope?
4. Which conclusion belongs to you and which belongs to QA/supervisory disposition?
