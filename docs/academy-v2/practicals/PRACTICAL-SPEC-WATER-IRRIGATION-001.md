# Advanced Water Quality and Irrigation Specialist Practical

**Credential:** THC Advanced Water Quality and Irrigation Certificate  
**Competencies:** `COMP-WATER-QUALITY-ADV-001`, `COMP-IRRIGATION-ADV-001`

## Purpose

Assess whether the candidate can integrate source-water chemistry, meter quality, irrigation distribution, scheduling, root-zone response and mass balance into a defensible irrigation decision.

## Scenario

A facility changes water source seasonally. The new source arrives at similar pH but higher alkalinity. One flowering zone develops rising root-zone EC and variable dryback despite a stable feed recipe. Emitter measurements reveal some variation, and the recirculating reservoir is managed largely from bulk EC without complete inflow/drain records.

## Candidate tasks

1. Compare source-water pH, alkalinity, EC and relevant composition evidence and explain why matching pH does not mean equivalent buffering behavior.
2. Review meter verification/calibration evidence and identify whether supplied measurements are decision-ready.
3. Calculate average emitter output and a simple uniformity indicator from supplied data.
4. Map low/high delivery positions and compare them with plant/root-zone outcomes.
5. Evaluate irrigation timing/frequency relative to substrate water-holding behavior and crop demand.
6. Interpret feed-to-root-zone pH/EC trends without assuming input values equal root-zone conditions.
7. Calculate applied volume, drain fraction and reservoir make-up where data are supplied.
8. Explain how stable bulk EC in recirculation can coexist with changing ion composition.
9. Rank source-water, delivery, scheduling, uptake and measurement-quality hypotheses.
10. Select targeted verification checks before changing recipe or system-wide settings.
11. Produce a corrective plan defining action, expected response, verification measurements and stop/escalation conditions.

## Evidence packet

- source-water analysis for two sources
- alkalinity data
- meter verification/calibration records
- emitter catch-test results
- irrigation schedule
- substrate/root-zone trends
- feed and drain pH/EC records
- reservoir volume and make-up history
- crop observation map

## Calculations

Candidate should perform:
- average emitter output
- percent deviation from mean or target
- applied volume versus drain volume
- drain fraction
- reservoir make-up volume
- feed-to-root-zone EC difference
- trend comparison over multiple irrigation cycles

## Evidence injects

### Inject A — biased meter
A reference check shows one field meter outside tolerance. Candidate should quarantine the questionable dataset or remeasure before major action.

### Inject B — low-output zone
Catch testing confirms one lateral has materially reduced output. Candidate should address delivery before assuming a room-wide nutrient problem.

### Inject C — composition shift
Laboratory source-water data show seasonal ion changes despite similar bulk EC. Candidate should explain why EC alone cannot establish equivalent composition.
