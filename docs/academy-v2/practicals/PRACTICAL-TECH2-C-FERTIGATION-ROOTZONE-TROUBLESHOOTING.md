# Technician II Practical C — Fertigation & Root-Zone Troubleshooting

## Purpose
Demonstrate that the candidate can execute an approved fertigation batch, verify the delivered solution, interpret root-zone and irrigation evidence, and troubleshoot whether a crop-water problem originates in recipe execution, delivery uniformity, irrigation timing, substrate behavior, or plant demand.

## Tasks and competencies
- `TASK-CULT2-FERTIGATION-001`
- `TASK-CULT2-ROOTZONE-001`
- `COMP-WATER-QUALITY-ADV-001`
- `COMP-IRRIGATION-ADV-001`
- `COMP-NUTRIENT-DIAG-001`
- `COMP-PRO-QA-001`

## Assessment format
The assessment combines a hands-on or simulated batch-verification station with a trend-analysis case. The candidate is given an approved recipe/work order; they are not expected to design a nutrient program.

Recommended assessment time: 75–90 minutes.

## Candidate receives
- approved batch/work-order instructions
- source-water information
- identified input materials and measuring equipment
- calibrated/verified pH and EC instruments or their verification records
- target batch volume and target acceptance ranges
- representative emitter/output data
- irrigation event timestamps, duration or volume data
- root-zone measurements or substrate water-content/EC trends
- crop stage and representative crop observations
- affected and comparison-zone information
- applicable sanitation, mixing, measurement and deviation procedures

## Candidate must perform
### Part 1 — Batch execution and verification
1. Confirm recipe/work-order identity, version, target batch volume and material identities before beginning.
2. Verify measuring equipment and pH/EC instruments are suitable for use.
3. Follow the prescribed addition/mixing sequence and record actual quantities added.
4. Bring the batch to its approved final volume using the work instruction.
5. Obtain a representative sample using the prescribed sampling method.
6. Measure and document EC and pH with units, timestamp and instrument identity.
7. Compare actual results with the approved target or acceptance range.
8. If the batch is outside specification, stop the release decision, identify likely execution/measurement causes, and follow the deviation path rather than concealing the result.

### Part 2 — Delivery and root-zone troubleshooting
1. Compare source/batch values with representative point-of-delivery measurements.
2. Calculate delivery deviation or coefficient measures supplied by the assessor.
3. Examine spatial output pattern for clogged, restricted, leaking, pressure-related, or localized delivery problems.
4. Interpret root-zone trend timing: post-irrigation response, daytime dryback, overnight behavior, cumulative EC movement, and plant demand where those measurements are available.
5. Distinguish among likely categories: solution/batch issue, distribution issue, schedule/timing issue, excessive or insufficient applied volume, substrate hydraulic behavior, sensor/data problem, or demand mismatch.
6. Select the next discriminating verification step.
7. Recommend only the action supported by the evidence and applicable work instruction; document unresolved conditions for escalation.

## Calculation set
Each form must contain at least three calculations, including one rate/percentage calculation. Options include:
- dilution or quantity scaling from an approved recipe
- percent difference between target and measured EC
- emitter deviation = (measured − target) / target × 100
- irrigation volume per plant = total zone volume / number of emitters or plants
- dryback percentage-point change between two substrate readings
- percent dryback relative to a stated starting value
- runoff/drain fraction = collected drain volume / applied volume × 100 when that metric is part of the supplied SOP

The assessment should score the candidate on correct units, calculation method, and interpretation. It should not reward a number that is mathematically correct but applied to the wrong sample or time window.

## Diagnostic case pattern
A high-quality case should contain a conflict such as:
- batch EC and pH are within range but one irrigation lateral has low measured output
- average room delivery is normal but affected plants cluster at line ends
- delivery is uniform but root-zone EC rises over several cycles as dryback increases
- root-zone sensor trend appears abnormal but gravimetric/manual comparison does not support it
- scheduled irrigation commands are present while actual flow evidence is missing

## Assessor injects
After the candidate's first conclusion, provide one new measurement that either changes the leading hypothesis or narrows the affected scope. The candidate must explain whether the evidence changes immediate action, additional checks, and escalation.

## Scoring rubric — 100 points
| Domain | Points | Full-credit evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Work-order/material verification | 10 | Correct identity, version, target and material checks |
| Batch execution/documentation | 15 | Accurate prescribed sequence, quantities and traceable record |
| pH/EC measurement quality | 15 | Representative sample, valid instrument use, units and result interpretation |
| Delivery-system analysis | 15 | Uses measured output and spatial pattern to classify distribution problems |
| Root-zone trend interpretation | 20 | Correctly relates irrigation timing, dryback, EC and plant response |
| Calculations | 10 | Correct method, units and meaning |
| Troubleshooting decision | 10 | Chooses evidence-discriminating next step and proportionate action |
| Deviation/handoff | 5 | Preserves out-of-spec evidence and communicates next ownership |

**Pass standard:** 80/100 and no critical error.

## Critical errors
- using the wrong identified material after the identity check should have caught the error
- intentionally releasing an out-of-spec batch as acceptable
- fabricating pH, EC, volume, or material-use records
- changing multiple delivery or program variables without preserving the baseline or identifying the reason
- creating a preventable cross-contamination or unsafe chemical-handling condition contrary to the supplied SOP

## Evidence produced
- fertigation batch record
- pH/EC verification record
- emitter/delivery measurement sheet
- root-zone trend interpretation
- calculation sheet
- troubleshooting decision tree
- deviation or supervisor handoff when required

## Assessor closeout questions
1. What evidence separates a batch problem from a distribution problem?
2. Why does a controller irrigation event not by itself prove water reached every plant?
3. Which trend would make you question whether elevated root-zone EC is an accumulation problem rather than a stronger input solution?
4. What single measurement would you repeat first on the next shift?
